# Chemistry 2e — Chapter 3: Composition of Substances and Solutions

> Source: https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/3-introduction

Quantitative aspects of the composition of substances (such as a calcium-containing compound) and mixtures (such as pool water) are the subject of this chapter. Proper management of a system like a swimming pool requires precise additions of compounds in measured quantities — for example, calcium ion (Ca²⁺) concentrations must stay within specific ranges, which requires knowing both the relative amount of Ca²⁺ within the ionic compound added and the volume of water it is dissolved into.

## 3.1 Formula Mass and the Mole Concept

### Formula Mass

Formula mass is calculated by summing the average atomic masses of all atoms represented in a substance's chemical formula, expressed in atomic mass units (amu).

#### Formula Mass for Covalent Substances

For covalent compounds, formula mass may be called **molecular mass** since the formula represents a single molecule.

- **Chloroform (CHCl₃):** C 1×12.01 = 12.01; H 1×1.008 = 1.008; Cl 3×35.45 = 106.35 → molecular mass = **119.37 amu**
- **Ibuprofen (C₁₃H₁₈O₂):** C 13×12.01 = 156.13; H 18×1.008 = 18.144; O 2×16.00 = 32.00 → molecular mass = **206.27 amu**

#### Formula Mass for Ionic Compounds

Ionic compound formula mass is calculated the same way but does not represent a discrete molecule. Use average atomic masses of neutral atoms; electron mass differences from ionization are negligible.

- **Sodium chloride (NaCl):** Na 22.99 + Cl 35.45 → formula mass = **58.44 amu**
- **Aluminum sulfate (Al₂(SO₄)₃, i.e. Al₂S₃O₁₂):** Al 2×26.98 = 53.96; S 3×32.06 = 96.18; O 12×16.00 = 192.00 → formula mass = **342.14 amu**

### The Mole

The mole is an amount unit similar to familiar counting units (pair, dozen, gross). It provides a specific measure of the number of atoms or molecules in a sample.

**One mole contains 6.02214076 × 10²³ discrete entities.** This constant is **Avogadro's number (Nₐ)**, conventionally reported as **6.022 × 10²³ /mol** (i.e. 6.022 × 10²³ mol⁻¹).

### Molar Mass

The molar mass of an element (or compound) is the mass in grams of 1 mole of that substance. Units: **g/mol**.

**Key relationship:** the molar mass of any substance (in g/mol) is numerically equivalent to its atomic or formula mass (in amu).

Examples: ¹²C → 12 g/mol; C → 12.01 g/mol; H → 1.008 g/mol; O → 16.00 g/mol; Na → 22.99 g/mol; Cl → 35.45 g/mol.

### Mole–Mass–Number Conversions (Elements)

- **Grams → moles:** moles = mass (g) ÷ molar mass (g/mol).
  - 4.7 g K ÷ 39.10 g/mol = **0.12 mol K**; 3.24 g Be ÷ 9.01 g/mol = **0.360 mol Be**
- **Moles → grams:** mass (g) = moles × molar mass (g/mol).
  - 9.2×10⁻⁴ mol Ar × 39.95 g/mol = **0.037 g Ar**; 2.561 mol Au × 196.97 g/mol = **504.5 g Au**
- **Grams → number of atoms (two steps):** atoms = (mass ÷ molar mass) × Nₐ.
  - 5.00 g Cu: (5.00 ÷ 63.55) × 6.022×10²³ = **4.74×10²² Cu atoms**; 15.00 g Au → **4.586×10²² Au atoms**

### Mole–Mass–Number Conversions (Compounds)

- **Grams → moles:** moles = mass ÷ molar mass.
  - Glycine (C₂H₅O₂N), molar mass 75.07 g/mol (C 24.02 + H 5.040 + O 32.00 + N 14.007): 28.35 g ÷ 75.07 = **0.378 mol**
  - Sucrose (C₁₂H₂₂O₁₁), 342.30 g/mol: 25 g → **0.073 mol**
- **Moles → grams:** mass = moles × molar mass.
  - Vitamin C (C₆H₈O₆), 176.124 g/mol: 1.42×10⁻⁴ mol → **0.0250 g**
  - Hydrazine (N₂H₄), 32.05 g/mol: 0.443 mol → **14.2 g**
- **Grams → molecules/atoms (three steps):** molecules = (mass ÷ molar mass) × Nₐ; atoms = molecules × (atoms per molecule).
  - Saccharin (C₇H₅NO₃S), 183.18 g/mol: 40.0 mg (0.0400 g) → (0.0400 ÷ 183.18) × 6.022×10²³ = **1.31×10²⁰ molecules**; C atoms = ×7 = **9.17×10²⁰**
  - Butane (C₄H₁₀), 58.12 g/mol: 9.213 g → **9.545×10²² molecules**; H atoms = ×10 = **9.545×10²³**

## 3.2 Determining Empirical and Molecular Formulas

### Percent Composition

Percent composition is the percentage by mass of each element in a compound:

%Element = (mass of element ÷ mass of compound) × 100%

Example: a 10.0 g gas with 2.5 g H and 7.5 g C → %H = 25%, %C = 75%.

**Example 3.9 (from experimental data):** 12.04 g sample with 7.34 g C, 1.85 g H, 2.85 g N → %C = 61.0%, %H = 15.4%, %N = 23.7%. (Check Your Learning: 12.1% C, 16.1% O, 71.79% Cl.)

### Percent Composition from a Formula

When the formula is known, percent composition derives from atomic/molar masses.

**Example 3.10 (aspirin, C₉H₈O₄, molar mass 180.159 g/mol):**
- %C = (9 × 12.01) / 180.159 × 100% = 60.00%
- %H = (8 × 1.008) / 180.159 × 100% = 4.476%
- %O = (4 × 16.00) / 180.159 × 100% = 35.52%
(Check Your Learning: 69.9% Fe in Fe₂O₃.)

### Determining the Empirical Formula

The empirical formula gives the relative numbers (not masses) of atoms. Three-step method:
1. Derive moles of each element by dividing its mass by its molar mass.
2. Divide each molar amount by the smallest value to get tentative subscripts.
3. If subscripts are not whole numbers, multiply all by a common integer.

- **CH₂:** 1.71 g C → 0.142 mol; 0.287 g H → 0.284 mol. ÷0.142 → C₁H₂ = **CH₂**.
- **Cl₂O₇ (decimal case):** 5.31 g Cl → 0.150 mol; 8.40 g O → 0.525 mol. ÷0.150 → Cl₁O₃.₅; ×2 → **Cl₂O₇**.
- **Example 3.11 (hematite):** 34.97 g Fe → 0.6261 mol; 15.03 g O → 0.9394 mol. ÷0.6261 → Fe₁O₁.₅; ×2 → **Fe₂O₃**. (Check Your Learning: N₂O₅.)

### Empirical Formula from Percent Composition

When percentages are given instead of absolute masses, assume a convenient sample mass (typically 100 g) so percentages become grams, then apply the standard method.

**Example 3.12 (CO₂):** 27.29% C, 72.71% O. In 100 g: 27.29 g C → 2.272 mol; 72.71 g O → 4.544 mol. ÷2.272 → C₁O₂ = **CO₂**. (Check Your Learning: CH₂O.)

### Deriving the Molecular Formula

The empirical formula shows only relative numbers; determining absolute atoms per molecule requires both the empirical formula and the molecular/molar mass:

n = (molecular or molar mass) / (empirical formula mass)

where n = number of empirical formula units per molecule. Then (AₓBᵧ)ₙ = AₙₓBₙᵧ.

- **Glucose example:** CH₂O (empirical mass ~30 amu); if molecular mass = 180 amu, n = 180/30 = 6 → (CH₂O)₆ = **C₆H₁₂O₆**.
- **Example 3.13 (nicotine):** 74.02% C, 8.710% H, 17.27% N; a 40.57 g sample is 0.2500 mol. In 100 g: 6.163 mol C, 8.641 mol H, 1.233 mol N. ÷1.233 → C₅H₇N (empirical mass 81.13 g/mol). Molar mass = 40.57 g / 0.2500 mol = 162.3 g/mol; n = 162.3 / 81.13 = 2 → (C₅H₇N)₂ = **C₁₀H₁₄N₂**. (Check Your Learning: C₈H₁₀N₄O₂ — caffeine.)

## 3.3 Molarity

### Solutions, Solvent, Solute, Concentration

A **solution** is a homogeneous mixture: its composition (and therefore its properties) is uniform throughout. The relative amount of any component is its **concentration**. The **solvent** is the component present in much greater amount (the dispersing medium); the **solute** is present at much lower concentration. Solutions with water as solvent are **aqueous solutions**. Qualitative descriptors: **dilute** (relatively low concentration) and **concentrated** (relatively high concentration).

### Molarity (M)

Molarity is the number of moles of solute per liter of solution:

M = (mol solute) / (L solution)

- **Example 3.14:** 355 mL soft drink with 0.133 mol sucrose → 0.133 mol / 0.355 L = **0.375 M**. (Check: 0.01 mol in 200 mL = 0.05 M.)
- **Example 3.15 (M → moles):** mol solute = M × L. 0.375 M × 0.010 L = **0.004 mol sugar**. (Check: 80 mL.)
- **Example 3.16 (mass → M):** 0.500 L vinegar with 25.2 g acetic acid (CH₃CO₂H, 60.052 g/mol): (25.2/60.052) / 0.500 = **0.839 M**. (Check: 6.52 g CoCl₂ at 128.9 g/mol in 75.0 mL = 0.674 M.)
- **Example 3.17 (M, V → mass):** NaCl in 0.250 L of 5.30 M: mol = 5.30 × 0.250 = 1.325 mol; ×58.44 g/mol = **77.4 g NaCl**. (Check: 250.0 mL of 0.200 M CaCl₂ at 110.98 g/mol = 5.55 g.) Retain guard digits in intermediate steps to avoid rounding errors.
- **Example 3.18 (single-step volume):** volume holding 75.6 g acetic acid at 0.839 M: 75.6 g × (1 mol/60.05 g) × (1 L/0.839 mol) = **1.50 L**. (Check: 66.0 g KBr at 1.50 M = 0.370 L.)

### Dilution of Solutions

Dilution lowers concentration by adding solvent without changing the amount of solute. Since moles of solute are conserved (n₁ = n₂) and n = M·L:

M₁V₁ = M₂V₂  →  general dilution equation: **C₁V₁ = C₂V₂**

(C = concentration, V = volume; any consistent units allowed.)

- **Example 3.19 (find C₂):** 0.850 L of 5.00 M Cu(NO₃)₂ diluted to 1.80 L → C₂ = (5.00 × 0.850)/1.80 = **2.36 M**. (Check: 25.0 mL of 2.04 M CH₃OH → 500.0 mL = 0.102 M.)
- **Example 3.20 (find V₂):** from 11 mL (0.011 L) of 0.45 M HBr at 0.12 M → V₂ = (0.45 × 0.011)/0.12 = **0.041 L**. (Check: 0.250 L of 1.88 M HNO₃ → 0.125 M = 3.76 L.)
- **Example 3.21 (find V₁):** volume of 1.59 M KOH to make 5.00 L of 0.100 M → V₁ = (0.100 × 5.00)/1.59 = **0.314 L**. (Check: 50.00 mL of 3.00 M glucose → 0.575 M = 0.261 L.)

## 3.4 Other Units for Solution Concentrations

### Mass Percentage

mass percentage = (mass of component / mass of solution) × 100%

Also called percent mass / percent weight / weight-weight percent (w/w)% / %mass. Any mass unit may be used, but the same unit must apply to solute and solution so units cancel.

- **Example 3.22:** 5.0 g spinal fluid with 3.75 mg (0.00375 g) glucose → (0.00375/5.0) × 100% = **0.075% glucose**.
- **Example 3.23:** concentrated HCl, 37.2% HCl, density 1.19 g/mL; mass of HCl in 0.500 L: 500 mL × 1.19 g/mL = 595 g solution; 595 g × (37.2/100) = **221 g HCl**.

### Volume Percentage

volume percentage = (volume of solute / volume of solution) × 100%   (also %vol or (v/v)%)

- **Example 3.24:** rubbing alcohol 70%vol, isopropanol density 0.785 g/mL; mass in 355 mL: 355 mL × (70/100) = 248.5 mL; × 0.785 g/mL = **195 g isopropyl alcohol**.

### Mass-Volume Percentage

A mixed unit: solute mass per solution volume, expressed as a percentage (m/v). Common in biochemical/medical contexts.
- Physiological saline 0.9% (m/v) = 0.9 g solute per 100 mL solution.
- Blood glucose is reported as mg/dL (mg per 100 mL blood); normal fasting range ≈ 70–100 mg/dL.

### Parts Per Million (ppm) and Parts Per Billion (ppb)

For very low (trace) concentrations such as pollutants in water:

ppm = (mass of solute / mass of solution) × 10⁶
ppb = (mass of solute / mass of solution) × 10⁹

Relationship: **1 ppm = 10³ ppb** (ppm is 1000× ppb). Regulatory context: EPA maximum safe fluoride in tap water = 4 ppm; lead triggers remedial action at 15 ppb.

- **Example 3.25:** 15 ppb lead → 15 ppb × (1 ppm / 10³ ppb) = **0.015 ppm**. Mass of lead in a 300 mL glass of water at 15 ppb: solution mass = 300 mL × 1.00 g/mL = 300 g; mass solute = (15 × 300)/10⁹ = 4.5×10⁻⁶ g = **4.5 μg**.
