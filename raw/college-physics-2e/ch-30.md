## 30.0 Introduction

Atoms are matter's fundamental building blocks — invisible to the eye yet able to explain phenomena throughout nature. The chapter traces how atoms were discovered, how their internal parts (electrons, nuclei) were identified, and how quantum mechanics explains atomic organization, spectra, and interactions. Key threads: quantization (discrete, step-like energy states), atomic excitation/de-excitation, X-ray origins, quantum numbers, and the Pauli exclusion principle. Applications include scanning tunneling microscopy, X-ray imaging, atomic spectroscopy, and quantum-aware electronic device design.

## 30.1 Discovery of the Atom

**Objective:** Describe the fundamental architecture of the atom and how it constitutes matter's structure.

**Terms:**
- **Atom** — historically the smallest indivisible unit of matter (Greek concept); modern view reveals internal substructure.
- **Brownian motion** — random movement of small suspended particles, driven by collisions with thermally agitated fluid molecules.
- **Avogadro's number** — fixed count of atoms/molecules per mole (named for Amedeo Avogadro).
- **Kinetic theory of gases** — explains gas behavior via random thermal motion of atoms/molecules.

**Conceptual framework / evidence progression:**
- Greek atomic hypothesis (5th c. BCE): matter cannot be subdivided infinitely.
- Chemical mass ratios: reactants combine in consistent mass proportions → discrete fundamental units.
- Periodic systematics: element properties recur periodically (Mendeleev), enabling prediction of unknown elements.
- Indirect evidence (19th c.): chemical mass ratios + kinetic theory consistency.
- Nearly-direct (1827): Robert Brown observed pollen-grain motion under microscope.
- Direct (1905+): Einstein's mathematical treatment of Brownian motion; Perrin's experiments confirmed it, allowing calculation of atomic/molecular dimensions (~10⁻¹⁰ m) and a precise Avogadro's number.

**Applications:** mass spectrometry; scanning tunneling microscopy (imaging individual atoms).

## 30.2 Discovery of the Parts of the Atom: Electrons and Nuclei

**Objectives:** Explain discovery of the electron; analyze Millikan oil-drop method; describe Rutherford's alpha-scattering; summarize Rutherford's planetary model.

**Terms:**
- **Cathode rays** — electron streams from a negative electrode accelerated toward a positive one in a low-pressure tube.
- **Charge-to-mass ratio (q/m)** — ratio of a particle's charge to its inertial mass.
- **Alpha particles** — doubly-charged helium nuclei from radioactive decay (~5 MeV).
- **Planetary model** — light electrons orbiting a dense, massive nucleus.

**Principles:**
- Deflection of charged particles is proportional to applied force; acceleration depends on force/mass.
- Crossed-field velocity selection: when perpendicular E and B fields balance, v = E/B.
- Quantization of charge: charge exists only in integer multiples of a fundamental unit.

**Equations:**
- F = qₑE
- a = F/mₑ
- qₑ/mₑ = a/E ; also qₑ/mₑ = a/(vB)
- Millikan equilibrium: m_drop·g = qₑ·E
- Electron charge: q = (m_drop·g·d)/V
- Electron mass: mₑ = qₑ / (qₑ/mₑ)

**Worked examples (concepts):**
- **Thomson's gas-discharge study** — cathode rays carry momentum and deflect in magnetic fields consistent with negative charge → particles smaller than atoms exist.
- **Thomson's q/m measurement** — crossed E and B fields gave qₑ/mₑ ≈ −1.76×10¹¹ C/kg → electrons have very small mass per unit charge.
- **Millikan oil-drop** — balancing gravity vs. electric force on charged droplets directly measured electron charge ≈ −1.60×10⁻¹⁹ C, proving charge quantization.
- **Rutherford alpha scattering** — alpha particles on thin gold foil: most passed through, but rare large-angle scatters revealed a tiny (~10⁻¹⁵ m) massive nucleus, ~10⁵× smaller than the atom.

**Key values:** mₑ = 9.11×10⁻³¹ kg; m_p = 1.67×10⁻²⁷ kg ≈ 1836·mₑ; nuclear diameter ~10⁻¹⁵ m; atomic diameter ~10⁻¹⁰ m; nuclear density ~10¹⁵ g/cm³.

## 30.3 Bohr's Theory of the Hydrogen Atom

**Objectives:** Explain puzzling atomic spectra; Bohr's hydrogen model; planetary orbits + energy-level diagrams; triumphs and limitations.

**Terms:**
- **Quantized orbits** — only certain orbits are allowed; electrons cannot exist at arbitrary radii.
- **Angular-momentum quantization** — orbital angular momentum takes only integer multiples of h/2π.
- **Energy levels** — discrete energy states for allowed orbits, shown in energy-level diagrams.
- **Hydrogen-like atoms** — single-electron systems with nuclear charge Z.
- **Ground state** — lowest-energy orbit (n=1).
- **Spectral series** — line groupings by final orbit: Lyman (n_f=1), Balmer (n_f=2), Paschen (n_f=3).

**Laws / equations:**
- Photon emitted/absorbed in a transition: ΔE = hf = E_i − E_f
- Rydberg formula: 1/λ = R(1/n_f² − 1/n_i²), R = 1.097×10⁷ m⁻¹
- Coulomb = centripetal: mₑv²/r_n = kZqₑ²/r_n²
- Angular-momentum quantization: L = mₑvr_n = nh/2π (n = 1,2,3,…)
- Allowed radii: r_n = n²·(a_B/Z) ; Bohr radius a_B = h²/(4π²mₑkqₑ²) = 0.529×10⁻¹⁰ m
- Orbital energy: E_n = −(Z²/n²)·E₀
- Ground-state energy (H): E₀ = 13.6 eV → E_n = −13.6 eV/n²
- Transition photon: hf = 13.6 eV·(1/n_f² − 1/n_i²)

**Worked example (concept):** *Example 30.1* — applying the Rydberg formula to predict a hydrogen emission wavelength (the second Balmer line, n_i=4→n_f=2, at 486 nm). Shows the empirical formula predicts spectral lines; Bohr's theory explains *why*.

**Triumphs:** first quantitative explanation of hydrogen series; correct atomic dimensions; angular-momentum quantization; explains why electrons don't collapse into the nucleus; predicts 13.6 eV ionization energy.
**Limitations:** fails for multi-electron atoms (even helium); assumes definite circular orbits (reality = probability clouds); doesn't explain fine-structure doublets; semiclassical mix of classical orbits + quantum rules.

## 30.4 X-Rays: Atomic Origins and Applications

**Objectives:** Describe X-ray tube design/spectrum; characteristic X-ray production; medical applications; CT operation.

**Terms:**
- **Bremsstrahlung (braking radiation)** — continuous-spectrum X-rays from electrons decelerating in the anode.
- **Characteristic X-rays** — discrete photons from inner-shell vacancies filled by higher-shell transitions; element-specific.
- **X-ray tube** — a cathode-ray tube generating X-rays by bombarding a target anode.
- **Compton effect** — X-ray photon scatters off an outer electron, losing energy.
- **Computed tomography (CT/CAT)** — rotational X-ray projections + computer reconstruction → cross-sectional/3-D images.

**Principles:** max X-ray energy = incident electron kinetic energy; inner shells modeled hydrogen-like with effective charge Z_eff due to shielding; attenuation depends on photon energy and material density.

**Equations:**
- Max photon energy: E_max = hf_max = qₑV
- Hydrogen-like orbital energy: E_n = −(Z²/n²)·E₀ (E₀ = 13.6 eV)
- Characteristic Kα: E_Kα = ΔE = E₂ − E₁
- Wavelength: λ = hc/E

**Worked example (concept):** *Example 30.2* — using effective nuclear charge + hydrogen-like formula to estimate tungsten's (Z=74) Kα energy (~54.4 keV), explaining why inner-shell vacancies emit high-energy photons and why tungsten needs ~72.5 kV minimum accelerating voltage.

**Applications:** dental/skeletal imaging; foreign-object detection; mammography (supplemented by MRI); CT scanning; X-ray crystallography (von Laue 1912 proved X-rays are EM waves; DNA structure; materials science); radiotherapy; astronomy; X-ray fluorescence analysis of art/archaeology.

## 30.5 Applications of Atomic Excitations and De-Excitations

**Objectives:** Explain fluorescence; metastable states; laser coherent-light generation; population inversion; holography.

**Terms:**
- **Fluorescence** — absorption of a high-energy photon (often UV) then de-excitation via lower-energy (often visible) photons; emitted energy differs from absorbed.
- **Metastable state** — a level where electrons linger far longer than the typical ~10⁻⁸ s (ms to hours), because quantum-number differences inhibit fast de-excitation.
- **Phosphorescence** — slow de-excitation from metastable states (glow-in-the-dark).
- **Stimulated emission** — an incoming photon triggers an excited electron to emit an identical photon (same frequency, phase, direction, polarization).
- **Population inversion** — most atoms occupy an excited metastable state rather than the ground state; required for net laser output.
- **Holography** — recording true 3-D images via laser-light interference patterns ("lensless photography").

**Principles:**
- Material color arises from atomic energy-level spacing (absorb matching wavelengths, reflect others).
- Laser mechanism: (1) energy pumping excites many atoms; (2) population inversion builds in metastable states; (3) stimulated-emission cascade amplifies, with mirrors recirculating photons and one partial mirror releasing output.
- Einstein (1917): stimulated-emission and absorption probabilities are equal, so a majority in the excited state yields net amplification.

**Equations / energy relationships (He-Ne laser):** He first metastable ~20.61 eV; Ne first metastable ~20.66 eV; collisional energy transfer ~1.96 eV → 633 nm red output. Lower-energy transitions → longer wavelengths.

**Applications:** retinal repair and laser surgery (wavelength-selective absorption); dental lasers; CD/DVD pit reading; inertial-confinement fusion (laser heating + photon-momentum compression); holography (security, 3-D imaging, endoscopy). Laser power ranges from ~10⁻³ W (pointers) to >10¹² W (fusion); pulses as short as 10⁻¹⁴ s.

**History:** Einstein 1917 (stimulated emission); Maiman 1960 (first ruby laser); Townes/Basov/Prokhorov Nobel 1964; Gabor invented holography 1949 (Nobel 1971); Strickland & Mourou Nobel 2018 (chirped-pulse amplification).

## 30.6 The Wave Nature of Matter Causes Quantization

**Objectives:** State Bohr's model; explain angular-momentum quantization mathematically; characterize matter wave-particle duality.

**Terms:**
- **De Broglie wavelength** — wavelength associated with any particle (matter's wave nature).
- **Constructive interference (allowed orbits)** — orbit where the electron matter-wave fits an integer number of wavelengths around the circumference.
- **Destructive interference (forbidden orbits)** — orbit where no stable standing pattern forms.

**Core principle:** A confined electron behaves like a string fixed at both ends; it exists stably only where its de Broglie wavelength fits an integer number of times around the orbit. This standing-wave requirement *produces* quantized energy levels and prevents nuclear collapse.

**Equations:**
- Standing-wave condition: nλ_n = 2πr_n (n = 1,2,3,…)
- De Broglie wavelength: λ = h/p = h/(mₑv)
- Substituting: nh/(mₑv) = 2πr_n
- Derived angular-momentum quantization: L = mₑvr_n = nh/2π

**Interpretation:** only orbits with whole-number wavelength cycles survive; the n=1 orbit sets a minimum radius; the electron is best described as a probability cloud (consistent with Heisenberg uncertainty).

**Applications/extensions:** atomic spectra; blackbody radiation; particle-in-a-box; correspondence principle (macroscopic states so dense quantization is invisible); free (unbound) electrons have continuous energies.

## 30.7 Patterns in Spectra Reveal More Quantization

**Objectives:** Explain Zeeman effect; orbital magnetic fields; quantization of angular-momentum direction; space quantization.

**Terms:**
- **Zeeman effect** — splitting of spectral lines into multiple lines by an external magnetic field (Zeeman & Lorentz, Nobel 1902).
- **Orbital magnetic field (B_orb)** — field from an orbiting electron acting as a current loop.
- **Orbital angular momentum (L_orb)** — quantized in magnitude and direction.
- **Space quantization** — orbital angular momentum can point only in certain discrete directions relative to an external field.
- **Intrinsic spin (S)** — internal angular momentum independent of orbital motion, producing B_int.
- **Fine structure** — closely spaced doublets from spin-orbit interaction, present even without an applied field.
- **Spin states** — only two allowed orientations, "spin up" / "spin down."

**Principles:** orbiting electron = current loop (B_orb and L_orb co-axial); external field torques magnetic moments, shifting energies; only discrete angles allowed → multiple lines; both orbital and spin angular momenta have fixed magnitudes but quantized directions; spin-orbit coupling splits lines into doublets.

**Equations/relationships:** Zeeman splitting ∝ external field strength B; ΔE_mag = interaction energy between internal/orbital magnetic moments and the external field (discrete eigenvalues depend on quantum numbers).

**Phenomena:** basic Zeeman splitting (one line → 3 or 5 with field); fine-structure doublets with no field; electron spin makes ~54.7° angles with an external field (two states).

**Applications:** spectroscopy refinement; MRI (nuclear intrinsic magnetic fields in external fields); multiplet structure in high-resolution spectra.
**History:** Goudsmit & Uhlenbeck (1925) proposed electron intrinsic spin to explain fine structure.

## 30.8 Quantum Numbers and Rules

**Objectives:** Explain quantum numbers; compute angular-momentum direction angles; identify the spin quantum number.

**Terms / quantum numbers:**
- **Principal (n)** — labels energy states; n = 1,2,3,… (ground state n=1).
- **Angular momentum (l)** — magnitude of orbital angular momentum; l = 0…n−1.
- **Angular momentum projection (m_l)** — z-component; m_l = −l…+l in integer steps.
- **Spin (s)** — intrinsic; s = 1/2 for electrons (only value).
- **Spin projection (m_s)** — z-component; m_s = ±1/2.

**Principles:** energy states are quantized because particle wavelengths fit a bound system only in certain ways (E_n ∝ 1/n² for H); angular-momentum magnitude and direction are both quantized; intrinsic spin is quantized in magnitude and direction; correspondence principle restores classical continuity at large quantum numbers.

**Equations:**
- n = 1,2,3,…
- L = √[l(l+1)]·(h/2π), l = 0…n−1
- L_z = m_l·(h/2π), m_l = −l…+l
- cos θ = L_z / L
- S = √[s(s+1)]·(h/2π), s = 1/2 (electron)
- S_z = m_s·(h/2π), m_s = ±1/2

**Worked example (concept):** allowed angular-momentum directions for l=1 — three m_l values (−1,0,+1) give discrete angles 45°, 90°, 135° to the z-axis; the vector lies on cones, not arbitrary directions (space quantization).

**Applications:** the set (n,l,m_l,m_s) fully describes an electron's state; explains Zeeman/fine structure; underlies electron configuration and bonding; spin (half- vs. integer) classifies particle types.

## 30.9 The Pauli Exclusion Principle

**Objectives:** Describe atomic composition; state the Pauli exclusion principle; identify shell/subshell notation; place electrons in shells; connect periodic-table position to shell filling.

**Terms:**
- **Pauli exclusion principle** — no two electrons can have the same set of quantum numbers (n,l,m_l,m_s); no two electrons in the same state.
- **Atomic number (Z)** — number of electrons in a neutral atom; sets chemistry.
- **Shell** — orbitals sharing a principal quantum number n.
- **Subshell** — orbitals grouped by l within a shell; fill by increasing l.
- **Spectroscopic notation** — nl^x, with l as letters (s,p,d,f,g,h,i) and x = electron count.

**Principle:** electrons have spin s=1/2; identical half-integer-spin particles cannot share a quantum state → each orbital holds at most 2 electrons (opposite spins).

**Equations:**
- Max electrons per subshell: 2(2l+1) → s:2, p:6, d:10
- Max electrons per shell: 2n² → n=1:2, n=2:8, n=3:18

**Subshell symbols:** l=0→s, 1→p, 2→d, 3→f, 4→g, 5→h, 6→i.

**Worked examples (concepts):**
- *n=2 shell* — l=0,1 → 2s (2) + 2p (6) = 8 = 2(2²); full = 1s² 2s² 2p⁶.
- *n=3 shell* — l=0,1,2 → 3s(2)+3p(6)+3d(10) = 18 = 2(3²); full = 3s² 3p⁶ 3d¹⁰.

**Filling order:** lowest n first; within a shell lowest l first; orbital penetration matters (s-orbitals penetrate closest to the nucleus, so 4s fills before 3d — e.g., K = [Ar]4s¹).

**Periodic-table structure / chemistry:**
- Noble gases: filled outer subshells → high ionization energy, inert.
- Halogens: p⁵ (one vacancy) → highly reactive, form −1 ions.
- Alkali metals: s¹ outer electron → readily lost, form +1 ions, conductive.
- Transition elements: filling d-subshells → multiple oxidation states.
- Group 14 (C, Si, Ge): s²p² outer → similar chemistry, chain-forming.
- The periodic table's structure emerges naturally from quantum mechanics + Pauli exclusion.
