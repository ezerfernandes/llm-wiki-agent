Co-founder of [[google|Google]] (with [[LarryPage]]) — incorporated September 1998 out of a [[stanforduniversity|Stanford]] computer-science PhD project. Co-inventor of the **[[PageRank]]** algorithm — the stationary-distribution-of-a-teleporting-random-walk ranking signal that, combined with [[AnchorText|anchor-text]] propagation, gave early Google a measurable quality advantage over the keyword-matching search engines that preceded it.

Co-author of the foundational papers: *"The PageRank Citation Ranking: Bringing Order to the Web"* (Page, Brin, Motwani, Winograd 1999, Stanford TR) and *"The Anatomy of a Large-Scale Hypertextual Web Search Engine"* (Brin & Page 1998, WWW7) — the architectural blueprint of the original Google search engine including the dual inverted-index + repository design, the lexicon + posting lists, and the integration of PageRank with content-based scoring. Brin's PhD work pre-PageRank included data-mining for association rules.

Roles at Google: president of technology 2001–2011, head of Google X (the moonshot lab — Glass, self-driving cars, Loon balloon internet) 2011–2019, president of Alphabet Inc. 2015–2019. Returned to active involvement in 2023 around the [[Gemini]] LLM project as a hands-on technical contributor. Born in Moscow (1973); emigrated to the United States in 1979.
