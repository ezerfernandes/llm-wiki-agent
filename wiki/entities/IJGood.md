# I. J. Good (1916–2009)

**Irving John ("Jack") Good** was a British mathematician and statistician who worked as [[AlanTuring|Alan Turing]]'s statistical assistant at [[BletchleyPark|Bletchley Park]] during WWII, helping crack the German [[EnigmaMachine|Enigma]] cipher. With Turing's permission, he later published (in *Biometrika*, 1953) the analysis of the **missing-mass / unseen-species estimators** they had developed during the war — now known as the [[GoodTuringEstimator|Good-Turing estimator]]. Good framed the problem in terms of **"species"**, anticipating the ecological view of estimation that underlies modern [[SpeciesDiscovery|STADS]].

## Role in The Fuzzing Book
[[fuzzingbook-30-when-to-stop-fuzzing|Chapter 30]] credits Good as co-inventor (with Turing) of the estimator of "the probability of an event occurring that has never previously occurred," and notes that his 1953 *Biometrika* paper — talking about *species*, not trigrams — is what lets the same statistics apply to estimating the [[DiscoveryProbability|discovery probability]] of new program paths and the [[ResidualRisk|residual risk]] of a fuzzing campaign.
