# Ada Lovelace (1815–1852)

English mathematician, daughter of Lord Byron. Wrote extensive notes on [[CharlesBabbage|Charles Babbage]]'s [[AnalyticalEngine]] (1843), including what is widely considered the **first published algorithm intended for execution by a machine** — a procedure for computing Bernoulli numbers.

Per [[dis-5-1-history|*Dive into Systems* Ch 5.1]], Lovelace is the **first programmer** in the lineage Ch 5.1 traces. She also articulated — far ahead of her time — the conceptual distinction between **what a programmable machine *can* be commanded to do** versus **what it *originates*:**

> "The Analytical Engine has no pretensions whatever to originate anything. It can do whatever we know how to order it to perform."

This *programming-as-instruction* framing anticipates by a century the [[VonNeumannArchitecture|stored-program]] model and remains relevant to modern debates about the limits of machine intelligence.
