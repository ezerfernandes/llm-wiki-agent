## Overview
Henry Cavendish (1731–1810) was an English natural philosopher who, in 1798, performed the first accurate laboratory measurement of the gravitational constant G — more than a century after [[IsaacNewton]] published the [[NewtonsLawOfUniversalGravitation|law of universal gravitation]].

## Contributions
- **Measuring G:** used a torsion balance to detect the tiny gravitational attraction between ordinary-sized masses (tens of kilograms), reading the deflection via a mirror-mounted suspension. His value of G is within about 1% of the modern figure (G = 6.674 × 10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg²).
- **"Weighing the Earth":** knowing G, surface gravity g, and Earth's radius r allowed Earth's mass to be computed from M = gr²/G.
- His experiment turned Newton's proportionality into a quantitative, universal law and seeded later precision tests of gravity's dependence on composition and distance scale.
