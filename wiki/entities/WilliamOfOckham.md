# William of Ockham

**William of Ockham** (c. 1287–1347) was a medieval English philosopher and logician. His name attaches to **Ockham's razor** (Occam's razor) — the principle of **parsimony** that, all else equal, the explanation positing the fewest entities or processes is to be preferred.

## In this corpus
Named explicitly by [[logic-text-v2|Van Cleave]] (§3.2) as the namesake of the **"simplicity"** explanatory virtue — one of the seven [[InferenceToBestExplanation|explanatory virtues]] used to grade an inference to the best explanation: *"Explanations that posit fewer entities or processes are preferable … This is sometimes referred to as 'Ockham's razor' after William of Ockham (1287–1347), the medieval philosopher and logician."*

The wiki's existing [[OccamsRazor]] page develops the *quantitative* (Bayesian / marginal-likelihood) realization of the same principle in machine learning; this entity records its **historical origin** and its role in critical-thinking-style abduction.
