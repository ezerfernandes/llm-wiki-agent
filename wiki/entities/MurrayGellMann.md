## Who
**Murray Gell-Mann** (1929–2019) was an American theoretical physicist central to the development of [[ParticlePhysics|particle physics]] and the [[Quark|quark]] model.

## Key Contributions
- In 1963, independently with George Zweig, proposed that [[Hadron|hadrons]] are built from [[Quark|quarks]] — fundamental fermions carrying fractional electric charge.
- Introduced the quantum number **strangeness** to explain the production and slow decay patterns of "strange" particles (conserved by the strong force, violated by the weak), a key piece of the [[ConservationLawsParticlePhysics|conservation-law]] framework.
- His classification scheme organized the proliferating "particle zoo" into orderly patterns and successfully **predicted the then-unobserved Ω⁻** baryon, validating the quark picture.
- This work underlies quantum chromodynamics and the [[StandardModel]]'s treatment of the [[StrongNuclearForce|strong force]] via color charge.
