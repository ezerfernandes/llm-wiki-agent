## Overview
Charles-Augustin de Coulomb (1736–1806) was a French physicist who established the quantitative law governing the [[Force|force]] between electric charges. His name is given both to [[CoulombsLaw]] and to the SI unit of [[ElectricCharge|charge]], the coulomb (C).

## Key Points
- [[CoulombsLaw]] (F = k|q₁q₂|/r²) describes the electrostatic force as inversely proportional to the square of the distance between point charges — an inverse-square law like [[GravitationalField|Newtonian gravitation]].
- The **coulomb** is the SI unit of charge; the elementary charge is |q_e| = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ C, so 1 C ≈ 6.25 × 10¹⁸ elementary charges.
- Coulomb's experimental work used a torsion balance to measure tiny electrostatic forces.
