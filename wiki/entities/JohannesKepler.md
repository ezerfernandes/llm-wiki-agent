## Overview
Johannes Kepler (1571–1630) was a German astronomer and mathematician who, working from Tycho Brahe's precise observational data, formulated three empirical laws describing planetary motion. His laws supported the Copernican (heliocentric) model over the older Ptolemaic system and were later explained by [[IsaacNewton]]'s [[NewtonsLawOfUniversalGravitation|law of universal gravitation]].

## Contributions
- **First law (ellipses):** planets orbit the Sun in ellipses with the Sun at one focus.
- **Second law (equal areas):** the Sun–planet line sweeps equal areas in equal times, so a planet moves fastest near the Sun.
- **Third law (harmonic law):** the square of a planet's orbital period is proportional to the cube of its average orbital radius (T² ∝ r³), the same constant for all bodies orbiting the same parent.
- His laws are descriptive; Newton supplied the underlying gravitational cause — illustrating the unity and simplicity of physical law.
