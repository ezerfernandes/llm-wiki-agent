## Overview
Archimedes (ca. 287–212 BC) was a Greek mathematician and inventor, best known in fluid statics for [[ArchimedesPrinciple]] — that the buoyant force on an object equals the weight of the fluid it displaces.

## Key Facts
- Greek; lived approximately 287–212 BC.
- Formulated the buoyancy principle long before rigorous concepts of [[Force]] existed.
- By legend, he realized that a submerged body displaces its own volume of water while bathing, then ran out shouting "Eureka!" ("I have found it").
- His principle underlies ship design, submarine ballast, hydrometers, and hydrostatic density measurement.
