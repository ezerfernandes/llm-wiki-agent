# Niels Bohr

**Niels Bohr** (Danish physicist) proposed in **1913** the [[BohrModel|Bohr model]] of the atom, the first to explain the hydrogen [[AtomicSpectra|line spectrum]]. He postulated that electrons occupy quantized, stationary orbits (E_n = −kZ²/n², k = 2.179 × 10⁻¹⁸ J) and emit or absorb photons only when transitioning between them — rejecting the classical prediction that an orbiting electron would spiral into the nucleus.

His theoretical value for the Rydberg constant matched experiment with remarkable precision, persuading scientists to abandon classical physics for the developing quantum theory. Though the model fails for multi-electron atoms, its core idea — **quantized energy levels** — is foundational to the modern [[QuantumNumbers|quantum-mechanical model]]. In College Physics 2e (Ch.30) his quantization postulate is stated as angular-momentum quantization, L = nh/2π, later justified by [[DeBroglieWavelength|de Broglie]] standing waves.
