Computational linguist; chair of computational linguistics at Ludwig Maximilian University of Munich (LMU); previously at [[stanforduniversity|Stanford]] and Xerox PARC. Third co-author of *Introduction to Information Retrieval* (Cambridge University Press 2008, with [[ChristopherManning]] & [[PrabhakarRaghavan]]) — the canonical IR textbook ingested across `iir-ch01`–`iir-ch21` of this wiki.

Earlier textbook (with Manning): *Foundations of Statistical Natural Language Processing* (MIT Press 1999). Doctoral work at Stanford on word-sense disambiguation and distributional semantics — among the early proponents of representing word meaning as vectors derived from co-occurrence statistics, an idea whose modern descendants are [[GloVe]], [[Word2Vec]], and transformer-era contextual embeddings ([[ContextualEmbedding]]).

The IIR chapters most directly carrying Schütze's signature are the classification + clustering + LSI chapters (13–18), where the distributional-semantics through-line is most visible.
