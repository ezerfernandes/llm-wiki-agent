# Albert Einstein

**Albert Einstein** (1879–1955) was a theoretical physicist whose 1905 work reshaped both quantum theory and our understanding of space, time, and energy.

## Quantum / Photon Model
He explained the [[PhotoelectricEffect|photoelectric effect]] in **1905** by extending [[MaxPlanck|Planck]]'s [[EnergyQuantization|energy quantization]] to light itself. He proposed that light consists of discrete particles — [[PhotonQuantum|photons]] — each with energy **E = hν**, dependent only on frequency. This accounted for the threshold-frequency behavior that classical wave theory could not (KE_e = hf − BE, College Physics 2e Ch.29), and helped establish [[WaveParticleDuality|wave-particle duality]].

## Special Relativity
Also in 1905, Einstein founded [[SpecialRelativity|special relativity]] on [[EinsteinPostulates|two postulates]]: the laws of physics are the same in all inertial [[ReferenceFrame|reference frames]], and the [[SpeedOfLightPhysics|speed of light]] is invariant for all observers. From these follow [[TimeDilation|time dilation]], [[LengthContraction|length contraction]], [[RelativisticVelocityAddition|relativistic velocity addition]], [[RelativisticMomentum|relativistic momentum]], and the [[MassEnergyEquivalence|mass-energy equivalence]] E = mc² (College Physics 2e Ch. 28).

## General Relativity
Einstein later extended these ideas to accelerated frames and gravitation in [[GeneralRelativity|general relativity]] (College Physics 2e Ch. 34). Built on the **equivalence principle** (acceleration and gravity are locally indistinguishable), it recasts gravity as the curvature of spacetime. It predicts that gravity bends light — confirmed by the 1919 solar eclipse — and forecasts [[BlackHole|black holes]] (event horizon at the Schwarzschild radius R_S = 2GM/c²), [[GravitationalWave|gravitational waves]] (directly detected by LIGO in 2015), and a cosmological constant linked to [[DarkEnergy|dark energy]]. Reconciling general relativity with quantum mechanics ([[QuantumGravity|quantum gravity]]) remains an open frontier.
