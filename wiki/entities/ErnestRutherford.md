# Ernest Rutherford

**Ernest Rutherford** was a New Zealand–born physicist whose **gold-foil (α-scattering) experiment** — performed with Hans Geiger and Ernest Marsden — revealed the [[AtomicNucleus|atomic nucleus]]. He directed positively charged α particles (2 [[Proton|protons]] + 2 [[Neutron|neutrons]]) at thin gold foil: most passed straight through, but a few deflected sharply backward, which he described as "almost as incredible as if you fired a 15-inch shell at a piece of tissue paper and it came back and hit you."

He concluded the [[Atom]] is mostly empty space with a tiny, dense, positively charged **nucleus** at its center — the **nuclear model**, replacing [[JJThomson|Thomson's]] plum-pudding model. College Physics 2e (Ch.30) frames this as [[RutherfordScattering|Rutherford scattering]]: the rare large-angle deflections imply a nucleus ~10⁻¹⁵ m across (~10⁵× smaller than the atom) holding nearly all the mass. Rutherford also identified the **[[Proton|proton]]** as the hydrogen-nucleus building block of all nuclei.
