# Stuart Russell

**Stuart Russell** is the [[UCBerkeley|UC Berkeley]] computer scientist best known for co-authoring (with [[PeterNorvig|Peter Norvig]]) the field-defining textbook *Artificial Intelligence: A Modern Approach* (Prentice Hall, 1995; multiple editions). [[ChipHuyen|Huyen]] anchors [[ai-engineering-ch06-rag-agents|*AI Engineering* Ch 6]]'s agent definition in Russell & Norvig's framing:

> *"The classic book by Stuart Russell and Peter Norvig, Artificial Intelligence: A Modern Approach (Prentice Hall, 1995) defines the field of artificial intelligence research as 'the study and design of rational agents.'"*

And in a footnote:

> *"Artificial Intelligence: A Modern Approach (1995) defines an agent as anything that can be viewed as perceiving its environment through sensors and acting upon that environment through actuators."*

This is the definitional foundation Huyen builds her entire agent chapter on — agent = perception + action.

## Position in the AI safety conversation

Beyond the textbook, Russell is a prominent voice in AI safety — author of *Human Compatible: Artificial Intelligence and the Problem of Control* (2019) and originator of the *cooperative inverse reinforcement learning* (CIRL) framing.
