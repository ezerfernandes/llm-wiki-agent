# John von Neumann (1903–1957)

Hungarian-American mathematician and polymath. In 1945 wrote *"First Draft of a Report on the EDVAC"* — the paper that synthesized the [[ENIAC]] team's hardware insights and [[AlanTuring|Turing]]'s theoretical framework into the **[[VonNeumannArchitecture|stored-program architecture]]**: a single internal [[RAM|memory]] that holds **both program instructions and data**, fetched and executed sequentially by a [[CPU]] containing an [[ALU]], [[ControlUnit|control unit]], and [[Register|registers]].

Per [[dis-5-1-history|*Dive into Systems* Ch 5.1]], von Neumann's contribution was the **synthesis**, not invention from scratch — his paper distilled engineering practices already emerging in [[ENIAC]] / [[EDVAC]] / [[Z3]] into a clean architectural template that became the structural basis of essentially every modern computer. The credit asymmetry (the architecture is named *only* for him despite the team contributions of [[JohnMauchly]] / [[PresperEckert]] and others) is part of Ch 5.1's broader theme about how historical attribution has often misallocated credit in computing.

Also contributed to game theory, quantum mechanics, fluid dynamics, the [[ManhattanProject]], and the foundations of cellular automata.
