# Daniel Kahneman

**Daniel Kahneman** (1934–2024) was a psychologist whose work with [[AmosTversky|Amos Tversky]] on **judgment under uncertainty** and **heuristics and biases** reshaped how we understand human reasoning. He won the **Nobel Prize in Economics in 2002** and authored the popular *Thinking, Fast and Slow* (2011).

## In this corpus
Cited by [[logic-text-v2|Van Cleave]] as the psychological backbone of Chapter 3's treatment of **probabilistic fallacies**. Kahneman's explanation for why these fallacies persist — even among trained mathematicians — is **representativeness substitution**: the mind, wired to find patterns, unwittingly answers an easy question about how *representative* a description is in place of the hard question about *probability*.

- The **[[ConjunctionFallacy|conjunction fallacy]]** ("Linda the bank teller") comes directly from **Tversky & Kahneman (1983)**, *Extension versus intuitive reasoning: The conjunction fallacy in probability judgment*, *Psychological Review* 90(4), 293–315.
- The **[[BaseRateFallacy|base-rate fallacy]]** — ignoring prior prevalence when interpreting a positive test — is part of the same heuristics-and-biases program.
- Van Cleave credits Kahneman for the book's design choice to **explain why each fallacy is seductive** rather than make it sound obvious.
