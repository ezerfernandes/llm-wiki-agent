# John Dalton

**John Dalton** (1766–1844) was a British schoolteacher credited in OpenStax *Chemistry 2e* with putting the ancient idea of the [[Atom|atom]] on a quantitative, experimental footing in the early nineteenth century. The notion that matter is composed of indivisible particles dated back to the Greek philosophers Leucippus and Democritus (5th century BCE), but Dalton supported the atomic hypothesis with measurements, advancing chemistry from speculation toward a quantitative science.

## Dalton's Atomic Theory (Ch 2)

In 1807 Dalton published the postulates now called [[AtomicTheory|Dalton's atomic theory]]: matter is composed of atoms; all atoms of an element are identical in mass and properties; atoms of different elements differ; a [[ChemicalCompound|compound]] combines atoms of two or more elements in a small whole-number ratio; and atoms are neither created nor destroyed in a chemical change ([[ConservationOfMatter]]). These postulates explain the [[LawOfDefiniteProportions|law of definite proportions]] and the [[LawOfMultipleProportions|law of multiple proportions]]. Dalton's atoms were thought indivisible; the later [[EvolutionOfAtomicTheory|experimental work]] of [[JJThomson]], [[RobertMillikan]], [[ErnestRutherford]], and [[JamesChadwick]] revealed subatomic [[AtomicStructure|structure]].

## Dalton's Law of Partial Pressures (Ch 9)

Dalton also formulated the **law of partial pressures**: the total pressure of a mixture of non-reacting ideal gases equals the sum of the partial pressures of the components, $P_{Total} = \sum_i P_i$, with each gas's partial pressure equal to its mole fraction times the total ($P_A = X_A P_{Total}$). See [[DaltonsLawOfPartialPressures]]. This is consistent with the [[KineticMolecularTheory|kinetic-molecular theory]]: because gas molecules are far apart and exert no forces, each gas contributes pressure independently.
