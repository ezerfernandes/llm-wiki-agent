# J.J. Thomson

**J.J. Thomson** was an English physicist who, in the late 1800s, discovered the [[Electron|electron]] using cathode-ray-tube experiments. He observed that the cathode-ray beam deflected toward positive charges and behaved identically regardless of electrode metal, and by combining electric and magnetic deflection he measured the particles' **charge-to-mass ratio** (1.759 × 10¹¹ C/kg). He concluded that atoms contain fundamental, negatively charged constituents — overturning [[AtomicTheory|Dalton's]] indivisible-atom assumption.

In 1904 he proposed the **"plum pudding" model** of the [[Atom]]: electrons embedded in a diffuse positive charge. This was later superseded by [[ErnestRutherford|Rutherford's]] nuclear model.
