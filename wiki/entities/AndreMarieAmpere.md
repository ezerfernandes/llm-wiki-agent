## Overview
André-Marie Ampère (1775–1836) was a French physicist and mathematician, a founder of the science of electromagnetism (which he termed "electrodynamics"). His name is attached to [[AmperesLaw]], relating magnetic fields to the currents that produce them, and to the SI unit of [[ElectricCurrent]], the ampere (A).

## Relevance
- [[AmperesLaw]] is one of Maxwell's equations and the framework for computing the [[MagneticField]] produced by currents (straight wires, loops, and the [[Solenoid]]).
- The ampere is operationally defined through the [[MagneticForce]] between two parallel current-carrying conductors: two infinite parallel wires 1 m apart in vacuum each carrying 1 A exert exactly 2×10⁻⁷ N/m on each other.
