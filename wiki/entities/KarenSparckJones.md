(1935–2007) British computer scientist; Cambridge University. **Coined inverse document frequency** ([[InverseDocumentFrequency|idf]]) in *"A Statistical Interpretation of Term Specificity and Its Application in Retrieval"* (Journal of Documentation 28(1), 1972) — the single most-cited paper in IR, and the conceptual seed of [[TfIdf|tf-idf]], [[BM25]], and every term-weighting scheme since.

Subsequent work with [[StephenRobertson]] on the **[[BinaryIndependenceModel]]** and **[[OkapiBM25|Okapi BM25]]** — the probabilistic-IR retrieval-function line that culminates in the modern BM25. Earlier work in computational linguistics on synonymy, thesaurus-based query expansion, and machine translation evaluation.

Career: Cambridge Language Research Unit (1953 onward); University of Cambridge Computer Laboratory (Reader, then Professor of Computers and Information until retirement 2002, then emeritus). ACM SIGIR Gerard Salton Award (2000, joint with Robertson); Lovelace Medal of the British Computer Society (2007); ACL Lifetime Achievement Award (2004); fellow of the Royal Academy of Engineering and the British Academy.

Best-known public statement: *"Computing is too important to be left to men."* — said in a 2007 interview shortly before her death. The Karen Spärck Jones Award (BCS-IRSG, since 2008) is given annually to a young IR or NLP researcher in her memory.
