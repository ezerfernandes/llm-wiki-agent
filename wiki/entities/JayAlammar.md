# Jay Alammar

Co-author (with [[MaartenGrootendorst]]) of *[[HandsOnLLM|Hands-On Large Language Models]]* ([[OReilly|O'Reilly Media]], 2024, ISBN 978-1-098-15096-9). Director and Engineering Fellow at [[Cohere]]; widely known in the NLP / LLM community as the author of an influential series of illustrated technical blog posts — most notably *The Illustrated Transformer*, *The Illustrated BERT, ELMo, and co.*, and *The Illustrated GPT-2*. The combination of unusually clear hand-drawn architecture diagrams + careful step-by-step exposition made these posts canonical pedagogical entry points to the modern LLM stack.

## In *Hands-On LLMs*

In Ch 1 ([[hands-on-llm-ch01-introduction-to-llms]]), Alammar's pedagogical style — *the recent history of Language AI* told as a connected arc from [[BagOfWords|bag-of-words]] through [[Word2Vec|word2vec]] and [[Attention|attention]] to the [[transformer|Transformer]] and the [[bert|BERT]] / [[GPT]] split — sets the book's overall structure: every major concept is introduced first via a high-level intuition + diagram, then operationalized in code with Hugging Face `transformers` on Google Colab.

## From Hands-On LLMs Ch 12

Ch 12 — co-authored by Alammar — **completes the 12-chapter Hands-On LLMs ingest**. The chapter delivers the wiki's first runnable QLoRA-SFT recipe and first runnable DPO recipe, consolidating both stages of post-training behind a single QLoRA substrate. Alammar's structural commitment to *intuition-first, runnable-code* pedagogy carries through the chapter's three-step LLM training pipeline framing (language modeling → SFT → preference tuning), the *"the same training script flips between QLoRA and full FT by toggling two arguments"* lesson, and the chapter's deliberate *"modern LLM fine-tuning is accessible to anyone with a free Colab account"* statement.
