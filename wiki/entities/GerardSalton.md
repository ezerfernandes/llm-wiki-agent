(1927–1995) "The father of information retrieval." Cornell University computer scientist; principal architect of the [[VectorSpaceModel]] in IR and of the **SMART** ([[SmartNotation|System for the Mechanical Analysis and Retrieval of Text]]) — an experimental IR system that ran continuously from the early 1960s through the 1990s and was the test bed for nearly every classical-IR technique now in textbooks.

Key contributions: term weighting schemes including the original [[TfIdf|tf-idf]] family, the cosine similarity measure for documents-as-vectors, the [[RocchioAlgorithm|Rocchio]] feedback rule (advised by Salton, named for his student J.J. Rocchio Jr.), automatic indexing, evaluation methodology preceding TREC. Wrote *Automatic Information Organization and Retrieval* (1968), *Introduction to Modern Information Retrieval* (1983, with Michael McGill — a direct predecessor of the [[ChristopherManning|Manning]] / [[PrabhakarRaghavan|Raghavan]] / [[HinrichSchutze|Schütze]] 2008 textbook), and *Automatic Text Processing* (1989).

ACM SIGIR's lifetime-achievement award is named the **Gerard Salton Award** in his honor. The [[SmartNotation]] `ddd.qqq` term-weighting code (e.g. `ltc.ltc`) used throughout chapter 6 of [[iir-ch06-vector-space-model]] is a direct artifact of the SMART system.
