## Overview
James Clerk Maxwell was the 19th-century physicist who unified all known electric and magnetic phenomena into [[MaxwellsEquations|four equations]], the foundation of classical electromagnetism. His theoretical work mathematically predicted the existence of [[ElectromagneticWave|electromagnetic waves]] and showed they travel at the speed of light — leading him to conclude that light itself is an electromagnetic wave.

## Key Contributions
- Formulated the four equations of electromagnetism (mid-19th century), adding the crucial term that a *changing electric field* generates a magnetic field — the symmetry that makes EM-wave propagation possible.
- Derived `c = 1/sqrt(μ₀ε₀) = 3.00×10⁸ m/s` for EM waves and recognized the match with the measured speed of light, unifying electricity, magnetism, and optics (see [[SpeedOfLightPhysics]]).
- His prediction was confirmed experimentally by [[HeinrichHertz]] in 1887, years after Maxwell's death — a result hailed as "the greatest triumph of physics since Newton."
