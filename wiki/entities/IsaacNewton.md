## Overview
Sir Isaac Newton (1642–1727) was an English physicist and mathematician whose three laws of motion form the foundation of classical (Newtonian) mechanics. His work in Chapter 4 of College Physics 2e marks the transition from kinematics (describing motion) to dynamics (explaining its causes through [[Force]]).

## Contributions
- **First law (inertia):** objects keep constant velocity unless acted on by a net external force — see [[NewtonsFirstLaw]] and [[Inertia]].
- **Second law:** acceleration is proportional to net force and inversely proportional to mass, F_net = ma — see [[NewtonsSecondLaw]]. The SI unit of force, the **newton (N = kg·m/s²)**, is named after him.
- **Third law:** forces occur in equal-and-opposite pairs acting on different bodies — see [[NewtonsThirdLaw]].
- These laws are accurate for objects moving well below light speed and larger than molecular scales; relativity and quantum mechanics extend physics beyond those limits.
- **Universal gravitation (Ch.6):** every mass attracts every other with F = GmM/r² — see [[NewtonsLawOfUniversalGravitation]]. This single inverse-square law connects terrestrial weight (w = mg) to celestial mechanics, supplying the [[CentripetalForce]] that holds the Moon and planets in orbit and providing the causal explanation for [[KeplersLaws|Kepler's laws]].
