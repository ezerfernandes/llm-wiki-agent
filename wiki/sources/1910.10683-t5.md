## Summary
Raffel, Shazeer, Roberts, Lee, Narang, Matena, Zhou, Li & Liu ([[Google]]) introduce **T5** — the **Text-to-Text Transfer Transformer** — and use it as the substrate for an enormous, controlled empirical study of every axis of NLP transfer learning. The framing collapses every task (translation, classification, regression, QA, summarization) to *string-in, string-out* with a task-prefix prompt and a shared maximum-likelihood objective, letting the same model, loss and decoder be applied to all of them. The systematic ablation — across architectures (encoder-decoder vs decoder-only vs prefix-LM), unsupervised objectives (LM / BERT-style / deshuffling / span corruption with varied rates and span lengths), pre-training data ([[c4]] vs Wikipedia vs RealNews vs WebText-like), data-set size, fine-tuning method (full / [[adapterlayers]] / [[gradualunfreezing]]), multi-task mixing strategies (examples-proportional, temperature-scaled, equal), and scale — converges on a recipe: **encoder-decoder + span-corruption denoising + C4 + multi-task pre-training + full fine-tuning + lots of compute**. The largest variant **T5-11B** (11 billion parameters, ~1 trillion pre-training tokens) achieves state-of-the-art on **18 of 24** benchmarks evaluated, including a SuperGLUE score of 88.9 (vs. 89.8 human, +4.3 over prior best), and ships alongside the released [[c4]] dataset (~745 GB) and code/checkpoints. Together with [[1706.03762-attention-is-all-you-need]] and [[1810.04805-bert]], this is the third Google paper in the wiki that defined a load-bearing piece of modern NLP infrastructure.

## Key Claims
- **Text-to-text unification works.** Casting *every* task as string→string with a task prefix ("translate English to German:", "cola sentence:", "summarize:", "stsb sentence1: ... sentence2: ...") lets one model, loss, decoder and set of hyperparameters span translation, classification, QA, regression (STS-B treated as 21-class via 0.2-rounded literal strings), and abstractive summarization with no architectural changes. Performance is "comparable to task-specific architectures" — and, with scale, state of the art.
- **Encoder-decoder beats decoder-only in this setting.** Across matched compute, the standard encoder-decoder Transformer (2P parameters, M FLOPs) outperformed the decoder-only LM and the prefix-LM at equivalent FLOPs, on every task. Sharing parameters across encoder and decoder (P-parameter, M-FLOP) preserved most of the performance at half the parameter count.
- **Denoising > language modeling, robustly.** A BERT-style denoising objective always beat causal language modeling for pre-training, regardless of architecture; this confirms the conventional view but isolates it from confounders.
- **Among denoising variants, all behave similarly — pick the cheapest.** I.i.d. mask, MASS-style, replace-corrupted-spans, drop-corrupted-tokens, span-corruption with varying length, corruption rates of 10–25% all produced near-identical downstream performance. The chosen baseline ([[spancorruption]] — replace corrupted spans of mean length 3 at 15% corruption rate) was selected because it yields shorter targets and therefore faster training, not because it scores higher.
- **C4 matters; heuristic filtering matters; in-domain data sometimes helps.** Unfiltered C4 (~6.1 TB) performed strictly worse than the filtered C4 (745 GB) on every task. In-domain corpora — Wikipedia+Toronto Books for MultiRC, RealNews-like for ReCoRD, Wikipedia for SQuAD — each beat the diverse C4 baseline on their matched downstream task, but at the cost of substantially smaller data sets. Repeating a small corpus 64× during pre-training did limited damage; 1,024–4,096× caused memorization (visible as a steeply lower pre-training loss) and degraded fine-tuning.
- **Full fine-tuning beats adapter / gradual unfreezing.** Adapter layers ([[adapterlayers]]) and gradual unfreezing ([[gradualunfreezing]]) both lost to fine-tuning all parameters on every task in this setup, although adapters with appropriately-scaled inner dimensionality came close on low-resource tasks.
- **Multi-task pre-training matches unsupervised pre-training when followed by per-task fine-tuning.** Plain multi-task training underperformed pre-train-then-fine-tune; but **multi-task pre-training + fine-tuning** matched it. "Leave-one-out" multi-task training (omitting one downstream task from the pre-training mixture, then fine-tuning on it) was only marginally worse — suggesting limited task interference. Examples-proportional mixing with an artificial cap K worked best; temperature-scaled mixing with T=2 was a close runner-up.
- **Scale is the dominant ingredient.** Given 4× more compute, training a larger model and training longer were both effective; ensembling 4 separately-trained models gave additional orthogonal gains. The final model family spans 60M (Small) → 220M (Base) → 770M (Large) → 2.8B (3B) → **11B**, all sharing the recipe; performance increases monotonically with size, and T5-11B exceeds T5-3B on essentially every task.
- **Final state of the art.** T5-11B achieved SOTA on 18 of 24 tasks evaluated: **GLUE 90.3**, **SuperGLUE 88.9** (vs 89.8 human), **SQuAD F1 96.22 / EM 91.26**, **CNN/DM ROUGE-2 21.55**, **WMT En-De BLEU 32.1** (did not beat backtranslation-augmented prior best), CoLA 71.6, RTE 92.5, MultiRC 88.1 F1a, ReCoRD 94.1 F1. On MultiRC and ReCoRD, T5-11B exceeds human performance.
- **"Bitter Lesson" confirmed for NLP transfer learning.** Across this paper's whole ablation, scaling dominates careful method engineering: every careful architectural/objective tweak yielded ≤1 point of improvement, while doubling compute yielded several.

## Architecture & Recipe Reference
- **Base baseline.** Standard encoder-decoder Transformer matching [[1706.03762-attention-is-all-you-need]] with two minor deviations: (1) LayerNorm bias removed and placed outside the residual path; (2) simplified relative-position embedding — a *scalar* added to the attention logit, shared across layers, 32 buckets log-spaced up to offset 128 (positions beyond that bucket share an embedding). Encoder and decoder are each 12-block BERT_BASE-sized stacks: d_model=768, d_ff=3072, d_kv=64, 12 heads, dropout 0.1, ~220M total params (~2× BERT_BASE since two stacks).
- **Tokenizer.** [[sentencepiece]] WordPiece, 32k vocab, trained on a 10:1:1:1 EN:DE:FR:RO mixture of C4 so the same vocabulary supports translation targets.
- **Pre-training (baseline).** [[c4]], 2¹⁹ = 524,288 steps, batch 128 × seq-len 512 with packing → ~2¹⁶ tokens/batch → 2³⁵ ≈ 34B tokens (≪ BERT 137B, ≪ RoBERTa 2.2T). AdaFactor optimizer, inverse-sqrt LR schedule (constant 0.01 for first 10⁴ warm-up steps then exp decay). Span-corruption objective with mean span length 3, 15% corruption rate, sentinel tokens added to vocab.
- **Fine-tuning (baseline).** 2¹⁸ = 262,144 steps, same batch, constant LR 0.001, checkpoint every 5,000 steps and select best on validation. Greedy decoding (beam search width 4, length penalty 0.6 for translation / summarization in the final run).
- **Architecture variants studied.** Encoder-decoder (2P params, M FLOPs), shared-parameter encoder-decoder (P, M), half-depth encoder-decoder (P, M/2), decoder-only LM (P, M), prefix-LM ([[prefixlm]]: causal LM with fully-visible mask over the prefix, P, M).
- **Final T5 family.** Small (60M, d_model=512, 6 layers, 8 heads), Base (220M, the baseline above), Large (770M, d_model=1024, d_ff=4096, 24 layers, 16 heads — BERT_LARGE-equivalent stacks), 3B (~2.8B, d_model=1024, d_ff=16,384, 32 heads, 24 layers, d_kv=128), 11B (d_ff=65,536, 128 heads, 24 layers, ~11B params). All final variants pre-trained for 1M steps × batch 2¹¹ × seq-len 512 ≈ **1 trillion tokens** (≈ 32× the baseline). Final recipe also adds span corruption length 3, multi-task pre-training (examples-proportional with model-size-scaled K), beam search and per-task fine-tuning for GLUE/SuperGLUE.

## Key Quotes
> "The basic idea underlying our work is to treat every text processing problem as a 'text-to-text' problem, i.e. taking text as input and producing new text as output."

> "Our text-to-text framework allows us to directly apply the same model, objective, training procedure, and decoding process to every task we consider."

> "We emphasize that our goal is not to propose new methods but instead to provide a comprehensive perspective on where the field stands."

> "We found that the basic approach of updating all of a pre-trained model's parameters during fine-tuning outperformed methods that are designed to update fewer parameters."

> "Increasing the model size resulted in an additional bump in performance compared to solely increasing the training time or batch size."

> "Different scaling methods have different trade-offs that are separate from their performance. For example, using a larger model can make downstream fine-tuning and inference more expensive. In contrast, the cost of pre-training a small model for longer is effectively amortized if it is applied to many downstream tasks."

> "We suspect that this simplistic technique [span-corruption denoising] may not be a very efficient way to teach the model general-purpose knowledge. More concretely, it would be useful to be able to attain good fine-tuning performance without needing to train our models on 1 trillion tokens of text first."

## Connections
- [[1706.03762-attention-is-all-you-need]] — T5 inherits the encoder-decoder Transformer wholesale; the only deviations are LayerNorm placement and the simplified scalar-bucket relative position embedding. T5's main contribution is *not* a new architecture but a controlled exploration of what to do with this one.
- [[1810.04805-bert]] — T5 generalizes BERT in three ways: (1) decoder is back so generative tasks are first-class, (2) the [[maskedlanguagemodel]] objective is replaced with [[spancorruption]] (predict corrupted spans, not the full uncorrupted sequence — shorter targets, faster), (3) the task interface is a prompt prefix rather than a [CLS]/[SEP] structural head. T5-Base ≈ 2× BERT_BASE because it has an encoder *and* a decoder.
- [[1409.3215-seq2seq]] — the original encoder-decoder framing T5 vindicates at scale; T5 confirms encoder-decoder beats decoder-only on text-to-text tasks at matched compute.
- [[t5]] — concept page for the model family itself.
- [[c4]] — the Colossal Clean Crawled Corpus released alongside T5; now the canonical large diverse pre-training corpus.
- [[texttotextframework]] — the unifying interface T5 introduces.
- [[spancorruption]] — the pre-training objective T5 ultimately uses.
- [[prefixlm]] — one of three architecture variants compared; loses to encoder-decoder at matched compute.
- [[encoderdecoder]] — vindicated as the winning structure for text-to-text learning.
- [[transformer]] — the underlying architecture.
- [[pretraining]] — T5's empirical study is the definitive 2020-era systematic exploration of the pre-train-then-finetune recipe.
- [[bleu]] — translation metric reported for WMT.

- [[Google]] — author affiliation; same lab that produced [[1706.03762-attention-is-all-you-need]], [[1810.04805-bert]], and [[2312.11805-gemini]].
- Downstream LLM work in this wiki (e.g. [[2601.21343-self-improving-pretraining]]) implicitly inherits the T5 finding that the choice of pre-training data and objective is the major lever once architecture is held fixed.

## Contradictions
None with current wiki content. T5 predates every 2026 source in the LLM corpus by ~6 years and supplies the methodology (controlled ablation across the full transfer-learning stack) that subsequent papers presume.

A useful asymmetry with [[2604.27707-agentic-memory-is-a-memo]]: T5 shows that *parametric* memory (weights updated by pre-training on a trillion tokens) generalizes broadly to downstream tasks; the agentic-memory paper proves *retrieval-based* memory has an Ω(k²) ceiling on compositional novelty no retrieval store can fix. The two papers point in the same direction — weight consolidation is the load-bearing form of memory.

## Notes
- JMLR 21 (2020) 1-67; arXiv:1910.10683 (v4, 19 Sep 2023). v4 differs from v1 only in minor edits.
- Code and checkpoints at https://github.com/google-research/text-to-text-transfer-transformer. C4 distributed via TensorFlow Datasets (`tensorflow_datasets.load('c4')`).
- The paper is unusually long (44 main pages + appendix) and explicitly framed as a *survey + empirical comparison*, not a methods paper.
- Author contributions appendix (A) is unusually detailed: Colin Raffel led the project, Noam Shazeer contributed the text-to-text framing and the unsupervised objectives and ran the final 11B experiments, Adam Roberts owned the C4 pipeline, etc. All eight authors listed before Liu are equal contributors.
- This paper popularized the prefix-prompting interface (e.g. "summarize: ...") that GPT-3 (Brown et al., 2020) generalized into in-context learning.
