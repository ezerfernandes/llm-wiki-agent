## Summary
Wang et al. (Meituan LongCat Team + Peking U) argue that the apparent gains of orchestrated agentic harnesses come not from elaborate scaffolding but from an **inner skill** of "heavy thinking": **parallel reasoning** producing K trajectories, followed by **sequential deliberation** over a serialized memory cache. They show this two-stage workflow beats Best-of-N across STEM/coding/general benchmarks, that stronger LLMs approach Pass@N with it, and that depth+width of heavy thinking can be jointly improved with **RLVR** (reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards).

## Key Claims
- **Harness ≈ heavy thinking** — orchestration loops can be abstracted into parallel-reason-then-deliberate, internalized as one model skill.
- **Performance hierarchy** — Heavy-Pass@k ≥ Heavy-Mean@K ≥ Vote@K ≥ Mean@k on STEM benchmarks with verifiable answers.
- **Implicit verification in deliberation** — qualitative analysis: models compare trajectory differences during deliberation, acting as implicit verifiers.
- **Trajectory quality + diversity dominates** — ablations show parallel-stage trajectories' quality and diversity are the two main performance drivers; deliberation depends mostly on the deliberator's general capability, suggesting separate optimization may help.
- **RLVR scales heavy thinking** — depth (deliberation) and breadth (parallel generation) can be co-optimized to improve Heavy-Mean@k and Pass@k simultaneously.
- **Iterative deliberation** — re-feeding deliberation output back as cache enables progressive refinement, mirroring human iteration.

## Contradictions
- Pushes against "elaborate orchestration" as the source of gains — partly aligned with [[2605.03310-coordination-architectural-layer|Nechepurenko & Shuvalov]]'s finding that simple single-agent baselines often match multi-agent designs at lower cost, but disagrees on remedy: HEAVYSKILL internalizes thinking; the architectural-layer paper externalizes coordination as a configurable layer.
