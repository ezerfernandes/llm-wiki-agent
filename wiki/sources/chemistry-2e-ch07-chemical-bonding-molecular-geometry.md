## Summary
Chapter 7 of OpenStax *Chemistry 2e* develops bonding theory — how atoms connect to form molecules and ionic solids. It covers ionic bonding (electron transfer, lattice energy, ion electronic structures), covalent bonding (electron sharing, bond length, electronegativity, and the pure/polar/ionic continuum), Lewis symbols and structures with the octet rule and its exceptions, formal charge and resonance for choosing and averaging Lewis structures, the quantitative strengths of bonds (bond energy, lattice energy, the Born-Haber cycle), and VSEPR theory for predicting molecular geometry and overall molecular polarity.

## Key Claims
- **Ionic bonds** are electrostatic attractions between cations and anions; because they are isotropic, ions form 3-D **lattices**, not discrete molecules. Dissociating one mole of solid NaCl into gaseous ions requires 769 kJ.
- Main-group cations lose all valence electrons to a noble-gas configuration (Ca → Ca²⁺ = [Ar]); transition metals lose outermost *s* electrons before *d* (Fe → Fe²⁺ → Fe³⁺). Anions fill *s*/*p* to the next noble gas (O²⁻ = [Ne]).
- **Covalent bonds** share electron pairs; bond length is the internuclear distance at minimum potential energy (H₂: 74 pm). Breaking a bond is endothermic, forming one exothermic (H₂: 436 kJ).
- **Electronegativity** (Pauling scale, F = 4.0, increases across, decreases down) sets bond polarity: |ΔEN| < 0.4 nonpolar covalent, 0.4–1.8 polar covalent, > 1.8 ionic. Electronegativity is calculated and dimensionless; electron affinity is measured (kJ/mol).
- **Lewis structures** show bonding and lone pairs and follow the **octet rule** (8 valence electrons; H needs 2). Exceptions: odd-electron radicals (NO), electron-deficient molecules (BeH₂, BF₃), and hypervalent/expanded-octet molecules (PCl₅, SF₆, XeF₄).
- **Formal charge** = valence e⁻ − lone-pair e⁻ − ½(bonding e⁻); the best Lewis structure minimizes formal charges and places negative charge on the more electronegative atom.
- **Resonance**: when multiple equivalent Lewis structures exist, the real structure is the average (resonance hybrid), never any single form (NO₂⁻, CO₃²⁻ have equivalent bonds).
- **Bond energy** (D) measures covalent bond strength; bond order up → strength up, length down; ΔH = ΣD(broken) − ΣD(formed) estimates reaction enthalpy.
- **Lattice energy** ΔH_lattice = C(Z⁺)(Z⁻)/R₀ measures ionic bond strength; larger charge and smaller ions → larger (quadruples when both charges double). The **Born-Haber cycle** computes it via Hess's law (ΔHf° = ΔH_s° + ½D + IE + EA − ΔH_lattice).
- **VSEPR theory**: electron-density regions arrange to minimize repulsion — linear (180°), trigonal planar (120°), tetrahedral (109.5°), trigonal bipyramidal (90°/120°), octahedral (90°). Lone pairs repel more than bonding pairs and shrink angles; electron-pair geometry ≠ molecular structure when lone pairs are present (NH₃ trigonal pyramidal, H₂O bent).
- A molecule is **polar** only if it has a polar bond *and* a geometry in which the bond dipole moments (μ = Qr) do not cancel — CO₂ is nonpolar (dipoles cancel), H₂O is polar.

## Key Quotes
> "The tendency of main group atoms to form enough bonds to obtain eight valence electrons is known as the octet rule."
> "Electronegativity is a measure of the tendency of an atom to attract electrons (or electron density) towards itself."
> "A molecule described as a resonance hybrid never possesses an electronic structure described by either resonance form... the actual electronic structure is always the average of that shown by all resonance forms."
> "The order of electron-pair repulsions from greatest to least repulsion is: lone pair-lone pair > lone pair-bonding pair > bonding pair-bonding pair." — VSEPR
> "If the bonds in a molecule are arranged such that their bond moments cancel (vector sum equals zero), then the molecule is nonpolar."
