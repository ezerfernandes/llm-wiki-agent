# MedVAL — Toward expert-level medical text validation with language models

**Aali, Bikia, Varma, Chiou, Ostmeier, Singhvi, Paschali, Kumar, Johnston, Amador-Martinez, Perez Guerrero, Cruz Rivera, Gatidis, Bluethgen, Pontes Reis, Zandee van Rilland, Hosamani, Keet, Go, Ling, Larson, Langlotz, Daneshjou, Hom, Koyejo, Alsentzer & Chaudhari ([[stanforduniversity|Stanford University]]), arXiv:2507.03152v5, 6 Feb 2026.** Corresponding author: [[AsadAali|asadaali@stanford.edu]]. PDF anchor: `raw/papers/2507.03152v5.pdf` (34 pages, 8.1 MB). Funded by [[ARPAH|ARPA-H]] (Chatbot Accuracy and Reliability Evaluation / **CARE** project) + [[MIDRC]] / NIH NIBIB contract 75N92020C00021.

## Summary

Introduces **[[MedVAL]]** — a self-supervised, data-efficient distillation method for training LM validators that assess whether an LM-generated medical output is **factually consistent** with its input, *without requiring physician labels or reference outputs*. The validator emits a 4-class **[[RiskLevelTaxonomy|risk-level grade]]** (Level 1 no risk / Level 2 low risk / Level 3 moderate risk / Level 4 high risk) and an error-category breakdown ([[Hallucination|hallucinations]] / omissions / certainty misalignments). Three-stage training pipeline: (1) **synthetic data generation** via a generator LM with controlled perturbation injection $\delta \in [0, 1]$, (2) **data filtering** via the proposed **[[GeneratorValidatorConsistency|$\mathcal{M}_\mathrm{MedVAL}$ consistency metric]]** that combines absolute + relative consistency between generator-intended and validator-predicted degradation levels, (3) **single-pass [[BootstrapFinetune|supervised fine-tuning]]** of an arbitrary student LM on the filtered subset using [[QLoRA|QLoRA]] (rank-? PEFT + 4-bit quantization via `BitsAndBytesConfig`) — extends [[DSPy]]'s existing local PEFT pipeline ([GitHub PR](https://github.com/stanfordnlp/dspy)). Released alongside **[[MedVALBench]]** — a 6-task, 840-output physician-annotated benchmark — and **[[MedVAL4B|MedVAL-4B]]**, the best open-source distilled model (Qwen3-4B base, F1 = 0.527).

Across **10 LMs × 2 methods × 6 tasks**, MedVAL distillation **lifts average baseline four-class F1 from 36.7% → 51.0%** ($p < 0.001$) and **safe/unsafe binary F1 from 66.2% → 82.8%**. GPT-4o post-distillation achieves the highest score (F1 = 0.587); MedVAL-4B (Qwen3-4B) reaches 0.527 — **statistically non-inferior to a single human expert** on the multi-physician-annotated subset ($p < 0.001$) and outperforming much larger un-distilled baselines including MedGemma-27B and Llama-3.3-70B.

This is the wiki's first paper to demonstrate **[[LLMAsAJudge|LLM-as-a-judge]] reaching expert-level reliability on a high-stakes medical domain** via reference-free input-only validation, and the first to make `dspy.BootstrapFinetune` + QLoRA the recipe for a deployable open clinical evaluator.

## Key Claims

- **Reference-free clinical validation is feasible.** MedVAL evaluates an LM output's factual consistency using **input only** — no physician labels at training time, no reference outputs at test time. Three prior-art constraints — physicians-in-the-loop ([[MedHAL]]), reference outputs (most ROUGE/BLEU/BERTScore pipelines), or retrieval-based evidence ([[VeriFact]]) — are all dropped.
- **Self-supervised synthetic data + consistency filtering beats unfiltered distillation.** Ablation: filtering at $\mathcal{M}_\mathrm{MedVAL} \ge 0.9$ retains **57% of synthetic examples (1,131/2,000)** but **outperforms the unfiltered 100% set** on all five tested students. The single-pass filter (not iterative) is sufficient.
- **GPT-4o as data curator beats GPT-4o as evaluator.** Self-distillation (model teaches itself) + MedVAL filtering recovers a meaningful portion of GPT-4o-distilled performance — e.g. **Llama-3.2-3B jumps 12.8% → 22.1% on only 3% of training data** when self-distilled+filtered, vs 12.8% → 13.6% self-distilled without filtering. **Filtering drives improvements, not just teacher strength.**
- **Small open models match frontier baselines.** [[MedVAL4B|MedVAL-Qwen3-4B]] (F1 = 0.527) **exceeds the baselines of GPT-4o Mini (0.474), Gemini 2.0 Flash (0.515), and MedGemma-27B (0.482)** — a **4B open student** trained for hours on a single A6000 GPU beats much larger proprietary + medically-adapted baselines.
- **Generalization to unseen tasks.** MedVAL distillation yields **+65% F1 improvement on seen tasks** ([[MedicationQA|medication2answer]] / [[MeQSum|query2question]] / [[Openi|report2impression]]) and **+84% on unseen tasks** ([[MIMICIV|impression2simplified]] / [[MIMICIVBHC|bhc2spanish]] / [[ACIBench|dialogue2note]]) — gains *increase* on the hardest held-out tasks rather than decay.
- **Non-inferiority to a single physician.** On 90/840 multi-annotated test cases with majority-consensus reference labels, GPT-4o MedVAL is **statistically non-inferior to a randomly-sampled single physician expert** at the clinically-negligible margin of $\Delta = -0.05$ ($p < 0.001$) via a paired item-level bootstrap (B = 10,000). The first time this wiki has recorded an LM-as-validator reaching this bar.
- **Out-of-distribution robustness.** Evaluated on **[[MEDEC]]** (an external medical-error-detection benchmark on the [[MedHELM]] leaderboard), MedVAL distillation improves accuracy **53.3% → 54.4% for GPT-4o Mini** and **58.0% → 63.3% for GPT-4o** — generalizes outside the training task suite.
- **Strong correlation with physicians, weak with surface metrics.** On `report2impression` ($n = 190$), MedVAL Pearson correlation with physician risk grades is **$r = 0.825$ (GPT-4o) / $r = 0.833$ (Qwen3-4B)**; [[AlignScore]] is moderate ($r = 0.678$); [[BERTScore]] ($r = 0.141$) and [[ROUGE]]-L ($r = 0.259$) are **weak** — *"reference-based similarity is not a faithful proxy for reference-free, input-only clinical risk grading."*
- **Risk-grade sensitivity at the decision boundary.** Largest gains are concentrated at **Levels 2 (low risk) and 3 (moderate risk)** — the threshold between "safe with minimal oversight" and "human review required." MedVAL sharpens precisely the decision boundary clinical deployment cares about.
- **Frontier-teacher → small-student → frontier-rival closes the cost/latency gap.** In agentic workflows validation runs *per section / per note / per agent action* — efficient small validators are practical for routine validation, with frontier models reserved for escalations. MedVAL directly addresses this economic constraint.

To address these challenges, we propose MedVAL, a novel, self-supervised, data-efficient distillation method that leverages synthetic data to train evaluator LMs to assess whether LM-generated medical outputs are factually consistent with inputs, without requiring physician labels or reference outputs." — abstract.

> "MedVAL distillation significantly improves ($p < 0.001$) alignment with physicians across seen and unseen tasks, increasing average F1 scores from 66% to 83%. > "$\mathcal{M}_\mathrm{consistency} = \underbrace{\lVert v_\phi(x, \hat y) \rVert^2_2 + \lVert v_\phi(x, \hat y_\delta) - \delta \rVert^2_2}_{\mathcal{M}_\mathrm{absolute}} + \underbrace{\lVert v_\phi(x, \hat y_\delta) - v_\phi(x, \hat y) - \delta \rVert^2_2}_{\mathcal{M}_\mathrm{relative}}$; $\mathcal{M}_\mathrm{MedVAL} = 1 - \mathcal{M}_\mathrm{consistency} / 6$." — §2.1, the load-bearing filter metric.

> "Reference-based similarity is not a faithful proxy for reference-free, input-only clinical risk grading, whereas MedVAL aligns closely with the physician rubric." — §3.7.

> "Improving small/open models is directly relevant for real-world deployment as hospital systems face barriers to using proprietary APIs at scale. In agentic workflows, validation is a high-frequency step (per section, per note, per agent action), so efficient models are a practical path for routine validation, with frontier models reserved for escalations." — §4 Discussion.

## Method — The MedVAL Pipeline (§2)

### Stage 1: Synthetic data generation

For input $x$, the generator $g_\theta$ produces:
- a **clean** output $\hat y = g_\theta(x)$ (or $\hat y = y$ if reference $y$ exists), and
- a **perturbed** output $\hat y_\delta = g_\theta(x, \delta)$ at randomly-sampled perturbation level $\delta \in \{0, 0.33, 0.67, 1.0\}$.

The four discrete $\delta$ values map to physician-defined risk strata (see [[RiskLevelTaxonomy|the taxonomy]]):

| $\delta$ | Risk Level | Safety | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0%** | Level 1 (no risk) | Safe | Expert review **not required** |
| **33%** | Level 2 (low risk) | Acceptable | Expert review **optional** |
| **67%** | Level 3 (moderate risk) | Potentially unsafe | Expert review **required** |
| **100%** | Level 4 (high risk) | Unsafe | Expert **rewrite** required |

Each $\delta$ pairs with an instructional prompt telling the generator the *severity* of inconsistencies to inject — e.g. $\delta = 1.0$ → *"inconsistencies that could result in incorrect or unsafe clinical decisions."*

### Stage 2: Data filtering — the $\mathcal{M}_\mathrm{MedVAL}$ metric

The validator $v_\phi$ scores both $\hat y$ and $\hat y_\delta$, producing predicted degradation $\hat\delta_\mathrm{clean} = v_\phi(x, \hat y)$ and $\hat\delta_\mathrm{corrupt} = v_\phi(x, \hat y_\delta)$. The filter combines:

- **Absolute consistency** — $v_\phi(x, \hat y) \approx 0$ on clean outputs and $v_\phi(x, \hat y_\delta) \approx \delta$ on perturbed outputs.
- **Relative consistency** — $v_\phi(x, \hat y_\delta) - v_\phi(x, \hat y) \approx \delta$ (the validator preserves the perturbation gap).

Both squared-error terms enter $\mathcal{M}_\mathrm{consistency}$; divide by 6 (the maximum) and subtract from 1 to bound in $[0, 1]$, ↑ score = ↑ generator-validator agreement. Filter retains examples with $\mathcal{M}_\mathrm{MedVAL} \ge \tau = 0.9$.

### Stage 3: Fine-tuning

Apply standard PEFT [[BootstrapFinetune|SFT]] to an arbitrary LM $v_\alpha$ on the filtered $\mathcal{D}_\mathrm{train}$ → $v_\alpha^* = \mathrm{SFT}(v_\alpha, \mathcal{D}_\mathrm{train})$. Uses **[[QLoRA]]** (4-bit weight quantization + LoRA adapters) on a single NVIDIA A6000 GPU, 5 epochs, Adam optimizer, lr $1\times 10^{-5}$ linear decay, batch size 1, **zero-shot CoT prompting via DSPy** (default `dspy.ChainOfThought`). For proprietary OpenAI models, the team uses OpenAI's managed fine-tune API (PEFT details not public).

### Error taxonomy

Each output receives both a 4-class risk grade *and* an error-category breakdown:
- **Hallucinations**: Fabricated claim (45.7%, most common), Misleading justification, Detail misidentification, False comparison, Incorrect recommendation (12.6%).
- **Omissions**: Missing claim (14.0%), Missing comparison, Missing context.
- **Certainty misalignments**: Overstating intensity, Understating intensity.
- **Other**.

Error frequency rises with risk grade (grades 1–4 avg 0.14 → 3.24 errors).

## Experimental setup (§2.3)

- **10 LMs**: open — Llama-3.1-8B, Llama-3.2-3B, Llama-3.3-70B, Qwen3-4B, Gemma3-27B, [[MedGemma|MedGemma-27B]]; proprietary — GPT-4o Mini, GPT-4o, Claude Sonnet 4, Gemini 2.0 Flash. Selection criteria: license, context length, size.
- **Open-source fine-tune budget**: models **< 8B parameters only** to keep deployment on consumer GPUs; QLoRA 4-bit, 5 epochs, batch 1, Adam lr 1e-5 linear decay, single A6000.
- **Proprietary fine-tune**: OpenAI managed PEFT (details internal); only GPT-4o + GPT-4o Mini.
- **GPT-4o is the synthetic-data curator** for Stages 1 + 2 (unless specified otherwise); filter threshold $\tau = 0.9$ heuristically chosen.
- **DSPy zero-shot chain-of-thought (`dspy.ChainOfThought`)** for all prompting; no in-context examples.
- **Statistical analysis**: per-task / per-risk-level macro-F1; Cohen's linearly-weighted $\kappa$ for ordinal agreement; **[[McNemarTest|McNemar's test]]** with Bonferroni correction (40 comparisons, $\alpha = 0.05$) for baseline-vs-MedVAL pairwise significance; **[[KrippendorffAlpha|Krippendorff's $\alpha$]]** for inter-physician agreement; **[[NonInferiorityTest|paired-bootstrap non-inferiority test]]** (B = 10,000, $\mathrm{LCB}_{95\%}(\Delta) > -0.05$) vs single human expert.

## The MedVAL-Bench dataset (§2.2)

**6 tasks × 840 physician-annotated test outputs** (10 LMs × ~14 outputs/task × 6 tasks), built by a panel of **12 physicians** (7 internal-medicine MDs + 5 radiologists + 3 bilingual IM residents):

| Task | Source dataset | In-dist? | Train | Test | Avg tokens (test) | # physicians |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `medication2answer` | [[MedicationQA]] | ✓ | 500 | 135 | 10±4 | 2 |
| `query2question` | [[MeQSum]] | ✓ | 500 | 120 | 82±66 | 3 |
| `report2impression` | [[Openi]] | ✓ | 500 | 190 | 50±22 | 5 |
| `report2simplified` | Open-i | ✓ | 500 | — | — | — (train only) |
| `impression2simplified` | [[MIMICIV|MIMIC-IV]] | ✗ | — | 190 | 69±61 | 5 |
| `bhc2spanish` | [[MIMICIVBHC|MIMIC-IV-BHC]] | ✗ | — | 120 | 543±391 | 3 |
| `dialogue2note` | [[ACIBench]] | ✗ | — | 85 | 1497±445 | 2 |

Train set is **self-supervised** (no physician labels — generator-validator agreement is the only supervision signal). Test set has gold physician assessments. **15 random examples per task** annotated by multiple physicians for inter-rater $\alpha$ estimation. Multilingual coverage: `bhc2spanish` (English → Spanish translation of brief hospital course sections) reflects the U.S.'s second most spoken language.

## Reader study design

- Each test-time output was generated by sampling $\delta$ uniformly from $\{0, 0.33, 0.67, 1.0\}$ and injecting the corresponding perturbation prompt into the generator — so the test set has a **balanced spectrum** across risk levels.
- Physicians graded based **solely on observed output** (no knowledge of injection), making the rating model-blind and perturbation-blind.
- *"Models sometimes diverge from requested perturbations; consequently, outputs often contain a blend of prompted and natural errors committed by LMs"* — i.e. the test set is **realistic-error-distributed**, not synthetic-only.
- **Most common errors**: fabricated claim (45.7%), missing claim (14.0%), incorrect recommendation (12.6%). Error frequency rises **0.14 → 3.24** as risk grade moves 1 → 4.

## Contradictions and live tensions

- **vs reference-based metrics in [[2025-bionlp-archehr-qa-neural|ArchEHR-QA (Neural)]].** The Neural team's reward composite averages BLEU + ROUGE + SARI + BERTScore + AlignScore + MEDCON. **MedVAL §3.7 directly shows** that on a radiology subset ($n = 190$), BERTScore correlates with physicians at $r = 0.141$, ROUGE-L at $r = 0.259$, AlignScore at $r = 0.678$ — and MedVAL itself at $r = 0.825$. **Implication**: the optimization signal in the ArchEHR-QA paper, while operationally robust against any single-metric pathology, is *not a faithful proxy for physician judgment in reference-free clinical risk grading*. Both papers can be right under different framings (Neural optimizes against reference outputs; MedVAL grades reference-free), but a future system that combines both would need to choose which signal to trust.
- **vs MedHAL / DocLens / VeriFact** — physician-in-the-loop and reference-output dependencies that MedVAL drops. MedVAL claims (and demonstrates) the reference-free, self-supervised path matches or exceeds these.
- **vs RadGraph / RadGraph-F1 / FineRadScore / GREEN / ReXTrust / ReXErr** — radiology-specific prior art. MedVAL generalizes beyond chest-X-ray to 6 diverse tasks including translation and dialogue summarization.
- **vs FActScore (Min et al. 2023)** — domain-specific claim extractors and structured KBs. MedVAL is end-to-end input-output, no extraction step.
- **vs the "GPT-4o is the strongest medical evaluator" zero-shot baseline** — MedVAL distillation lifts GPT-4o itself from 0.545 → 0.587 (+8%), so even the strongest LM benefits from teacher-student self-distillation on filtered synthetic data.
- **vs single-pass filtering limitation** (paper's own §5). The authors acknowledge an iterative refinement scheme where the validator improves progressively could further increase data quality — but leave systematic study to future work.
- **vs the "broader medical tasks" critique** (§5). MedVAL-Bench covers risk-based triage tasks but **does not cover** EHR tabular reasoning, complex free-text reasoning, or consultation dialogues — broader benchmarks like [[MedHELM]], MedS-Bench, HealthBench remain future targets.
