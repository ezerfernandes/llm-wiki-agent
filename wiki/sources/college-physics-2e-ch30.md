## Summary
Chapter 30 of OpenStax College Physics 2e traces atomic physics from the historical discovery of the atom and its parts (electron, nucleus) through the Bohr model of hydrogen and atomic spectra, the atomic origin of X-rays, laser physics, and the full quantum-mechanical description of the atom. It explains how the wave nature of matter forces quantization, introduces the four electron quantum numbers (n, l, m_l, m_s) and electron spin, and shows how the Pauli exclusion principle builds electron shells and the structure of the periodic table.

## Key Claims
- Atoms have internal substructure; the electron was identified via cathode rays (Thomson, q/m ≈ −1.76×10¹¹ C/kg) and its charge measured by Millikan (≈ −1.60×10⁻¹⁹ C), proving charge is quantized.
- Rutherford's alpha-particle scattering off gold foil revealed a tiny (~10⁻¹⁵ m), massive, positive nucleus, ~10⁵ times smaller than the atom, supporting a planetary model.
- Bohr quantized electron orbits by requiring angular momentum L = nh/2π, yielding hydrogen energy levels E_n = −13.6 eV/n² and reproducing the empirical Rydberg formula for spectral series (Lyman, Balmer, Paschen).
- The Bohr model succeeds for hydrogen-like (single-electron) atoms but fails for multi-electron atoms and cannot explain fine-structure doublets.
- X-rays arise atomically as broad-spectrum bremsstrahlung (decelerating electrons) plus element-specific characteristic lines (inner-shell vacancies refilled), with max photon energy E_max = qₑV.
- Fluorescence, phosphorescence (metastable states), and lasers all stem from atomic excitation/de-excitation; lasing requires population inversion plus stimulated emission (Einstein 1917; Maiman's first laser 1960).
- The wave nature of matter explains quantization: an electron's de Broglie standing wave must fit an integer number of times around an orbit (nλ = 2πr), which directly yields L = nh/2π.
- Spectra reveal further quantization: the Zeeman effect (line splitting in magnetic fields) and fine structure show that angular-momentum direction is quantized (space quantization) and that electrons carry intrinsic spin (s = 1/2).
- An electron's state is fully specified by four quantum numbers: principal n, angular-momentum l (0…n−1), projection m_l (−l…+l), and spin projection m_s (±1/2), with L = √[l(l+1)]·h/2π.
- The Pauli exclusion principle (no two electrons share all four quantum numbers) limits subshells to 2(2l+1) electrons and shells to 2n², generating electron configurations and the periodic table's structure.

## Key Quotes (paraphrased)
> Only certain electron orbits are allowed; an electron cannot sit at an arbitrary distance from the nucleus. — Bohr's quantization premise (30.3)
> An electron's matter wave must fit a whole number of wavelengths around its orbit, just as a string fixed at both ends supports only certain standing waves. — wave origin of quantization (30.6)
> No two electrons in an atom can occupy the same quantum state — they cannot share the same set of quantum numbers. — Pauli exclusion principle (30.9)
> Stimulated emission and absorption are equally probable, so a population inversion is what lets a laser produce net light output. — Einstein's 1917 insight (30.5)
