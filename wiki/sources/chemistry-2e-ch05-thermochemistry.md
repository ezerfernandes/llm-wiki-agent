## Summary
OpenStax Chemistry 2e Chapter 5 introduces thermochemistry — the study of energy changes accompanying chemical and physical processes. It defines [[ThermalEnergy]], [[HeatThermodynamics|heat]], temperature, and work; states the law of conservation of energy (the first law of thermodynamics); and develops heat capacity and specific heat with the relation q = cmΔT. It explains [[Calorimetry]] (coffee-cup and bomb calorimeters) for measuring heat transfer between a [[ThermodynamicSystem|system]] and its surroundings, then builds [[EnthalpyChemistry|enthalpy]] from internal energy (ΔU = q + w; H = U + PV; ΔH = q_p at constant pressure). It closes with [[ThermochemicalEquation|thermochemical equations]], standard states, the [[StandardEnthalpyOfFormation|standard enthalpy of formation]], and [[HessLaw|Hess's law]] for computing reaction enthalpies.

## Key Claims
- **Energy** is the capacity to supply heat or do work; **heat (q)** is the transfer of thermal energy between bodies at different temperatures, while **temperature** measures the average kinetic energy of particles. Heat flows spontaneously from hot to cold until thermal equilibrium.
- The **law of conservation of energy** (the first law of thermodynamics): energy cannot be created or destroyed, only changed in form.
- **Heat capacity** C = q/ΔT (extensive) and **specific heat** c = q/(mΔT) (intensive) quantify heat storage; for a substance with no reaction/phase change, **q = c·m·ΔT**. Water's specific heat is high (4.184 J/g·°C); most metals are < 1 J/g·°C.
- In an isolated **calorimeter**, heat lost by one body equals heat gained by another: q₁ = −q₂; for reactions in solution, **q_reaction = −q_solution**. Coffee-cup calorimeters run at constant pressure; bomb calorimeters at constant volume (for combustion).
- Sign convention: **exothermic** processes release heat (q, ΔH < 0); **endothermic** processes absorb heat (q, ΔH > 0).
- **First law**: ΔU = q + w (q = heat into system, w = work done on system); expansion work PΔV = −w. Internal energy and enthalpy are **state functions** (path-independent); heat and work are not.
- **Enthalpy** H = U + PV; at constant pressure with only expansion work, ΔH = q_p — the heat of reaction equals the enthalpy change.
- **Thermochemical equations** pair a balanced reaction with ΔH; ΔH scales with coefficients (extensive) and reverses sign when the reaction is reversed; physical states must be specified.
- A **standard state** is 1 bar (1 M for solutions), conventionally at 298.15 K. The **standard enthalpy of formation** ΔHf° forms 1 mol of a substance from elements in their most stable states; ΔHf° of an element in its standard state is **zero**.
- **Hess's law**: the ΔH of an overall process equals the sum of the ΔH of its steps — valid because enthalpy is a state function. Equivalently, **ΔH°_rxn = Σn·ΔHf°(products) − Σn·ΔHf°(reactants)**.

## Key Quotes
> "During a chemical or physical change, energy can be neither created nor destroyed, although it can be changed in form." — statement of the first law of thermodynamics

> "The value of a state function depends only on the state that a system is in, and not on how that state is reached." — defining state functions (internal energy, enthalpy)

> "If a process can be written as the sum of several stepwise processes, the enthalpy change of the total process equals the sum of the enthalpy changes of the various steps." — Hess's law
