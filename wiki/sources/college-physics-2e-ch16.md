## Summary
OpenStax College Physics 2e Chapter 16 develops oscillatory motion and waves from elastic restoring forces. It builds from Hooke's law and elastic potential energy to simple harmonic motion, the simple pendulum, energy conservation in oscillators, and the projection-of-circular-motion view of SHM. It then treats damped harmonic motion, forced oscillation and resonance, before introducing waves: transverse vs. longitudinal, wave speed, superposition and interference, standing waves with harmonics, beats, and wave intensity.

## Key Claims
- A restoring force proportional to displacement (Hooke's law, `F = -kx`) produces oscillation; elastic potential energy is `PE_el = ½ k x²`.
- Period and frequency are reciprocals (`f = 1/T`); the SI frequency unit is the hertz.
- Simple harmonic motion occurs when the net force obeys Hooke's law without dissipation; its period `T = 2π √(m/k)` depends only on mass and stiffness, never on amplitude.
- A simple pendulum is SHM for angles below ~15° (small-angle approximation sin θ ≈ θ), with `T = 2π √(L/g)`, enabling measurement of g.
- In an undamped oscillator total mechanical energy is conserved, cycling between kinetic and elastic potential, giving `v_max = X √(k/m)`.
- The projection of uniform circular motion onto a fixed axis is exactly SHM, so `x(t) = X cos(2π t / T)`.
- Damping removes mechanical energy; critical damping returns a system to equilibrium fastest without overshoot, between underdamped (oscillating) and overdamped (slow) regimes.
- Driving an oscillator at its natural frequency f₀ produces resonance — maximum amplitude; less damping yields a taller, narrower resonance peak.
- A wave is a disturbance that transports energy without net transport of the medium; wave speed obeys `v_w = f λ`.
- Overlapping waves superimpose (constructive vs. destructive); two counter-propagating identical waves form standing waves with nodes, antinodes, a fundamental `f₁ = v_w/2L`, and harmonics `f_n = n f₁`.
- Two close frequencies beat at `f_B = |f₁ - f₂|`.
- Wave intensity is `I = P/A` (W/m²) and is proportional to amplitude squared, so constructive interference (amplitude 2X) quadruples intensity.

## Key Quotes (paraphrased)
> A wave is a disturbance that travels outward from its source carrying energy. — chapter introduction
> Critical damping returns an oscillator to equilibrium as quickly as possible without oscillating about that position. — §16.7
> The natural frequency is the frequency a system would oscillate at with no driving force and no damping. — §16.8
> In a wave, the disturbance moves through the medium while the particles of the medium largely stay put. — §16.9
