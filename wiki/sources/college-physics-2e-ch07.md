## Summary
Chapter 7 of OpenStax College Physics 2e develops energy as a unifying, conserved quantity and work as the mechanism that transfers or transforms it. It builds the scientific definition of work, the work-energy theorem, kinetic and gravitational potential energy, the distinction between conservative and nonconservative forces, the general law of conservation of energy, power and efficiency, and closes with energy in human metabolism and a survey of world energy resources.

## Key Claims
- Work in physics is W = F d cos θ — only the force component along the displacement counts, so holding or carrying horizontally does no work.
- The work-energy theorem states net work equals the change in kinetic energy, W_net = ½mv² − ½mv₀², and holds for any combination of forces.
- Kinetic energy (½mv²) scales with speed squared, which is why fast impacts are so destructive (echoed by du Châtelet's clay-penetration experiments).
- Gravitational potential energy (ΔPE_g = mgh) is a property of the Earth–object system and its change is path-independent — only height difference matters.
- A conservative force's work depends only on endpoints (gravity, springs), allowing a potential energy; elastic/spring PE is ½kx².
- Under conservative forces alone, mechanical energy (KE + PE) is conserved: KE_i + PE_i = KE_f + PE_f.
- Nonconservative forces such as friction are path-dependent, change mechanical energy by exactly W_nc, and convert mechanical energy to unrecoverable thermal energy.
- The general conservation law adds "other energy": KE_i + PE_i + W_nc + OE_i = KE_f + PE_f + OE_f; total energy is always constant.
- Efficiency is useful output over total input; real conversions are lossy (coal-to-electricity ~42%, human cycling ~20%).
- Power is the rate of energy transfer, P = W/t, measured in watts; commercial energy is billed in kilowatt-hours.
- Human metabolism converts food chemical energy mostly to heat (~75%), tracked by oxygen use at ~20 kJ per liter of O₂.
- About 85% of world energy comes from nonrenewable fossil fuels; degradation of energy to waste heat is the core argument for conservation.

## Key Quotes
> Energy appears in many forms and is always conserved, yet not all of a system's energy can be turned into useful work. — chapter framing of energy and work.
> Potential energy belongs to a system (the object plus Earth) rather than to a single object, and only differences in it are physically meaningful. — paraphrase, §7.3.
> Although energy is never destroyed, most transformations leak energy as waste heat that can no longer do work — which is why conservation matters despite the conservation law. — paraphrase, §7.9.
