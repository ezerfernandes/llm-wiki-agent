## Summary
OpenStax *Chemistry 2e* Chapter 21 — the final chapter — turns from electronic to **nuclear** changes. It covers nuclear structure and stability (nucleons, the [[NuclideNotation|nuclide notation]] $^{A}_{Z}X$, the [[StrongNuclearForce|strong force]], [[NuclearBindingEnergyChemistry|mass defect and binding energy]] via E=mc², and the [[BandOfStability|band of stability]]); how to write and balance [[NuclearEquation|nuclear equations]] by conserving mass number and charge; the modes of [[RadioactiveDecayChemistry|radioactive decay]] (alpha, beta, gamma, positron emission, electron capture) and their first-order [[RadioactiveHalfLife|kinetics and radiometric dating]]; [[NuclearTransmutation|transmutation]] and the energy of [[NuclearFissionChemistry|fission]] and [[NuclearFusionChemistry|fusion]]; the many uses of [[Radioisotope|radioisotopes]] in medicine, research, and industry; and the units and [[IonizingRadiationChemistry|biological effects of ionizing radiation]]. Nuclear energy changes (billions of kJ/mol) dwarf chemical ones (thousands of kJ/mol).

## Key Claims
- A **nuclide** is a specific nucleus written $^{A}_{Z}X$, where Z (atomic number) = protons and A (mass number) = protons + neutrons. Protons and neutrons are **nucleons**, packed at ~10⁻¹⁵ m and nuclear density ~1.8 × 10¹⁴ g/cm³, bound by the short-range **strong nuclear force** that overcomes proton-proton repulsion.
- The **mass defect** is the difference between a nucleus's calculated constituent mass and its measured mass; via **E = mc²** it equals the **nuclear binding energy** (the energy to assemble or to break apart the nucleus). For He-4 the defect is 0.0305 amu → 28.4 MeV (2.74 TJ/mol).
- **Binding energy per nucleon** measures relative stability and peaks near A ≈ 56 (iron-56 ≈ 8.820 MeV/nucleon, nickel-58) — the reason fusion of light nuclei and fission of heavy nuclei both release energy.
- ~250 stable nuclides lie in the **band of stability**; light stable nuclei have n:p ≈ 1, heavier ones need more neutrons (Pb-207 n:p = 1.52). Even-even nuclei and **magic numbers** (2, 8, 20, 28, 50, 82, 126) are extra stable; all nuclides with Z > 83 are unstable.
- **Nuclear equations** are balanced by two conservation laws: the sum of **mass numbers (A)** and the sum of **charges/atomic numbers (Z)** are each conserved across the arrow. An unknown product is found by difference.
- **Radioactive decay** modes: **alpha** (emit $^{4}_{2}\text{He}$; A−4, Z−2), **beta** (emit electron; A unchanged, Z+1; n→p), **gamma** (excited→ground; A,Z unchanged), **positron emission** (emit $^{0}_{+1}\text{e}$; Z−1; p→n), and **electron capture** (absorb inner electron; Z−1; p→n). Position relative to the band predicts the mode: high n:p → β; low n:p → β⁺ or electron capture; heavy → α.
- Heavy natural radioisotopes decay through a **radioactive decay series** (uranium, actinide, thorium) ending in a stable lead isotope.
- Radioactive decay is strictly **first-order**: $t_{1/2} = 0.693/\lambda$, $N_t = N_0 e^{-\lambda t}$, activity = λN (units becquerel, 1 Bq = 1 decay/s; curie, 1 Ci = 3.7 × 10¹⁰ Bq). **Radiocarbon dating** uses C-14 (t₁/₂ = 5,730 y); other clocks: U-238→Pb-206 (4.5 Gy), K-40→Ar-40 (1.25 Gy), Rb-87→Sr-87 (48.8 Gy).
- **Transmutation** converts one nuclide to another, often using **particle accelerators**; bombardment built the **transuranium elements** (e.g. U-238 → Np-239 → Pu-239).
- **Fission** splits a heavy nucleus (U-235 + n → Ba-141 + Kr-92 + 3n), releasing ~1.8 × 10¹⁰ kJ/mol and 2–3 neutrons that can sustain a **chain reaction** above a **critical mass**. **Reactors** control it with five parts: fuel (~5% U-235), moderator, coolant, control rods, and shielding.
- **Fusion** joins light nuclei (4 ¹H → ⁴He in the sun, ~3.6 × 10¹¹ kJ/mol; ²H + ³H → ⁴He + n, 1.69 × 10⁹ kJ/mol) but needs ~15 million K; controlled fusion (ITER, NIF) is not yet self-sustaining.
- **Radioisotopes** serve as tracers and in medicine (Tc-99m imaging from Mo-99 generators; I-131 thyroid; Tl-201 heart; cobalt-60 radiotherapy and brachytherapy), research (C-14 mapped photosynthesis), industry (thickness gauging, flaw detection, food/pest control), and smoke detectors (Am-241).
- **Ionizing radiation** (α, β, γ, X-rays, high-energy UV) damages tissue directly and via hydroxyl radicals from ionized water. Penetration: α < β < neutrons < γ; ionizing power: α ≈ 10× β. Units: activity (Bq, Ci), absorbed dose (gray, 1 Gy = 1 J/kg = 100 rad), dose equivalent (sievert = RBE × Gy; 1 Sv = 100 rem). LD₅₀ ≈ 500 rem; average US exposure ≈ 620 mrem/yr; radon-222 is the leading cause of lung cancer in nonsmokers.

## Key Quotes
> "Changes of nuclei that result in changes in their atomic numbers, mass numbers, or energy states are nuclear reactions." — §21.2
> "the spontaneous change of an unstable nuclide into another" — definition of radioactive decay, §21.3
> "the time required for half of the atoms in a sample to decay" — definition of half-life, §21.3
> "the breaking of a large nucleus into smaller pieces" — definition of fission, §21.4
> "emissions energetic enough to knock electrons out of molecules (for example, α and β particles, γ rays, X-rays, and high-energy ultraviolet radiation)" — definition of ionizing radiation, §21.6
