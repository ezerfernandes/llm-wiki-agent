## Summary
Chu, Zhang, Lin et al. (HKUST + NUS + Oxford + NTU + CUHK + HKU + UW + UTSD + SMU + HKUST-Guangzhou) survey **agentic world modeling** under a unifying **"levels × laws"** taxonomy. The capability axis distinguishes **L1 Predictor** (one-step local transition operators) → **L2 Simulator** (multi-step action-conditioned rollouts that respect domain laws) → **L3 Evolver** (autonomously revises its own model when predictions fail against new evidence). The law axis identifies four governing-law regimes — **physical, digital, social, scientific** — each constraining what a world model must satisfy and where it most likely fails. The paper synthesizes 400+ works and ~100 representative systems across model-based RL, video generation, web/GUI agents, multi-agent social simulation, and AI-driven scientific discovery, and proposes decision-centric evaluation principles plus a minimal reproducible evaluation package.

## Key Claims
- **Three capability levels** form a strict elevation hierarchy. L2 requires composing L1 transitions while respecting domain laws; L3 additionally requires online self-revision against new evidence.
- **Four law regimes** — physical, digital, social, scientific — drive different failure modes and require different inductive biases. Cross-domain analysis (§4.2.5) shows where techniques transfer and where they do not.
- **Prediction vs. decision-centric evaluation.** Prediction accuracy alone is insufficient; the paper proposes evaluating world models against the decisions they enable, with three boundary conditions and L1/L2/L3 differentiation criteria.
- **Architectural building blocks (§7).** State inference, forward dynamics, observation decoding, inverse dynamics — assembled differently across regimes (e.g. video generation vs. web agents vs. multi-agent social simulation).
- **Roadmap claim.** A path from passive next-step prediction toward world models that can simulate and ultimately reshape the environments in which agents operate; explicit "Beyond L3" speculation.
- **Connects previously isolated communities.** Model-based RL, video generative models, GUI/web agents, social simulation, and AI4Science were largely talking past each other; the levels × laws frame proposes a shared vocabulary.
