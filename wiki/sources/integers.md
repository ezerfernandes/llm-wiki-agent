## Summary
Among the different [[types-of-numbers|types of numbers]], the integers emerge when we extend the [[natural-numbers|natural numbers]] to include the additive opposites of every positive quantity. In this enlarged system we find all whole quantities, both positive and negative, together with zero. The [[sets|set]] is denoted by \\(\mathbb{Z}\\). Symbolically we write: \\[ \mathbb{Z} = \{\ldots,-3,-2,-1,0,1,2,3,\ldots\} \\] an infinite collection of evenly spaced points along the number [[Lines|line]].

## Key Claims
- **The Integers as an algebraic ring** — When we say that the integers form a [[rings|ring]], we mean that the set \\(\mathbb{Z}\\) comes equipped with two operations, addition and multiplication, that interact in a structured and predictable way.
- **Fundamental properties of the integers** — Compatibility with equality: whenever two integers satisfy \\(a = b\\), any operation applied to both sides preserves that equality.
- **Integers in base 10** — Integers are typically written using the decimal system, that is, base 10.
- **The binary system** — Although integers are commonly written in base 10, other numeral systems are equally valid and sometimes more convenient.
- **The modulo operator** — Modular arithmetic describes how integers behave when we are interested only in their remainders after division by a fixed integer \\(n\\).
- **Integers and the role of induction** — In mathematics, several structural properties of the integers depend on the recursive nature of the natural numbers.
