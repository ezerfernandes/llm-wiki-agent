## Summary
Chapter 27 of OpenStax College Physics 2e covers wave (physical) optics — the phenomena that only make sense when light is treated as a wave rather than as rays. It develops [[HuygensPrinciple]], interference (Young's double slit, diffraction gratings), single-slit diffraction, the [[RayleighCriterion]] resolution limit, [[ThinFilmInterference]], and [[Polarization]] (Malus's law, Brewster's angle), closing with how wave behavior both limits and enhances microscopy.

## Key Claims
- Wave behavior (interference, diffraction) dominates when light interacts with structures comparable to its wavelength; ray optics suffices only for large objects.
- Huygens's principle (every wavefront point emits forward wavelets; the new wavefront is their tangent) explains reflection, refraction, and diffraction.
- When light enters a medium, frequency is preserved while speed and wavelength scale by 1/n (`λₙ = λ/n`, `v = c/n`); perceived color tracks frequency, not wavelength.
- Young's double slit gives bright fringes at `d sin θ = m λ` and dark fringes at `d sin θ = (m + ½) λ`, proving light's wave nature and enabling wavelength measurement.
- A diffraction grating (`d sin θ = m λ`) produces sharper, brighter, better-separated maxima than two slits; sharpness improves with slit count, making gratings the basis of spectroscopy.
- Single-slit diffraction has minima at `D sin θ = m λ` (m = ±1, ±2, …), with a bright central maximum twice as wide as the secondary maxima.
- The Rayleigh criterion `θ = 1.22 λ/D` sets the diffraction limit on resolution; larger aperture (or higher numerical aperture) resolves finer detail.
- Thin-film color arises from interference between rays reflected off the film's two surfaces, including a 180° phase shift when reflecting off a higher-index medium; anti-reflective coatings exploit destructive interference.
- Reflection and scattering partially polarize light; Malus's law `I = I₀ cos²θ` governs transmission through a polarizer, and Brewster's angle `tan θ_b = n₂/n₁` gives fully polarized reflection.
- The wave nature of light caps optical resolution near one wavelength (linked to Heisenberg uncertainty) yet enables contrast-enhancing techniques: interference, phase-contrast, polarization, and confocal microscopy.

## Key Quotes (paraphrased)
> Each point on a wavefront acts as a source of forward-spreading wavelets, and the next wavefront is the surface tangent to all of them. — statement of Huygens's principle (27.2)

> Two sources are just barely resolvable when the central peak of one diffraction pattern sits over the first minimum of the other. — the Rayleigh criterion (27.6)

> Reflecting off a medium of higher refractive index flips the wave's phase by 180° (a λ/2 shift). — phase change on reflection, key to thin-film color (27.7)
