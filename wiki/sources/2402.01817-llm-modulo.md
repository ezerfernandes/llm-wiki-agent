## Summary

[[SubbaraoKambhampati]] et al. ([[ArizonaStateUniversity]]) argue a **two-sided position**: autoregressive LLMs cannot by themselves plan or self-verify (planning is a System-2 task; LLMs are a giant pseudo-[[System1And2|System 1]] of approximate retrieval), but they can still be enormously useful in planning if embedded in an **[[LLMModuloFramework|LLM-Modulo Framework]]** — a Generate-Test-Critique loop where the LLM proposes candidate plans (and helps acquire domain models, reformulate between syntaxes, refine specs, enumerate critics) while a bank of *external* sound critics — hard model-based verifiers like [[PDDL|VAL]] plus soft style critics — judge and backprompt. Soundness is inherited from the hard critics; LLMs are never ascribed planning or verification abilities. The paper marshals empirical evidence ([[PlanBench]] across GPT-4/GPT-4-Turbo/GPT-4o/Claude-3-Opus/Gemini-Pro/LLaMA-3 70B on [[Blocksworld]] and obfuscated *Mystery BW*) that LLMs alone produce executable plans only ~12% of the time on average, that performance collapses near-zero under name obfuscation (proof of approximate retrieval, not planning), and that iterative self-critique does *not* improve results. Two case studies — Blocksworld w/ VAL backprompting (reaching 82% in 15 rounds) and travel planning (6× over baselines with GPT-3.5-Turbo) — instantiate the framework. The paper positions itself against both *over-optimism* (agentic frameworks like AutoGPT/LangChain that conflate acting with planning) and *over-pessimism* (using LLMs as glorified syntax translators to symbolic solvers).

## Key Claims

- **LLMs are pseudo-System 1, not System 2.** Constant-time-per-token autoregressive generation cannot host principled search/reasoning; LLMs are best viewed as *universal approximate knowledge sources* gleaned from civilizational text — System 1 imagination, not System 2 deliberation.
- **LLMs cannot generate executable plans autonomously.** Across GPT-4o, GPT-4-Turbo, Claude-3-Opus, Gemini-Pro, LLaMA-3 70B on [[PlanBench]] *Blocksworld* (600 instances): one-shot accuracy ranges 11–48%; zero-shot 0.5–59%. On *Mystery BW* (same problems with action/object names obfuscated): one-shot drops to 0.4–4.3%; zero-shot collapses to 0–0.16%. Fine-tuning doesn't help materially. Choice of LLM doesn't change the qualitative result.
- **The Mystery BW collapse is the smoking gun for retrieval-not-planning.** Obfuscating names — a change irrelevant to a real planner — destroys performance, indicating LLMs do approximate plan *retrieval* rather than planning.
- **LLMs cannot self-verify and thus cannot self-improve.** The assumption that verification is easier than generation is a classical-complexity argument that doesn't transfer; experiments on graph coloring and on the [[PlanBench]] domains show LLM self-critique makes things *worse* (the critic accepts wrong plans, rejects correct ones, and converges on incorrect solutions). One corollary: claims of "self-improvement" via generating synthetic plans + self-critique + fine-tuning are unsound without external verifiers.
- **The [[ChainOfThought]] / [[Reflexion|self-reflection]] / ReAct family does not produce planning competence.** [[TreeOfThoughts]] is "essentially a way to generate diverse priming prompts" — there is no deeper connection to search-based agents; the soundness it appears to provide comes entirely from problem-specific external verifiers (e.g., the 24-puzzle arithmetic checker).
- **[[LLMModuloFramework|LLM-Modulo]] (Generate-Test-Critique with external sound critics).** Architecture (Fig 3): Problem Specification → LLM proposes plan onto a Plan Blackboard → bank of Critics (hard model-based — e.g. [[PDDL|VAL]] — plus soft style critics, possibly LLM-based) evaluates → Meta (Backprompt) Controller pools critiques into the next iterative prompt → LLM regenerates. **Soundness of the compound system is inherited from the hard critic, not the LLM.** Reformulators (LLM-driven syntax translation between candidate-plan form and each critic's representation) attach to individual critics. Humans engage only "once per domain" (model acquisition) and "once per problem" (spec refinement) — *not* in the inner loop.
- **LLMs play many constructive roles** in the framework: (1) candidate plan guesser; (2) reformulator (LLM is good at format change across syntactic representations); (3) domain-model acquisition partner (extracting PDDL with human sign-off, cf. Guan et al. 2023); (4) problem-spec refiner with end-users; (5) critic enumerator; (6) soft-style critic itself (where soundness isn't required).
- **Case study 1 — Blocksworld with VAL backprompting:** Blocks World performance climbs from baseline to **82% within 15 backprompt rounds**; Logistics improves to ~70%. Mystery BW still only ~10% — because the LLM struggles to *propose* plausible candidates, even though VAL would correctly verify them if it could.
- **Case study 2 — Travel planning** (Xie et al. 2024, *TravelPlanner*): Chain-of-Thought + ReAct on GPT-3.5-Turbo achieve only 0.7% pass rate; LLM-Modulo with operationalized hard constraints (budget) and soft constraints (diversity, common sense) as critics yields **6× improvement** even on GPT-3.5-Turbo with ≤10 backprompt rounds (Gundawar et al. 2024). LLMs also reliably implement *reformatter* duties.
- **Composability with [[ReinforcementLearning]]/simulators.** Simulators (AlfWorld, Minecraft/Voyager, robotics) play the *verifier* role in RL-with-Simulators settings; the paper subsumes this under LLM-Modulo. Also relevant: LLMs as reward designers (Eureka, Kwon et al. 2022), as quasi-symbolic transition/reward modelers (Hao et al. 2023), as policy filters (SayCan, Ahn et al. 2022).
- **Anti-pattern: LLMs as glorified solver front-ends.** Approaches that use LLMs *only* to translate natural-language problems into PDDL/SAT for symbolic solvers (Liu et al. 2023 LLM+P; Pan et al. 2023 Logic-LM; Xie et al. 2023) inherit the *expressivity & search-complexity limits* of the solver. LLM-Modulo deliberately keeps the LLM as a Generate-Test front-end against critics, not as a translator to back-end solvers.
- **Anti-pattern: agentic acting framed as planning.** AutoGPT-style frameworks confuse *acting* (invoking actions via API) with *planning* (guaranteeing the sequence achieves the goal). The mere ability to invoke an action carries no soundness guarantee on the trajectory.
- **Compositional naming: "LLM-Modulo"** is inspired by **SAT-Modulo Theories** (Nieuwenhuis & Oliveras 2006) — SAT solvers extended with theory-specific decision procedures. By analogy, LLM-Modulo = LLM extended with task-specific verifiers/critics.
- **[[PolanyisRevenge]].** Kambhampati's earlier claim that LLMs *avenge* Polanyi's paradox by making tacit knowledge transferable without explicit articulation. Old knowledge-engineering AI required human experts to articulate domain models painfully; LLMs deliver approximate models cheaply — provided we are willing to relax correctness guarantees and use external critics for soundness.
- **Multi-modality does not bestow System 2 competence.** GPT-4V-class systems gain broader System 1 imagination but no principled deliberation.

## Key Quotes

> "LLMs cannot plan themselves but can play a variety of constructive roles in solving planning tasks–especially as approximate knowledge sources and candidate plan generators in so-called LLM-Modulo Frameworks, where they are used in conjunction with external sound model-based verifiers." — central position statement (§1, §6)

> "Even from a pure engineering perspective, a system that takes constant time to produce the next token cannot possibly be doing principled reasoning on its own." — §1, the System-1 argument

> "On average, only about 12% of the plans that the best LLM (GPT-4) generates are actually executable without errors and goal-reaching." — §2.1 PlanBench result

> "We report that the performance is in fact *worse* because the system can't recognize a correct coloring and thus merrily passes over fortuitously correct colorings it has generated, ending up with a wrong one!" — §2.2, on iterative self-critique making things worse

> "In other words, despite the use of terminology of problem-solving agents (Russell & Norvig, 2010)—search tree, expansion etc., there is really no deeper connection to search-based agents." — §2.3, on Tree of Thoughts

> "How would you do robust planning if you have some doddering know-it-all ready to give you any kind of knowledge?" — §2.3, the framing question motivating LLM-Modulo

> "The soundness of the LLM-modulo framework is inherited from the soundness of the correctness (hard) critics." — §3.1, the central soundness claim

> "While multi-modality is a great addition that increases the coverage of their System 1 imagination (Figure 1), it is not clear that this gives them System 2 competence." — §5

## Contradictions

- **Sharp methodological disagreement with the LLM-as-policy / RL-with-simulator papers reframed here**: SayCan, Voyager, AlfWorld-style work is recast as LLM-Modulo *instances* rather than evidence of LLM planning competence — the paper argues that prior framings overcredited the LLM and undercredited the simulator-as-verifier. Worth flagging if those works are ingested.
- **Disagreement with "self-improvement" claims** (Huang et al. 2023b "LLMs can self-improve"; Wang et al. 2022 *Self-Instruct*-style closures): the paper's footnote 3 explicitly notes such claims actually depend on external knowledge (curated seeds, filtering critics), not pure self-bootstrapping.
- **Disagreement with [[TreeOfThoughts]]'s self-positioning as a search/agent framework**: the paper reframes ToT as prompt diversification with task-specific verifier as the actual source of any guarantee.
- **No direct contradiction with current wiki content yet** — no prior source ingested makes a strong "LLMs can plan" claim. If [[2604.21590-agenticqwen]] or [[2604.22748-agentic-world-modeling]] are read as ascribing planning competence to LLMs, this paper's position should be cross-referenced as the counter-argument.
