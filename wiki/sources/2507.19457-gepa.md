## Summary

Agrawal, Tan, Soylu, Ziems et al. ([[UCBerkeley]] + [[stanforduniversity|Stanford]] + [[BespokeLabsAI|BespokeLabs.ai]] + [[NotreDame|Notre Dame]] + [[Databricks]] + [[MITAcademic|MIT]]; lead authors include [[MateiZaharia]], [[OmarKhattab]], [[DanKlein]], [[IonStoica]], [[AlexandrosDimakis]], [[ChristopherPotts]]) introduce **GEPA** (**Ge**netic-**Pa**reto), a **reflective prompt optimizer** for [[CompoundAISystem|compound AI systems]] that **outperforms [[grpo|GRPO]] reinforcement learning by up to 20% while using up to 35× fewer rollouts**, and beats the prior leading prompt optimizer [[MIPROv2]] by **>10%** (e.g. +12% on AIME-2025). Core thesis: **execution traces serialized into natural language carry far richer learning signal than scalar reward**, so an LLM that *reflects* on those traces and proposes new prompts can extract more learning per rollout than policy gradients can. GEPA combines three ingredients — (1) **[[ReflectivePromptMutation|reflective prompt mutation]]** (an LLM reads execution + evaluation traces, diagnoses failures, rewrites one module's prompt); (2) **[[ParetoBasedCandidateSelection|Pareto-based candidate selection]]** (maintain non-dominated prompts on per-instance score, sample stochastically by frequency on the Pareto frontier — avoids local-optimum trap that single-best-candidate strategies hit); (3) optional **[[SystemAwareMerge|System Aware Merge]]** crossover (combine the best-evolved version of each module from distinct lineages). Across six benchmarks (HotpotQA, IFBench, HoVer, PUPA, AIME-2025, LiveBench-Math) on Qwen3 8B and GPT-4.1 Mini, GEPA achieves +9.62 aggregate (Qwen3) and +12.19 (GPT-4.1 Mini) over baseline, vs +3.68 / +5.64 for the strongest prior optimizers. The paper also demonstrates GEPA as **[[InferenceTimeSearch|inference-time search]]** for CUDA / [[NPU|NPU]] kernel generation (NPUEval, KernelBench) and as an **[[AdversarialPromptSearch|adversarial prompt search]]** that reduces GPT-5 Mini AIME-2025 pass@1 from 76% → 10%. Accepted **ICLR 2026 (Oral)**.

## Key Claims

- **Reflective prompt evolution outperforms RL on compound AI systems.** Across four benchmarks, GEPA exceeds [[grpo|GRPO]] (24,000 rollouts) by up to **20%** while using **up to 35× fewer rollouts**. It reaches optimal test performance with 4–35× fewer rollouts and exceeds GRPO on 5 of 6 tasks by 19.0% / 2.73% / 13.66% / 5.19% / 0.7%. With **GEPA+Merge**, the gap widens to **+21% over GRPO at a comparable rollout budget**. Argument: serialized rollout traces (reasoning chains, tool calls, compiler error messages) are readable by modern LLMs, so a *language-based* learning algorithm can leverage strong LLM language priors better than scalar-reward policy gradients can.

- **Pareto-based candidate selection beats single-best-candidate strategies.** Holding the rest of the harness fixed and comparing only the candidate-selection rule on Qwen3 8B: **SelectBestCandidate** (TextGrad-style top-1) gives +6.05% aggregate, **BeamSearch(N=4)** (APO-style) gives +5.11%, **Pareto-based** gives **+12.44%** — roughly double either alternative. Mechanism: per-instance Pareto frontier preserves *all* prompts that win on *some* task instance and prunes only strictly-dominated ones; sampling weighted by appearance count on the frontier diversifies the search and prevents the iteration-budget being spent refining a single dominant strategy that has stalled in a local optimum.

- **Instruction-only optimization now beats joint instruction + few-shot optimization.** GEPA tunes *only* prompts (no demos) and outperforms [[MIPROv2]] (instruction-only, joint, and few-shot variants), [[TraceOptoPrime|Trace (OptoPrime)]], and [[TextGrad]] across all six benchmarks on both Qwen3 8B and GPT-4.1 Mini. **GEPA+Merge** doubles the aggregate gain prior best optimizers achieved over baseline (+13.33% / +12.19% vs +5.64% for MIPROv2). Attributed to LLMs' improved instruction-following + self-reflection capabilities making **declarative natural-language instructions** outperform exemplar-based few-shot demonstrations.

- **GEPA-optimized prompts cross-model-generalize.** "GEPA-Qwen-Opt" — prompts optimized using the smaller Qwen3-8B model and evaluated on GPT-4.1 Mini without any modification — achieves **+9.00% aggregate** over baseline across 6 benchmarks (up to +27.67% on HotpotQA), beating MIPROv2 (+5.64%), TextGrad (+6.11%), and Trace (+3.27%) **even when those baselines optimize directly on GPT-4.1 Mini**.

- **Instruction-optimized prompts are computationally cheaper than few-shot prompts.** GEPA / GEPA+Merge prompts are up to **9.2× shorter** than MIPROv2 prompts (which jointly optimize multiple few-shot demonstrations). Better optimizer accuracy correlates with shorter prompts in aggregate, reducing per-call latency and serving cost.

- **GEPA generalizes as inference-time search for code generation.** Treating the set of target programs as both $D_{train}$ and $D_{pareto}$, GEPA "overfits" the set by iteratively proposing better kernels. On **NPUEval** ([[XDNA2|AMD XDNA2]] AIE-ML NPU kernels): Sequential10 baseline scores **4.25%** mean vector utilization; +RAG → 16.33%; +MIPROv2 → 19.03%; **GEPA → 30.52%** (peaks at 70% for individual kernels). A *single* prompt produced by GEPA enables the same Sequential10 agent (no RAG) to hit **26.85%**. On **KernelBench** (NVIDIA V100 CUDA): GEPA + GPT-4o pushes the $fast_1$ score (kernel beats PyTorch-eager) from ~0% to >20% on the 35-task representative subset.

- **GEPA enables systematic adversarial-prompt discovery.** Inverting the reward to *minimize* task performance while requiring the prompt still describe the task, GEPA evolved a universal adversarial instruction in 150 generations that **drops GPT-5 Mini AIME-2025 pass@1 from 76% (clean prompt) to 10% (adversarial prompt)** — the adversarial prompt injects irrelevant trivia ("honey never spoils", "the Nile is 6,650 km long") plus the formatting demand `'### <final answer>'`, which causes the model to end most responses with the literal placeholder `### <final answer>`. Reveals brittle instruction-formatting interactions reusable as a regression test suite.

- **Evaluation traces are first-class signal.** Beyond execution traces (what the LLM produced), the paper formalizes **[[EvaluationTrace|evaluation traces]]** — the natural-language text the *environment* produces while computing the reward (compiler error messages, profiler output, judge rationales). GEPA's [[FeedbackFunction|feedback function]] $\mu_f$ extends the scalar metric $\mu$ to return `(score, feedback_text)` per rollout, optionally module-specific. This is the **explicit non-metric supervision signal** that lets a single rollout teach far more than its scalar reward alone could.

- **System Aware Merge (crossover) is conditional value-add.** GEPA+Merge identifies distinct optimization lineages (e.g. an iteration that evolved module A while leaving module B unchanged, vs the inverse), picks the best version of each module across lineages, and proposes the union as a candidate. Adds **+2% aggregate** for GPT-4.1 Mini (largest single benefit: +5% on HotpotQA) but **degraded performance on Qwen3 8B** in 3/4 tasks — hyperparameter timing/budget allocation matters. Adaptive scheduling left as future work.

## Algorithm sketch

1. **Initialize.** Candidate pool $\mathcal{P} \leftarrow [\Phi]$ (base system); split $D_{train}$ into a small minibatch-source $D_{feedback}$ and a larger $D_{pareto}$ for full-set scoring; eval baseline on $D_{pareto}$ to seed the per-instance Pareto scores matrix $S$.
2. **While budget remains:**
   - $k \leftarrow \textsc{SelectCandidate}(\mathcal{P}, S)$ — sample from the Pareto frontier weighted by instance-win count.
   - $j \leftarrow \textsc{SelectModule}(\Phi_k)$ — round-robin across the $|M|$ modules of $\Phi$.
   - $\mathcal{M} \leftarrow$ minibatch of $b$ instances from $D_{feedback}$.
   - Run $\Phi_k$ on $\mathcal{M}$ with feedback function $\mu_f$, gather `(score, feedback_text)` per rollout.
   - $\pi'_j \leftarrow \textsc{UpdatePrompt}(\pi_j, \text{feedbacks}, \text{traces})$ — LLM reflects on traces + feedback and rewrites module $j$'s system prompt.
   - Evaluate $\Phi'$ (copy of $\Phi_k$ with module $j$ replaced) on minibatch $\mathcal{M}$; if minibatch score improved, full-evaluate on $D_{pareto}$, add to $\mathcal{P}$, record ancestry.
3. **Return** the $\Phi^* \in \mathcal{P}$ maximizing average score on $D_{pareto}$.

### Pareto-based candidate selection (Algorithm 2)

For each training instance $i$, the per-instance Pareto set $\mathcal{P}^*[i]$ contains every candidate that attains the maximum score on $i$. The union $\mathcal{C} = \bigcup_i \mathcal{P}^*[i]$ is the set of "winning" candidates. Strictly-dominated candidates in $\mathcal{C}$ are removed (a candidate is dominated if some other candidate in $\mathcal{C}$ achieves $\geq$ its score on every instance and $>$ on at least one). The remaining $\hat{\mathcal{P}}^*[i]$ is the per-instance Pareto frontier; the next-iteration candidate is sampled with probability proportional to $f[\Phi_k] = |\{i : \Phi_k \in \hat{\mathcal{P}}^*[i]\}|$. This is the **Mouret & Clune (2015) "illumination" strategy** from quality-diversity evolutionary search, adapted to per-instance scoring.

### Cross-model generalization (GEPA-Qwen-Opt)

Optimize prompts on Qwen3-8B, evaluate on GPT-4.1 Mini *without modification*: **+9.00 aggregate** (HotpotQA +27.67), beating MIPROv2 / TextGrad / Trace that all optimized directly on GPT-4.1 Mini.

### Inference-time search

- **NPUEval (AMD XDNA2 NPU):** GEPA + GPT-4o reaches **30.52% mean vector utilization** vs Sequential10 baseline 4.25%, +RAG 16.33%, +MIPROv2 19.03%. Several individual kernels reach **70%**.
- **KernelBench (V100 CUDA):** GEPA boosts GPT-4o $fast_1$ from ~0% to **>20%** on 35 representative tasks.

### Adversarial search

GEPA evolves a universal adversarial instruction; **GPT-5 Mini AIME-2025 pass@1: 76% → 10%**. The adversarial prompt is task-preserving (still describes the answer-format requirement) — the breakage comes from injected distractors + strict literal-formatting demand interacting badly with the model's instruction-following.

## Key Quotes

> "*We argue that the interpretable nature of language often provides a much richer learning medium for LLMs, compared to policy gradients derived from sparse, scalar rewards.*"

> "*Across six tasks, GEPA outperforms GRPO by 6% on average and by up to 20%, while using up to 35× fewer rollouts.*"

> "*GEPA samples trajectories (e.g., reasoning, tool calls, and tool outputs) and reflects on them in natural language to diagnose problems, propose and test prompt updates, and combine complementary lessons from the Pareto frontier of its own attempts.*"

> "*Algorithms that learn deliberately in natural language by reflecting on these trajectories can make more effective use of the strong language priors that LLMs have, compared with standard RL approaches.*"

> "*GEPA returns the candidate with the best aggregate performance on $D_{pareto}$.*"

> "*Reflectively evolved instructions — such as those generated by GEPA — maintain compactness while providing large performance gains. ... prompts produced by GEPA and GEPA+Merge are up to 9.2× shorter than those from MIPROv2.*"

> "*The learned adversarial instruction reduced pass@1 on AIME-2025 from 76% (clean prompt) to 10% (adversarial prompt), despite preserving the nominal task description and the required answer-format directive.*"

- [[GEPA]] — the [[DSPy]] concept page is updated by this paper as the **canonical primary source** (previously the page summarized only the DSPy *Learn* docs framing).

- [[GeneticPareto]] — "GEPA" backronym; the genetic-algorithm + Pareto-frontier combination is the architectural distinguisher vs both pure GAs (no Pareto) and pure RL (no language).
- [[InferenceTimeSearch]] — the second GEPA mode: treat target tasks as $D_{train}$, "overfit" them by iterating better solutions per problem.
- [[AdversarialPromptSearch]] — the inverted-reward GEPA mode that discovers brittle instruction-formatting interactions.
- [[PromptOptimization]] — the general activity; GEPA is the first prompt optimizer to (a) outperform RL on compound AI systems, (b) cross-generalize from a smaller to a larger model.
- [[reinforcementlearning|Reinforcement Learning]] — the family GEPA argues against using for sample-constrained compound AI system adaptation.
- [[rlvr|RLVR]] — Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards; GRPO is the typical algorithm.

### Position-paper resonance
- [[2402.01817-llm-modulo]] — Kambhampati et al.'s **LLM-Modulo Framework** is the natural-language-reflection precedent: external sound critics + LLM as candidate generator. GEPA's `(reflection, feedback function)` pair is a concrete instance — the feedback function $\mu_f$ supplies the *external* critic signal (compiler errors, judge text), and reflection is the LLM's role as candidate proposer. Where Kambhampati cautions that LLMs cannot self-verify, GEPA never asks the LLM to self-verify — it uses the LLM only to *propose* and grounds correctness in $\mu_f$ from the environment. **Read GEPA's results as confirmation of the LLM-Modulo prescription**, not as a counter-claim about LLM planning competence.
- [[2605.12966-agentic-ai-to-agi]] — Liao et al.'s DAG/topology framing places GEPA as an **agentic AI** whose per-iteration evolution loop is a controlled-topology system. The Pareto-selection vs SelectBestCandidate ablation (+12.44% vs +6.05%) is empirical evidence for the [[CompositionalCapacity]] claim that *topology* not *node strength* drives generalization.

## Contradictions

### Updates / overwrites the prior [[GEPA]] concept page
The pre-existing [[GEPA]] concept page in this wiki summarized GEPA *only* through the [[DSPy]] *Learn* documentation framing — "reflection-based instruction optimizer + domain-specific textual feedback + carve-out from the 20/80 split rule". **This paper is the canonical primary source** and adds five non-DSPy-doc facts:
1. The full name: **Genetic-Pareto**.
2. The **Pareto-frontier candidate-selection** mechanism (the DSPy docs name reflection-based mutation but not the selection rule).
3. The **System Aware Merge** crossover (not in the DSPy docs).
4. The **rollout-efficiency claim** vs GRPO (specific quantitative gap).
5. The **inference-time-search** and **adversarial-search** generalizations (DSPy docs frame GEPA as instruction-optimization only).

The DSPy docs' "GEPA works well on small training sets" claim is **consistent** with the paper: the Pareto-selection mechanism is precisely *why* GEPA doesn't need a large val set to avoid local optima.

### Productive tension with [[grpo|GRPO]]'s sample-efficiency claim
The GRPO literature (Shao et al. 2024 and follow-ups) argues critic-free policy-gradient is *more* sample-efficient than PPO. GEPA's empirical claim is that **even compared to GRPO**, language-based reflective evolution is **35× more sample-efficient on compound AI systems**. The contradiction is real but scope-limited: GEPA *only* updates prompts $\Pi_\Phi$, not weights $\Theta_\Phi$. The settings differ: when the underlying model itself must change (e.g. to learn a new capability the prompt can't elicit), GRPO is in scope and GEPA is not. The paper acknowledges this — *"GEPA evolves only the set of prompts, denoted as $\Pi_\Phi$, whereas the underlying LLM weights, denoted by $\Theta_\Phi$, remains fixed."* Read: GEPA wins where prompt-tuning is sufficient; weight-space RL wins where it isn't.

### Tension with [[2604.27707-agentic-memory-is-a-memo]]'s Ω(k²) gap
Xu et al. argue retrieval-based "agentic memory" has an Ω(k²) compositional-generalization gap that no context size can close — only weight consolidation crosses it. GEPA-evolved prompts are *not* memory in the retrieval sense, but they *are* prompts (context, not weights). Where does the GEPA learning signal end up? In the **system prompt** of each module, i.e. context. This *should* be subject to Xu et al.'s Ω(k²) ceiling on compositional novelty — yet GEPA generalizes across model families (Qwen-Opt → GPT-4.1 Mini) and beats weight-tuning RL on the benchmarks tested. Two possible resolutions: (a) the benchmarks tested don't exercise compositional novelty beyond what fits in a prompt (Hotpot/IFBench/HoVer/PUPA are within-distribution evaluation), or (b) the Ω(k²) bound applies to retrieval-mediated context, not to deliberately-optimized declarative prompts. **Open question** — neither paper acknowledges the tension and the benchmarks don't decide it.

### Reinforces [[2605.02396-heavyskill]]'s "internalizable inner skill" finding
Wang et al. (HEAVYSKILL) argue apparent harness gains come from an internalizable inner skill that can be RL'd into one model. GEPA's evidence cuts the other way: even when only prompts change (not weights), 35× more learning-per-rollout is extractable than a same-rollout RL run produces. **Both can be true**: the GEPA-evolved prompt captures the "inner skill" in declarative form and the model already knows how to execute it; RL would re-internalize the same skill at higher cost. The two papers together suggest reflective prompt evolution is the *cheaper path to the same skill discovery* that RLVR also produces, when the base model is capable enough to follow the resulting prompt.

### Tension with the "few-shot examples dominate instruction tuning" prior
Wan et al. (2024) and others showed instruction optimization yields gains primarily by producing *quasi-exemplars* and that few-shot is the dominant signal. GEPA contradicts this in the 2026 model generation: **declarative instructions** (not exemplars) drive its gains, and GEPA's prompts visibly contain detailed strategy descriptions, lesson lists, and how-to-build-the-query guidance rather than worked examples (see paper's Figure 2 — the optimized prompt for second-hop QA is purely prose). Attributed to recent improvements in LLM instruction-following + self-reflection. The paper frames this explicitly as *"an exciting shift in this trend."*
