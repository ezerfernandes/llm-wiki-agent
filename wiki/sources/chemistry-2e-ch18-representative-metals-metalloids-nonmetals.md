## Summary
Chapter 18 of OpenStax *Chemistry 2e* is a long descriptive-chemistry survey of the main-group (representative) elements organized by periodic family. It begins with periodicity and the metal/metalloid/nonmetal classification, then treats the occurrence, preparation (metallurgy, electrolysis, chemical reduction), and characteristic reactions and compounds of the representative metals (groups 1, 2, 12, 13, 14, Bi), the metalloids (B, Si, Ge, As, Sb, Te), and the nonmetals — hydrogen, carbon/carbonates, nitrogen, phosphorus, oxygen, sulfur, the halogens, and the noble gases. Recurring organizing ideas are metallic character, passivation, the inert pair effect, amphoterism, allotropy, and oxidizing/reducing strength.

## Key Claims
- Representative elements are those filling *s* and *p* orbitals; group 12 counts as representative because its last electron enters an *s* orbital. Metallic character (ease of losing valence electrons) increases down a group and decreases across a period.
- Reactive metals (groups 1, 2, Al) cannot be reduced chemically and are isolated by electrolysis: sodium via the molten-NaCl **Downs cell** (CaCl₂ lowers the mp to 600 °C), aluminum via the **Hall–Héroult cell** (Al₂O₃ in molten cryolite). Cheaper chemical reduction (C, Si, Mg) isolates Zn, Sn, and most of the world's Mg (the **Pidgeon process**).
- The **inert pair effect** gives heavier p-block elements (Tl, Sn, Pb, Bi) a stable oxidation state two below the group value; oxides/hydroxides of Al and Ga are **amphoteric**.
- Metalloids are semiconductors; ultrapure silicon (zone-refined, ≤1 ppm impurity) underpins the electronics industry, and SiO₂ is a covalent network solid (contrast molecular CO₂) because period-3 elements form weak π bonds.
- Nonmetals form covalent, mostly acidic oxides (acid anhydrides), do not form monatomic cations, and span wide oxidation-state ranges; fluorine and oxygen are the strongest elemental oxidizers.
- Hydrogen has three isotopes (protium, deuterium, tritium); two-thirds of world H₂ output goes to ammonia via the **Haber process**. Nitrogen's inertness comes from its triple bond; nitrogen fixation (nitrogenase, lightning) makes N biologically available.
- Sulfur is mined by the **Frasch process** and exists chiefly as S₈ rings with the richest allotropy of any element. Halogens are prepared by oxidizing halide ions (difficulty F > Cl > Br > I) and form interhalogens and polyhalides. Noble gases, long thought inert, form real compounds (XeF₂/XeF₄/XeF₆, XeO₃, perxenate, KrF₂, RnF₂) since 1962.
