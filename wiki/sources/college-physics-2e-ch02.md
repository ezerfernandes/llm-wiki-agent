## Summary
Chapter 2 of OpenStax College Physics 2e introduces kinematics — the description of motion without regard to its causes — for one-dimensional (straight-line) motion. It develops position, displacement, distance, velocity, speed, and acceleration; distinguishes scalar from vector quantities; presents the equations of motion for constant acceleration; applies them to free fall; and covers graphical analysis and a systematic problem-solving method.

## Key Claims
- Displacement is the change in position (Δx = x_f − x₀), a vector depending only on start and end points, not the path.
- Distance is the magnitude of displacement (scalar); distance traveled is total path length and can exceed displacement.
- Vector quantities have magnitude and direction; scalar quantities have magnitude only (and a scalar can be negative as a scale point).
- Velocity is displacement per time (vector); speed is distance per time (scalar) and equals the magnitude of velocity.
- Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity; it points along the velocity *change*, not the motion direction.
- Deceleration (acceleration opposing velocity) is distinct from negative acceleration; same-sign a and v speed up, opposite signs slow down.
- For constant acceleration, average velocity is the mean of initial and final velocities, yielding five standard kinematic equations.
- In free fall (air resistance negligible), all objects accelerate at the same g ≈ 9.80 m/s² regardless of mass.
- On graphs, slope of x–t is velocity and slope of v–t is acceleration; tangent slopes give instantaneous values.
- A six-step method (principles, knowns, unknowns, equations, solve, check reasonableness) structures problem solving.

## Key Quotes
> Kinematics studies motion without considering its causes — paraphrased section framing.
> When air resistance is negligible, all objects fall with the same constant acceleration regardless of mass — paraphrased free-fall principle.
> Acceleration and velocity sharing the same sign means speeding up; opposite signs mean slowing down — paraphrased sign rule.
