## Summary

[[AstonZhang|Zhang]], [[ZacharyLipton|Lipton]], [[MuLi|Li]] & [[AlexanderSmola|Smola]]'s nine-section *Attention Mechanisms and Transformers* chapter — D2L's pivot from gated-RNN [[SeqToSeq|seq2seq]] ([[d2l-recurrent-modern]]) into the [[Transformer|Transformer]] regime and the foundation-model era. Builds the [[Attention|attention mechanism]] from the [[QueryKeyValue|database query]] analogy up: [[NadarayaWatson|Nadaraya–Watson]] kernel regression as the earliest precursor → [[ScaledDotProductAttention|dot-product]] vs [[AdditiveAttention|additive]] [[AttentionScoringFunctions|scoring functions]] (with [[MaskedSoftmax|masked softmax]] for padding) → [[BahdanauAttention|Bahdanau attention]] as differentiable alignment for [[MachineTranslation|MT]] → [[MultiHeadAttention|multi-head attention]] → [[SelfAttention]] + sinusoidal [[PositionalEncoding]] → the full [[1706.03762-attention-is-all-you-need|Vaswani et al. 2017]] [[Transformer]] (encoder + decoder, post-norm) → the foundation-model triptych (encoder-only [[BERT]], encoder–decoder [[T5]], decoder-only [[GPT]] / [[GPT-2]] / [[GPT-3]]) with [[ScalingLaws|power-law scaling]] (Kaplan et al. 2020), in-context learning, [[ChainOfThought|chain-of-thought]], [[RLHF]], [[InstructGPT]] → and the [[VisionTransformer|Vision Transformer]] (ViT, [[Dosovitskiy|Dosovitskiy]] et al. 2021), patches-as-tokens + learnable [[ClsToken|`<cls>` token]] + pre-norm + [[GELU]] MLP. Closes by recasting Transformers as scalable backbones across modalities (CLIP, Imagen, Parti, Gato).

## Key Claims

- **Attention is a soft database lookup.** Given a database $\mathcal{D} = \{(\mathbf{k}_i, \mathbf{v}_i)\}_{i=1}^m$ and a query $\mathbf{q}$, attention computes $\textrm{Attention}(\mathbf{q},\mathcal{D}) = \sum_i \alpha(\mathbf{q},\mathbf{k}_i)\mathbf{v}_i$ — a *differentiable* generalization of an exact key lookup, recovering exact lookup as the one-hot $\alpha$ case and [[AveragePooling|average pooling]] as the uniform case. The "code" (the query) is concise and the database can be arbitrarily large without changing the layer.
- **Nadaraya–Watson kernel regression is the half-century-old precursor.** Treating each training feature–label pair $(\mathbf{x}_i, y_i)$ as a $(\mathbf{k}_i, \mathbf{v}_i)$ and the validation feature as a query, the Gaussian / Boxcar / Epanechikov kernel estimator $f(\mathbf{q}) = \sum_i \mathbf{v}_i \alpha(\mathbf{q},\mathbf{k}_i)/\sum_j\alpha(\mathbf{q},\mathbf{k}_j)$ is exactly attention pooling — except hand-crafted, not learned. Motivates moving to *learned* representations of queries and keys.
- **Softmax + exponentiation is the universal normalization.** Setting $\alpha(\mathbf{q},\mathbf{k}_i) = \exp(a(\mathbf{q},\mathbf{k}_i))/\sum_j\exp(a(\mathbf{q},\mathbf{k}_j))$ for any score $a$ guarantees nonnegativity, convex combination, differentiability, and a never-vanishing gradient — all desirable. Non-differentiable RL-trained attention exists ([[mnih2014recurrent|Mnih, Heess, Graves]] 2014) but is harder to train.
- **Two dominant scoring functions: scaled dot-product and additive.** [[ScaledDotProductAttention]] $a(\mathbf{q},\mathbf{k}) = \mathbf{q}^\top\mathbf{k}/\sqrt{d}$ — derived from the Gaussian-kernel argument, with the $1/\sqrt{d}$ scaling controlling the variance of the dot product (variance $d$ for iid unit-variance components) so softmax stays in a high-gradient regime; the mainstay of modern [[Transformer|Transformers]]. [[AdditiveAttention]] $a(\mathbf{q},\mathbf{k}) = \mathbf{w}_v^\top\tanh(\mathbf{W}_q\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{W}_k\mathbf{k})$ — equivalent to a one-hidden-layer MLP on $[\mathbf{q};\mathbf{k}]$; used when query and key have different dimensions.
- **Masked softmax is the engineering primitive for variable-length sequences.** Set masked-out positions' scores to a large negative constant ($-10^6$) before softmax — making their contribution to gradients and values vanish — rather than branching with `if/else`, because optimized linear-algebra kernels prefer wasted-compute-no-branches. This single primitive powers padding masks, causal/decoder masks, and any structured-attention pattern.
- **Bahdanau attention dynamically builds the context vector.** Where [[1409.3215-seq2seq|Sutskever-Vinyals-Le 2014]] compressed the entire source sentence into a fixed-length vector, [[BahdanauAttention|Bahdanau, Cho & Bengio 2014]] redefine the decoder context as a per-step weighted sum $\mathbf{c}_{t'} = \sum_{t=1}^T \alpha(\mathbf{s}_{t'-1},\mathbf{h}_t)\mathbf{h}_t$ — the previous decoder state $\mathbf{s}_{t'-1}$ acts as the query, encoder hidden states $\mathbf{h}_t$ as both keys and values. Differentiable end-to-end via additive attention. The "single most influential idea of the past decade in deep learning."
- **Multi-head attention diversifies via projection.** Single attention pooling averages; running $h$ heads in parallel with independently-learned projections $\mathbf{W}_i^{(q)}, \mathbf{W}_i^{(k)}, \mathbf{W}_i^{(v)}$, then concatenating and projecting via $\mathbf{W}_o$, lets each head specialize on different representation subspaces (short-range vs long-range, syntactic vs semantic). To keep cost constant, set $p_q = p_k = p_v = p_o/h$.
- **Self-attention is all-pairs: $O(n^2 d)$ but $O(1)$ sequential ops and $O(1)$ max path length.** Comparing layers for $n$ $d$-dim tokens: CNN $O(knd^2)$ / $O(1)$ seq / $O(n/k)$ path; RNN $O(nd^2)$ / $O(n)$ seq / $O(n)$ path; [[SelfAttention]] $O(n^2 d)$ / $O(1)$ seq / $O(1)$ path. Self-attention trades sequence-length-quadratic compute for full parallelism and shortest dependency paths — the structural reason Transformers train faster on accelerators and learn long-range dependencies better. The quadratic cost is also the structural reason it becomes "prohibitively slow for very long sequences" (motivates FlashAttention, Swin, Longformer).
- **Sinusoidal positional encoding injects order into a permutation-equivariant operator.** Self-attention is order-blind by construction; sinusoidal [[PositionalEncoding]] $p_{i,2j} = \sin(i/10000^{2j/d})$, $p_{i,2j+1} = \cos(i/10000^{2j/d})$ is *added* to input embeddings before the first encoder/decoder layer. Encodes absolute position (analog of binary representation with decreasing-frequency bits) and relative position (any $p_{i+\delta}$ is a fixed $2\times 2$ linear projection of $p_i$, independent of $i$ — so attention can learn to attend by relative offset).
- **The Transformer block is the unit: MultiHeadAttention + FFN + residual + LayerNorm.** Encoder block: self-attention sublayer → AddNorm → positionwise [[FeedForwardNetwork|FFN]] (two-layer MLP applied identically at every position) → AddNorm. Residual connection requires $\textrm{sublayer}(\mathbf{x}) \in \mathbb{R}^d$ for $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^d$. [[LayerNormalization|LayerNorm]] (Ba, Kiros, Hinton 2016) is used over BatchNorm because it normalizes per-example, not per-batch — crucial for variable-length sequences.
- **Decoder uses masked self-attention to preserve the autoregressive property.** Decoder block adds a third sublayer: encoder–decoder cross-attention (decoder queries, encoder keys + values), so every target position can attend to all source positions. The decoder self-attention is causally masked — position $t$ can only attend to positions $\le t$ — implemented by setting future entries to $-\infty$ before softmax. This "causal" naming is "common in the literature but misleading."
- **Three Transformer modes underwrite three pretraining paradigms.** *Encoder-only* (BERT-family): all tokens attend to all tokens; pretrained via [[MaskedLanguageModeling|masked language modeling]] (predict masked tokens given bidirectional context); ideal for classification, QA, tagging. *Encoder–decoder* (T5, BART): autoregressive target generation conditioned on encoder output; pretrained via [[SpanCorruption|span corruption]] (T5) or denoising (BART); ideal for text-to-text reformulations of any task. *Decoder-only* (GPT-1/2/3/4): causal language modeling on raw text; "the *de facto* architecture in large-scale language modeling" — leverages essentially unlimited unlabeled text.
- **Pretraining + transfer is the dominant paradigm.** [[BERT]] (350M params, 250B training tokens) advanced SOTA across single-text classification, text-pair classification/regression, tagging, and QA — fine-tuned with task-specific heads. [[T5]] (T5-11B, 1T training tokens of [[C4]]) unifies all NLP as text-in/text-out; "Summarize:" task descriptions allow a single model to handle every benchmark. GPT-2 (1.5B params, 40 GB text) demonstrated zero-shot transfer; [[GPT-3]] (175B params, 300B tokens) demonstrated in-context learning (zero/one/few-shot) without parameter update.
- **Power-law scaling drives the design space.** Performance of Transformer LMs scales as a [[PowerLaw]] in parameters $N$, training tokens $D$, and compute $C$ ([[2001.08361-scaling-laws|Kaplan et al. 2020]]); all three must scale in tandem. Larger models are also more sample-efficient — they reach a given loss with fewer tokens. GPT-3 extended Kaplan's empirical curve by two orders of magnitude. The Chinchilla follow-up ([[hoffmann2022training|Hoffmann et al. 2022]]) re-balanced the recipe toward more tokens per parameter.
- **Vision Transformer (ViT) treats image patches as tokens.** [[VisionTransformer|ViT]] ([[Dosovitskiy|Dosovitskiy]] et al. 2021) flattens an $h\times w\times c$ image into $m = hw/p^2$ patches of size $p\times p$, linearly projects each patch (via a stride-$p$ conv) into a $d$-dim embedding, prepends a learnable [[ClsToken|`<cls>` token]], adds *learned* positional embeddings, and feeds the $m+1$ vectors into a stack of Transformer encoder blocks — using [[PreNorm|pre-normalization]] (LayerNorm *before* MHA/MLP, not after the residual) and [[GELU]] activations. The `<cls>` representation is projected to the classification label. With enough data (300M+ images) ViTs outperform ResNets — "scalability trumps inductive biases" (locality, translation invariance).
- **Post-norm vs pre-norm is the one architectural difference between Vanilla and ViT-style Transformers.** Original Transformer applies LayerNorm *after* the residual addition (post-norm); ViT applies it *before* MHA/MLP (pre-norm). Pre-norm "leads to more effective or efficient training" ([[baevski2018adaptive|Baevski & Auli 2018]]; [[xiong2020layer|Xiong et al. 2020]]) — the dominant convention in modern LLMs.
- **The instruction-tuning / RLHF arc closes the chapter.** [[FLAN|Wei et al. 2021]] and Sanh et al. 2021 fine-tune on instructions to improve zero-shot held-out task performance; [[ouyang2022training|Ouyang et al. 2022]] fine-tune GPT-3 via [[RLHF|reinforcement learning from human feedback]] to follow instructions — [[InstructGPT]] → [[ChatGPT]]. [[bai2022constitutional|Bai et al. 2022]] partially automate this via RL from AI feedback (Constitutional AI). [[ChainOfThought|Chain-of-thought prompting]] ([[wei2022chain|Wei et al. 2022]]) and "Let's think step by step" zero-shot CoT ([[kojima2022large|Kojima et al. 2022]]) elicit reasoning at inference time without retraining.
- **Transformers are now the cross-modal backbone.** Examples in-chapter: [[CLIP]] (image + text contrastive embedding, GPT-2 + ViT encoders), [[DALLE2|DALL-E 2]] (CLIP embeddings → diffusion decoder), [[Imagen]] (frozen T5-XXL encoder → diffusion image decoder), [[Parti]] (all-Transformer text-to-image), [[Gato]] (single Transformer over serialized text + image + joint torques + button presses). Multimodality reduces to serializing every modality into tokens.

## Key Quotes

> "At the present moment, the dominant models for nearly all natural language processing tasks are based on the Transformer architecture." — chapter opening, framing the regime change.

> "Nadaraya–Watson kernel regression is an early precursor of the current attention mechanisms. It can be used directly with little to no training or tuning, either for classification or regression." — situating attention's intellectual ancestry in half-century-old kernel statistics.

> "Bahdanau's attention mechanism provided a simple means by which the decoder could dynamically attend to different parts of the input at each decoding step. … While quite innocuous in its description, this Bahdanau attention mechanism has arguably turned into one of the most influential ideas of the past decade in deep learning, giving rise to Transformers and many related new architectures." — historiographic framing of the attention pivot.

> "Multi-head attention allows the model to jointly attend to information from different representation subspaces at different positions. With a single attention head, averaging inhibits this." — quoting Vaswani et al. 2017's motivation that the chapter formalizes.

> "All in all, both CNNs and self-attention enjoy parallel computation and self-attention has the shortest maximum path length. However, the quadratic computational complexity with respect to the sequence length makes self-attention prohibitively slow for very long sequences." — the structural trade-off that defines both Transformers' strengths and the entire efficient-attention research program.

> "Transformers lack those useful principles in convolution, such as translation invariance and locality. However, the picture changes when training larger models on larger datasets … where vision Transformers outperform ResNets by a large margin in image classification, demonstrating intrinsic superiority of Transformers in scalability." — the data-and-scale-over-inductive-bias thesis applied to vision.
