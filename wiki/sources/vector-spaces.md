## Summary
A vector space is an algebraic structure that formalises the idea of quantities that can be scaled and [[linear-combinations|combined linearly]]. The concept arises wherever one encounters objects that can be added together and multiplied by numbers in a coherent way: geometric arrows in the plane, [[polynomials|polynomials]] with real coefficients, sequences of [[properties-of-real-numbers|real numbers]], and [[ContinuousFunctions|continuous functions]] on an interval all share this common pattern.

- **Examples** — The set \\(\mathbb{R}^n\\) of all ordered \\(n\\)-tuples of real numbers is a vector space over \\(\mathbb{R}\\) under componentwise addition and scalar multiplication.
- **Subspaces** — A nonempty subset \\(W \subseteq V\\) is called a subspace of \\(V\\) if \\(W\\) is itself a vector space over \\(F\\) under the operations inherited from \\(V\\).
