## Summary
Chapter 22 of OpenStax College Physics 2e develops magnetism from first principles: permanent magnets and poles, ferromagnetism and magnetic domains, magnetic fields and field lines, and the quantitative force laws governing moving charges and current-carrying conductors. It builds to the Lorentz force, circular/helical particle motion, the Hall effect, torque on current loops (motors/meters), Ampère's law and fields from currents (including solenoids), the force between parallel wires that defines the ampere, and applications such as mass spectrometers, velocity selectors, and MRI.

## Key Claims
- All magnetism originates from moving electric charge (electric current); magnetic and electric forces are aspects of one electromagnetic interaction.
- Magnetic poles always occur in north-south pairs; isolated monopoles have never been observed, so cutting a magnet yields two complete magnets.
- Ferromagnetic materials magnetize via alignment of magnetic domains and lose magnetization above the Curie temperature (iron: 1043 K / 770 °C).
- Magnetic field lines are continuous closed loops (no endpoints), unlike electric field lines which begin and end on charges — a direct consequence of the absence of monopoles.
- A charge experiences force only when moving: F = qvB sin θ, always perpendicular to both velocity and field (right-hand rule RHR-1); the SI unit is the tesla (1 T = 10⁴ G).
- Because the magnetic force is perpendicular to velocity it does no work; a charge perpendicular to a uniform field moves in a circle of radius r = mv/(qB).
- The Hall effect produces a transverse voltage ε = Blv whose polarity identifies the sign of the charge carriers.
- A current-carrying wire in a field feels F = IlB sin θ; a current loop feels torque τ = NIAB sin θ, the basis of motors and analog meters.
- Currents produce fields described by Ampère's law: straight wire B = μ₀I/(2πr); solenoid interior B = μ₀nI; with μ₀ = 4π×10⁻⁷ T·m/A.
- Parallel currents exert F/l = μ₀I₁I₂/(2πr) on each other (same direction attract, opposite repel); this force operationally defines the ampere.
- Crossed E and B fields form a velocity selector (v = E/B); combined with circular deflection, this underlies the mass spectrometer.

## Key Quotes (paraphrased)
> Electric current is the source of all magnetism — every magnetic effect, including ferromagnetism, ultimately traces back to moving charge. — 22.2
> Magnetic poles cannot be isolated; dividing a magnet always produces two smaller magnets, each retaining both a north and a south pole. — 22.1
> The magnetic force on a moving charge is always perpendicular to its velocity, so it changes direction without changing speed and does no work. — 22.5
> Defining the ampere through the measurable force between two parallel current-carrying wires gives an operational standard for current. — 22.10
