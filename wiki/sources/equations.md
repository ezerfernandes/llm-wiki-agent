## Summary
An equation is a mathematical statement asserting that two expressions take the same value, typically written in the form \\( F(x) = G(x) \\), where one or more variables act as unknowns ranging over some specified [[types-of-numbers|set]], such as \\( \mathbb{R} \\) or \\( \mathbb{C} \\). To solve an equation is to determine every value of the variable, within the appropriate [[DeterminingDomainOfAFunction|domain]], for which the equality holds. - **The solution to an equation** — The solution to an equation is any value of the variables that makes the equality true.
- **Equivalent equations and admissible operations** — Two equations are considered equivalent if they possess identical solution sets.
- **Algebraic equations** — Algebraic equations are equations in which both sides consist entirely of [[polynomials|polynomials]].
- **Rational equations** — [[RationalEquations|Rational equations]] are equations that contain at least one fractional expression whose numerator and denominator are polynomials.
- **Irrational equations** — [[IrrationalEquations|Irrational equations]] have variables inside a radical.
- **Absolute value equations** — [[AbsoluteValueEquations|Absolute value equations]] are equations in which the unknown appears inside an [[absolute-value|absolute value]] expression.
