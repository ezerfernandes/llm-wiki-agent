## Summary
Chapter 28 of OpenStax College Physics 2e introduces special relativity: Einstein's two postulates and the surprising consequences that follow when the speed of light is the same for every inertial observer. It develops time dilation, length contraction, relativistic velocity addition (and the relativistic Doppler shift), relativistic momentum, and relativistic energy including E = mc². Throughout, the [[LorentzFactor|Lorentz factor]] γ governs the size of every relativistic effect, and the classical Newtonian results reappear as the low-speed limit.

## Key Claims
- All physical laws take the same simplest form in every [[ReferenceFrame|inertial reference frame]] (first postulate); there is no preferred frame ([[EinsteinPostulates]]).
- The vacuum speed of light c is invariant — independent of the motion of source or observer (second postulate); the Michelson–Morley experiment supports this ([[SpeedOfLightPhysics]]).
- Simultaneity is relative: events simultaneous in one frame need not be in another, though causal order is preserved ([[TimeDilation]]).
- Moving clocks run slow: Δt = γΔt₀; proper time Δt₀ is the shortest interval, measured in the frame where the events are co-located ([[TimeDilation]]).
- Lengths contract along the direction of motion: L = L₀/γ, with proper length L₀ measured at rest relative to the endpoints ([[LengthContraction]]).
- The twin paradox is resolved by acceleration: the traveling twin's frame is non-inertial, so the situation is genuinely asymmetric and that twin really ages less.
- Velocities combine relativistically, u = (v + u′)/(1 + vu′/c²), so no combination exceeds c ([[RelativisticVelocityAddition]]).
- The relativistic Doppler shift gives observed wavelength/frequency for moving light sources; receding sources are redshifted (basis of cosmological observations).
- Momentum is redefined as p = γmu so that [[ConservationOfMomentum]] holds in all inertial frames; p → ∞ as u → c ([[RelativisticMomentum]]).
- Mass and energy are equivalent: rest energy E₀ = mc², total energy E = γmc², kinetic energy KE = (γ−1)mc² ([[MassEnergyEquivalence]], [[RelativisticEnergy]]).
- The energy–momentum relation is E² = (pc)² + (mc²)²; for massless or ultra-relativistic particles E ≈ pc.
- No object with mass can reach c, because total energy and momentum diverge as v → c.

## Key Quotes (paraphrased)
> The laws of physics hold in their simplest form within every inertial frame — no frame is privileged. — first postulate
> Light's vacuum speed is the same for all observers, no matter how source or observer move. — second postulate
> A clock moving relative to you is observed to tick more slowly than one at rest. — time dilation
> An object's length along its direction of motion is measured shorter when it moves relative to you. — length contraction
> Mass is a concentrated form of energy; even a tiny mass corresponds to an enormous energy via E = mc². — mass-energy equivalence
