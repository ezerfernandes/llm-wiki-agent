# Machine Learning Systems (mlsysbook Vol 1) — Ch 2: ML Systems

## Summary

Chapter 2 of [[VijayJanapaReddi|Vijay Janapa Reddi]]'s open-access *Machine Learning Systems* ([[mlsysbook-ch01-introduction|Vol 1, "Foundations"]], mlsysbook.ai, 2026) answers one question: *why does deploying the same model to a phone vs. a data center demand fundamentally different engineering?* Its thesis is that **constraints drive architecture** — and that three immutable *physical* laws, not engineering preference, carve ML deployment into four distinct paradigms. The **light barrier** ($L_{lat,min} = 2\cdot\text{Distance}/c_{fiber}$, with $c_{fiber}\approx200{,}000$ km/s) sets an irreducible latency floor (~36 ms cross-continental US round-trip); the **[[PowerWall|power wall]]** (post-[[DennardScaling|Dennard]] $P \propto f^3$) caps computation per watt; and the **[[MemoryWall|memory wall]]** (compute growth ~1.33× faster than memory-bandwidth growth) makes data movement the dominant cost. These boundaries produce **[[CloudML|Cloud ML]]** (MW power, TB memory, 100–500 ms latency), **[[EdgeML|Edge ML]]** (~100 W, GB, 10–100 ms), **[[MobileML|Mobile ML]]** (2–5 W, GB, 5–50 ms), and **[[TinyML]]** (mW, KB, 1–10 ms) — spanning ~nine orders of magnitude in both power (MW→mW) and memory (TB→KB).

The chapter's analytical machinery layers onto Ch 1's [[IronLawOfMLSystems|iron law]] ($T = D_{vol}/\text{BW} + O/(R_{peak}\cdot\eta_{hw}) + L_{lat}$): the **[[BottleneckPrinciple|bottleneck principle]]** turns the additive form into a max under pipelined execution (optimizing a non-dominant term yields *zero* speedup), and four **[[WorkloadArchetype|workload archetypes]]** (Compute Beast, Bandwidth Hog, Sparse Scatter, Tiny Constraint) classify workloads by which iron-law term binds. These map onto the five recurring **[[LighthouseModel|Lighthouse Models]]** ([[ResNet|ResNet-50]], [[GPT2|GPT-2]]/Llama, [[DLRM]], [[MobileNetV2|MobileNet]], [[KeywordSpotting|KWS]]). After a paradigm-by-paradigm tour (each with worked "napkin-math" callouts proving cloud, edge, or local processing is *physically* mandatory or impossible), the chapter delivers a four-layer **[[DeploymentDecisionFramework|decision framework]]** (privacy → latency → compute → cost), three **[[HybridML|hybrid integration patterns]]** (Train-Serve Split, Hierarchical Processing, Progressive Deployment), the **[[ComplexityTax|complexity tax]]** of choosing ML over a heuristic, the **[[SystemEntropy|system-entropy]]** warning (every deployed model decays from the moment it ships — the [[Zillow|Zillow]] Offers $304M collapse as evidence), and a Fallacies & Pitfalls section. Closing insight: the four paradigms share the same *systems jobs* (data pipelines, resource management, reliable architecture), which is why optimizations like [[Quantization|quantization]] and operator fusion transfer between scales.

## Key Claims

- **Physics, not engineering, creates the four paradigms.** "A self-driving car *cannot* be served from a data center 36 ms away, and a 1.5-billion-parameter model *cannot* be trained on a microcontroller." Privacy regulation, economics, and data sovereignty *reinforce* the boundaries but did not create them: no regulation makes light faster, no economic model repeals thermodynamics.
- **The light barrier sets the latency floor.** California→Virginia (~3,600 km straight-line) requires ~36 ms round-trip in fiber before any computation; real cloud services add 60–150 ms of software overhead. Sub-10-ms safety tasks *cannot* use distant cloud — the [[SpeedOfLight|speed of light]] forbids it. A 1,500 km cloud datacenter gives a robotic-arm safety monitor a −5 ms deficit against a 10 ms budget.
- **The power wall ended "free" frequency scaling.** Under [[DennardScaling|Dennard scaling]] (held until ~2005–2006, ~90 nm node), $P \propto f^3$ — doubling clock frequency needs ~8× more power. Its breakdown forced parallelism (multi-core) and specialization (GPUs, [[GoogleTPU|TPUs]]). Mobile devices hit hard thermal limits at 2–5 W; exceeding it triggers **[[ThermalThrottling|throttling]]** (a 60 FPS model may drop to 15 FPS within a minute).
- **The [[MemoryWall|memory wall]] makes data movement the dominant bottleneck.** Compute capacity doubles ~every 18 months, but memory bandwidth improves only ~20%/year (≈1.33× annual divergence). Term coined by Wulf & McKee (1995). Especially acute for TinyML (kilobytes of memory).
- **The [[BottleneckPrinciple|bottleneck principle]]: ML systems are dominated by their slowest stage.** Under pipelined execution the iron-law sum becomes a max: $T_{bottleneck} = \max(D_{vol}/\text{BW},\ O/(R_{peak}\cdot\eta_{hw}),\ T_{network}) + L_{lat}$. If memory-bound, buying faster processors yields *exactly 0% speedup* — "like widening a six-lane highway when all traffic funnels through a two-lane bridge."
- **Four [[WorkloadArchetype|workload archetypes]] classify by bottleneck, not model family.** **Compute Beast** (high FLOP/byte; ResNet training), **Bandwidth Hog** (autoregressive LLM token generation), **Sparse Scatter** (irregular access; DLRM embedding tables), **Tiny Constraint** (<1 mW, <256 KB; always-on sensing). Misidentifying the archetype "wastes optimization effort on the wrong term."
- **Both cloud A100 and mobile NPU are memory-bound for batch-1 ResNet-50 inference.** Single-image inference has low [[ArithmeticIntensity|arithmetic intensity]], below the hardware balance point. The A100's higher bandwidth (vs. mobile LPDDR5) — not its peak FLOP/s — drives the inference speedup. "This explains why quantization (reducing bytes) often beats faster hardware (increasing FLOP/s) for deployment."
- **The hardware spectrum spans ~250 million-fold in memory and nine orders in power.** TPU v4 Pod (4,096 chips, >1 EFLOP/s, 131 TB HBM2, ~MW) → NVIDIA DGX Spark edge (GB10 Grace Blackwell, 1 PFLOP/s AI, ~100 W) → flagship smartphone SoC (CPU+GPU+NPU, GB, 2–5 W) → ESP32-CAM (dual-core @ 240 MHz, 520 KB RAM, 4 MB flash, ~sub-watt, ~$10). Cost spans ~six orders ($10 MCU → $10M+ pod).
- **Latency reference spans eight orders of magnitude.** GPU matmul ~1 ns; L1 cache ~1 ns; HBM read 20–50 ns; mobile DRAM 50–100 ns; same-datacenter network 0.5 ms; same-region 1–5 ms; cross-region 50–150 ms; wake-word detection 100 µs; face detection 10–30 ms; GPT-4 first token 200–500 ms; ResNet-50 training step 200–400 ms. **Decision rule: an operation with latency > X cannot appear on the critical path of a system whose latency budget is X.**
- **Cloud ML trades latency for elastic compute.** Training GPT-3 took ~3,640 PFLOP-days, ~10,000 V100 GPUs for ~15 days, MW power, ~$4.6M at 2020 rates — "no single machine on Earth could have performed this." But the same infrastructure's 100–500 ms round-trip makes it unsuitable for real-time inference. Cloud is the home of three of four archetypes (Compute Beast, Bandwidth Hog, Sparse Scatter).
- **The Data Gravity Invariant limits cloud's "unlimited compute."** As data volume scales, the cost of moving data to compute eventually dominates ($C_{move}(D_{vol}) \gg C_{move}(\text{Compute})$). Pinterest/Infor/Intuit-scale cloud bills, plus [[GDPR]] (deleting a user's data may force model retraining) and [[HIPAA]] (15–30% infra overhead) push computation toward the edge.
- **The voice-assistant wall: cloud-only is physically impossible at scale.** 1 billion devices × 20 wake-word queries/day @ 200 ms/query = ~$500M/year (economic wall) AND ~20+ dedicated data centers at peak (infrastructure wall); streaming 16 kHz/16-bit audio from 1B devices = a significant fraction of global internet backbone. "Local wake-word detection is an infrastructure necessity, not an optimization."
- **Edge ML eliminates the network I/O term of the iron law.** Trading unlimited compute for sub-100 ms latency and local data retention, edge processing collapses latency to $\max(D_{vol}/\text{BW},\ O/(R_{peak}\cdot\eta_{hw})) + L_{lat}$. Edge memory bandwidth (25–100 GB/s) constrains deployable models to 100 MB–1 GB; compression buys 2–4× speedup.
- **The bandwidth bottleneck forces edge processing.** 100 cameras × 1080p × 30 FPS ≈ 18.7 GB/s — ~15× more than a dedicated 10 Gbps line can carry. Transmitting only defect metadata (~1 KB/event) cuts bandwidth by ~10,000×. "Physics has made cloud streaming impossible for this application. Edge processing is not optional — it is mandatory."
- **The [[DataLocalityInvariant|data locality invariant]]: data must stay local when transmit time exceeds remote processing time.** $D_{vol}/\text{BW}_{network} > L_{lat,network} + O/(R_{peak,remote}\cdot\eta_{hw,remote})$. A drone's 4K frame at 100 Mbps takes far longer to transmit than the ~110 ms remote response budget — "the cloud could have an infinite processor and the drone would still crash because it cannot move the bits fast enough." 5G/6G improve bandwidth but cannot beat the speed-of-light latency floor.
- **Edge case studies.** Tesla FSD (multi-camera edge inference, millisecond latency); Amazon Go (>1 TB/hour/store, kept on-premises); Industry 4.0 quality control (welds at >60 parts/min, 10,000+ assets/facility, 25–35% downtime reduction); smart buildings (order-of-magnitude bandwidth cut, sub-second response). Edge fleets add fragility: version drift, hardware heterogeneity, physical tampering ($500–2,000/server; 1,000 sites = $500K–$2M upfront).
- **Mobile ML adds energy as a first-order constraint.** Unlike plugged-in edge, $\text{Energy} = \text{Power}\times T$ binds. The **battery tax**: a 2 W always-on object detector drains a ~15 Wh phone in <8 hours (~320% of a full-day budget). The **[[ThermalWall|thermal wall]]** is a separate, harder ceiling: a passively-cooled SoC dissipates ~3 W max; a 12 W LLM hits the 80 °C thermal trip in ~60 s and throttles 100→30 FPS regardless of battery. "Physics sets a hard ceiling that no optimization can exceed."
- **Mobile hardware specifics.** Flagship SoCs: GB RAM, GB storage, NPU AI compute (tens of INT8 TOPS), 2–5 W, LPDDR5 bandwidth limiting models to 10–100 MB. [[NeuralProcessingUnit|NPUs]] give 10–100× energy efficiency (TOPS/W) via fixed-function low-precision matmul. [[ComputationalPhotography|Computational photography]] runs 10–15 ML models within ~200 ms shutter delay sharing one thermal budget. Apple's Face ID / Secure Enclave: 30,000 IR dots, 1:1,000,000 false-acceptance, templates never leave device.
- **TinyML completes the spectrum at the physical limit.** Devices < $10 and < 1 mW make **ubiquitous sensing** economically viable. A KWS model at ~10 µJ/inference runs *years* on a coin-cell (CR2032: 225 mAh @ 3 V ≈ 675 mWh). The **1 mW threshold** is where ambient [[EnergyHarvesting|energy harvesting]] (thumbnail solar ~10 mW outdoors, RF ~10 µW) flips the model from "battery-limited" to "deploy-and-forget." Microcontrollers (32–512 KB SRAM, no virtual memory/DRAM) demand a memory-fit constraint ($M_{model} \le C_{mem}$) the iron law does not explicitly capture.
- **TinyML is qualitatively distinct, not "scaled-down mobile."** 256 KB–1 MB vs. mobile's 4–12 GB (≈10,000× gap); 10K–100K parameters vs. millions; 1–10 mW vs. 1–5 W; FP32→INT8 quantization is *insufficient* below 64 KB, forcing full architectural redesign. Inference-only (full backprop needs per-layer activation storage exceeding MCU RAM; TinyTL fine-tunes final layers in <50 KB). Ecosystem fragmentation across ARM Cortex-M / RISC-V / Xtensa multiplies cost.
- **Energy per inference spans 100,000,000× across paradigms.** TinyML KWS ~10 µJ → Mobile MobileNet ~50 mJ → Edge ResNet-50 (Jetson) ~500 mJ → Cloud ResNet-50 (A100) ~10 J → Cloud GPT-4 query ~1 kJ. A TinyML wake-word detector is 100M× more energy-efficient than a cloud LLM query — *why always-on sensing is only feasible at the TinyML tier.*
- **Paradigm selection: privacy → latency → compute → cost, evaluated sequentially.** Worked autonomous-braking example: privacy permits cloud, but latency (100 ms = 2.8 m of travel at 100 km/h) eliminates it before compute or cost are even considered → Edge ML with a local GPU (NVIDIA Drive Orin class), cloud reserved for training/updates. **Critical pitfall: choosing on model accuracy alone.** A 99%-accurate cloud model is useless for emergency braking if latency exceeds reaction time.
- **The [[ComplexityTax|complexity tax]]: ML is not always the right choice.** A 50-line heuristic (~1 h/month maintenance, no drift) vs. a 2,000-line ML pipeline (~40 h/month debugging drift/infra). An accuracy gain from 90%→95% may not justify a 40× complexity increase. "If the operational cost of maintaining model quality is unaffordable, the simpler heuristic is the superior systems choice."
- **Three [[HybridML|hybrid integration patterns]].** **Train-Serve Split** (cloud training, local inference; training cost vs. inference latency; ≥1,000,000× cost asymmetry). **Hierarchical Processing** (sensor→edge→cloud tiers by timescale; local autonomy vs. global optimization). **Progressive Deployment** (one model family → compressed artifacts per tier; quality vs. reach). Voice assistants combine all three. Production hybrids are defined by *bidirectional flow*: models deploy *down*, data/telemetry flows *up*.
- **[[SystemEntropy|System entropy]]: deployment is the beginning, not the end.** Unlike a sorting algorithm (correct forever), an ML model decays as the world drifts from its training distribution (degradation equation). Reliability is "a property of the monitoring and retraining infrastructure, not of the code." The Zillow Offers collapse (2021): a $304M inventory write-down, 25% (2,000) layoffs, division shut down — distribution shift as *business risk*.
- **Fallacies & pitfalls.** "One paradigm solves all" (100× latency gap is physics, not implementation); "optimization overcomes mobile power/thermal limits" (5 W drains in ~3 h; sustained ~3 W ceiling immovable); "TinyML is scaled-down mobile"; "minimizing compute minimizes TCO" ($2K cloud compute vs. ~$6K edge total — 3× — once network/maintenance/reliability labor counted); "model optimization translates linearly to speedup" ([[AmdahlsLaw|Amdahl's Law]]: 10× faster ML in a 200 ms pipeline where ML = 30% yields only 1.37× end-to-end); "more training data always improves" (250K-param KWS: 95%→96.5% for 20× data); "deploy one binary across all edge devices" (per-target optimization yields 3–5× efficiency).

## Key Quotes

> "The defining insight of ML systems engineering is that constraints drive architecture." — opening thesis; the chapter's organizing principle

> "The question is never simply 'how do I make this model work?' but rather 'which physical constraints govern my problem, and how do they shape what is even possible?'" — on treating deployment as a first-order engineering decision

> "These four paradigms exist not because of *engineering choices* but because of *physical laws* that no amount of optimization can overcome." — on the light barrier, power wall, and memory wall

> "Just as widening a six-lane highway yields no benefit when all traffic must funnel through a two-lane bridge." — the [[BottleneckPrinciple|bottleneck principle]]: optimizing a non-dominant iron-law term gives 0% speedup

> "Both platforms are memory bound for single-image inference! ... This explains why quantization (reducing bytes) often beats faster hardware (increasing FLOP/s) for deployment." — the ResNet-50 cloud-vs-mobile roofline insight

> "Cloud-only voice processing is not merely expensive; it is *physically impossible* at global scale. Local wake-word detection is an infrastructure necessity, not an optimization." — the voice assistant wall

## Contradictions

- **No direct factual contradictions** with existing wiki pages. This chapter *deepens* the [[mlsysbook-ch01-introduction|Ch 1]] sketch of the deployment spectrum into a full physics-grounded framework, and complements the practitioner framings in [[dmls-ch07-model-deployment|DMLS Ch 7]] and *AI Engineering* Ch 9.
- **Reframes the cloud-vs-edge cost question.** Where [[dmls-ch07-model-deployment|DMLS Ch 7]] argues batch prediction is a legacy MapReduce artifact and online prediction is becoming the default, mlsysbook Ch 2 frames the cloud-vs-edge choice through [[TotalCostOfOwnership|TCO]]: edge saves ~45% only at high *steady* volume (with labor as the dominant edge cost), while cloud elasticity wins below the crossover. Not a conflict — a different (TCO/utilization-density) lens on the same cost trade-off, consistent with DMLS's request-density point (Grubhub 622K/31M).
- **"Latency" is multi-scale here too.** This chapter's latency table spans GPU-op nanoseconds to cross-region hundreds-of-milliseconds, distinct from the foundation-model TTFT/TPOT vocabulary in *AI Engineering* Ch 9. The wiki's [[Latency]] page already flags this multi-scale nature; no conflict.
