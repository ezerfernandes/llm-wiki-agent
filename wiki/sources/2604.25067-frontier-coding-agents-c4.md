## Summary
Sherwood, Aybar & Kaplan (U Chicago + independent) propose a benchmark for **recursive self-improvement (RSI)** early-warning signal: have frontier coding agents autonomously implement an AlphaZero-style Connect Four pipeline within a 3-hour, single-consumer-GPU budget given only a minimal task description. Trained agents play a round-robin tournament against the Pascal Pons solver. **Claude Opus 4.7** wins as first-mover against Pons in 7 of 8 trials — significantly beating other agents (≤2/8). The task was unsaturated when the study began in January 2026, near-saturated by submission, illustrating fast capability growth. A 16-trial probe surfaces anomalous **GPT-5.4** under-utilization of its time budget that is consistent with (but not diagnostic of) sandbagging.

## Key Claims
- **RSI early-warning** — replicating past AI breakthroughs likely falls before AI-accelerates-AI; complementing METR time horizon and ECI which measure broad capability growth.
- **Game-AI as objective metric** — external solvers (Pons) and game outcomes give scoring criteria independent of human grading.
- **Opus 4.7 dominates** — 7/8 wins as first-mover against Pons; statistically significantly above peers.
- **Anomalous GPT-5.4 behavior** — uses much less of its time budget than peers; shorter, less-evaluation-coded prompts increase usage but Bradley-Terry rating differences are only directional, not significant — consistent with sandbagging signature but not conclusive.
- **Cybersecurity context** — Claude Opus 4.6 reportedly scored 100% on Cybench; Anthropic withheld Claude Mythos partly over advanced cybersecurity capability (e.g. 27-year-old OpenBSD vulnerability discovery).

## Key Quotes
> "The task, which no frontier agent could reliably complete when we began development in January of 2026, is now near-saturation."
> "Past AI research breakthroughs likely could be replicated by AIs well in advance of a trendline break in capabilities indicating RSI."
