## Summary
Chapter 6 of OpenStax *Chemistry 2e* traces the discovery of atomic electronic structure from the wave/particle nature of light through the modern quantum-mechanical model of the atom. It develops the relationship between wavelength, frequency, and photon energy; the quantization of energy (Planck, photoelectric effect, line spectra, Bohr model); wave-particle duality and the uncertainty principle; the four quantum numbers and atomic orbitals; the rules governing electron configurations (Pauli, Aufbau, Hund); and how those configurations explain periodic trends in atomic radius, ionization energy, and electron affinity.

## Key Claims
- Electromagnetic radiation in a vacuum obeys **c = λν** (c = 2.998 × 10⁸ m/s); wavelength and frequency are inversely proportional.
- Energy is **quantized**: Planck (E = nhν, h = 6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s) resolved the ultraviolet catastrophe; Einstein's photon model (E = hν = hc/λ) explained the photoelectric effect — kinetic energy of ejected electrons depends on frequency, not brightness.
- Atomic **line spectra** are discrete; hydrogen's lines fit the **Rydberg formula** 1/λ = R∞(1/n₁² − 1/n₂²), R∞ = 1.097 × 10⁷ m⁻¹.
- The **Bohr model** postulated quantized stationary orbits with E_n = −kZ²/n² (k = 2.179 × 10⁻¹⁸ J), explaining hydrogen's spectrum but failing for multi-electron atoms.
- **Matter has wave nature** (de Broglie λ = h/mv), confirmed by Davisson–Germer electron diffraction; the **Heisenberg uncertainty principle** (Δx·Δp ≥ ℏ/2) follows from wave-particle duality.
- The **Schrödinger equation** (Ĥψ = Eψ) describes electrons as wavefunctions; |ψ|² gives probability density. Each electron is specified by four quantum numbers (n, l, m_l, m_s).
- **Electron configurations** follow the Pauli exclusion principle, the Aufbau filling order, and Hund's rule; configurations explain the structure of the periodic table (s/p/d/f blocks).
- **Periodic trends** arise from effective nuclear charge (Z_eff = Z − shielding): atomic radius increases down a group and decreases across a period; ionization energy and (the magnitude of) electron affinity show the opposite trend, with exceptions at filled/half-filled subshells.

## Key Quotes
> "Only those waves having an integer number, n, of half-wavelengths between the end points can form." — on standing waves and quantization
> "Somehow, at a deep fundamental level still not fully understood, light is both wavelike and particle-like." — on wave-particle duality
> "No two electrons in the same atom can have exactly the same set of all the four quantum numbers." — the Pauli exclusion principle
> "The lowest-energy configuration for an atom with electrons within a set of degenerate orbitals is that having the maximum number of unpaired electrons." — Hund's rule
