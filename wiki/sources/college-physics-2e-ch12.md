## Summary
Chapter 12 of OpenStax *College Physics 2e* treats fluids in motion. It builds from flow rate and the equation of continuity, through energy conservation for moving fluids ([[BernoulliPrinciple|Bernoulli's equation]] and Torricelli's theorem), to internal friction ([[Viscosity]]) and the laminar-flow law of [[PoiseuilleLaw|Poiseuille]]. It then characterizes the laminar-to-turbulent transition with the [[ReynoldsNumber]], drag on objects via Stokes' law and [[TerminalVelocity|terminal speed]], and molecular-scale transport by [[MolecularDiffusion|diffusion]] and [[Osmosis]]. Biological and medical applications — blood circulation, plaque, IV infusion, kidney dialysis — recur throughout.

## Key Claims
- Flow rate Q = A·v̄; for an incompressible fluid the equation of continuity (A₁v̄₁ = A₂v̄₂) forces velocity to rise where a conduit narrows (v ∝ 1/r²).
- The vast combined cross-section of capillaries makes blood flow very slowly there even though cardiac output is constant.
- Bernoulli's equation (P + ½ρv² + ρgh = constant) is [[ConservationOfEnergy|energy conservation]] per unit volume for an incompressible, frictionless fluid.
- Bernoulli's principle: at constant height, faster-moving fluid has lower [[Pressure]] — explaining lift, entrainment, and atomizers.
- Torricelli's theorem: efflux speed depends only on the vertical fall height (v² = 2gh from rest).
- Viscosity (η) is internal fluid friction; Poiseuille's law gives Q = (P₂−P₁)πr⁴/(8ηl), with resistance R = 8ηl/(πr⁴) extremely sensitive to radius (Q ∝ r⁴).
- A ~16% reduction in vessel radius roughly halves blood flow, forcing the heart to raise pressure.
- The Reynolds number N_R = 2ρvr/η predicts flow regime: laminar below ~2000, turbulent above ~3000.
- Stokes' law F_S = 6πRηv describes laminar drag on small spheres; an object's wake turns turbulent at large N_R′ = ρvL/η.
- Terminal speed occurs when drag balances the driving force; large fast objects (e.g. a thrown ball, N_R′ ≈ 2×10⁵) trail turbulent wakes.
- Diffusion (x_rms = √(2Dt)) is random-thermal transport down concentration gradients; it is far too slow over macroscopic distances, motivating bulk circulation.
- Osmosis moves water across semipermeable membranes; the back pressure that stops it is the osmotic pressure — exploited in dialysis and reverse-osmosis desalination.

## Key Quotes (paraphrased)
> Fluid dynamics is the physics of fluids in motion — it lets us understand smoke rising, water leaving a dam, and blood circulating.

> Pressure falls in a rapidly moving fluid; this single idea (Bernoulli's principle) explains wing lift, entrainment, and sprayers.

> Because flow resistance scales as the inverse fourth power of radius, even small narrowing of a blood vessel sharply reduces flow.

> Diffusion alone is hopelessly slow across centimeter distances, which is why organisms evolved circulatory systems instead of relying on it.
