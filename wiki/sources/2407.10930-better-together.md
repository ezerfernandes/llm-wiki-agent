# Fine-Tuning and Prompt Optimization: Two Great Steps that Work Better Together

## Summary

Soylu, [[ChristopherPotts|Potts]] & [[OmarKhattab|Khattab]] ([[stanforduniversity|Stanford]], arXiv 2024) introduce **[[BetterTogether]]** — the first published algorithm to **alternate prompt optimization and LM weight fine-tuning over the same compound LM program**. Released as `dspy.BetterTogether` in [[DSPy]]. The thesis: prompt-tuning before fine-tuning generates **higher-quality bootstrap traces** for fine-tuning, and prompt-tuning *after* fine-tuning **adjusts behavior** to the new weights; the two steps together outperform either in isolation. Algorithm 1's three-line core (`Π′ ← OptimizePrompts(Φ); Θ′ ← FinetuneWeights(Φ_{Π′}); Π″ ← OptimizePrompts(Φ_{Θ′})`) instantiates Lines 1–3 with [[BootstrapFewShotWithRandomSearch|BootstrapFewshotRS (BFRS)]] for prompt steps and [[BootstrapFinetune|BootstrapFinetune (BFT)]] for the weight step (Algorithm 2). Experiments on **three tasks** ([[hotpotqa|HotPotQA]] multi-hop QA / [[GSM8K]] arithmetic / [[Iris]] classification) × **three models** ([[Mistral7BInstructV02|mistral-7b-instruct-v0.2]] / [[Llama2_7BChat|llama-2-7b-chat]] / [[Llama3_8BInstruct|llama-3-8b-instruct]]) — 9 (dataset, LM) pairs total — show BetterTogether strategies are best in **7 of 9** cells. Headline gains over the better of *prompts-only* or *weights-only*: **5–78% on HotPotQA, 2.5–10% on GSM8K, 3.5–88% on Iris**. The paper also introduces the [[CompoundAISystem|compound-AI-system formalism]] $\Phi_{\langle \Theta, \Pi \rangle}$ that [[2507.19457-gepa|GEPA (ICLR 2026)]] subsequently adopts and extends. **75 A100-GPU-hours** to produce Table 1.

## Key Claims

- **Alternation beats either step alone.** Across 7 of 9 (dataset, LM) cells, the best strategy uses both Π (prompts) and Θ (weights) optimization steps. Gains over the *better* of prompts-only or weights-only: **5–78% HotPotQA, 2.5–10% GSM8K, 3.5–88% Iris** (averaged across the three LMs). The strongest single configuration is typically **Π → Θ → Π** (prompt-optimize, then fine-tune, then re-prompt-optimize): best on HotPotQA mistral (37.6) and HotPotQA llama-2 (34.8), and best on Iris llama-2 (65.3).

- **Prompt optimization *before* fine-tuning yields better fine-tuning traces.** The bootstrap step of [[BootstrapFinetune|BFT]] runs the program on training inputs and keeps only metric-passing traces as the SFT dataset. If the program is *first* prompt-optimized, **more inputs produce correct outputs** → larger and higher-quality SFT dataset → better fine-tuned weights. The paper makes this concrete with the **Iris-llama-2 cell**: vanilla zero-shot accuracy is 0.0%, so the bare Θ-only and Θ→Π strategies have **no successful traces to fine-tune on** (marked "—" in Table 1) — but after prompt optimization the same LM bootstraps enough successful traces to fine-tune, reaching 65.3% with Π → Θ → Π.

- **Prompt optimization *after* fine-tuning adjusts behavior to new weights.** A fine-tuned LM behaves differently from the prompted-only LM — its prior shifts toward the fine-tuning distribution, so the *previously optimized* prompts are no longer optimal. Re-running prompt optimization against the fine-tuned LM produces module-specific instructions and demonstrations matched to the new weights. The empirical signal: on Iris-mistral the Θ→Π strategy (66.7) beats both Π→Θ (30.7) and Π→Θ→Π (52.7), and on GSM8K-mistral Θ→Π hits 48.3 (best in column).

- **Eight strategies form a Cartesian grid; the three BetterTogether cells dominate.** Table 1's grid:
  - **Baselines**: Vanilla / Π / Θ / Π→Π / Θ→Θ.
  - **BetterTogether**: Π→Θ / Θ→Π / Π→Θ→Π.
  - The repeated-prompt (Π→Π) and repeated-weight (Θ→Θ) baselines control for *more optimization steps on the same axis* — so the BetterTogether gains are attributable to **axis-alternation**, not just additional compute.

- **The compound-AI-system formalism.** The paper formalizes an LM program $\Phi_{\langle \Theta, \Pi \rangle} : \mathcal{X} \to \mathcal{Y}$ with $|M| \geq 1$ language modules $M = \langle M_1, \ldots, M_{|M|} \rangle$, each module $M_i$ having a prompt $\pi_i$ (instructions + few-shot demos) and LM weights $\theta_i$. Learnable parameters: $\Pi = [\pi_1, \ldots, \pi_{|\Phi|}]$ and $\Theta = [\theta_1, \ldots, \theta_{|\Phi|}]$. Objective: $\arg\max_{\Theta, \Pi} \frac{1}{|X|} \sum_{(x,m)\in X} \mu(\Phi_{\langle\Theta,\Pi\rangle}(x), m)$. **This $\Phi$-formalism is the same one [[2507.19457-gepa|GEPA]] (ICLR 2026) adopts** — see [[CompoundAISystem]].

- **Bootstrap-* family carries forward to both steps.** Prompt optimization uses **BFRS** ([[BootstrapFewShotWithRandomSearch]]), which self-generates few-shot candidates by executing the program and keeping high-scoring traces (filter by $\mu(\Phi(x_i), m_i) \geq \lambda$, e.g. $\lambda = 1.0$ for binary accuracy). Fine-tuning uses **BFT** ([[BootstrapFinetune]]), which: (1) runs Φ on all training examples; (2) keeps metric-passing traces; (3) builds module-level prompt-completion pairs; (4) **replaces the optimized prompts with vanilla prompts** in each trace; (5) concatenates across modules into one SFT dataset; (6) fine-tunes Φ's LM with [[lora|LoRA]] on that dataset; (7) returns Φ with the new LM weights swapped in for every module. The vanilla-prompt substitution at step (4) is what makes the resulting model run *without needing the optimized prompts at inference* — the prompt-driven behavior is internalized into the weights.

- **LoRA with shared base weights across modules.** All modules in Φ share the same base LM weights at initialization (e.g. all use mistral-7b-instruct-v0.2), distinct only in their prompt templates. Fine-tuning targets the **query and key self-attention layers** with **LoRA rank 32, alpha 64, no dropout, bfloat16, lr 1e-5, 5 epochs, effective batch size 8**.

- **The paper acknowledges a limitation.** "We do not yet understand *why* both [steps] are important." Section 6 names this as a risk: prompt optimization and fine-tuning in multi-stage LM programs are *both new*, and their interactions are less studied than gradient descent for monolithic neural nets.

## Algorithm sketch

**Algorithm 1 — `BetterTogether(Φ_{⟨Θ,Π⟩}, X, μ)`:**

```
1: Π′  ← OptimizePrompts(Φ_{⟨Θ,Π⟩}, X, μ)
2: Θ′  ← FinetuneWeights(Φ_{⟨Θ,Π′⟩}, X, μ)
3: Π″  ← OptimizePrompts(Φ_{⟨Θ′,Π⟩}, X, μ)
4: return Φ_{⟨Θ′, Π″⟩}
```

**Algorithm 2 — instantiation with `BootstrapFewShotRS` and `BootstrapFinetune`:**

`BootstrapFewShotRS(Φ, X, μ)`:
1. Split X into train T and validation V.
2. $\tau \leftarrow \text{BootstrapTraces}(\Phi, T)$.
3. $\tau \leftarrow \text{FilterTraces}(\tau, \mu)$.
4. For each sampled few-shot subset $\tau' \in \text{SampleFewShotSubsets}(\tau)$: construct prompts $\Pi'$, score $\sigma = \frac{1}{|V|}\sum \mu(\Phi_{\Pi'}(x_i), m_i)$; keep $(\sigma, \Pi')$.
5. Return $\Pi'_{\max}$.

`BootstrapFinetune(Φ, X, μ)`:
1. $\tau \leftarrow \text{BootstrapTraces}(\Phi, X)$.
2. $\tau \leftarrow \text{FilterTraces}(\tau, \mu)$ — keep only metric-passing traces.
3. $\Theta' \leftarrow \text{TrainLM}(\tau)$.
4. Return $\Theta'$.

Set `OptimizePrompts ← BootstrapFewShotRS`; set `FinetuneWeights ← BootstrapFinetune`.

## Benchmarks and results

### Three datasets × three LMs

**Datasets / programs:**

- **HotPotQA** (multi-hop QA, full-wiki setting): a **3-module** DSPy program — `generate_query[0]` → retrieve (frozen [[ColBERTv2]]) → `generate_query[1]` → retrieve → `generate_answer`. Each module uses [[chainofthought|Chain-of-Thought]]. 1000 train / 500 dev / 1500 test; 100/250 sub-sample for prompt opt train/val. Exact-match metric.
- **GSM8K** (grade-school math, [[CobbeEtAl2021]]): a **1-module** CoT program. 1000 train / 500 dev / 1319 test; 100/250 for prompt opt. Last-number extraction metric.
- **Iris** (flower classification, [[Fisher1988]]): a **1-module** CoT program over a `petal_length, petal_width, sepal_length, sepal_width → species` signature. 50 train / 50 dev / 50 test; 15/35 for prompt opt. Exact-match metric.

**LMs:** [[Mistral7BInstructV02|mistral-7b-instruct-v0.2]], [[Llama2_7BChat|llama-2-7b-chat]], [[Llama3_8BInstruct|llama-3-8b-instruct]]. All 7-8B instruction-tuned; deliberately kept small to fit one A100. Hosted via Docker + HuggingFace text-generation-inference; sampling temp 0.1, top_k 0.97.

**Headline gains** (averaged across LMs, over the better of Π-only or Θ-only):

- HotPotQA: **5–78%**.
- GSM8K: **2.5–10%**.
- Iris: **3.5–88%**.

### Where BetterTogether wins, and where it doesn't

- **HotPotQA**: BetterTogether dominates baselines on mistral and llama-2; on llama-3-8b the prompts-only baseline (46.9) edges out BetterTogether (46.7). The 3-module pipeline (largest) is the strongest case for the alternation.
- **GSM8K**: Smallest gains (2.5–10%); the 1-module CoT program has limited "compound" structure for the alternation to exploit.
- **Iris**: Largest *relative* gains; the small training set (50 examples) means prompt optimization is essential — both Π→Θ→Π (mistral, llama-2) and Θ→Π (mistral) and Π→Π (llama-3, 82.0) are dominant configurations.

### Insufficient-data cases

The "—" cells (Θ-only, Θ→Θ, Θ→Π on Iris-llama-2) reflect a hard-stop: vanilla llama-2-7b-chat on Iris zero-shot accuracy is **0%**, so the BFT bootstrap step has no metric-passing traces and there is no SFT dataset to fine-tune on. Prompt optimization fixes this — Π→Θ and Π→Θ→Π *do* work (26.7 and 65.3 respectively) because the Π step generates a working initial program whose traces can then be filtered.

## Key Quotes

> "*We propose for the first time combining the weight and prompt optimization strategies to optimize a modular LM pipeline by alternating between the two to get the same LM to teach itself.*"

> "*We hypothesize that, when a large LM is used to teach itself how to tackle the task defined by an LM program, fine-tuning LM weights and prompts are both essential to achieve the highest quality.*"

> "*(1) prompt optimization before fine-tuning can lead to more successful datapoints for fine-tuning, and, (2) prompt optimization after fine-tuning can make adjustments to the behavior of the LM program, leading to higher quality outputs.*"

> "*Existing work has only considered BFT in a very narrow setting for LM programs: on HotPotQA, Khattab et al. (2024) train a T5-Large model using traces from a few-shot Llama2-13b program, without considering getting an LM to teach itself via BFT nor considering a role for BFRS in the fine-tuned program. In this work, we focus on allowing models to teach themselves and self-improve.*"

> "*In 7 out of the 9 dataset and LM pairs, we observe that the best-performing strategies are the strategies that utilize prompt (Π) and weight (Θ) optimization steps together, although there is no clear winner among the benchmarked BetterTogether strategies that optimize both.*"

> "*Strategies where weight optimization is the first step use the vanilla (zero-shot) strategy to generate the initial fine-tuning dataset. If a model generates very few or no correct outputs under the vanilla strategy on the training set used to bootstrap the fine-tuning data, there will not be a sufficient dataset for fine-tuning.*"

> "*We do not yet understand why both are important. The role of prompt optimization and the role of fine-tuning in multi-stage LM programs are both new, and the relative lack of understanding of these roles in the emerging literature could pose risks in unanticipated interactions between these components.*"

## Contradictions

- **None with the wiki's existing content.** This paper is the canonical citation that [[CompoundAISystem|CompoundAISystem.md]] had been pointing to via the dangling stub `soylu2024fine`; ingest replaces that with this paper's slug.
- **Continuity with [[2507.19457-gepa|GEPA]]**: GEPA's *only-prompts* result is partially in tension with this paper's *prompts + weights is best* finding — but GEPA's regime is **sample-constrained at the rollout budget**, where weight optimization (RL) gets fewer effective updates than prompt optimization (reflective mutation). The two are not direct contradictions: BetterTogether's claim is about SFT + prompt-opt alternation on small instruction-tuned LMs; GEPA's claim is about prompt-only reflection beating GRPO RL on larger frontier LMs. Both can be true; the regime determines which step pays.
