## Summary
Chapter 32 of OpenStax *College Physics 2e* surveys the practical uses of nuclear physics: diagnostic and therapeutic medicine, food/material sterilization, and energy from fission and fusion (including weapons). It introduces radiopharmaceutical tracers and the imaging systems built around them (gamma camera, SPECT, PET), the units and biology of ionizing-radiation dose (gray, sievert, rad, rem, RBE), radiotherapy, food irradiation, and the physics of nuclear fission, chain reactions, reactors, and fusion. A recurring theme is that the same knowledge enables both great benefit and great harm.

## Key Claims
- Radiopharmaceuticals are radioactive drugs that localize in target organs; because gamma rays penetrate tissue, their distribution can be imaged externally with an Anger (gamma) camera, SPECT (~1 cm resolution), or PET (~0.5 cm, via 0.511-MeV annihilation photon pairs).
- Technetium-99m (t½ = 6.0 h, 0.142-MeV gamma) is used in roughly 80% of nuclear-medicine procedures.
- Absorbed dose is energy per mass: 1 Gy = 1 J/kg = 100 rad. Dose equivalent weights this by RBE: dose (Sv) = dose (Gy) × RBE, and 1 Sv = 100 rem.
- Relative biological effectiveness captures how concentrated ionization is: alpha particles have RBE ≈ 10–20 versus 1 for X-rays and gamma rays, so internal alpha emitters are especially hazardous.
- Ionizing radiation damages DNA; the dominant low-dose risk model is the linear no-threshold hypothesis, with LD50/32 ≈ 4.5 Sv for acute whole-body exposure.
- Radiotherapy exploits the greater radiosensitivity of fast-dividing tumor cells, maximizing the therapeutic ratio via external beams, brachytherapy seeds, or targeted radiopharmaceuticals.
- Food irradiation kills microbes via free radicals and radiolytic products but leaves no residual radioactivity, since the photon/electron energies used induce no nuclear reactions.
- Nuclear fusion combines light nuclei (releasing energy because BE/A rises toward iron-56) and requires overcoming the Coulomb barrier; it powers stars (proton-proton cycle) and is pursued via magnetic (tokamak/ITER) and inertial (laser) confinement.
- Nuclear fission splits heavy nuclei; a self-sustaining chain reaction needs more than one neutron emitted per fission (x > 1) and a critical mass, and reactors control this with moderators and neutron-absorbing control rods.
- 1 kg of U-235 releases ~8.2 × 10¹³ J by fission; breeder reactors convert abundant U-238 into fissile Pu-239.
- Nuclear weapons assemble a supercritical mass either by gun-type (U-235) or implosion (Pu-239) designs; thermonuclear bombs use a fission trigger to ignite fusion of lithium deuteride, with yields in megatons.

## Key Quotes (paraphrased)
- Gamma radiation passes through tissue readily, which is exactly why internally placed gamma-emitting tracers can be detected from outside the body.
- A positron meeting an electron annihilates into two 0.511-MeV photons flying apart in nearly opposite directions — the basis of PET coincidence imaging.
- The damage done by radiation depends not just on energy deposited but on how densely the ionization is packed, which is what RBE measures.
- Fission and fusion both release energy because the products are more tightly bound per nucleon than the reactants, with the mass difference appearing as kinetic energy via E = Δm c².
