## Summary
Chapter 13 of OpenStax *Chemistry 2e* introduces chemical equilibrium as the dynamic state in which the forward and reverse reaction rates of a reversible reaction are equal and macroscopic concentrations stay constant. It defines the reaction quotient Q and the equilibrium constant K (in concentration form Kc and pressure form Kp, related by Kp = Kc(RT)^Δn), distinguishes homogeneous from heterogeneous equilibria, and uses the law of mass action. It then covers Le Châtelier's principle — how equilibria shift in response to changes in concentration, pressure/volume, and temperature (and why catalysts do not shift them) — and finishes with quantitative equilibrium calculations built on the ICE-table method, including the x-is-small approximation.

## Key Claims
- A reaction is at equilibrium when the forward and reverse reaction rates are equal; equilibrium is **dynamic**, not stopped, and macroscopic concentrations remain constant. Reversible reactions are written with a double arrow (⇌), e.g. N₂O₄(g) ⇌ 2NO₂(g).
- The reaction quotient for mA + nB ⇌ xC + yD is Qc = [C]^x[D]^y / ([A]^m[B]^n) (or Qp using partial pressures). At equilibrium Q takes a constant value, the equilibrium constant K (K ≡ Q at equilibrium).
- **Law of mass action**: at a given temperature, the reaction quotient of a system at equilibrium is constant — every system reaches the same K regardless of starting amounts.
- A **large K** means equilibrium lies toward products; a **small K** means it lies toward reactants. K's magnitude says nothing about how fast equilibrium is reached.
- Comparing Q to K predicts direction: Q < K → net forward, Q > K → net reverse, Q = K → at equilibrium.
- Kp = Kc(RT)^Δn, where Δn = (moles of gaseous products) − (moles of gaseous reactants); when Δn = 0, Kp = Kc.
- Pure **solids and pure liquids are omitted** from Q and K expressions (their relative activities equal 1); terms appear only for gases and solutes. This distinguishes homogeneous (one phase) from heterogeneous (two or more phases) equilibria.
- **Le Châtelier's principle**: a stressed equilibrium shifts to relieve the stress. Adding reactant / removing product shifts right; removing reactant / adding product shifts left; the constant K is unchanged by concentration changes.
- A volume decrease (pressure increase) shifts a gas equilibrium toward the side with **fewer moles of gas**; if both sides have equal moles, there is no shift.
- **Temperature changes K itself.** Treating heat as a reactant (endothermic) or product (exothermic): heating shifts endothermic reactions right and exothermic reactions left. K_c = k_f/k_r.
- **Catalysts speed forward and reverse equally**, so they reach equilibrium faster but do not shift it or change K.
- The **ICE table** (Initial, Change, Equilibrium) organizes stoichiometric changes (±x scaled by coefficients) for computing K or unknown concentrations; small-K problems often allow the **x-is-small approximation**, valid when x is under ~5% of the initial concentration.

## Key Quotes
> "Chemical and physical processes are subject to this phenomenon; these processes are at equilibrium when the forward and reverse reaction rates are equal." — chapter introduction
> "chemical equilibria are dynamic; a reaction at equilibrium has not 'stopped,' but is proceeding in the forward and reverse directions at the same rate." — §13.1
> "the magnitude of K does not indicate how rapidly or slowly equilibrium will be reached." — §13.2
> "if an equilibrium system is stressed, the system will experience a shift in response to the stress that re-establishes equilibrium." — §13.3 (Le Châtelier's principle)
