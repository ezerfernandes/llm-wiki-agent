## Summary
OpenStax *Chemistry 2e* Chapter 20 introduces organic chemistry — the chemistry of carbon compounds — beginning with Wöhler's 1828 synthesis of urea overturning vitalism. It surveys **hydrocarbons** (alkanes/sp³/saturated, alkenes/sp²/C=C, alkynes/sp/C≡C, and aromatic benzene), their IUPAC nomenclature, structural and geometric isomerism, and their characteristic reactions (substitution for alkanes and aromatics; addition for alkenes/alkynes; combustion). The second half organizes molecules by **functional group**: alcohols (–OH) and ethers (–O–); the carbonyl (C=O) family of aldehydes (–CHO), ketones (–CO–), carboxylic acids (–COOH), and esters (–CO₂R) — connected by an oxidation ladder and esterification; and the nitrogen-containing amines (basic, lone-pair N) and amides (N on a carbonyl), which build proteins via peptide bonds.

## Key Claims
- **Organic compounds** are natural or synthetic substances whose principal element is carbon bonded to hydrogen and other carbons; carbonates, cyanides, CO, and CO₂ are excluded. Carbon's ability to form up to four strong C–C bonds yields ~10 million known and an estimated 10⁶⁰ possible organic compounds.
- **Friedrich Wöhler's 1828 synthesis of urea** from non-living materials disproved **vitalism**, showing organic molecules obey the same natural laws as inorganic ones.
- **Hydrocarbons** contain only C and H. **Alkanes** (saturated, CₙH₂ₙ₊₂) have only single bonds, sp³ carbons, and ~109.5° angles; **alkenes** have C=C (sp², 120°, one σ + one π); **alkynes** have C≡C (sp, 180°, one σ + two π); **aromatic** hydrocarbons (benzene C₆H₆) have a delocalized π ring.
- **Saturated** = only single C–C bonds; **unsaturated** = one or more C=C or C≡C bonds.
- **Structural (constitutional) isomers** share a molecular formula but differ in atom connectivity (e.g., n-butane vs 2-methylpropane, both C₄H₁₀). **Geometric isomers** (cis/trans, e.g., 2-butene) have the same connectivity but differ in 3-D arrangement because rotation about the rigid C=C bond is blocked.
- **Reaction patterns by class:** alkanes undergo **substitution** (a halogen replaces H; no C–C bonds break, hybridization unchanged) and combustion; alkenes/alkynes undergo **addition** (the π bond breaks and is replaced by two σ bonds; sp²→sp³), with alkynes reacting with twice as much reagent; **aromatic** rings favor **substitution** over addition, preserving the delocalized π system.
- A **functional group** is the part of a molecule that imparts a specific chemical reactivity and is the basis for classifying organic compounds.
- **Alcohols** replace an H with a covalent **–OH (hydroxyl)** group (named "-ol"); **ethers** contain **–O–** between two R groups. Ethanol comes from fermentation or acid-catalyzed addition of water to ethylene; dehydration of two alcohols (H₂SO₄, 140 °C) gives an ether.
- The **carbonyl group (C=O)** is a polar, sp², trigonal-planar functional group. **Aldehydes** (–CHO) have ≥1 H on the carbonyl carbon; **ketones** (–CO–) have two carbons on it. Organic **oxidation** replaces a C–H with a C–O bond: primary alcohol → aldehyde → carboxylic acid; secondary alcohol → ketone; tertiary alcohols resist oxidation.
- **Carboxylic acids** (–COOH) are **weak acids** (~1% ionized) and react with bases to give salts. **Esters** (–CO₂R) form by **esterification** of a carboxylic acid with an alcohol (losing water); their lack of intermolecular H-bonds gives low vapor pressure and the pleasant odors of fruits/perfumes. **Fats and oils** are esters of glycerine with long-chain acids (saturated palmitic/stearic; unsaturated oleic).
- **Amines** (C–N, with a lone pair on N) are **weak bases** like ammonia, protonating to ammonium ions; **alkaloids** (nicotine, morphine, codeine, heroin) are plant amines forming ammonium salts with acid.
- **Amides** have N bonded to a carbonyl carbon and form by **amidation** (carboxylic acid + amine → amide + H₂O). This reaction links amino acids via **peptide bonds** into polypeptides and proteins; Kevlar is an aromatic-amide polymer ~5× stronger than steel by weight.
