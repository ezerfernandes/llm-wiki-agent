## Summary
Chapter 10 of OpenStax College Physics 2e develops rotational mechanics as a systematic analog of
translational mechanics. It introduces [[AngularAcceleration]] and [[RotationalKinematics]], the
dynamics of rotation through [[Torque]] and [[MomentOfInertia]] (a rotational [[NewtonsSecondLaw]],
net τ = Iα), [[RotationalKineticEnergy]], and [[AngularMomentum]] with its
[[ConservationOfAngularMomentum|conservation law]]. It closes with [[CollisionsOfExtendedBodies]]
and the vector/[[GyroscopicPrecession|gyroscopic]] aspects of angular momentum.

## Key Claims
- Every translational quantity has a rotational counterpart: x↔θ, v↔ω, a↔α, m↔I, F↔τ, p↔L, and the kinematic and dynamic equations carry over with these substitutions.
- Angular acceleration relates to tangential acceleration through the radius (aₜ = rα), distinct from centripetal acceleration which only redirects velocity.
- Moment of inertia is the rotational analog of mass but depends on how mass is distributed about the axis (I = Σmr²), so distribution matters as much as amount.
- The rotational form of Newton's second law is net τ = Iα.
- Rotational kinetic energy is ½Iω²; for rolling objects, energy partitions between translational and rotational forms, and the mass distribution (I) — not total mass or radius — determines which rolls fastest.
- Angular momentum L = Iω, and net τ = ΔL/Δt is the rotational second law in momentum form.
- When net external torque is zero, angular momentum is conserved (Iω = I′ω′); reducing I increases ω.
- An ice skater speeds up by pulling limbs inward with no external torque; KE rises from internal muscular work while L stays constant.
- In collisions about a pivot, angular momentum can be conserved even when linear momentum is not, because a pivot force can have zero moment arm; such collisions are generally inelastic.
- Angular momentum, angular velocity, and torque are vectors (right-hand rule); a torque perpendicular to L causes precession rather than toppling — Earth precesses with a ~26,000-year period.

## Key Quotes (paraphrased)
> Angular acceleration is the rotational equivalent of linear acceleration — how fast the spin rate changes. — §10.1
> A torque must act over time to change angular momentum, mirroring how force over time changes linear momentum. — §10.5
> When no net external torque acts, total angular momentum stays constant; pulling mass inward lowers I and spins the body faster. — §10.5
> The change in angular momentum ΔL points in the direction of the torque that produces it, so a spinning axis precesses instead of falling. — §10.7
