## Summary

Chapter 8 of OpenStax *Chemistry 2e* moves beyond Lewis structures and VSEPR to two quantum-mechanical models of covalent bonding. **Valence bond theory** describes a bond as the overlap of half-filled atomic orbitals (giving σ and π bonds) and introduces **orbital hybridization** (sp, sp², sp³, sp³d, sp³d²) to reconcile atomic-orbital shapes with the molecular geometries VSEPR predicts. **Molecular orbital (MO) theory** combines atomic orbitals by LCAO into delocalized bonding and antibonding orbitals, letting bond order and electron configuration be read from MO energy diagrams. The chapter's recurring motivation is O₂'s paramagnetism — two unpaired electrons that Lewis structures cannot explain but MO theory predicts.

## Key Claims

- A covalent bond forms when a half-filled orbital on one atom overlaps a half-filled orbital on another and the two single electrons pair; bond strength rises with the extent of overlap, and the bond distance is the internuclear separation of minimum energy (H₂: 74 pm, 436 kJ/mol).
- A **σ bond** concentrates electron density along the internuclear axis (s–s, s–p, or end-to-end p–p overlap); a **π bond** comes from side-by-side p-orbital overlap with a node on the internuclear axis. All single bonds are σ; a double bond is σ+π, a triple bond is σ+2π. Each π bond is weaker than a σ bond between the same atoms.
- **Hybrid orbitals** form only in bonded atoms; the number formed equals the number of atomic orbitals combined, and the hybridization (sp/sp²/sp³/sp³d/sp³d²) maps one-to-one onto the VSEPR electron-pair geometry (linear/trigonal planar/tetrahedral/trigonal bipyramidal/octahedral). Hybrid orbitals make σ bonds and hold lone pairs; unhybridized p orbitals make π bonds.
- Hybridization is assigned by counting regions of electron density (each bond — single, double, or triple — radical, and lone pair counts as one); π bonds and resonance do not affect the assignment. Hybridization is invoked only where needed: large central atoms (H₂S 92.1°, H₂Te ~90°) often use near-pure p orbitals.
- **MO theory** combines atomic orbitals via the **linear combination of atomic orbitals (LCAO)**: in-phase (constructive) combination yields a lower-energy bonding orbital, out-of-phase (destructive) yields a higher-energy antibonding orbital (marked *), with a node between the nuclei.
- **Bond order** = (bonding electrons − antibonding electrons) / 2; a bond order of zero means no stable molecule (e.g., He₂, Be₂, Ne₂). For second-period diatomics, **s–p mixing** raises σ₂ₚ above the π₂ₚ orbitals for B₂/C₂/N₂ but not for O₂/F₂/Ne₂.
- **O₂** has bond order 2 and, by Hund's rule, two unpaired electrons in its degenerate π* orbitals, making it **paramagnetic** — a key success of MO theory over Lewis structures. Substances with all electrons paired are **diamagnetic**.

## Key Quotes

> "Valence bond theory describes a covalent bond as the overlap of half-filled atomic orbitals (each containing a single electron) that yield a pair of electrons shared between the two bonded atoms." — 8.1

> "While all single bonds are σ bonds, multiple bonds consist of both σ and π bonds." — 8.1

> "The number of hybrid orbitals in a set is equal to the number of atomic orbitals that were combined to produce the set." — 8.2

> "MO theory uses the combination of atomic orbitals to yield molecular orbitals that are *delocalized* over the entire molecule rather than being localized on its constituent atoms." — 8.4

> "Each O₂ molecule has two unpaired electrons. The Lewis-structure model does not predict the presence of these two unpaired electrons." — 8.4
