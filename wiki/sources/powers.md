## Summary
Powers are mathematical operations that show how many times a [[types-of-numbers|number]] is to be multiplied by itself. The standard way of writing a power is \\(a^{\large{n}}\\) where \\(a\\) is the base and \\(n\\) is the exponent: \\[a^n = \underbrace{a \cdot a \cdot a \cdots a}_{n \text{ times}} \quad \text{with} \quad a \in \mathbb{R}, \quad n \in \mathbb{Z}^+ \tag{1}\\]

## Key Claims
- **Introduction to powers** — Powers are mathematical operations that show how many times a [[types-of-numbers|number]] is to be multiplied by itself.
- **Powers with real exponents** — The definition \\( a^n \\) in \\(1\\) is valid only for positive [[integers|integers]] \\( n \\), but the idea of repeated multiplication does not work when the exponent is not an integer.
- **Fundamental rules of powers** — The following rules govern the manipulation of expressions involving powers.
- **Why is \\( a^0 = 1 \\)?** — Among the properties of exponents, the result \\(a^0 = 1\\) may initially seem counterintuitive, but this outcome follows directly from the quotient rule, which states that dividing any power by itself yields \\(1\\).
- **Power and exponential** — Sometimes, people mistakenly mix up the concepts of power and exponential function.
- **Powers with complex exponents** — The definition \\( a^x = e^{x \ln a} \\) extends naturally to cases where the exponent is complex.
