## Summary
Chapter 18 of OpenStax College Physics 2e introduces electrostatics: the forces and fields produced by stationary electric charge. It develops the two-sign nature of [[ElectricCharge]], the law of [[ConservationOfCharge]], the distinction between [[ConductorsAndInsulators]] and the three ways objects become charged, [[CoulombsLaw]] for the force between point charges, and the [[ElectricField]] as force per unit charge. It then covers [[ElectricFieldLines]] and superposition, biological electrostatics (polar water, DNA, screening), conductor behavior in static equilibrium, and practical [[Electrostatics]] technologies.

## Key Claims
- Electric charge comes in exactly two types (positive, negative); like charges repel and unlike charges attract.
- Charge is quantized in units of the elementary charge |q_e| = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ C; one coulomb is 6.25 × 10¹⁸ elementary charges.
- Charge is conserved: it is only transferred, never created or destroyed (oppositely charged particles appear together in pair creation).
- Objects gain static charge by contact (conduction), by induction (separation), or by induction with grounding; neutral objects can become polarized and are attracted by either charge sign.
- [[CoulombsLaw]]: F = k|q₁q₂|/r² with k = 8.99 × 10⁹ N·m²/C², an inverse-square law verified to ~1 part in 10¹⁶ — mathematically parallel to [[GravitationalField]] gravitation.
- At subatomic scale the electrostatic force vastly exceeds gravity (factor ~2.27 × 10³⁹ for electron–proton in hydrogen).
- The [[ElectricField]] E = F/q depends only on the source charge; for a point charge E = k|Q|/r², and the force on any charge is F = qE.
- Fields superpose vectorially; [[ElectricFieldLines]] start on + charges, end on − charges, never cross, and their density encodes field strength.
- In [[ElectrostaticInduction|electrostatic equilibrium]] a conductor has zero internal field, surface-perpendicular external field, and all excess charge on its outer surface; sharp points concentrate charge.
- A Faraday cage / metal car body shields its interior because excess charge stays on the outside and the internal field is zero.
- Electrostatic technologies include the Van de Graaff generator (~15 MV), xerography/laser printers (photoconductive selenium drum), ink-jet printers, electrostatic painting, and precipitators (>99% particulate removal).

## Key Quotes (paraphrased)
- The total electric charge in any process stays constant — charge is only moved around, never made or lost.
- An electric field belongs to its source charge and is the same at a given distance regardless of the test charge used to detect it.
- Inside a conductor at static equilibrium the field is zero and all excess charge sits on the outer surface, with the surface field pointing straight out.
- Because the electrostatic force falls off with distance, the closer induced attraction outweighs the farther repulsion, so neutral objects are drawn toward any charged body.
