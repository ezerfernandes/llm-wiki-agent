## Summary
Chapter 6 of OpenStax [[Prealgebra]] 2e ([[OpenStax]]) develops the **[[Percent|percent]]** — a [[Ratio|ratio]] whose denominator is 100 — and shows it is interchangeable with the [[Fraction|fraction]] and [[Decimal|decimal]] notations for the same number. From the single relationship **amount = percent × base** the chapter builds a uniform problem-solving method (translate a sentence into an [[Equation|equation]], solve, check) and applies it to **general percent problems** and **percent increase/decrease** (§6.2), then to the everyday money applications **sales tax, commission, discount, and mark-up** ([[PercentApplications]], §6.3) and **[[SimpleInterest|simple interest]]** `I = Prt` (§6.4). Finally it introduces the **[[Proportion|proportion]]** `a/b = c/d`, the **Cross Products Property** `a·d = b·c`, and the **percent proportion** `amount/base = percent/100` as a second, equivalent route through every percent problem (§6.5). All rates are converted from percents to decimals before computing, and every solution is checked.

## Key Concepts & Definitions
- **[[Percent]]** — a [[Ratio|ratio]] whose denominator is 100; `n% = n/100`. Interchangeable with a [[Fraction|fraction]] and a [[Decimal|decimal]] for the same value; percents over 100% represent values greater than 1.
- **Percent equation** — `amount = percent × base`; the **base** is the whole the percent is taken *of*, the **amount** is the part, and the **percent is written as a decimal** before multiplying.
- **Percent increase / decrease** — the change measured against the **original**: increase = `(new − original)/original`; decrease = `(original − new)/original`, each expressed as a percent.
- **Sales tax / commission / discount / mark-up** ([[PercentApplications]]) — four rate × base applications (see procedures below); discount **subtracts** from the original price, sales tax and mark-up **add** to the base.
- **[[SimpleInterest|Simple interest]]** — `I = Prt`: interest computed only on the principal; `P` principal, `r` annual rate (decimal), `t` time in **years**, `I` interest.
- **[[Proportion]]** — an equation `a/b = c/d` (`b,d ≠ 0`) stating two [[Ratio|ratios]]/rates are equal; read "a is to b as c is to d."
- **Cross Products Property** — for `a/b = c/d`, the cross products are equal: `a·d = b·c`; equal cross products ⇔ a true proportion.
- **Percent proportion** — `amount/base = percent/100`, the proportion form of the percent equation.

## Key Procedures / Rules
- **Percent ↔ fraction ↔ decimal** ([[Percent]], [[DecimalFractionConversion]]) — *percent → fraction*: write over 100, simplify (`36% = 9/25`). *percent → decimal*: divide by 100, i.e. move the point two places left, drop `%` (`78% = 0.78`). *decimal → percent*: move the point two places right, add `%` (`0.05 = 5%`). *fraction → percent*: fraction → decimal (divide), then decimal → percent (`3/4 = 0.75 = 75%`); round as specified for non-terminating cases.
- **Solve a percent application (six steps)** ([[Equation]]) — (1) identify the unknown and choose a variable; (2) write a sentence with the given information; (3) translate to an equation (`amount = percent × base`); (4) solve with good algebra; (5) check it makes sense; (6) answer in a complete sentence.
- **Percent increase / decrease** — find the amount of change, then divide by the **original** amount and express as a percent (round to nearest tenth of a percent).
- **Sales tax / commission / discount / mark-up** ([[PercentApplications]]) — sales tax = tax rate × purchase price, total = price + tax; commission = commission rate × total sales; discount = discount rate × original price, sale price = original − discount; mark-up = mark-up rate × wholesale price, list price = wholesale + mark-up. Convert each rate to a decimal first; the sale price is below, the list price above, the base.
- **Simple interest** ([[SimpleInterest]]) — substitute known values into `I = Prt` and solve for the unknown (`P = I/(rt)`, `r = I/(Pt)`, `t = I/(Pr)`); convert the rate percent → decimal and any months → years (÷12) first.
- **Test / solve a proportion** ([[Proportion]]) — test with cross products (`a·d = b·c` ⇒ true proportion); solve with the variable in a numerator by clearing the LCD, or with the variable in a denominator by setting cross products equal; check by substitution.
- **Percent proportion** — set up `amount/base = percent/100` (the "of" word marks the base), place the unknown, and solve by cross products — an alternative to the percent equation for finding the amount, base, or percent.
