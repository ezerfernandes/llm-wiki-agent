## Summary
Chapter 13 of OpenStax *College Physics 2e* develops the thermal behavior of matter from the
measurable quantity of temperature down to the molecular motion that underlies it. It defines
temperature scales and the zeroth law, treats thermal expansion of solids and liquids, states the
ideal gas law in both molecular (PV = NkT) and molar (PV = nRT) forms, and uses kinetic theory to
connect pressure and temperature to molecular speeds. It closes with phase changes (PV/PT diagrams,
critical and triple points, Dalton's law) and humidity/evaporation/boiling driven by vapor pressure.

## Key Claims
- Temperature is operationally "what a thermometer measures," is an intensive property, and the
  zeroth law of thermodynamics (transitivity of thermal equilibrium) makes it well defined.
- The three common scales relate linearly: T(°F) = (9/5)T(°C) + 32 and T(K) = T(°C) + 273.15;
  absolute zero is 0 K = −273.15 °C, where a constant-volume gas's pressure extrapolates to zero.
- Solids expand by ΔL = αLΔT (and ΔV = βVΔT with β ≈ 3α); constrained expansion produces large
  thermal stress. Water is anomalous, reaching maximum density at 4 °C so ice floats.
- The ideal gas law PV = NkT = nRT treats gas particles as having negligible volume and forces
  except in elastic collisions; pressure and absolute temperature must be used.
- Kinetic theory derives PV = (1/3)Nmv̄² and identifies average translational KE = (3/2)kT, so
  temperature is a direct measure of molecular kinetic energy; v_rms = √(3kT/m).
- Thermal energy depends only on absolute temperature, not on molecular identity; lighter molecules
  (helium, hydrogen) move fast enough to escape Earth's atmosphere.
- Molecular speeds follow the Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution; collisions cause Brownian motion of
  small particles.
- Phase diagrams map solid/liquid/gas regions; the triple point is where all three coexist and the
  critical point is where the liquid–gas distinction ends (above the critical temperature no liquid
  exists at any pressure).
- Dalton's law: total pressure is the sum of the partial pressures of the component gases.
- Vapor pressure depends only on temperature; a liquid boils when its vapor pressure equals the
  surrounding pressure (so pressure cookers raise the boiling point).
- Relative humidity = vapor density / saturation vapor density × 100; the dew point is the
  temperature at which air reaches saturation. Evaporation cools the remaining liquid.

## Key Quotes (paraphrased)
- Temperature is defined by what a thermometer reads, not by human sensation; identical thermometers
  in the same environment agree.
- If A is in thermal equilibrium with B, and B with C, then A is in equilibrium with C — the zeroth
  law, which justifies thermometry.
- Average translational kinetic energy of a gas molecule equals (3/2)kT, so temperature directly
  measures molecular kinetic energy.
- The total pressure of a gas mixture equals the sum of the pressures each component would exert
  alone (Dalton's law).
- A liquid boils when its vapor pressure rises to equal the ambient pressure.
