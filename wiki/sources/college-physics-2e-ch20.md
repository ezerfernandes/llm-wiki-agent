## Summary
Chapter 20 of OpenStax *College Physics 2e* moves from electrostatics into dynamic electricity, treating moving charge as electric current. It defines current and drift velocity, develops Ohm's law (V = IR), separates resistance from the material property resistivity (including temperature dependence), and derives electrical power and energy cost. It then contrasts alternating and direct current with rms quantities, surveys electrical safety (thermal and shock hazards), and closes with bioelectricity — nerve conduction and the electrocardiogram.

## Key Claims
- Electric current is the rate of charge flow, I = ΔQ/Δt, measured in amperes (1 A = 1 C/s); microscopically I = nqAv_d.
- Charge carriers drift slowly (~10⁻⁴ m/s) while the electrical signal propagates near light speed (~10⁸ m/s), because each charge pushes its neighbors almost instantly.
- Conventional current is defined as the direction positive charge would move — opposite to electron drift in metals.
- Ohm's law (V = IR) holds for ohmic materials, whose resistance is independent of applied voltage; current is directly proportional to voltage and inversely proportional to resistance.
- Resistance depends on geometry and material: R = ρL/A, where resistivity ρ is intrinsic and classifies conductors, semiconductors, and insulators.
- Resistivity is temperature-dependent: ρ = ρ₀(1 + αΔT). Conductors gain resistance when heated; semiconductors lose it; some materials become superconductors at low temperature.
- Electric power is P = IV = I²R = V²/R; energy is E = Pt and is billed in kilowatt-hours (1 kW·h = 3.6 × 10⁶ J).
- AC reverses direction sinusoidally while DC is one-directional; rms values (V_rms = V₀/√2, I_rms = I₀/√2) give meaningful averages, and average power P_ave = I_rms·V_rms.
- AC dominates power distribution because transformers efficiently step voltage up for low-loss high-voltage transmission.
- Electrical hazards are thermal (P = I²R wire heating, P = V²/r in short circuits) or shock; current magnitude governs shock severity, with ventricular fibrillation likely at 100–300 mA.
- Nerve signals are action potentials propagating along membranes (resting potential ≈ −90 mV), with myelin enabling fast saltatory conduction (~100 m/s vs ~1 m/s unmyelinated).
- The ECG records cardiac depolarization/repolarization as P wave, QRS complex, and T wave.

## Key Quotes (paraphrased)
> Current is the rate at which charge moves through a cross-section, and one ampere equals one coulomb per second. — §20.1
> A wire's current is directly proportional to the applied voltage; this proportionality, with resistance as the constant, is Ohm's law. — §20.2
> Resistivity is an intrinsic property of a material, independent of its size or shape, while resistance also depends on the object's length and cross-sectional area. — §20.3
> Because average voltage and current of a sinusoid are zero, rms values are used so that AC power calculations behave like DC ones. — §20.5
> The severity of an electric shock is determined mainly by the magnitude of the current passing through the body, not merely the voltage. — §20.6
