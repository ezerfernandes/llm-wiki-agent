## Summary
The real numbers are introduced as a structure characterised by a combination of algebraic and order properties. These properties determine their behaviour and distinguish it from all other numerical fields. The real numbers form a [[fields|field]] under addition and multiplication. This means that both operations are associative and commutative, multiplication distributes over addition, and every nonzero real number admits a multiplicative inverse. ## Key Claims
- **Field and order structure** — The real numbers are introduced as a structure characterised by a combination of algebraic and order properties.
- **The real line** — The real numbers admit a geometric interpretation that makes their order and completeness clear.
- **The completeness axiom** — The property that distinguishes \\(\mathbb{R}\\) from \\(\mathbb{Q}\\) is completeness.
- **The Archimedean property** — A consequence of completeness is the Archimedean property of \\(\mathbb{R}\\).
- **Dedekind cuts** — A Dedekind cut is a subset \\(A \subseteq \mathbb{Q}\\) satisfying three conditions: \\(A\\) is non-empty and \\(A \neq \mathbb{Q}\\).
- **Cauchy sequence construction** — A second construction of \\(\mathbb{R}\\) starts from a limitation of \\(\mathbb{Q}\\).
- **Consequences of completeness** — The rational numbers are dense in \\(\mathbb{R}\\): between any two distinct real numbers there exists a rational number.
- **Uniqueness of \\(\mathbb{R}\\)** — The real number system is the unique complete ordered field.
- **Intervals** — Among the subsets of \\(\mathbb{R}\\), [[intervals|intervals]] occupy a central role.
