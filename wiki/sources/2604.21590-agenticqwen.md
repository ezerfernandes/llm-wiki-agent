## Summary
Lyu, Wang et al. (Alibaba) train **AgenticQwen** — a family of 8B / 30B Qwen-based agentic models — primarily on synthetic data via multi-round RL using two complementary **data flywheels**. The **reasoning flywheel** rewrites model errors into harder verifiable variants (self-instruct expansion + persona injection + multi-teacher consensus filter). The **agentic flywheel** expands linear single-path workflows into multi-branch behavior trees, with simulated users intentionally pushing the model into unexpected branches to force decision-making. On public benchmarks AgenticQwen 8B/30B competes with much larger open-source models; on Alibaba's internal industrial agent system it closes the gap with Qwen3-235B on search/data-analysis tasks at significantly lower serving cost.

## Key Claims
- **Industrial gap.** Frontier proprietary models (GPT-5, Claude) are too expensive at million-user scale; major foundation-model developers rarely release small models with strong agentic capabilities, leaving a deployment gap.
- **Two-stage RL.** Reasoning RL: GRPO-style training on Omni / 2WikiMultiHopQA / HotpotQA with binary final-answer reward + web-search and code-interpreter tools. Agentic RL: rubric-based 0–1 rewards over verifiable subgoals with simulated users + tools (Qwen3-235B as simulator).
- **Reasoning data flywheel.** Self-instruct expansion (change values, add constraints, deepen theory), persona-injection rewrites (physics / chemistry scenarios), consensus filter (multi-teacher agreement check) — keep only items where independent teachers agree.
- **Agentic data flywheel.** Round-by-round expansion: linear → branched workflows. Branches injected by simulated users introducing unexpected conditions (sold-out tickets → search HSR / nearby airport, gold-tier compensation logic, etc.). Back-translates branched workflows into new training contexts.
- **Diversity over volume.** Synthetic samples saturate quickly when homogeneous; the flywheels exist precisely to keep diversity growing as the model improves.
- **Empirical.** 8B/30B models substantially improve real-world tool use and narrow the gap to much larger models on public benchmarks and internal deployment, with significantly lower serving cost.
- **Open release.** Model checkpoints + part of synthetic data on HuggingFace (alibaba-pai/agenticqwen); pipeline integrated into EasyDistill.

## Key Quotes
> "RL alone can quickly reach a performance ceiling: even with additional data, the training distribution may become overly homogeneous, limiting further gains."
> "Linear workflows gradually grow into multi-branch behavior trees that better reflect real-world diversity."
> "Simulated users may intentionally attempt to mislead the model into taking incorrect actions."

## Contradictions
- Pitches small-model viability against the prevailing "scale is all you need" trend; the dual-flywheel idea inherits from synthetic-data critique that pure SFT or one-shot synthesis saturates. Sits in friendly tension with [[2604.27707-agentic-memory-is-a-memo|Xu, Dai & Zhang]]: this paper *does* push training signal into the weights (as that paper urges) — the question is whether multi-round RL on synthesized branches produces rule-based or merely richer exemplar-based competence.
