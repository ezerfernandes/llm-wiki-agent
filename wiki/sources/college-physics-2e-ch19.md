## Summary
Chapter 19 of OpenStax College Physics 2e builds the energy-and-potential view of electrostatics on top of the force-and-field view from Chapter 18. It introduces electric potential energy, electric potential (voltage) and potential difference, links the electric field to the gradient of potential, derives the potential of a point charge, visualizes potential with equipotential lines, and develops capacitors, dielectrics, series/parallel combinations, and the energy they store. Worked examples emphasize concepts — battery energy vs. voltage, electron acceleration, the point-charge sphere model, and the defibrillator — rather than arithmetic.

## Key Claims
- Electric potential V is potential energy per unit charge (V = PE/q); potential difference (voltage) ΔV = ΔPE/q, and energy moved is ΔPE = qΔV — a property of two points, not one.
- The electrostatic force is conservative, so a well-defined electric potential energy exists and energy is conserved (KE + PE constant), letting one compute accelerated-charge speeds via v = √(2qV/m).
- The electron volt (1 eV = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ J) is the natural energy unit for submicroscopic processes such as bond-breaking.
- In a uniform field, voltage and field are simply related: V_AB = Ed and E = V_AB/d; generally E = −ΔV/Δs, and 1 N/C = 1 V/m. Air breaks down near 3.0 × 10⁶ V/m, limiting achievable voltages.
- The potential of a point charge is V = kQ/r (scalar, ∝ 1/r), while its field is E = kQ/r² (vector, ∝ 1/r²); potentials add algebraically but fields add vectorially.
- A uniformly charged conducting sphere has the same external potential as a point charge at its center, validating the point-charge model for real conductors (e.g., Van de Graaff generators).
- Equipotential lines are always perpendicular to field lines, no work is done moving along them, and any conductor in electrostatic equilibrium is an equipotential surface; grounding fixes a conductor at 0 V.
- Capacitance C = Q/V (farads); a parallel-plate capacitor has C = κε₀A/d, increasing with area and dielectric constant and decreasing with separation.
- Dielectrics raise capacitance and breakdown voltage by molecular polarization that opposes the applied field, not by adding charge (e.g., Teflon stores ~42× more than air).
- Capacitors in series give 1/C_S = Σ 1/Cᵢ (total below the smallest); in parallel C_P = Σ Cᵢ (total above the largest).
- Stored capacitor energy is E_cap = QV/2 = CV²/2 = Q²/(2C); the ½ arises because voltage rises from 0 to V during charging.

## Key Quotes (paraphrased)
> Two batteries can carry the same voltage rating yet store very different amounts of energy — voltage and energy are related but not the same.
> Equipotential lines run perpendicular to electric field lines everywhere, because moving a charge along an equipotential does no work.
> A dielectric increases capacitance through molecular realignment rather than by introducing new charge, simultaneously lowering the internal field and raising breakdown resistance.
