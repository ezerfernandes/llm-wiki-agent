## Summary
Chapter 11 of OpenStax *College Physics 2e* covers the statics of fluids — fluids that are not flowing. It defines fluids by their inability to resist shear, introduces [[Density]] and [[Pressure]], and derives how pressure varies with depth ([[FluidPressure]]). It then presents the two foundational laws of hydrostatics — [[PascalsPrinciple]] (pressure transmission in confined fluids, the basis of hydraulics) and [[ArchimedesPrinciple]] (buoyancy equal to displaced-fluid weight) — and the measurement framework of gauge vs. absolute pressure via barometers and manometers. The chapter closes with molecular surface effects ([[CohesionAndAdhesion]], [[SurfaceTension]], [[CapillaryAction]]) and a survey of pressures throughout the human body.

## Key Claims
- A fluid is any substance (liquid, gas, or plasma) that flows and yields to shearing forces; solids resist shear because their atoms are locked in a lattice.
- Density ρ = m/V is an intensive property; liquids and solids are far denser than gases because their atoms are in contact rather than widely spaced.
- Pressure P = F/A is a scalar; in a static fluid it acts perpendicular to every surface and is exerted equally in all directions, since fluids cannot sustain shear.
- Pressure from a fluid's own weight increases linearly with depth: P = hρg, depending only on depth and density, not container shape or size.
- Pascal's principle: a pressure change applied to a confined fluid transmits undiminished throughout it, enabling hydraulic force multiplication via the area ratio F₁/A₁ = F₂/A₂.
- Absolute pressure = gauge pressure + atmospheric pressure (P_abs = P_g + P_atm); because fluids only push, minimum absolute pressure is zero, so gauge pressure can be as low as −P_atm.
- Standard atmospheric pressure is 1 atm = 1.01×10⁵ Pa ≈ 101 kPa, equivalent to only ~10.3 m of water (water is ~775× denser than air).
- Archimedes' principle: the buoyant force on a submerged or floating object equals the weight of fluid it displaces; an object floats when its average density is below the fluid's.
- An object's fraction submerged equals the ratio of its average density to the fluid's density; specific gravity (ρ̄/ρ_water) predicts floating (<1) or sinking (>1).
- Surface tension γ = F/L makes liquid surfaces minimize area, supporting small objects and giving a spherical bubble internal gauge pressure P = 4γ/r.
- Capillary rise h = 2γ cos θ/(ρgr) increases as tube radius shrinks; the contact angle θ encodes whether cohesion or adhesion dominates.
- Hydrostatic pressure (P = hρg) explains physiological pressure differences — e.g., blood pressure in the feet exceeds that at the heart by the weight of the intervening blood column.

## Key Quotes (paraphrased)
> A fluid is matter that gives way to shearing forces and flows — liquids, gases, and plasmas qualify; rigid solids do not. — §11.1
> Pressure applied to an enclosed fluid is passed along undiminished to every part of the fluid and the walls that contain it. — Pascal's principle, §11.5
> The upward buoyant force on an object equals the weight of the fluid that the object pushes aside. — Archimedes' principle, §11.7
> Because a fluid can push but never pull, the smallest absolute pressure attainable is zero. — §11.6
