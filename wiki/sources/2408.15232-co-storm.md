## Summary

[[YuchengJiang]], [[YijiaShao]] (co-first; [[stanforduniversity|Stanford]]), [[DekunMa]] ([[YaleUniversity|Yale]]), [[SinaSemnani]] & [[MonicaLam]] ([[stanforduniversity|Stanford]]) propose **[[CoSTORM|Collaborative STORM (Co-STORM)]]** — a successor to the [[STORM]] writing system that recasts the [[InformationSeeking|information-seeking task]] from one-question-one-answer [[rag|RAG]] / [[QASystem|QA]] into a **mixed-initiative collaborative discourse** between a human observer and multiple LM agents. Co-STORM simulates a roundtable of **perspective-guided expert agents** + a **moderator agent**, where the user can passively observe, take a turn, or ask the moderator to redirect — while a dynamically maintained hierarchical **[[CoSTORMMindMap|mind map]]** tracks shared knowledge and grounds the final cited report. The paper introduces the [[WildSeek]] dataset (100 in-the-wild topic+goal pairs across 24 domains from the public STORM web app) for automatic evaluation, and reports that Co-STORM significantly outperforms both **RAG chatbot** and **STORM + QA** baselines on report quality (Relevance / Breadth / Depth / Novelty / Info Diversity) and question-answering turn quality (Consistency / Engagement / # Unique URLs). In a 20-participant human evaluation, **70%** preferred Co-STORM over [[GoogleSearch|Google Search]] and **78%** preferred it over a RAG chatbot for the overall information-seeking experience. Implemented with [[gpt-4o|gpt-4o-2024-05-13]] + [[YouCom|You.com]] search via the [[DSPy]] framework.

The contribution is **the explicit framing of [[UnknownUnknowns|unknown unknowns]] as a tractable LM-system design target** — leaning on the educational-psychology framing of [[Nussbaum2008|Nussbaum (2008)]] that students learn by *observing and occasionally participating* in expert discourse rather than by formulating every question themselves. The empirical core is the **multi-agent ablation** (Table 3): removing the moderator hurts more than reducing expert count, because the moderator generates questions from *uncited / unused* search results — i.e. it carries a much larger known-unknowns space than any single expert.

## Key Claims

- **[[UnknownUnknowns]] is the gap** that single-turn QA and conversational QA systems fail to address — users with low prior knowledge cannot formulate the questions that would reveal what they should be asking ([[Kuhlthau1991|Kuhlthau 1991]]; [[Belkin1982|Belkin et al. 1982]]). Existing assistance systems each support at most two of: multiple sources, ongoing interaction, curated report ([[CoSTORMComparisonTable|Table 1]]). Co-STORM supports all three.
- **Mixed-initiative > pure user-initiative or pure agent-initiative.** Co-STORM uses a [[TurnManagement|turn-management protocol]]: when the user is idle, agents take turns; after $L$ consecutive expert turns with intent in {Potential Answer, Further Details}, the system asks the moderator to intervene to prevent repetitive niche discussion.
- **The moderator role is load-bearing.** Ablating the moderator ("w/o Moderator") drops all four report-quality scores (Relevance 3.78→3.56, Breadth 3.79→3.69, Depth 3.77→3.41, Novelty 3.05→2.89) — a larger hit than ablating multi-experts. The moderator reranks **uncited / unused** information from prior search calls via $\cos(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{t})^\alpha (1-\cos(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{q}))^{1-\alpha}$ (similarity to topic $t$, dissimilarity to original question $q$, $\alpha=0.5$) to steer toward unexplored regions.
- **Dynamic [[CoSTORMMindMap|mind map]] is the shared conceptual space.** A tree $\mathcal{M}=(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E})$ of concepts where each $c$ stores a subset of retrieved info $I^c\subset\mathcal{I}$. Two operations — `insert` (embedding-similarity candidate selection → LM picks final placement) and `reorganize` (when concept exceeds $K$ pieces, LM generates new sub-topic names and re-inserts) — keep depth bounded. Bottom-up cleaning prunes single-subtopic chains and concepts with no supporting info. Used as outline for the final cited long-form report.
- **[[WildSeek]] dataset**: 100 (topic, goal) pairs across 24 domains, collected from the publicly deployed STORM web app, rule-filtered + GPT-4o-classified for motivation quality, downsampled by domain. Sample point — Domain: Economics; Topic: *"Development of a Shared Trading Currency to Facilitate International Trade"*; Goal: *"Investigate how a new shared currency could eliminate transaction costs and boost GDP among member countries."*
- **Report-quality metrics** scored by **[[Prometheus2|Prometheus 2]]** ([[KimEtAl2024|Kim et al. 2024]], 7B evaluator LM) on a 5-point rubric for Relevance / Breadth / Depth / Novelty; **[[InformationDiversity]]** computed as $1 - \frac{\sum_{i,j\in\mathcal{I}, i\neq j}\cos(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j})}{|\mathcal{I}|(|\mathcal{I}|-1)}$ over `text-embedding-3-small` embeddings.
- **Co-STORM > both baselines on every dimension.** Co-STORM scores: Relevance 3.78 / Breadth 3.79 / Depth 3.77 / Novelty 3.05 / Info Diversity 0.602; vs RAG Chatbot 3.57 / 3.50 / 3.26 / 2.44 / 0.595 and STORM+QA 3.61 / 3.61 / 3.43 / 2.50 / 0.592. Depth and Novelty gains are statistically significant ($p<0.05$, paired $t$-test).
- **Question-answering turn quality**: Co-STORM Consistency 4.40 / Engagement 4.33 / # Unique URLs 6.04 vs RAG Chatbot 4.37 / 4.13 / 2.94 — Co-STORM cites ~2× more unique URLs per turn.
- **Human evaluation (n=20)**: Co-STORM rated strictly higher than [[GoogleSearch|Search Engine]] in Breadth by 50% / Depth by 60% of participants; vs RAG Chatbot, strictly higher in Breadth 67% / Depth 56%. Serendipity gain: 70% over Search ($p=0.030$), 67% over RAG Chatbot ($p=0.009$). On pairwise experience: 70% prefer Co-STORM over Search overall; 78% prefer it over RAG Chatbot. **Less Effort** agreed by 80% (vs Search) / 67% (vs RAG).
- **Implementation is [[DSPy]]-native**, zero-shot prompting, [[gpt-4o|gpt-4o-2024-05-13]] for all LM calls; hyperparameters: $N=3$ experts initially, $K=10$ pieces of info per concept before reorganize, $L=2$ consecutive non-question turns triggering moderator, $\alpha=0.5$ for moderator reranking, temperature 1.0, top-p 0.9; embeddings via `text-embedding-3-small`; search via [[YouCom|You.com]] API filtered to [[Wikipedia]] *Reliable sources* whitelist following [[Shao2024|Shao et al. 2024]].
- **Live preview**: <https://storm.genie.stanford.edu>. Code: <https://github.com/stanford-oval/storm>.

## Key Quotes

> *"While the concept of unknown unknowns originally referred to unexpected risks in the military, it is linked to the serendipitous discovery of information in the information research context."* — §1, framing the term.

> *"Tell me and I forget. Teach me and I remember. Involve me and I learn."* — Benjamin Franklin, epigraph to §3 (Method). The system's pedagogical thesis in one sentence.

> *"Having just one expert and one moderator can already provide most of the benefits."* — §5.4, ablation: the moderator role is the structural innovation; expert multiplicity adds less than the moderator does.

> *"Co-STORM allows for almost full automation and much better understanding as it brings up topics that the user may not even think of."* — quoted participant feedback (§6.2). Participants rated **80 mind map snapshots** as accurately tracking the discourse 71% of the time.

> *"Co-STORM is so much less mentally taxing for me to use."* — quoted participant feedback (§6.2).

## Contradictions

- **Co-STORM's "DSPy-as-orchestration-only" stance vs the MIPRO/GEPA prescription.** [[2406.11695-mipro|MIPRO (2024)]] and [[2507.19457-gepa|GEPA (2026)]] both argue that prompt-optimizing the modules of a multi-stage [[LMProgram|LM program]] yields large gains over zero-shot baselines. Co-STORM does *not* apply such an optimizer despite being a multi-module DSPy program. **No contradiction in claims**, but a **methodological gap**: an open question whether running MIPRO or GEPA over Co-STORM's signatures would further widen the gap over the RAG chatbot baseline. The paper was submitted before GEPA existed, and roughly contemporaneous with MIPRO — so the gap is historical rather than principled.
- **"Moderator is load-bearing" vs the [[2402.01817-llm-modulo|LLM-Modulo critique]].** Co-STORM positions the moderator as a *facilitator* and an *LM agent* that asks questions; Kambhampati et al. (2024) would frame this as a soft critic — useful, but **not sound**: an LM moderator inherits its base model's failure modes (hallucinated questions, omitted unknowns), and Co-STORM offers no hard external verifier. Mind-map accuracy (71% per human judges) is consistent with "useful soft critic" but inconsistent with any claim that the moderator *reliably* uncovers unknown unknowns. Co-STORM does not claim soundness; the framing is *engagement* and *serendipity* rather than *correctness*.
- **Information-Diversity metric vs report Depth.** Information Diversity is computed over *retrieved* info, not over the *generated* report; the diversity of what's *cited* in the report is not directly measured. A system that retrieves diverse sources but writes a shallow report could score high on Info Diversity and low on Depth — and indeed Co-STORM's Info Diversity edge (0.602 vs ~0.59) is much smaller than its Novelty / Depth edges. The metric is a **process** quality proxy, not an outcome proxy.
- **Latency.** §Limitations explicitly notes Co-STORM has **higher latency than the RAG chatbot baseline** (decide utterance intent → search → update mind map per turn). The 78%-prefer-Co-STORM figure was collected in an experimental setting where the participants were not paying their own latency cost; production deployment may not replicate the preference.

## Open questions surfaced

- Does optimizing Co-STORM's [[DSPy]] modules with [[MIPROv2]] or [[2507.19457-gepa|GEPA]] further widen its gap over baselines, or is the multi-agent orchestration already saturating the gains?
- Multilingual extension (acknowledged limitation) — Co-STORM's search-engine filter is the Wikipedia *Reliable sources* English-language guideline; the same orchestration in other languages depends on retrieval infrastructure not yet built.
- User-modeling — the system does not currently personalize its discourse to user prior knowledge; the same protocol runs for a novice and an expert.
