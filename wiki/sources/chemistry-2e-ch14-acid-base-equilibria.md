## Summary
OpenStax Chemistry 2e Chapter 14 develops acid-base chemistry as a study of equilibrium. It establishes the [[BronstedLowryAcidsAndBases|Brønsted-Lowry]] proton-transfer framework with [[ConjugateAcidBasePair|conjugate acid-base pairs]] and [[Amphoteric|amphoteric]] species, the [[WaterAutoionization|autoionization of water]] (Kw = [H₃O⁺][OH⁻] = 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁴ at 25 °C), and the [[pHScale|pH/pOH]] logarithmic scales. It quantifies acid/base strength via [[AcidIonizationConstant|Ka]] and [[BaseIonizationConstant|Kb]] (with Ka × Kb = Kw for a conjugate pair), [[PercentIonization|percent ionization]], and the [[LevelingEffect|leveling effect]]. It then applies these to [[SaltHydrolysis|salt hydrolysis]], [[PolyproticAcid|polyprotic acids]], [[BufferSolution|buffers]] (via the [[HendersonHasselbalch|Henderson-Hasselbalch equation]]), and [[AcidBaseTitration|acid-base titrations]] with [[TitrationCurve|titration curves]] and [[AcidBaseIndicator|indicators]].

## Key Claims
- A Brønsted-Lowry acid donates a proton; a base accepts one. Every acid-base reaction is a proton transfer producing a conjugate acid and conjugate base.
- Water is amphiprotic and autoionizes: 2H₂O ⇌ H₃O⁺ + OH⁻, with Kw = [H₃O⁺][OH⁻] = 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁴ at 25 °C. Kw rises with temperature (endothermic), so "neutral" pH falls below 7 above 25 °C.
- pH = −log[H₃O⁺], pOH = −log[OH⁻], and pH + pOH = pKw = 14.00 at 25 °C. Acidic: pH < 7; neutral: pH = 7; basic: pH > 7 (at 25 °C).
- Acid/base strength is quantified by Ka = [H₃O⁺][A⁻]/[HA] and Kb = [HB⁺][OH⁻]/[B]; larger constant = stronger. Percent ionization = ([H₃O⁺]eq/[HA]₀) × 100 decreases as concentration rises.
- For a conjugate pair, Ka × Kb = Kw — so the stronger an acid, the weaker its conjugate base.
- The leveling effect: H₃O⁺ is the strongest acid (and OH⁻ the strongest base) that can exist in water.
- Salt hydrolysis: salts of strong-acid/strong-base ions are neutral; weak-acid anions make basic solutions; weak-base cations (and most hydrated metal ions) make acidic solutions; with two reactive ions, compare Ka vs Kb.
- Polyprotic acids ionize stepwise with Ka1 > Ka2 > Ka3 (each ~10⁵–10⁶ smaller); the first ionization dominates [H₃O⁺].
- A buffer (weak conjugate acid-base pair) resists pH change; its capacity scales with component concentrations; pH = pKa + log([A⁻]/[HA]).
- In a titration curve, the equivalence point pH is 7 only for strong-acid/strong-base; weak-acid/strong-base equivalence is basic (e.g., 8.72). An indicator (a weak acid HIn) changes color over ~pKa ± 1 and must bracket the steep pH jump.

## Key Quotes
> "A compound that donates a proton to another compound is called a Brønsted-Lowry acid, and a compound that accepts a proton is called a Brønsted-Lowry base." — §14.1
> "The process in which like molecules react to yield ions is called autoionization." — §14.1, on water
> "In aqueous solution the strongest acid that can exist is H₃O⁺(aq)." — §14.3, leveling effect
> "Buffer capacity is the amount of acid or base that can be added to a given volume of a buffer solution before the pH changes significantly, usually by one unit." — §14.6
> "The color change interval ... for most indicators this range is approximately pKa ± 1." — §14.7
