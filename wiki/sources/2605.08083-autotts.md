# LLMs Improving LLMs: Agentic Discovery for Test-Time Scaling

Zheng, Liu, Huang, Bao, Zhang, Liu, Dai, Chen, Liu, Xiong, Wu, Zhang & Huang ([[universityofmaryland|UMD]] + [[universityofvirginia|UVA]] + [[washingtonuniversityinstlouis|WUSTL]] + [[universityofnorthcarolina|UNC]] + [[google|Google]] + [[meta|Meta]], arXiv 2605.08083v2, 12 May 2026). Reframes [[testtimescaling|test-time scaling]] (TTS) strategy design as an *automatic discovery* problem rather than a hand-crafting problem, and instantiates the reframing as **AutoTTS** — an environment-driven pipeline where an explorer LLM ([[claudecode|Claude Code]]) iteratively synthesizes a width–depth controller against an offline replay environment. The discovered controller (CMC) improves the accuracy–token Pareto frontier over four strong hand-crafted baselines across four Qwen3 model scales and held-out benchmarks AIME25, HMMT25, GPQA-Diamond. Total discovery cost: **$39.9 and 160 minutes**.

## Summary

Existing TTS methods — [[selfconsistency|SC@64]], ASC, ESC, ST-BoN, Parallel-Probe — are hand-designed special cases of a width × depth control space, with thresholds tuned by intuition. AutoTTS reframes this as **controller synthesis**: define an offline replay [[OfflineReplayEnvironment|environment]] of pre-collected reasoning trajectories and probe signals, then have an explorer LLM iteratively edit a Python controller (`OptimalController`) that decides — at each branch and depth — whether to BRANCH / CONTINUE / PROBE / PRUNE / ANSWER. Two design choices make the search tractable: (1) **[[BetaParameterization|beta parameterization]]** collapses the controller's hyperparameter space to a single scalar $\beta\in[0,1]$ governing aggressiveness (monotone, deterministic schedule for every internal knob), and (2) **execution-trace feedback** (full per-step decision traces, not just scalar acc/tokens) lets the explorer diagnose *why* a controller failed, not just whether. The discovered controller — *Confidence Momentum Controller* (CMC) — exhibits four non-obvious mechanisms: EMA-momentum stopping, coupled width–depth feedback, alignment-aware depth allocation, and conservative branch abandonment. Discovery generalizes from Qwen3 + AIME24 (search) to DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B + HMMT25 and Qwen3 + GPQA-Diamond (held-out, different model family and non-math domain).

## Key Claims

- **TTS strategies are special cases of one width–depth control space**, not fundamentally distinct algorithms. SC@64, ASC, ESC, Answer-Consistency, ST-BoN and Parallel-Probe each trace a distinct path through (width, depth) but all reduce to controller policies over the same state.
- **Strategy design should be automated**. Manual heuristic tuning leaves most of the control space unexplored; the right place to invest human effort is **environment design**, not strategy design ("the right place to invest human effort is in environment design, not strategy design").
- **Offline replay makes controller search affordable**. Pre-collecting $N=128$ reasoning trajectories at $\Delta=500$-token intervals per (model, problem) pair lets every candidate controller be evaluated *deterministically and without LLM calls* — collapsing search wall-clock to feasible budgets.
- **Beta parameterization is essential** (ablation: removing it drops held-out accuracy from 53.1 → 49.0, with token use collapsing to 93.3K from 575.5K — overly aggressive pruning that overfits the search set). One scalar trade-off knob + a deterministic $\beta\to\text{hyperparameters}$ schedule prevents collapse onto extreme search-set optima.
- **Execution traces are essential**. Without them (ablation: w/o execution traces), the discovered controller achieves *worse* accuracy with *more* tokens — scalar acc/cost numbers alone are insufficient to guide effective search.
- **The discovered controller (CMC) generalizes** beyond the search setting: from Qwen3 to DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B (3-out-of-4 model average improvement on held-out HMMT25), and from math to GPQA-Diamond (matches or exceeds SC@64 with substantially fewer tokens).
- **Discovery is cheap**: $39.9 + 160 minutes for the full five-round loop, dominated by API cost of the explorer LLM, not by inference on the base model (which is replayed).
- **Four non-obvious mechanisms emerged that would be hard to hand-craft**: trend-based stopping via EMA momentum (instead of instantaneous gating); coupled width–depth control through a shared evidence signal; alignment-aware depth allocation (branches matching the pool winner get burst probes); and conservative branch abandonment requiring persistent deviation.

## Method Details

**Width–depth state.** At decision step $t$, state $s_t = (q, m_t, I_t, \ell_t, Z_t, \Omega_t)$ where $m_t$ = branches instantiated, $I_t\subseteq[m_t]$ = active branches, $\ell_t$ = per-branch depth vector (#fixed-length generation intervals), $Z_t$ = generated prefixes, $\Omega_t$ = revealed probe-feedback set. Admissible actions: BRANCH, CONTINUE(i), PROBE(i), PRUNE(i), ANSWER. Cost: $\sum \ell_{t,i} + \kappa_{probe}|\Omega_t|$ ($\kappa_{probe}=0$ in offline replay).

**Replay environment.** Following [[2602.03845-parallel-probe|Parallel-Probe]]'s protocol: pre-sample $N=128$ trajectories per (model, problem) at temperature 0.7 with $\Delta=500$-token probe interval. PROBE retrieves pre-collected $\omega_{i,k}$ at zero cost; every controller decision is a deterministic table lookup.

**Discovery loop.** Five rounds. Each round: explorer ([[claudecode|Claude Code]]) reads history $\mathcal{H}$ (prior controllers + their accuracy/cost outcomes + per-question execution traces) and proposes a new `OptimalController` by directly editing `code_base/method.py`. Evaluated 64× on $\mathcal{Q}_{search}$ = AIME24. Final controller = highest-accuracy on union of AIME24 environments across all four Qwen3 model scales.

**Beta parameterization.** Each controller exposes a single $\beta\in[0,1]$ and maps it deterministically to every internal hyperparameter via a `_schedule(beta)` method using only simple analytic forms (linear, sigmoid, clipped ramp). Monotonicity required: parameters governing budget use non-decreasing in $\beta$; stopping thresholds non-increasing.

**Confidence Momentum Controller (CMC) — Appendix D.** All schedules are smooth analytic functions of $b=\beta$:
- `n_init = max(2, round(2 + 6b))` — initial branches.
- `max_branch_use = min(64, round(4 + 60b))`.
- `T_ema = max(2, round(2 + 6b))` — EMA window over pool confidence.
- `ema_alpha = 0.70 − 0.40·b` ∈ [0.30, 0.70] (NON-INCREASING; higher β → more inertia).
- `conf_thresh = 0.85 + 0.12·b` (stopping floor).
- `delta_slack = 0.04 − 0.03·b` (non-deteriorating-momentum tolerance).
- `burst_aligned = max(1, round(1 + 2b))` (extra probe steps for aligned branches).
- `widen_burst = max(1, round(1 + 3b))`.
- `trend_thresh = 0.04 − 0.03·b` (EMA-delta below which widening triggers).
- `min_complete = max(2, round(2 + 3b))`.

Stop fires when BOTH `ema_conf ≥ conf_thresh` AND `delta ≥ −delta_slack`. Three-tier branch classification (aligned / neutral / deviant) drives probe-multiplier allocation; deviants abandoned only after `abandon_patience` persistent disagreement rounds (≥2 branches always preserved).

## Empirical Results

**Main accuracy–token table (Table 1, avg over Qwen3-0.6B/1.7B/4B/8B on held-out):**

| Method | Type | Avg Acc ↑ | Avg Tokens ↓ |
|---|---|---|---|
| SC@64 | Handcrafted | 45.2 | 1083k |
| ASC | Handcrafted | 45.2 | 715k |
| ESC | Handcrafted | 45.2 | 1029k |
| Parallel-Probe | Handcrafted | 45.5 | 802k |
| **AutoTTS (β=0.5)** | **Discovered** | **45.3** | **334k** |
| **AutoTTS (β=1.0)** | **Discovered** | **46.6** | **621k** |

At β=0.5: **~69.5% token reduction vs SC@64** at matched accuracy. At β=1.0: peak accuracy beyond all handcrafted baselines in 5/8 cases.

**Generalization (Table 2).** DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B on HMMT25: AutoTTS(β=1) reaches 27.2 acc / 533.9K tokens vs SC@64's 26.7 / 985.7K. Qwen3-1.7B on GPQA-Diamond: AutoTTS(β=0.5) hits 41.6 / 151.0K vs SC@64's 41.3 / 510.0K.

**Ablations (Table 3, avg over 3 benchmarks):**
- Full: 53.1 / 575.5K, search cost $39.9.
- w/o Beta Parameterization: 49.0 / 93.3K (collapses to ultra-aggressive pruning), search cost $46.4.
- w/o Execution Traces: 51.6 / 824.0K (worse acc *and* more tokens), search cost $30.9.

**Discovery cost (§5.5).** $39.87 + 160 minutes for five rounds, dominated by explorer-LLM API; replay reuses pre-collected trajectories.

## Contradictions

- **Productive tension with [[2605.02396-heavyskill]]**: HEAVYSKILL argues the apparent gains of TTS orchestration can be *internalized* into a single model via RLVR (the "inner skill" hypothesis), leaving the harness obsolete. AutoTTS goes the opposite direction: it argues the harness is exactly where to invest research effort, but the *design* of that harness should be automated. Both can be right: an internalized inner skill removes the need for many runtime branches, but an *automatically discovered* controller can still squeeze the remaining accuracy–cost frontier. The contradiction is about *which axis pays off most* — model training vs harness design — and the answer may depend on base-model capability.

- **Tension with [[2605.10698-bystander-effect-mas]] / [[BystanderEffect]]**: AutoTTS's discovered controller uses *peer agreement* across branches (pool-winner alignment) as a positive signal to allocate burst probes. Shehata & Li show that aggregating peer signals in an LLM-on-LLM setting can collapse [[AgenticSovereignty]] via the [[SovereigntyDecayLaw]]. The two are not directly inconsistent — AutoTTS branches are *independent samples from one base model*, not separate auditor agents proposing reasoning to the focal model — but readers who naively generalize "pool-winner agreement = trustworthy evidence" beyond the offline-replay setting risk a Bystander-Effect-style collapse.

- **No direct contradictions with [[2602.03845-parallel-probe|Parallel-Probe]]**; AutoTTS instead generalizes Parallel-Probe's fixed-cohort + instantaneous-majority + stable-majority-termination policy into one point on a search space and discovers a different, EMA-momentum-based policy.
