## Summary
OpenStax Chemistry 2e Chapter 4 develops how chemical reactions are represented, classified, and quantified. It teaches writing and balancing chemical equations (using coefficients to satisfy conservation of matter, plus state symbols and molecular/complete-ionic/net-ionic forms), classifies reactions into precipitation, acid-base, and oxidation-reduction types (with solubility rules and oxidation-number rules), and builds reaction stoichiometry from mole ratios. It closes with reaction yields (limiting reactant, theoretical/actual/percent yield) and quantitative analysis methods (titration, gravimetric, and combustion analysis).

## Key Claims
- A balanced chemical equation must obey the law of conservation of matter — equal atoms of each element on both sides — and balance is achieved only by changing **coefficients**, never subscripts.
- Aqueous ionic reactions can be written at three levels of detail: **molecular**, **complete ionic** (dissolved ionics shown as dissociated ions), and **net ionic** (spectator ions removed).
- Common reactions sort into three classes: **precipitation** (form an insoluble solid, governed by solubility rules), **acid-base** (transfer of H⁺; neutralization gives salt + water), and **oxidation-reduction** (change in oxidation number; includes combustion, single-displacement, and disproportionation).
- **Oxidation** is loss of electrons / increase in oxidation number; **reduction** is gain of electrons / decrease in oxidation number; the oxidized species is the reducing agent and the reduced species is the oxidizing agent.
- Stoichiometric factors (mole ratios) derived from balanced-equation coefficients are the central tool for converting between amounts of reactants and products (mass→mole→mole→mass).
- The **limiting reactant** is fully consumed and caps product formed; **percent yield = (actual yield / theoretical yield) × 100%**.
- Quantitative analysis applies known-stoichiometry reactions: **titration** (titrant + analyte to an equivalence/end point), **gravimetric analysis** (isolate and weigh a precipitate), and **combustion analysis** (CO₂ and H₂O masses give an empirical formula).

## Key Quotes
> "The quantitative relationships between the amounts of substances consumed and produced by the reaction" are known as the reaction's stoichiometry.

> "The amount of product that may be produced by a reaction under specified conditions, as calculated per the stoichiometry of an appropriate balanced chemical equation, is called the theoretical yield of the reaction."
