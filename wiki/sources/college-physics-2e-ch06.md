## Summary
Chapter 6 of OpenStax College Physics 2e treats curved motion — chiefly uniform circular motion (constant-speed motion on a circular path) — and the forces, especially gravity, that produce it. It builds the rotational kinematics of angle and angular velocity, derives centripetal acceleration and the centripetal force that sustains circular motion, distinguishes real forces from fictitious ones in rotating frames, and culminates in Newton's universal law of gravitation and Kepler's laws of planetary motion. A recurring theme is that gravity supplies the centripetal force binding orbits at every scale.

## Key Claims
- A net force directed toward the center of curvature is required to keep an object on a curved path; absent it, the object moves in a straight line ([[NewtonsFirstLaw]]).
- Rotation angle is arc length over radius (Δθ = Δs/r), and all points on a rigid body share the same angular displacement; linear and angular speed relate by v = rω.
- Centripetal acceleration always points toward the center and has magnitude a_c = v²/r = rω².
- Centripetal force is not a new kind of force but whatever net inward force (tension, friction, gravity, normal force) produces a_c: F_c = mv²/r = mrω².
- On a flat curve, the required tire friction coefficient is μ_s = v²/(rg); an ideally banked curve needs no friction at angle θ = tan⁻¹(v²/(rg)), independent of vehicle mass.
- Fictitious forces (centrifugal, Coriolis) arise only in non-inertial (accelerating/rotating) frames and reflect inertia, not real physical interactions.
- The Coriolis force explains large-scale storm rotation: counterclockwise in the Northern Hemisphere, clockwise in the Southern.
- Newton's universal law of gravitation: F = GmM/r², attractive, along the line joining centers; the gravitational field gives g = GM/r².
- Cavendish first measured G ≈ 6.674 × 10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg² (1798) with a torsion balance, enabling computation of Earth's mass via M = gr²/G.
- The same gravitational force that produces surface weight supplies the centripetal force holding the Moon in orbit (Earth's g at lunar distance ≈ the Moon's required a_c).
- Kepler's three laws (ellipses, equal areas, harmonic law T²∝r³) describe orbits; Newton's gravitation provides their underlying cause.
- For circular orbits, T² = (4π²/GM)r³, so orbital period at a given radius is independent of the satellite's mass.

## Key Quotes
> Acceleration is any change in velocity — in magnitude, direction, or both; circular motion at constant speed still accelerates because direction changes. — paraphrased, centripetal acceleration derivation (6.2)
> Centripetal force is whatever real net force happens to point toward the center, not a distinct force of its own. — paraphrased, centripetal force (6.3)
> Apparently weightless astronauts are not beyond gravity's reach; they and their craft are in continuous free fall together. — paraphrased, universal gravitation (6.5)
> Kepler's empirical laws find their cause in Newton's single inverse-square gravitation law — an argument for the simplicity of nature. — paraphrased, satellites and Kepler's laws (6.6)
