## Summary
The law of sines states that in any triangle, the ratio between the length of a side and the [[sine-and-cosine|sine]] of its opposite angle is the same for all three sides. ## Key Claims
- **Example 1** — Consider a triangle in which \\(\alpha = 40^\circ\\), \\(\beta = 65^\circ\\) and \\(a = 10\\).
- **The ambiguous case** — In the Side-Side-Angle (SSA) configuration, where two sides \\(a\\) and \\(b\\) and an angle \\(\alpha\\) opposite to one of them are given, the law of sines does not necessarily determine a unique triangle.
- **Example 2** — Consider a triangle in which \\(\alpha = 35^\circ\\), \\(a = 7\\) and \\(b = 10\\).
- **A geometric criterion for the ambiguous case** — The algebraic analysis of the ambiguous case can be complemented by a geometric criterion that allows the number of valid triangles to be determined before performing any computation.
