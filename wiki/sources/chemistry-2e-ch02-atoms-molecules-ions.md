## Summary
Chapter 2 of OpenStax *Chemistry 2e* traces how the modern understanding of matter at the atomic scale developed and how that understanding is encoded in chemical symbolism. It begins with [[AtomicTheory|Dalton's atomic theory]] and the mass laws ([[LawOfDefiniteProportions]], [[LawOfMultipleProportions]]) that grounded it, follows the [[EvolutionOfAtomicTheory|experimental evolution]] of the model through Thomson, Millikan, Rutherford, and Chadwick, and then formalizes [[AtomicStructure]], [[AtomicNumberAndMassNumber|atomic/mass numbers]], [[Isotope|isotopes]], and [[AtomicMass|weighted-average atomic mass]]. The chapter closes with the language of chemistry: [[ChemicalFormula|chemical formulas]], the [[PeriodicTable]], [[IonicCompound|ionic]] vs [[MolecularCompound|molecular compounds]], and [[ChemicalNomenclature|nomenclature]].

## Key Claims
- Matter is composed of [[Atom|atoms]]; [[AtomicTheory|Dalton's five postulates]] (1807) explain conservation of matter and the constant composition of [[ChemicalCompound|compounds]].
- The [[LawOfDefiniteProportions]] (constant composition) and [[LawOfMultipleProportions]] (small whole-number mass ratios) are empirical consequences of atoms combining in fixed ratios.
- [[EvolutionOfAtomicTheory|Experiments]] showed the atom is divisible: Thomson found the [[Electron]] (charge-to-mass ratio), Millikan measured its charge (1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C), and Rutherford's gold-foil experiment revealed a tiny, dense, positively charged [[AtomicNucleus|nucleus]].
- An atom is protons + neutrons in a nucleus with surrounding electrons; the [[AtomicNumberAndMassNumber|atomic number Z]] (proton count) defines the element, and [[Isotope|isotopes]] differ only in neutron count (mass number A).
- An [[ChemicalIon|ion]] is a charged atom (cation = lost electrons; anion = gained electrons); [[AtomicMass|atomic mass]] on the periodic table is the abundance-weighted average over isotopes.
- [[ChemicalFormula|Molecular formulas]] give actual atom counts; empirical formulas give the simplest whole-number ratio and are always a divisor of the molecular formula.
- The [[PeriodicTable]] orders 118 elements by atomic number into 7 periods and 18 groups, sorting them into metals, nonmetals, and metalloids; periodic position predicts ion charges for main-group elements.
- Metal + nonmetal usually gives an [[IonicCompound]] (ionic bonds, charge-balanced formulas); nonmetal + nonmetal gives a [[MolecularCompound]] (covalent bonds). [[ChemicalNomenclature]] supplies systematic naming rules for both, plus acids.

> "Atoms are neither created nor destroyed during a chemical change, but are instead rearranged to yield substances that are different from those present before the change." — Dalton's fifth postulate ([[ConservationOfMatter]])
> "All samples of a pure compound contain the same elements in the same proportion by mass." — [[LawOfDefiniteProportions]] (Proust)
> "When two elements react to form more than one compound, a fixed mass of one element will react with masses of the other element in a ratio of small, whole numbers." — [[LawOfMultipleProportions]]
> "The properties of the elements are periodic functions of their atomic numbers." — modern [[PeriodicTable|periodic law]]
> "It was almost as incredible as if you fired a 15-inch shell at a piece of tissue paper and it came back and hit you." — Rutherford on alpha scattering ([[ErnestRutherford]])
