## Summary
Sutskever, Vinyals & Le (Google) introduce **sequence-to-sequence (seq2seq) learning**: an end-to-end neural approach that maps variable-length input sequences to variable-length output sequences using two stacked LSTMs — one encoder that reads the input into a fixed-dimensional vector and one decoder that emits the output conditioned on that vector. On the WMT'14 English→French task, an ensemble of 5 deep LSTMs reaches BLEU 34.81 (vs. SMT baseline 33.30), the first time a pure neural translation system beats a phrase-based SMT baseline at scale. Using the LSTM to rescore the SMT 1000-best lifts BLEU to 36.5, within 0.5 of the prior best (37.0). Two surprising findings: the LSTM handles long sentences well, and **reversing the source-sentence word order** (but not the target) dropped perplexity from 5.8 → 4.7 and lifted BLEU 25.9 → 30.6 by introducing many short-term dependencies that ease optimization. This is the architectural predecessor of [[1706.03762-attention-is-all-you-need]] — same encoder-decoder framing, recurrent backbone replaced three years later by attention.

## Key Claims
- **Seq2seq framing.** A general sequence-mapping problem can be cast as `p(y₁,…,y_{T'} | x₁,…,x_T) = ∏ p(yt | v, y₁,…,y_{t-1})`, where `v` is the encoder LSTM's final hidden state. Decoder is essentially an LSTM language model conditioned on `v`.
- **Two separate LSTMs.** Using distinct encoder and decoder LSTMs (rather than tying weights) adds parameters at negligible cost and supports training on multiple language pairs jointly.
- **Depth matters.** 4-layer LSTMs significantly outperform shallow ones; each added layer reduces perplexity ~10%.
- **Source-reversal trick.** Mapping `c, b, a → α, β, γ` instead of `a, b, c → α, β, γ` shortens the "minimal time lag" between aligned source/target tokens, easing SGD. BLEU lifts from 25.9 → 30.6, perplexity drops 5.8 → 4.7. Effect extends to long sentences (better memory utilization).
- **Beam search decoding works at small B.** B=2 captures most of the benefit; B=1 already works.
- **LSTM rescoring of SMT n-best lists.** Averaging LSTM log-prob with SMT score on the 1000-best list lifts the SMT baseline from 33.30 → 36.5 BLEU.
- **Representation quality.** 2D PCA projection of the encoder's final hidden state shows that learned sentence embeddings are sensitive to word order yet relatively invariant to active/passive voice — early evidence that recurrent encoders learn meaning-bearing representations.
- **Long-sentence performance.** Contrary to the experience of [Cho et al. 2014](https://arxiv.org/abs/1406.1078) and [Bahdanau et al. 2014](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.0473), reversed-source LSTMs degrade only marginally on long sentences — undermining the prior assumption that fixed-vector encoders cannot handle long inputs.

## Key Quotes
> "We present a general end-to-end approach to sequence learning that makes minimal assumptions on the sequence structure."

> "While we do not have a complete explanation to this phenomenon, we believe that it is caused by the introduction of many short term dependencies to the dataset. … By reversing the words in the source sentence, … the first few words in the source language are now very close to the first few words in the target language, so the problem's minimal time lag is greatly reduced."

> "It is the first time that a pure neural translation system outperforms a phrase-based SMT baseline on a large scale MT task by a sizeable margin, despite its inability to handle out-of-vocabulary words."

> "We conclude that it is important to find a problem encoding that has the greatest number of short term dependencies, as they make the learning problem much simpler."

## Architecture Reference (constants)
- 4-layer LSTM encoder + 4-layer LSTM decoder, 1000 cells per layer, 1000-dim word embeddings.
- Vocabulary: 160k source (English), 80k target (French); OOV → `UNK`. Sentence vector dimension: 8000.
- Parameters: 384M total, of which 64M are pure recurrent connections (32M encoder + 32M decoder).
- Training: WMT'14 EN→FR, 12M sentences (348M FR words, 304M EN words). Plain SGD, no momentum, LR 0.7 with halving every half-epoch after epoch 5, total 7.5 epochs. Uniform init [−0.08, 0.08]. Gradient norm clipped to 5 (per-128-batch). Length-bucketed minibatches (2× speedup).
- Hardware: 8 GPUs (one per LSTM layer + 4 for softmax parallelization), 6,300 words/sec, ~10 days training.
- Decoding: left-to-right beam search, B=12 for best results; B=2 captures most gains.

## Results (WMT'14 EN→FR, ntst14 test set, cased BLEU)

| System | BLEU |
|---|---|
| SMT baseline [Schwenk 2014] | 33.30 |
| Bahdanau et al. 2014 (attention) | 28.45 |
| Single forward LSTM (B=12) | 26.17 |
| Single reversed LSTM (B=12) | 30.59 |
| Ensemble of 5 reversed LSTMs (B=12) | **34.81** |
| Rescore SMT 1000-best w/ ensemble of 5 reversed LSTMs | **36.5** |
| Best WMT'14 result [Durrani et al. 2014] | 37.0 |
| Oracle rescoring of SMT 1000-best | ~45 |

## Contradictions
None with current wiki content. This source *predates* every other paper in the LLM corpus and supplies the encoder-decoder framing the Transformer paper inherits. The fixed-vector bottleneck that motivates the attention mechanism of Bahdanau et al. 2014 / [[1706.03762-attention-is-all-you-need]] is the central limitation of this paper's design — they are sequential refinements, not contradictions.

## Notes

- Authors went on to define much of modern deep learning: Sutskever co-founded OpenAI; Vinyals leads DeepMind research; Le led Google Brain's AutoML work.
