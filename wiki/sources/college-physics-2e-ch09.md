## Summary
Chapter 9 of OpenStax College Physics 2e covers statics — the analysis of systems in equilibrium — and the rotational quantity torque. It establishes the two conditions for equilibrium (zero net force and zero net torque), defines torque via the perpendicular lever arm, classifies equilibrium as stable/unstable/neutral based on the center of gravity relative to the base of support, and applies these ideas to simple machines (levers, pulleys, ramps, cranks) and to forces in human muscles and joints.

## Key Claims
- An object is in equilibrium when its net external force is zero (the first condition), so it stays at rest or moves at constant velocity.
- Zero net force alone is not sufficient for equilibrium; the net external torque must also be zero (the second condition).
- Torque is the rotational effectiveness of a force: `τ = r F sin θ = r⊥ F`, measured in newton-meters; it depends on force magnitude, lever arm, and angle.
- The second condition holds for any chosen pivot point, but the pivot must be applied consistently; choosing it at the line of an unknown force eliminates that force's torque.
- Equilibrium is stable, unstable, or neutral depending on whether a displacement produces a restoring, amplifying, or no net torque.
- A body is stable only while its center of gravity stays directly above its base of support; widening the base or lowering the cg increases stability.
- Simple machines multiply force at the expense of distance and obey energy conservation; mechanical advantage `MA = F_o/F_i`, and for a lever `MA = l_i/l_o`.
- Skeletal muscles typically have mechanical advantage well below 1: short lever arms force muscles to exert many times the supported load (e.g., the biceps ~7.38× the weight held).
- Poor lifting posture greatly amplifies spinal forces (~5.6×) versus lifting with an erect back, explaining injury risk.
- Both equilibrium conditions, combined with free body diagrams and a strategic pivot choice, form the standard problem-solving method for statics.

## Key Quotes (paraphrased)
> Statics is a special case of Newton's second law in which all forces are balanced and there is no acceleration. — Chapter introduction
> A force's torque is zero if it acts at the pivot or along a line passing through the pivot, which is why pivot choice can eliminate unknown forces. — §9.4
> Muscles attach close to joints, giving a mechanical advantage far below one — large muscle forces but useful gains in limb range and speed. — §9.6
