## Key Claims
- **Degree and classification** — The degree of a polynomial equation determines much of its behaviour and governs how many solutions one may expect to find.
- **The fundamental theorem of algebra** — The existence and count of roots of a polynomial equation are governed by the Fundamental Theorem of Algebra, which guarantees that every polynomial equation of degree \\(n \geq 1\\) with complex coefficients has exactly \\(n\\) roots in…
- **Multiplicity of roots** — A root \\(x_0\\) is said to have multiplicity \\(m\\) if the factor \\((x - x_0)^m\\) divides the polynomial but \\((x - x_0)^{m+1}\\) does not.
- **Rational root theorem** — When the coefficients of a polynomial equation are [[integers|integers]], it is possible to identify all candidates for rational roots without solving the equation directly.
- **Vieta's formulas** — The coefficients of a polynomial equation are not independent of its roots: they are related to them through a set of identities known as Vieta's formulas.
- **Reduction to a polynomial equation** — Many equations that do not appear polynomial at first sight can be reduced to polynomial form through algebraic manipulation, after which the methods discussed in this entry apply directly.
- **Solvability by radicals** — For polynomial equations of degree up to four, explicit formulas expressing the roots in terms of the coefficients by means of arithmetic operations and radicals are known.
- **Numerical methods** — When an analytic solution is unavailable or impractical, polynomial equations are typically solved by numerical methods.
