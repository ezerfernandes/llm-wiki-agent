## Summary
The opening chapter of OpenStax *Chemistry 2e* establishes the foundational vocabulary and methods of chemistry: what matter is, how it is classified, how it changes, and how chemists measure and reason about it quantitatively. It defines [[Chemistry]] as the study of the composition, properties, and interactions of [[Matter]], frames the discipline through the [[ScientificMethod]] and the three [[DomainsOfChemistry|domains of chemistry]] (macroscopic, microscopic, symbolic), and develops the measurement toolkit — [[SIUnits]], [[Density]], [[SignificantFigures]], [[AccuracyAndPrecision]], and [[DimensionalAnalysis]] — that the rest of the textbook relies on.

## Key Claims
- [[Matter]] is anything that occupies space and has mass; it exists as solid, liquid, gas, or plasma ([[StatesOfMatter]]), and is distinguished from weight (mass is intrinsic; weight is the gravitational force on that mass).
- The [[ConservationOfMatter|law of conservation of matter]] states the total quantity of matter is unchanged by physical or chemical changes.
- Matter is classified ([[MatterClassification]]) into pure substances ([[ChemicalElement|elements]] and [[ChemicalCompound|compounds]]) and mixtures (homogeneous/solutions and heterogeneous); the smallest units are [[Atom|atoms]] and [[Molecule|molecules]].
- [[PhysicalProperty|Physical properties/changes]] do not alter chemical identity, whereas [[ChemicalProperty|chemical properties/changes]] produce new substances; properties are further classed as extensive (amount-dependent) or intensive (amount-independent) — see [[ExtensiveAndIntensiveProperties]].
- Chemistry advances by the [[ScientificMethod]], which distinguishes a [[ScientificHypothesis|hypothesis]] from a scientific [[ScientificLawAndTheory|law and theory]].
- Every measurement carries a magnitude, a [[SIUnits|unit]], and an uncertainty; the seven SI base units and decimal prefixes underpin all quantitative chemistry.
- [[Density]] = mass/volume is a key derived, intensive property used to identify substances (e.g., water displacement methods).
- Measurement uncertainty is communicated with [[SignificantFigures]] and rounding rules; [[AccuracyAndPrecision]] are distinct (closeness to true value vs. closeness of repeated measurements).
- [[DimensionalAnalysis]] (the factor-label method) treats units algebraically to convert between units and compute derived quantities; [[TemperatureMeasurement|temperature conversions]] among Celsius, Fahrenheit, and Kelvin follow linear relations.

## Key Quotes
> "Chemistry [is] the study of the composition, properties, and interactions of matter." — definition of the discipline (1.1)

> "Matter is defined as anything that occupies space and has mass." — definition of matter (1.2)

> "There is no detectable change in the total quantity of matter present when matter converts from one type to another (a chemical change) or changes among solid, liquid, or gaseous states (a physical change)." — law of conservation of matter (1.2)

> "The units of quantities must be subjected to the same mathematical operations as their associated numbers." — principle of dimensional analysis (1.6)
