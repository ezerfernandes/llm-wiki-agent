## Summary
Kim et al. (Yonsei + Microsoft Research) construct controlled tasks where reasoning complexity is held constant but horizon length varies, isolating horizon as an independent training variable for LLM agents. Increasing horizon alone produces severe RL training instability (exploration difficulty, credit-assignment ambiguity, performance collapse). **Horizon reduction** stabilizes training and yields **horizon generalization**: models trained on shorter horizons generalize to longer ones at inference.

## Key Claims
- **Horizon as bottleneck** — even with identical decision rules and reasoning structure, longer required action sequences alone destabilize RL training and cause collapse.
- **Two confounded effects of horizon** — combinatorial state-action explosion (exploration) and delayed feedback (high-variance gradients, ambiguous credit).
- **Horizon reduction principle** — training on shorter goal distances is a simple, powerful stabilizer that improves both training stability and final performance.
- **Horizon generalization** — short-horizon-trained models extend to longer horizons at inference, suggesting a scalable path beyond curriculum or environmental tweaks.
- **Critic-free RL fits long-horizon** — pursues GRPO-style critic-free methods (vs PPO) for scale, with masked + truncated importance sampling to handle policy staleness.

## Key Quotes
> "Increasing horizon length alone constitutes a training bottleneck, inducing severe training instability driven by exploration difficulties and credit-assignment challenges."
> "Models trained under reduced horizons generalize more effectively to longer-horizon variants at inference time, a phenomenon we refer to as horizon generalization."
