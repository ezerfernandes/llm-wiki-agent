## Summary
OpenStax College Physics 2e Chapter 24 develops electromagnetic waves from Maxwell's four equations, which unified electricity, magnetism, and optics and predicted that oscillating electric and magnetic fields propagate through vacuum at the speed of light. It covers how antennas produce EM waves (mutually perpendicular, in-phase E and B fields, with `E/B = c`), surveys the electromagnetic spectrum band by band (radio through gamma rays), and quantifies the energy and intensity an EM wave transports.

## Key Claims
- Maxwell's four equations (Gauss for electricity, Gauss for magnetism, Faraday's induction, Ampère–Maxwell) encapsulate all classical electromagnetism; Maxwell's key addition was that a *changing electric field* produces a magnetic field.
- The equations predict EM waves travel at `c = 1/sqrt(μ₀ε₀) = 3.00×10⁸ m/s`, matching the measured speed of light — so light *is* an electromagnetic wave.
- Heinrich Hertz (1887) experimentally generated and detected EM waves with spark-gap loops, confirming Maxwell's prediction years after Maxwell's death.
- An EM wave's electric and magnetic fields oscillate in phase, perpendicular to each other and to the propagation direction (a transverse wave), with `E/B = c` in vacuum.
- The full EM spectrum — radio, microwave, infrared, visible light, ultraviolet, X-rays, gamma rays — is one phenomenon differing only by frequency/wavelength, related by `c = fλ`.
- Higher-frequency waves carry more energy, penetrate matter better, encode more information, and resolve finer detail (resolution ≈ wavelength).
- X-rays and gamma rays of the same frequency are physically identical, distinguished only by their source (atomic/electronic vs. nuclear).
- EM-wave intensity scales with field amplitude squared: `I_ave = cε₀E₀²/2 = cB₀²/(2μ₀) = E₀B₀/(2μ₀)`.
- Material transparency is frequency-dependent — radiation is variously transmitted, reflected, or absorbed at a boundary.
- Practical phenomena follow: AM/FM modulation, microwave dielectric heating of water (~2.45 GHz), the CO₂/H₂O greenhouse effect, UV-B skin damage and ozone-layer protection.
