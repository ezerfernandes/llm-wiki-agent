## Key Claims
- **Why exponents in a monomial must be non-negative integers** — The constraint that exponents be non-negative integers follows directly from the definition of a [[polynomials|polynomial]]: each variable must appear with an exponent in \\(\mathbb{N}\_0\\).
- **Degree of a monomial** — The degree of a monomial is the sum of the exponents of all its variables.
- **Similar, opposite and equal monomials** — Two monomials are defined as similar if they possess identical variable parts, meaning the same variables raised to the same exponents.
- **Addition and subtraction of monomials** — In polynomial algebra, the addition or subtraction of monomials is governed by specific rules.
- **Product of monomials** — Monomials can be multiplied by multiplying their coefficients and adding the [[powers|exponents]] of the like variables.
- **Division of monomials** — To divide monomials, divide their coefficients and subtract the exponents of like variables.
- **Powers** — When a monomial is raised to a [[powers|power]], the exponent applies to each factor: the coefficient is raised to that power, and the exponents of the variables are multiplied by it.
- **GCD and LCM of monomials** — The greatest common divisor (GCD) of two or more monomials is defined as the monomial with the largest coefficient that divides all given coefficients and the smallest exponent for each variable present in all monomials.
