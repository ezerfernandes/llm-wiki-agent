# Dive into Deep Learning — Generative Adversarial Networks

[[AstonZhang|Zhang]], [[ZacharyLipton|Lipton]], [[MuLi|Li]] & [[AlexanderSmola|Smola]]'s two-section *Generative Adversarial Networks* chapter — D2L's **first dedicated generative-modeling chapter** and the textbook's pivot from discriminative learning into the world where the *output* of training is not a classifier but a *sampler*. The chapter is concise (two notebooks: `gan.md`, `dcgan.md`) and tightly motivated: §`gan` builds the [[generativeadversarialnetwork|GAN]] from scratch on a 2-D Gaussian toy ("the world's lamest example") so the minimax math is in front of the reader without any image-generation noise; §`dcgan` then scales that scaffold to RGB images with the [[DCGAN|Deep Convolutional GAN]] architecture ([[AlecRadford|Radford]], Metz & Chintala 2015), trained on a Pokémon-sprite dataset.

## Summary

The chapter delivers the canonical GAN formulation in three steps. (1) **Framing**: discriminative learning ([[ImageNet|ImageNet]]-style classification) has dominated D2L so far, but *generative* modeling — concisely capturing a data distribution well enough to sample novel members — is a different problem. (2) **The GAN idea** ([[IanGoodfellow|Goodfellow]], Pouget-Abadie, Mirza et al. 2014): replace explicit density estimation with a *two-sample-test* training signal — train a [[Generator|generator]] $G$ that maps noise $\mathbf z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)$ to fake samples $\mathbf x' = G(\mathbf z)$ and a [[Discriminator|discriminator]] $D$ that tries to classify real-vs-fake; the two networks play a [[MinMaxGame|minimax game]] $\min_D \max_G \{-E_{x\sim\textrm{Data}}\log D(\mathbf x) - E_{z\sim\textrm{Noise}}\log(1 - D(G(\mathbf z)))\}$, and at equilibrium $G$ has matched the data distribution. (3) **The DCGAN scale-up** ([[AlecRadford|Radford]], Metz & Chintala 2015): the discriminator is a 4-block strided-conv classifier with [[BatchNormalization|batch normalization]] + [[LeakyReLU|Leaky ReLU]], the generator is a mirror-image 4-block stack of [[TransposedConvolution|transposed convolutions]] + [[BatchNormalization|batch normalization]] + [[ReLU]] that upsamples a 100-d noise vector through $(1{\times}1) \to (4{\times}4) \to (8{\times}8) \to (16{\times}16) \to (32{\times}32) \to (64{\times}64)$ with channel widths $100\!\to\!512\!\to\!256\!\to\!128\!\to\!64\!\to\!3$ and a final $\tanh$ to land in $[-1,1]$. Practical training tweaks: Adam $\beta_1$ lowered from $0.9$ to $0.5$ for less momentum smoothing (the gradients change fast because $D$ and $G$ fight); same LR for both networks; weights init from $\mathcal{N}(0, 0.02)$.

## Key Claims

- **Discriminative vs generative learning**: D2L explicitly frames GANs as the technique that converts the empirical success of discriminative deep nets ([[ImageNet]]-era classifiers) into a generative-modeling technique. The fundamental claim is the "two-sample test" insight — *"a data generator is good if we cannot tell fake data apart from real data."* GANs are constructive — they use the two-sample test as a *training signal*, not just as a hypothesis test.
- **The min-max objective** (Goodfellow et al. 2014): $\min_D \max_G \{-E_{x\sim\textrm{Data}}\log D(\mathbf x) - E_{z\sim\textrm{Noise}}\log(1 - D(G(\mathbf z)))\}$. Two networks competing on opposite signs of a single [[BinaryCrossEntropy|binary cross-entropy]] objective. At the global optimum, $D \equiv 1/2$ everywhere and the generator distribution equals the data distribution.
- **Generator architecture** is intentionally minimal — in the 2-D Gaussian toy, $G$ is a single $\mathbb{R}^2 \!\to\! \mathbb{R}^2$ linear layer (because the data is itself a linear transformation of Gaussian noise, $G$ literally only needs to learn the parameters to fake things perfectly). The chapter's pedagogical move is to keep $G$ minimal so the minimax dynamics are not confounded by capacity issues.
- **Discriminator architecture** in the toy example is a 3-layer MLP with $\tanh$ activations (2→5→3→1); deliberately more powerful than $G$ to ensure that early in training $D$ is informative.
- **The generator's gradient-vanishing fix**: instead of optimizing $\max_G \{-\log(1-D(G(\mathbf z)))\}$ (which has vanishing gradients when $D(G(\mathbf z))$ is small, as it is early in training), the standard trick is to minimize $-\log D(G(\mathbf z))$ — *"feeding $\mathbf x'=G(\mathbf z)$ into the discriminator but giving label $y=1$."* This is the canonical "non-saturating" loss; framework code uses `BCEWithLogitsLoss(fake_Y, ones)` for $G$.
- **DCGAN's all-convolutional architecture** ([[AlecRadford|Radford]], Metz & Chintala 2015) — discriminator = 4 strided convs (downsample by 2 each, ×2 channels each) terminating in a 1-channel logit; generator = 4 transposed convs (upsample by 2 each, ÷2 channels each) starting from a 1×1×100 noise tensor. **No FC layers anywhere** — the design principle that gave DCGAN its name and stability. ([[d2l-generative-adversarial-networks]] §`dcgan` "The Generator" and "Discriminator".)
- **Batch normalization is structural to DCGAN**: every G-block and every D-block (except the discriminator's input layer in Radford's original paper) interleaves [[BatchNormalization|BN]] between the convolution and the activation. BN stabilizes the dual-network training dynamics — without it, the architecture diverges or mode-collapses on natural images.
- **Leaky ReLU for $D$, ReLU for $G$**: D-blocks use [[LeakyReLU|Leaky ReLU]] ($\alpha=0.2$) so the discriminator's gradients flow even for the (initially abundant) negative pre-activations from misclassified fakes — fixing the "dying ReLU" pathology that would otherwise cripple $D$ early in training. G-blocks use standard ReLU.
- **Transposed convolution as the generator's upsampling primitive**: each $G$-block applies a $4\times4$ kernel with stride 2 and padding 1 (a "fractionally-strided convolution"); for the first block, kernel $4\times4$ stride $1$ padding $0$ inflates the $1\times1$ noise to a $4\times4$ feature map. The output-shape formula is $n'_h = k_h + s_h(n_h-1) - 2p_h$ — making this layer the canonical inverse of the spatial-downsampling convolution.
- **Pokémon dataset** (image preprocessing): resize to $64\times64$, then `Normalize(0.5, 0.5)` to map pixels to $[-1, 1]$ so they match the generator's $\tanh$ output range. This $[-1, 1]$ normalization is structural — without it, the BCE training signal is biased because $G$ is constrained to a different value range than $D$ expects.
- **Adam's $\beta_1 = 0.5$ override**: standard Adam uses $\beta_1 = 0.9$. DCGAN drops it to $0.5$ — *"it decreases the smoothness of the momentum, the exponentially weighted moving average of past gradients, to take care of the rapid changing gradients because the generator and the discriminator fight with each other."* This is the single most-cited hyperparameter tweak in the GAN literature.
- **Why "fractionally strided"**: in regular conv, stride $s>1$ downsamples by $s$; in transposed conv, the same stride parameter *upsamples* by $s$ — equivalent to running a regular conv at stride $1/s$ over a "stretched" input. The unifying matrix view: regular conv is $\mathbf y = \mathbf W\mathbf x$, transposed conv is $\mathbf x' = \mathbf W^\top\mathbf y'$ — they share the kernel weights but transpose the spatial-mapping matrix.

## Key Quotes

> "In 2014, a breakthrough paper introduced Generative adversarial networks (GANs), a clever new way to leverage the power of discriminative models to get good generative models. At their heart, GANs rely on the idea that a data generator is good if we cannot tell fake data apart from real data. In statistics, this is called a two-sample test - a test to answer the question whether datasets X and X' were drawn from the same distribution." — §`gan`, the foundational framing.

> "To sum up, D and G are playing a 'minimax' game with the comprehensive objective function: $\min_D \max_G \{-E_{x\sim\textrm{Data}}\log D(\mathbf x) - E_{z\sim\textrm{Noise}}\log(1 - D(G(\mathbf z)))\}$." — §`gan`, the canonical GAN objective.

> "If the generator does a perfect job, then $D(\mathbf x')\approx 1$, so the above loss is near 0, which results in the gradients that are too small to make good progress... So commonly, we minimize the following loss: $\min_G\{-\log(D(G(\mathbf z)))\}$, which is just feeding $\mathbf x'=G(\mathbf z)$ into the discriminator but giving label $y=1$." — §`gan`, the non-saturating loss reformulation.

> "Leaky ReLU is a nonlinear function that give a non-zero output for a negative input. It aims to fix the 'dying ReLU' problem that a neuron might always output a negative value and therefore cannot make any progress since the gradient of ReLU is 0." — §`dcgan`, the architectural motivation for [[LeakyReLU]] in the discriminator.

> "Compared to the basic GAN in `sec_basic_gan`, we use the same learning rate for both generator and discriminator since they are similar to each other. In addition, we change $\beta_1$ in Adam from $0.9$ to $0.5$. It decreases the smoothness of the momentum... to take care of the rapid changing gradients because the generator and the discriminator fight with each other." — §`dcgan`, the canonical Adam tweak.

D2L acknowledges this in the chapter summary ("The Discriminator is a 4-layer strided convolutions with batch normalization (except its input layer)") but the demo code is uniform.
