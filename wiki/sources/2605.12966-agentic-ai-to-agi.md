## Summary
Liao, Li, Wen, Wang & Zhang ([[ShanghaiJiaoTongUniversity]] + [[ShanghaiInnovationInstitute]] + [[universitycollegelondon|UniversityCollegeLondon]], ICML 2026 position paper) challenge the dogma that monolithic scaling alone reaches AGI. They formalize real-world tasks as the **[[StructuredRealWorldDistribution]]** — a union of $K$ low-dimensional Riemannian manifolds $\{\mathcal{M}_k\}_{k=1}^K$ with intrinsic dimensions $d_k \ll D$ — and prove three theoretical results: (1) a monolithic learner pays an irreducible **[[AverageTrap]]** penalty $\epsilon = \sum_k \frac{\alpha_k}{2}\|\theta^*_{\text{mono}}-\theta^*_k\|^2_{H_k}$ from conflicting per-task gradients; (2) a merely **[[RoutingBasedAgenticAI|routing-based Agentic AI]]** achieves error $\mathcal{E}_{\text{R-Agentic}} \approx \mathcal{O}(K \cdot N^{-1/d_{\max}})$ vs the monolith's $\mathcal{O}(N^{-1/D})$, an *exponential* sample-efficiency gain since $d_{\max} \ll D$; (3) general **[[AgenticAI]]** generalizes routing to topologically-sorted DAGs $\Psi = (\mathcal{G},\mathcal{F},\Lambda)$ whose generalization is jointly governed by the **[[CompositionalCapacity]]** $C(\mathcal{G}) = \sum_u \omega_u$ (a sum of per-agent **[[TopologicalWeight|Topological Weights]]**) and the effective intrinsic dimension $d_{\text{eff}}$. They reinterpret [[MixtureOfExperts]] as a single-layer routing instance and argue current multi-agent failures stem from poor topology, not the paradigm itself.

## Key Claims
- **No-Free-Lunch + structured manifolds.** Without priors, no learner beats average; real tasks however obey the [[StructuredRealWorldDistribution]] (Def. 2.1): $\text{supp}(P(x)) \subseteq \bigcup_k \mathcal{M}_k$ with task-specific labelers $f_k$. This converts the learning problem from probabilistic-mixture to piecewise-smooth manifold learning.
- **Minimax curse-of-dimensionality lower bound (Prop. 2.2, after Stone 1982).** For $L$-Lipschitz functions on a compact $\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^D$, the minimax risk is $\geq C \cdot N^{-\frac{1}{2+D}}$ — exponential in the ambient dimension $D$.
- **Natarajan-dimension upper bounds for routers ([[NatarajanDimension]], Theorems 2.3–2.5, Jin 2023, Daniely 2011).** Tree-based router: $d_N \leq \mathcal{O}(L \cdot 2^L \log(pd))$; neural router: $d_N \leq \mathcal{O}(d \cdot p^2)$. Yields explicit $\epsilon_\pi$ scaling: $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\sqrt{\log K / N_{\text{router}}})$ for trees, $\sqrt{K/N_{\text{router}}}$ for neural — *trees scale polylog in $K$, neural routers scale $\sqrt{K}$*.
- **The Average Trap (Prop. 3.3).** Under twice-differentiable per-task losses with positive-definite Hessian $H_k$ at $\theta^*_k$ (Assumption 3.1) and $\rho$-Lipschitz Hessians (Assumption 3.2), heterogeneous tasks impose $\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}}(\theta^*_{\text{mono}}) \approx \mathcal{L}_{\text{ideal}} + \sum_k \frac{\alpha_k}{2}\|\theta^*_{\text{mono}}-\theta^*_k\|^2_{H_k}$. The "Generalist's Penalty" is irreducible: a monolith must trade peak acuity for stability across conflicting manifolds.
- **Exponential routing advantage.** $\mathcal{E}_{\text{R-Agentic}}/\mathcal{E}_{\text{mono}} \approx K \cdot N^{1/D - 1/d_{\max}}$ → 0 as $N\to\infty$; data-requirement ratio $N_{\text{R-Agentic}}/N_{\text{mono}} \propto K^{d_{\max}} \epsilon^{D-d_{\max}}$ — for small $\epsilon$ and $D-d_{\max}$ large, the routing AI needs *exponentially fewer* samples.
- **U-shaped error in $K$ (Joint Bound).** Specialization gain $\mathcal{O}(N^{-1/d_{\max}})$ decreases with $K$; routing cost $\Delta_{\max}(K)\cdot\epsilon_\pi(K)$ grows with $K$. An **optimal $K^*$** solves $\partial \mathcal{E}_{\text{total}}/\partial K = 0$. Tree-based routers favor large $K^* \gg 1$ (polylog cost); neural routers cap $K^*$ unless $N_{\text{router}}\propto K$ (linear penalty $\sqrt{K}$).
- **Manifold Alignment with Orthogonal Subspaces (Assumption 3.4) + Mismatch Penalty (Lemma 3.5).** When task subspaces $S_k$ are orthogonal, mis-routing collapses information preservation $\rho(j,k) = \|P_k x\|^2/\|x\|^2 \to 0$. Maximum mismatch $\Delta_{\max}(K) \approx L_{\max}(1-1/\sqrt{K})$ saturates with $K$. Remark B.7 shows graceful degradation under partial subspace overlap parameter $\gamma$: the exponential advantage survives for any $\gamma < 1$.
- **General Agentic AI as topological DAG (Def. 4.1).** $\Psi = (\mathcal{G}, \mathcal{F}, \Lambda)$: a topologically-sorted DAG of $K$ heterogeneous learnable mappings $f_i$ composed through $\Lambda$ along parent-set inputs. Bernoulli success encoding $S_i$ yields additive loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{Agentic}}(\theta) = \sum_i \ell_i(\theta_i)$.
- **Topological Weight (Lemma 4.2).** $\omega_u = \|\sum_{v\in\text{Sinks}} \frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial x_v} \sum_{\rho\in\text{Paths}(u\to v)}\prod_{(a,b)\in\rho} J_{ba}\|$ — gradient flow aggregated along all paths from agent $u$ to all sinks. Equivalently $\boldsymbol\omega = \mathbf{M}^\top \mathbf{g}$ with $\mathbf{M} = (\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{J})^{-1} = \sum_k \mathbf{J}^k$ (Neumann series; $\mathbf{J}$ nilpotent because the graph is acyclic).
- **Agentic AI Convergence Superiority (Thm 4.3).** $\mathcal{E}_{\text{Agentic}} \approx C(\mathcal{G}) \cdot \mathcal{O}((N/K)^{-1/d_{\text{eff}}})$ — $d_{\text{eff}}$ governs convergence rate, $C(\mathcal{G})$ governs error magnitude. Agentic AI succeeds when topology *minimizes $C(\mathcal{G})$ while maximizing the dimensionality gap* $D - d_{\text{eff}}$.
- **Topological Edge Weight (Lemma 4.4).** For edge $e^* = (u,v)$: $\mathcal{W}(e^*) = (\underbrace{1+\sum_{k\in\mathcal{P}(u)}\|\dots\|}_{\text{Upstream History}}) \cdot \underbrace{\|J_{e^*}\|}_{\text{Local Valve}} \cdot \underbrace{\omega_v}_{\text{Downstream Future}}$. **Design principle:** edges after long chains (high Upstream History) must be **contractive** ($\|J_{e^*}\|<1$) — i.e., critic/judge edges; edges before critical decisions (high Downstream Sensitivity) need $\|J_{e^*}\|\ll 1$ — i.e., voting/verification edges collapsing multiple paths into a stable signal. Edges are *active variational filters*, not passive pipes.
- **MoE as a special case.** Mixture-of-Experts (Shazeer 2017; Switch Transformer, Fedus 2022) corresponds to the routing regime of §3.2 with $C(\mathcal{G})\approx\sum L_u$ and is therefore inherently stable. Agentic AI generalizes MoE in three axes: scope (fixed sub-networks per forward pass → autonomous multi-step agents with independent parameters), topology (single-layer router→expert → arbitrary DAG), and routing mechanism (differentiable gating end-to-end → autonomous routing with iterative refinement, external tools, dynamic retrieval).
- **Multi-agent failures are topological, not fundamental.** Pan et al. 2025 and Zhang et al. 2025 attribute failures to organizational entropy, inter-agent misalignment, verification gaps; the paper argues these are pathological Topological Weight / Edge Weight signatures, not flaws in the paradigm. Cites [[Anthropic]]'s multi-agent research system (2025) as a topology-aware exemplar.
- **Call to action.** Prioritize Agentic AI for *accessible* AGI research (small specialists beat brute-force monoliths at fraction of compute); invent automated discovery of optimal DAG topologies, agent-count scaling, and topology-aware evaluation that attributes failures to specific graph components.

## Contradictions
- **Direct theoretical antagonist to pure-scaling readings of [[2001.08361-scaling-laws]] and Hoffmann et al. 2022 ([[chinchillascalinglaws]]).** The paper accepts the power laws as empirical observations but argues their marginal returns are *diminishing precisely because* the monolithic learner is fighting the Average Trap. Reed et al. 2022 and Agüera y Arcas & Norvig 2023 are cited in the "Alternative Views" section as proponents of the monolithic-scaling-suffices position and explicitly rebutted.
- **Tension with [[2605.02396-heavyskill]].** HEAVYSKILL argues to *internalize* agentic harness gains as a single model's inner skill via RLVR. This paper formalizes why that direction sacrifices the $\mathcal{O}(N^{-1/d_{\max}})$ vs $\mathcal{O}(N^{-1/D})$ exponential gap. Reconciliation: HEAVYSKILL collapses a low-$K$ harness into a high-capacity monolith, which is locally viable when the task family is narrow (small Average Trap), but degrades as task diversity grows.
- **Aligns with [[2402.01817-llm-modulo]] on capability bounds but differs in remedy.** Kambhampati argues the LLM is a [[System1And2|System 1]] retriever and must be paired with external sound critics. This paper argues the LLM is a high-$D$ monolith that must be *decomposed* into routed specialists. The two critiques are largely orthogonal — the LLM-Modulo critic bank itself instantiates an [[AgenticAI]] DAG (LLM-as-generator + VAL-as-verifier) under Def. 4.1, and the contractive-edge prescription from Lemma 4.4 is precisely what makes such hybrid architectures stable.
- **Reframes [[2605.03310-coordination-architectural-layer]] and [[2604.22446-onemancompany]] as topology-engineering rather than coordination-engineering.** The architectural-layer literature studies what configurations of authority/aggregation/termination work empirically; this paper supplies the gradient-flow accounting (Topological Edge Weight) that should govern those choices. Implication: pre-deployment failure prediction in [[2605.03310-coordination-architectural-layer]] could in principle be derived analytically from $C(\mathcal{G})$ and $\mathcal{W}(e^*)$.
- **Empirical caveat (Remark B.6).** The router-sample limit $N_{\text{router}}$ assumed in §3.2 may be effectively infinite in agentic settings (continuous environment interaction), which *relaxes* the neural-router penalty and may flip the tree-vs-neural router preference in data-rich regimes.
