## Summary
Chapter 3 of OpenStax *Chemistry 2e* develops the quantitative description of composition for both pure substances and mixtures. It introduces formula/molecular mass, the mole and Avogadro's number, and molar mass as the bridge between mass and number of particles; uses these to compute percent composition and to derive empirical and molecular formulas; and defines molarity, the dilution relationship C₁V₁ = C₂V₂, and alternative concentration units (mass %, volume %, mass/volume %, ppm, ppb).

## Key Claims
- Formula mass is the sum of the average atomic masses of all atoms in a chemical formula; for covalent species it is called molecular mass, and the same arithmetic applies to ionic compounds (which have no discrete molecule).
- One mole is exactly 6.02214076 × 10²³ entities (Avogadro's number, Nₐ ≈ 6.022 × 10²³ mol⁻¹); the molar mass in g/mol is numerically equal to the atomic or formula mass in amu.
- Mass, moles, and number of particles interconvert via molar mass (g ↔ mol) and Avogadro's number (mol ↔ particles).
- Percent composition (mass fraction of each element × 100%) plus molar masses yields the empirical formula (assume a 100 g sample → moles → divide by smallest → scale to whole numbers); the molecular formula is the empirical formula times n = molar mass ÷ empirical-formula mass.
- Molarity M = mol solute ÷ L solution; diluting a solution conserves solute, giving C₁V₁ = C₂V₂.
- For trace or specialized concentrations, mass %, volume %, mass/volume %, ppm (×10⁶) and ppb (×10⁹) are used, with 1 ppm = 10³ ppb.

## Key Quotes
> "The mole is an amount unit similar to familiar units like pair, dozen, gross, etc." — defining the mole as a counting unit
> "The molar mass of an element (or compound) is the mass in grams of 1 mole of that substance." — definition of molar mass (g/mol)
> "mass percentage = mass of component / mass of solution × 100%" — mass-percent concentration
