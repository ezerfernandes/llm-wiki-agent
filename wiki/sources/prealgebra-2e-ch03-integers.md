## Summary
Chapter 3 of OpenStax [[Prealgebra]] 2e ([[OpenStax]]) extends arithmetic from the [[WholeNumbers|whole numbers]] to the **[[Integer|integers]]** — the counting numbers, their opposites, and zero. It introduces the [[NumberLine|number line]] as the organizing picture for signed numbers, defines **opposites** and **[[AbsoluteValue|absolute value]]**, and then builds the four arithmetic operations on signed numbers ([[SignedNumberArithmetic]]) using a concrete two-color counter model before stating the general sign rules. The chapter closes by adding the **[[DivisionPropertyOfEquality|Division Property of Equality]]** to the equation-solving toolkit so that equations of the form `ax = b` can be solved over the integers.

## Sections
- **3.1 Introduction to Integers** — negative numbers; integers as counting numbers, opposites, and zero; the number line; opposites and the three meanings of the "−" sign; absolute value; ordering with `<` and `>`.
- **3.2 Add Integers** — adding signed numbers with the blue/red counter (neutral-pair) model; same-signs and different-signs rules; evaluating and translating expressions.
- **3.3 Subtract Integers** — subtraction as "add the opposite": `a − b = a + (−b)` and `a − (−b) = a + b`; counter model with neutral pairs.
- **3.4 Multiply and Divide Integers** — sign rules for products and quotients; multiplication/division by −1 gives the opposite; exponents of negatives; order of operations with integers.
- **3.5 Solve Equations Using Integers; The Division Property of Equality** — checking solutions; the Addition, Subtraction, and **Division** Properties of Equality; envelope-and-counter model; translating word problems.
- **3 Key Concepts** — chapter summary of all definitions, properties, and rules.

## Key Concepts & Definitions
- **Negative number** — a value less than zero, written with a leading minus sign (e.g. `−1°F` is one degree below zero).
- **[[Integer]]** — the counting numbers, their opposites, and zero: `… −3, −2, −1, 0, 1, 2, 3 …`. Zero is neither positive nor negative.
- **Opposite** — the number the same distance from 0 on the [[NumberLine|number line]] but on the other side; `−a` is read "the opposite of `a`," and `−(−n) = n`.
- **Three meanings of "−"** — (1) subtraction between numbers (`10 − 4`); (2) a negative sign before a number (`−8`, "negative eight"); (3) "the opposite of" before a number or variable (`−x`).
- **[[AbsoluteValue|Absolute value]]** — `|n|` is the distance of `n` from 0 on the number line; `|n| ≥ 0` for every number.
- **Ordering** — on the number line, values increase left-to-right; `a < b` when `a` is left of `b`, `a > b` when `a` is right of `b`.
- **Neutral pair** — one positive (blue) counter and one negative (red) counter; together they model `+1 + (−1) = 0`.
- **Solution of an equation** — a value of the [[Variable|variable]] that makes the equation a true statement when substituted in (see [[Equation]]).

## Key Procedures / Rules
- **Add integers** — same signs: add absolute values, keep the common sign; different signs: subtract absolute values, take the sign of the larger absolute value. (See [[SignedNumberArithmetic]].)
- **Subtract integers** — subtract by adding the opposite: `a − b = a + (−b)` and `a − (−b) = a + b`.
- **Multiply / divide integers** — same signs → positive result; different signs → negative result. Multiplication and division by −1 give the opposite: `−1·a = −a` and `a ÷ (−1) = −a`.
- **Exponents on negatives** — `(−2)⁴ = 16` (base is `−2`) versus `−2⁴ = −16` (opposite of `2⁴`); the parentheses matter.
- **Order of operations** with integers follows the standard sequence (see [[OrderOfOperations]]).
- **Properties of Equality** — Addition: if `a = b` then `a + c = b + c`; Subtraction: if `a = b` then `a − c = b − c`; **Division**: if `a = b` then `a/c = b/c` for `c ≠ 0` (see [[DivisionPropertyOfEquality]]).
- **Check a solution** — substitute the value, simplify both sides, and confirm the equation is true.
- **Word problems** — identify what to find, write a phrase, translate to an expression/equation, simplify, and answer in a complete sentence.
