## Summary
Chapter 33 of OpenStax College Physics 2e surveys particle physics: the search for truly fundamental particles, the four fundamental forces and their carrier particles, the accelerators and detectors that create and study new matter, the classification of particles (leptons, hadrons), the quark model with color charge, the Standard Model, and the drive toward Grand Unified Theories. It builds on quantum, atomic, and nuclear physics (Chs. 29–32) and frames matter as a nested hierarchy ending (so far) in quarks and leptons.

## Key Claims
- Forces are transmitted by **carrier particles** (gauge bosons) exchanged as **virtual particles**, whose brief existence is permitted by the Heisenberg uncertainty principle (ΔE·Δt ≥ h/4π); carrier mass sets the force range.
- The four forces are gravity, electromagnetic, weak, and strong, with relative strengths ~10⁻³⁸, 10⁻², 10⁻¹³, 1; carriers are the graviton, photon, W/Z bosons, and gluons.
- Yukawa proposed the pion (a meson, ~100 MeV/c² estimated, ~135–140 measured) as the carrier of the residual strong force between nucleons.
- Accelerators (cyclotron, synchrotron, linac) and colliders turn kinetic energy into new matter via m = ΔE/c²; detectors read out the resulting particle spray.
- Particles split into **leptons** (no strong force; six in three families) and **hadrons** (feel the strong force), the latter into **baryons** (3 quarks) and **mesons** (quark–antiquark).
- **Antimatter**: every particle has an antiparticle of identical mass but opposite charge; they annihilate to energy. Predicted by Dirac (1928), positron found 1932.
- Allowed reactions obey conservation of charge, **baryon number**, **lepton family numbers**, and **strangeness** (strong-conserved, weak-violated).
- **Quarks** (Gell-Mann & Zweig, 1963) come in six flavors with fractional charge and **color charge**; they are confined, so only colorless combinations are observed. QCD describes their gluon interactions.
- The **Standard Model** combines QCD with **electroweak theory** (EM + weak unified above ~100 GeV; W ≈ 81, Z⁰ ≈ 90 GeV/c², confirmed at CERN 1983); the Higgs (~125 GeV) underlies symmetry breaking.
- **GUTs** seek to merge the strong with the electroweak force at ~10¹⁵ GeV (TOE adds gravity at ~10¹⁹ GeV); they predict rare proton decay (p → π⁰ + e⁺) not yet observed.

## Key Quotes (paraphrased)
> The more energy supplied in a collision, the more mass can be created, because m = ΔE/c².
> The electromagnetic and weak forces become one and the same at sufficiently high energies.
> Quarks cannot be found in isolation; only colorless combinations are observable.
