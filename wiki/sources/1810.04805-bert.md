## Summary
Devlin, Chang, Lee & Toutanova ([[Google]] AI Language) introduce **BERT** — Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers — a pre-trained Transformer encoder that conditions on left **and** right context at every layer. Two unsupervised pre-training objectives — a **Masked Language Model** (MLM, after the Cloze task of Taylor 1953) and **Next Sentence Prediction** (NSP) — replace the unidirectional next-token objective used by ELMo and OpenAI GPT, enabling a single pre-trained model to be fine-tuned with just one extra output layer for diverse downstream tasks. BERT advances the state of the art on 11 NLP benchmarks, including GLUE (80.5, +7.7 points absolute), MultiNLI (86.7, +4.6), SQuAD v1.1 F1 (93.2, +1.5) and SQuAD v2.0 F1 (83.1, +5.1). Together with [[1706.03762-attention-is-all-you-need]] it is the second load-bearing paper underlying every modern LLM.

## Key Claims
- **Deep bidirectionality is the major lever.** Ablations isolate bidirectional pre-training from training data, scheme and hyperparameters; "LTR & No NSP" (a GPT-style left-to-right baseline trained on BERT's data and scheme) is strictly worse on every GLUE/SQuAD task. Adding a randomly-initialized BiLSTM on top of an LTR model recovers some of the gap on SQuAD but hurts GLUE — bidirectionality must be in the pre-trained representations, not bolted on at fine-tune time.
- **Masked LM beats left-to-right LM.** Standard conditional LMs can only train left-to-right or right-to-left because bidirectional conditioning would let each token "see itself" in a multi-layer context. Randomly masking 15% of WordPiece tokens (80% `[MASK]` / 10% random / 10% unchanged, to mitigate the pre-train/fine-tune mismatch where `[MASK]` never appears) lets a deep bidirectional Transformer be pre-trained. MLM converges marginally slower than LTR but begins outperforming "almost immediately".
- **Next Sentence Prediction helps QA and NLI.** Binarized task — given sentence A, is B the actual next sentence or a random one — yields 97–98% accuracy and gives meaningful gains on QNLI / MNLI / SQuAD (≥0.5–1.5 F1 each).
- **One unified architecture across tasks.** Apart from output layers, the same architecture is used for sentence-pair classification, single-sentence classification, span prediction (SQuAD) and sequence tagging (CoNLL NER). All parameters are fine-tuned end-to-end with a task-specific output head, plus the `[CLS]` token's final hidden state used as the aggregate sequence representation.
- **Fine-tuning beats feature-based use for most tasks, but feature-based is competitive.** On CoNLL-2003 NER, concatenating the last four hidden layers as fixed features and feeding them to a BiLSTM is only 0.3 F1 behind full fine-tuning — useful when downstream tasks need non-Transformer architectures or pre-computed embeddings.
- **Model size matters even for tiny downstream tasks.** Scaling from BERT_BASE (L=12, H=768, A=12, 110M params) to BERT_LARGE (L=24, H=1024, A=16, 340M params) yields strict accuracy improvements on all four GLUE tasks studied, including MRPC (3.6k examples) — the first convincing demonstration that extreme model size also helps very small downstream tasks, provided the model has been sufficiently pre-trained.
- **Architecture-level near-identity with GPT.** BERT_BASE was chosen to match OpenAI GPT's parameter count; the substantive differences are (1) bidirectional vs left-only self-attention, (2) BooksCorpus + Wikipedia vs BooksCorpus alone, (3) `[SEP]` / `[CLS]` / segment embeddings learned during pre-training, (4) larger batch (128k vs 32k tokens), (5) task-specific fine-tuning learning rate. Ablations attribute the majority of the gain to (1) plus the two pre-training tasks.

## Key Quotes
> "BERT is designed to pre-train deep bidirectional representations from unlabeled text by jointly conditioning on both left and right context in all layers."

> "Standard conditional language models can only be trained left-to-right or right-to-left, since bidirectional conditioning would allow each word to indirectly 'see itself,' and the model could trivially predict the target word in a multi-layered context."

> "Unfortunately, standard conditional language models can only be trained left-to-right or right-to-left… To train a deep bidirectional representation, we simply mask some percentage of the input tokens at random, and then predict those masked tokens."

> "We believe that this is the first work to demonstrate convincingly that scaling to extreme model sizes also leads to large improvements on very small scale tasks, provided that the model has been sufficiently pre-trained."

## Architecture Reference (constants)
- **BERT_BASE**: L=12 Transformer encoder layers, H=768 hidden, A=12 heads, FFN=4H=3072, 110M params.
- **BERT_LARGE**: L=24, H=1024, A=16, FFN=4096, 340M params.
- **Tokenizer**: [[WordPiece]] with 30,000 vocab; `[CLS]` prepended, `[SEP]` separates segments; learned segment-A/segment-B embeddings; learned positional embeddings (max 512).
- **Pre-training corpus**: BooksCorpus (800M words) + English Wikipedia (2,500M words, text-only). Critical to use document-level not shuffled-sentence corpus to learn long contiguous sequences.
- **Pre-training schedule**: batch 256 × 512 = 128k tokens/batch, 1,000,000 steps (≈40 epochs over 3.3B words). Adam (lr=1e-4, β₁=0.9, β₂=0.999, L2=0.01, warmup 10k, linear decay), dropout 0.1, GELU activation. 90% of steps at seq-len 128 then 10% at 512 to learn positional embeddings.
- **Hardware**: BERT_BASE — 4 Cloud TPUs (16 chips), 4 days; BERT_LARGE — 16 Cloud TPUs (64 chips), 4 days.
- **Fine-tune ranges**: batch ∈ {16, 32}, LR (Adam) ∈ {5e-5, 4e-5, 3e-5, 2e-5}, epochs ∈ {2, 3, 4}; dropout fixed at 0.1.

## Results Reference (headline)
- **GLUE Test (avg)**: BERT_LARGE 82.1, BERT_BASE 79.6, OpenAI GPT 75.1, prior SOTA 74.0.
- **MultiNLI (m/mm)**: BERT_LARGE 86.7/85.9 vs prior 82.1/81.4.
- **SQuAD v1.1 Test EM/F1**: single-model 85.1/91.8, ensemble + TriviaQA 87.4/93.2.
- **SQuAD v2.0 Test EM/F1**: 80.0/83.1 (+5.1 F1 over prior best).
- **SWAG Test**: 86.3 (+27.1 over ESIM+ELMo, +8.3 over GPT; human-expert baseline 85.0).
- **CoNLL-2003 NER Test F1**: 92.8 fine-tuned, 92.4 feature-based (last 4 layers concat to BiLSTM).

- [[Transformer]] — the underlying architecture; BERT is "a multi-layer bidirectional Transformer encoder".

- [[MaskedLanguageModel]] — the central pre-training objective introduced (or popularized) here.
- [[NextSentencePrediction]] — the auxiliary pre-training objective for sentence-pair tasks.

## Contradictions
None. This paper predates every 2026 source in the LLM corpus by ~8 years and supplies (with [[1706.03762-attention-is-all-you-need]]) the architectural and training-recipe substrate they all build on.
