## Summary
FAIR at Meta proposes **Self-Improving Pretraining**: replace next-token prediction with prefix-conditioned suffix generation trained by RL, using an existing strong post-trained model as both **rewriter** (producing better suffixes) and **judge** (scoring rollouts) so safety, factuality and quality are baked in early instead of patched after the fact. They also introduce **thinking mid-training**, an intermediate stage that augments pretraining data with interleaved reasoning traces optimized via SFT + RL with a judge.

## Key Claims
- **Pretrain stage is the right place for safety/factuality** — patterns acquired in pretraining largely dictate downstream behavior; layered post-training cannot fully overwrite them.
- **Sequence-level objective replaces next-token** — policy generates a length-N suffix from the prefix; rewards come from a judge comparing rollouts to the original suffix and to a rewritten suffix.
- **Two roles for the teacher** — rewriter (improves suffix quality / safety, augments diversity), judge (per-axis scoring of rollouts/suffix/rewrite).
- **Empirical gains** — 36.2% relative factuality improvement, 18.5% safety, up to 86.3% win rate on overall quality vs standard continual pretraining; 31.1% absolute quality win-rate gain from-scratch.
- **Thinking mid-training** — interleaving reasoning traces during the mid-train stage improves reasoning benchmarks via SFT+RL with a judge.

## Key Quotes
> "Many desirable behaviors such as safety, factuality, overall generation quality, and reasoning ability are only added at a late stage, even though the patterns learned earlier strongly shape a model's capabilities."
> "Leveraging stronger models to shape pretraining data and objectives is a promising direction for building more capable and reliable language models."
