## Summary
OpenStax *Chemistry 2e* Chapter 9 develops the macroscopic behavior of gases and its microscopic explanation. It introduces pressure and its units, the simple empirical gas laws (Boyle's, Charles's, Avogadro's, Amontons's/Gay-Lussac's) and their unification into the ideal gas law PV = nRT, then extends the law to gas density, molar mass, mixtures (Dalton's law of partial pressures, mole fraction), and reaction stoichiometry. The kinetic-molecular theory provides a five-postulate model that explains every gas law and yields the root-mean-square speed and Graham's law of effusion/diffusion. The chapter closes with the limits of the ideal model — non-ideal behavior, the compressibility factor Z, and the van der Waals equation.

## Key Claims
- Pressure is force per unit area, P = F/A; standard atmospheric pressure equalities are 1 atm = 760 mmHg = 760 torr = 101,325 Pa = 1.01325 bar ≈ 14.7 psi. Barometers and manometers measure pressure via hydrostatic columns (p = hρg).
- The four empirical gas laws — Boyle's (PV = k at constant T, n), Charles's (V ∝ T at constant P, n), Avogadro's (V ∝ n at constant T, P), and Amontons's/Gay-Lussac's (P ∝ T at constant V, n) — combine into the ideal gas law PV = nRT, with R = 0.08206 L·atm/(mol·K) = 8.314 J/(mol·K).
- At STP (273.15 K, 1 atm) one mole of an ideal gas occupies a standard molar volume of ≈ 22.4 L (≈ 22.71 L at the IUPAC 1-bar standard), independent of chemical identity.
- Gas density gives molar mass via d = ℳP/RT and ℳ = mRT/PV. Dalton's law states the total pressure of a non-reacting mixture is the sum of partial pressures, with P_A = X_A · P_Total; gases collected over water must be corrected for water's vapor pressure.
- The kinetic-molecular theory rests on five postulates (continuous random straight-line motion, negligible molecular size, pressure from elastic wall collisions, no intermolecular forces, average KE ∝ kelvin temperature) and yields KE_avg = (3/2)RT and u_rms = √(3RT/ℳ).
- Graham's law: rate of effusion ∝ 1/√ℳ, so rate_A/rate_B = √(ℳ_B/ℳ_A); this underlies uranium enrichment via gaseous diffusion of UF₆.
- Real gases deviate from ideal behavior (measured by the compressibility factor Z = PV_m/RT; Z = 1 is ideal) most at high pressure and low temperature, because molecules have finite volume and exert intermolecular attractions; the van der Waals equation (P + an²/V²)(V − nb) = nRT corrects for both.

## Key Quotes
> "The total pressure of a mixture of ideal gases is equal to the sum of the partial pressures of the component gases." — Dalton's law of partial pressures (§9.3)

> "The rate of effusion of a gas is inversely proportional to the square root of its molar mass." — Graham's law (§9.4)

> "The average kinetic energy of the gas molecules is proportional to the kelvin temperature of the gas." — fifth postulate of the kinetic-molecular theory (§9.5)

> "When V is relatively large and n is relatively small, both of these correction terms become negligible, and the van der Waals equation reduces to the ideal gas law, PV = nRT." — §9.6
