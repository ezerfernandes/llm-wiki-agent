## Summary
Chapter 34 of OpenStax College Physics 2e surveys the open frontiers of modern physics: cosmology and the Big Bang, general relativity and quantum gravity, superstring theory, dark matter and cosmic closure, complexity and chaos, and high-temperature superconductors. It connects the very large (the expanding universe) with the very small (particle physics and force unification), emphasizing how each answer raises new questions. The chapter closes by enumerating the major unresolved questions physicists currently know how to ask.

## Key Claims
- Galaxies beyond the local cluster recede from us with velocity proportional to distance ([[HubblesLaw]]), implying an expanding universe born in a [[BigBang]] roughly 13–15 billion years ago.
- The cosmic microwave background radiation is a near-perfect 2.725 K blackbody remnant of the early hot universe, with ~200 µK fluctuations that seeded galaxy formation.
- An inflationary epoch of extremely rapid early expansion explains the observed smoothness of the CMBR.
- [[GeneralRelativity]] predicts that gravity bends light (confirmed by the 1919 solar eclipse) via spacetime curvature, generalizing [[SpecialRelativity]] through the equivalence principle.
- [[BlackHole]]s have an event horizon at the Schwarzschild radius R_S = 2GM/c²; they emit Hawking radiation through quantum pair production and were confirmed as merging sources by LIGO's 2015 [[GravitationalWave]] detection.
- [[QuantumGravity]] seeks to unify quantum mechanics and gravity; superstring theory ([[Superstring]]) models particles as vibrating one-dimensional strings at the ~10⁻³⁵ m Planck scale and is a candidate Theory of Everything.
- [[DarkMatter]] makes up ~90% of cosmic mass, inferred from flat galaxy rotation curves and gravitational lensing; candidates include MACHOs and WIMPs.
- Neutrino oscillations confirm that neutrinos have nonzero mass, contributing to the cosmic mass budget and the question of cosmic closure ([[DarkEnergy]] and the cosmological constant drive accelerating expansion).
- [[ChaosTheory]] (complexity and chaos) studies deterministic systems with extreme sensitivity to initial conditions that nonetheless self-organize into ordered, often fractal, structures.
- High-temperature [[Superconductivity]] (T_c up to 125 K, above liquid-nitrogen temperature) remains theoretically unexplained and is of major practical interest, though reports above 200 K remain irreproducible.
