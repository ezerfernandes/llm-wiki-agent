## Summary
Survey of the nested numerical systems \\(\mathbb{N} \subset \mathbb{Z} \subset \mathbb{Q} \subset \mathbb{R} \subset \mathbb{C}\\), with the irrationals \\(\mathbb{I}\\) as the complement of \\(\mathbb{Q}\\) in \\(\mathbb{R}\\), and the algebraic vs transcendental partition of \\(\mathbb{R}\\). Each extension is motivated by the limitation it removes (subtraction, division, completeness, polynomial closure).

## Key Claims
- \\(\mathbb{N}\\) is the smallest inductive subset of \\(\mathbb{R}\\); \\(\mathbb{Z}\\) makes subtraction total; \\(\mathbb{Q}\\) makes nonzero division total; \\(\mathbb{R}\\) is order-complete; \\(\mathbb{C}\\) is algebraically closed.
- A rational has terminating or eventually periodic decimal expansion; an irrational is non-terminating non-periodic.
- \\(\mathbb{R}\\) is uncountable (Cantor diagonal); \\(\mathbb{Q}\\) and the algebraic numbers \\(\mathbb{A}\\) are countable, so transcendentals are uncountable.
- \\(\mathbb{R}\\) is Archimedean and totally ordered; complex numbers \\(z=a+bi\\) admit no compatible total order.

## Key Quotes
> "The structure of these sets reflects how each extension resolves a limitation of the previous one."
> "Almost every real number is transcendental, even though explicit examples are comparatively rare."
