## Summary
Chapter 2 of OpenStax [[Prealgebra]] 2e (published by [[OpenStax]]) makes the transition from arithmetic (Ch 1, [[prealgebra-2e-ch01-whole-numbers]]) to algebra by introducing the **language** that algebra is written in. It defines [[Variable|variables]] and constants, the operation/equality/inequality symbols, exponential notation, and the **order of operations** (PEMDAS); distinguishes an [[AlgebraicExpression|expression]] from an [[Equation|equation]]; and teaches how to *evaluate* an expression (substitute a value and simplify), *simplify* it by combining **like terms**, and *translate* English word phrases into algebraic symbols. It then introduces the first equation-solving tools — the **Subtraction and Addition Properties of Equality** — for one-step equations of the form `x + a = b` and `x − a = b`. The chapter closes with number-theory groundwork for fractions: [[MultiplesAndFactors|multiples and factors]] (with divisibility tests), [[PrimeFactorization|prime factorization]] (factor-tree and ladder methods), and the [[LeastCommonMultiple|least common multiple]] (listing-multiples and prime-factors methods). A recurring theme is **translation** — turning words into expressions and equations — and the inverse relationship between operations used both in divisibility and in isolating a variable.

## Key Concepts & Definitions
- **Variable**: a letter (e.g. x, y, n) that represents a number whose value may change. **Constant**: a number whose value always stays the same.
- **Expression**: a number, a variable, or a combination of numbers, variables, and operation symbols (e.g. `3 + 5`, `x + 7`, `2x − 4`). **Equation**: two expressions connected by an equal sign (e.g. `x + 7 = 12`). An expression has *no* equals sign; an equation does.
- **Operation symbols**: addition `a + b` ("sum"), subtraction `a − b` ("difference"), multiplication `a · b`, `(a)(b)`, `a(b)`, `ab` ("product"), division `a ÷ b`, `a/b` ("quotient"). The `×` symbol is avoided in algebra to prevent confusion with the variable x.
- **Equality / inequality symbols**: `=` equal, `≠` not equal, `<` less than, `>` greater than, `≤` less than or equal, `≥` greater than or equal. The small side of `<`/`>` faces the smaller number.
- **Grouping symbols**: parentheses `( )`, brackets `[ ]`, braces `{ }`; innermost grouping is simplified first.
- **Exponential notation**: `aⁿ` means multiply `n` factors of `a`; `a` is the **base**, `n` is the **exponent**. `a²` is read "a squared," `a³` is "a cubed." (Distinct from the floating-point [[Exponent]] field; here it is whole-number repeated multiplication.)
- **Term**: a constant or the product of a constant and one or more variables (e.g. `7`, `y`, `5x²`, `13xy`). **Coefficient**: the constant factor in a term (the coefficient of `5x` is 5; the coefficient of `x` is 1).
- **Like terms**: terms that are constants, or have the *same variables raised to the same exponents* (e.g. `5x` and `3x`; `n²` and `9n²`). Only like terms may be combined.
- **Solution of an equation**: a value of the variable that makes the equation a true statement; finding it is **solving** the equation.
- **Multiple of n**: the product of a counting number and n. A number m is **divisible by** n when m is a multiple of n (the division has no remainder).
- **Factor**: in `a · b = m`, both `a` and `b` are factors of `m`, and `m` is their product.
- **Prime number**: a counting number greater than 1 whose only factors are 1 and itself. **Composite number**: a counting number greater than 1 that is not prime. **1 is neither prime nor composite.**
- **Prime factorization**: a composite number written as a product of prime numbers (unique up to order).
- **Least common multiple (LCM)**: the smallest number that is a multiple of two (or more) given numbers; used later to add and subtract fractions with unlike denominators.

## Key Procedures / Rules
- **Order of operations (PEMDAS)**: (1) simplify inside **P**arentheses/grouping symbols, innermost first; (2) simplify **E**xponents; (3) **M**ultiplication and **D**ivision left to right (equal priority — not all multiplication before division); (4) **A**ddition and **S**ubtraction left to right (equal priority).
- **Evaluate an expression**: substitute the given value for each variable, then simplify using the order of operations (note `x²` means `x · x`, not `2x`).
- **Combine like terms**: (1) identify like terms; (2) rearrange so like terms are adjacent (Commutative Property of Addition); (3) add their coefficients, keeping the variable part.
- **Translate word phrases**: *sum / plus / increased by / more than* → `+`; *difference / minus / decreased by / less than / subtracted from* → `−`; *product / times* → `×`; *quotient / divided by / ratio* → `÷`. The words "of" and "and" flag the quantities being combined. Note `5m + n` ("the sum of 5 times m and n") vs. `5(m + n)` ("5 times the sum of m and n").
- **Determine whether a number is a solution**: substitute it for the variable, simplify both sides, and check whether the statement is true.
- **Subtraction Property of Equality**: for any numbers `a, b, c`, if `a = b` then `a − c = b − c`. **Addition Property of Equality**: if `a = b` then `a + c = b + c`. Use these to isolate the variable (subtract to undo an addition: `x + a = b → x = b − a`; add to undo a subtraction: `x − a = b → x = b + a`), simplify, then check.
- **Translate a sentence into an equation**: locate the word(s) that mean "equals" (*is, is equal to, is the same as, gives, was, will be*), translate the phrase on each side into an expression, and join them with `=`.
- **Divisibility tests**: by **2** — last digit is 0/2/4/6/8; by **3** — digit sum divisible by 3; by **4** — last two digits form a number divisible by 4; by **5** — last digit 0 or 5; by **6** — divisible by both 2 and 3; by **10** — last digit 0.
- **Find all factors of a number**: divide it by each counting number in order (1, 2, 3, …); each whole-number quotient gives a factor pair (divisor, quotient); stop when the quotient becomes smaller than the divisor; list all factors from smallest to largest.
- **Determine whether a number is prime**: test each prime (2, 3, 5, 7, …) in order as a divisor; stop when a prime factor is found (→ composite) or the quotient drops below the divisor with none found (→ prime).
- **Prime factorization — factor-tree method**: write the number as any factor pair, branch downward, circle primes, keep factoring composite branches until every branch ends in a prime; the factorization is the product of all circled primes (least to greatest).
- **Prime factorization — ladder method**: divide by the smallest prime repeatedly, move up to the next prime when the current one no longer divides evenly, stop when the quotient is prime; the factorization is the product of the side primes and the final top quotient.
- **LCM — listing-multiples method**: list multiples of each number until a common one appears; the smallest common multiple is the LCM.
- **LCM — prime-factors method**: write each number's prime factorization, align matching primes in columns, bring down each prime the greatest number of times it appears in any one factorization, and multiply.
