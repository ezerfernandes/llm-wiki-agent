## Summary

[[AstonZhang|Zhang]], [[ZacharyLipton|Lipton]], [[MuLi|Li]] & [[AlexanderSmola|Smola]]'s eight-section *Modern RNN* chapter — the [[d2l-recurrent-neural-networks|prior chapter]]'s direct sequel, motivated entirely by the vanishing-gradient pathology of vanilla [[RNN|RNNs]]. Two foundational architectures fix the gradient-flow problem: **[[LSTM]]** ([[SeppHochreiter|Hochreiter]] & [[JurgenSchmidhuber|Schmidhuber]] 1997) introduces the *memory cell* with input / forget / output gates and a self-connected recurrent edge of weight 1; **[[GRU]]** ([[KyunghyunCho|Cho]] et al. 2014) streamlines this to a reset gate + update gate over a single hidden state. Two structural variants compose with both: **[[DeepRNN|deep RNN]]** stacking ($L$ layers, hidden states flow upward at each time step) and **[[BidirectionalRNN|bidirectional]] RNNs** ([[MikeSchuster|Schuster]] & [[KuldipPaliwal|Paliwal]] 1997) concatenating forward + backward unidirectional passes. The chapter then pivots to **[[MachineTranslation|machine translation]]** as the canonical [[SeqToSeq|seq2seq]] task: the **[[EncoderDecoder|encoder-decoder]]** template ([[KyunghyunCho|Cho]] et al. 2014; [[IlyaSutskever|Sutskever]], Vinyals & Le 2014) maps a variable-length source through an encoder RNN to a fixed-shape context $\mathbf{c}$ that conditions a decoder RNN trained with **[[TeacherForcing|teacher forcing]]**; **[[BLEU]]** ([[KishorePapineni|Papineni]] et al. 2002) is introduced as the modified-$n$-gram-precision-with-brevity-penalty evaluation metric; and **[[BeamSearch|beam search]]** with length-penalty $\frac{1}{L^\alpha}\sum_t \log P(y_t\mid \cdot)$ ($\alpha\!=\!0.75$) bridges greedy (cost $\mathcal{O}(|\mathcal{Y}|T')$, locally optimal) and exhaustive (cost $\mathcal{O}(|\mathcal{Y}|^{T'})$, globally optimal) decoding at $\mathcal{O}(k|\mathcal{Y}|T')$. The chapter is the textbook bridge from RNN-era sequence modeling to the [[transformer|Transformer]]: every architectural primitive needed to read [[1409.3215-seq2seq]] is established here; the next chapter introduces [[Attention|attention]] as the structural fix for the encoder's fixed-vector bottleneck.

## Key Claims

- **LSTM solves vanishing gradients via a memory cell with a self-connected weight-1 edge.** Each [[LSTM]] cell maintains an internal state $\mathbf{C}_t\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times h}$ whose recurrent edge has *fixed* weight 1 — gradients pass through this edge unchanged across many time steps. The cell update is $\mathbf{C}_t = \mathbf{F}_t\odot \mathbf{C}_{t-1} + \mathbf{I}_t\odot \tilde{\mathbf{C}}_t$: if forget gate $\mathbf{F}_t\!\to\!1$ and input gate $\mathbf{I}_t\!\to\!0$, the cell carries state unchanged indefinitely. "In practice, this design alleviates the vanishing gradient problem, resulting in models that are much easier to train, especially when facing datasets with long sequence lengths."
- **LSTM has three sigmoid gates and one tanh input node.** All four data-conditioned signals share the same form $\sigma$ or $\tanh$ of $\mathbf{X}_t\mathbf{W}_\textrm{x\cdot} + \mathbf{H}_{t-1}\mathbf{W}_\textrm{h\cdot} + \mathbf{b}_\cdot$: the **[[InputGate|input gate]]** $\mathbf{I}_t$ (sigmoid, gates how much new $\tilde{\mathbf{C}}_t$ enters), the **[[ForgetGate|forget gate]]** $\mathbf{F}_t$ (sigmoid, gates how much old $\mathbf{C}_{t-1}$ persists), the **[[OutputGate|output gate]]** $\mathbf{O}_t$ (sigmoid, gates how much of $\tanh(\mathbf{C}_t)$ is exposed as $\mathbf{H}_t$), and the **input node** $\tilde{\mathbf{C}}_t$ (tanh, the candidate cell update). Final hidden state: $\mathbf{H}_t = \mathbf{O}_t\odot\tanh(\mathbf{C}_t)\in(-1,1)$. The cell internal state $\mathbf{C}_t$ is *entirely internal*; only $\mathbf{H}_t$ feeds the output layer or the next layer of a stacked RNN.
- **The term "long short-term memory" names an intermediate storage tier.** D2L's pedagogical framing: weights are *long-term memory* (slow gradient updates encode general knowledge); activations / hidden states are *short-term memory* (ephemeral, pass to next node). The LSTM cell adds a *third* tier: a controllable memory that lasts longer than activations but is faster to update than weights — "long short-term." Per D2L: "LSTMs are the prototypical latent variable autoregressive model with nontrivial state control."
- **GRU is the streamlined LSTM: 3 gates → 2 gates, no separate cell state.** [[GRU|Gated Recurrent Units]] ([[KyunghyunCho|Cho]] et al. 2014; Chung, Gulcehre, Cho et al. 2014) merge LSTM's input + forget gates into a single **[[UpdateGate|update gate]]** $\mathbf{Z}_t$ ("how much of the new state is a copy of the old") and replace the output gate with a **[[ResetGate|reset gate]]** $\mathbf{R}_t$ ("how much of the previous state to remember"). Candidate hidden state: $\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_t = \tanh(\mathbf{X}_t\mathbf{W}_\textrm{xh} + (\mathbf{R}_t\odot \mathbf{H}_{t-1})\mathbf{W}_\textrm{hh} + \mathbf{b}_\textrm{h})$; final update: $\mathbf{H}_t = \mathbf{Z}_t\odot\mathbf{H}_{t-1} + (1-\mathbf{Z}_t)\odot\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_t$ — a *convex combination* of old and candidate. Mnemonic: "reset gates help capture short-term dependencies; update gates help capture long-term dependencies." Fewer parameters and matmuls than LSTM at comparable accuracy.
- **Deep RNNs stack RNNs in the depth direction; the recurrence is unchanged.** A [[DeepRNN|deep RNN]] with $L$ hidden layers feeds the previous layer's output at time $t$ as the input to the next layer at time $t$: $\mathbf{H}_t^{(l)} = \phi_l(\mathbf{H}_t^{(l-1)}\mathbf{W}_\textrm{xh}^{(l)} + \mathbf{H}_{t-1}^{(l)}\mathbf{W}_\textrm{hh}^{(l)} + \mathbf{b}_\textrm{h}^{(l)})$, with $\mathbf{H}_t^{(0)} = \mathbf{X}_t$. Output is computed only from the topmost layer's hidden state $\mathbf{H}_t^{(L)}$. "Common RNN layer widths ($h$) are in the range $(64, 2056)$, and common depths ($L$) are in the range $(1, 8)$." Stacking composes orthogonally with the gating choice: deep-gated RNNs (multilayer LSTM / GRU) are obtained by replacing the recurrence inside each layer.
- **Bidirectional RNNs concatenate a forward and a backward pass over the same input.** A [[BidirectionalRNN|bi-RNN]] ([[MikeSchuster|Schuster]] & [[KuldipPaliwal|Paliwal]] 1997) maintains a forward hidden state $\overrightarrow{\mathbf{H}}_t$ and a backward hidden state $\overleftarrow{\mathbf{H}}_t$ with *separate* parameter sets, concatenates them into $\mathbf{H}_t\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times 2h}$, and feeds this to the output layer (so $\mathbf{W}_\textrm{hq}\in\mathbb{R}^{2h\times q}$). Useful when the prediction at each time step is allowed to condition on both past and future context: part-of-speech tagging, sequence labeling, masked-token pretraining (anticipating BERT-style MLM). "Bidirectional RNNs are mostly useful for sequence encoding and the estimation of observations given bidirectional context. Bidirectional RNNs are very costly to train due to long gradient chains."
- **Bidirectional RNNs are inappropriate for next-token / autoregressive prediction at inference.** The backward pass requires the *entire* future of the sequence — which does not exist when the model is *generating* the future token by token. Bi-RNNs are appropriate for *encoder*-style usage (where the full input is known up front) but not for *decoder* / language-modeling usage. This is the structural reason bi-RNNs are common in encoders and absent from decoders, and the implicit motivation for [[CausalMask|causal masking]] in the [[Transformer|Transformer]] decoder.
- **Machine translation reframes sequence learning as variable-length-in → variable-length-out.** [[MachineTranslation]] is the canonical [[SeqToSeq|sequence-to-sequence]] task: the model receives a sentence in a source language and must emit a corresponding sentence in a target language. Sentences differ in length, and aligned words can occupy different positions due to grammatical differences. D2L uses the **English↔French Tatoeba** corpus (Anki bilingual pairs) as the running dataset; tokenization is **word-level** (modern systems use subword tokenization — see [[bpe|BPE]] / [[wordpiece|WordPiece]] in later D2L chapters), every sequence is terminated with the special `<eos>` token, and short sequences are padded with `<pad>` to a fixed `num_steps` (with valid lengths tracked separately so masked positions are excluded from the loss).
- **The encoder-decoder architecture decouples variable-length input from variable-length output.** [[EncoderDecoder|Encoder-decoder]] ([[KyunghyunCho|Cho]] et al. 2014; [[IlyaSutskever|Sutskever]], Vinyals & Le 2014) is the foundational pattern: an **encoder** consumes the variable-length source $x_1,\ldots,x_T$ and emits a fixed-shape state $\mathbf{c} = q(\mathbf{h}_1,\ldots,\mathbf{h}_T)$ — most simply the final encoder hidden state $\mathbf{h}_T$; a **decoder** behaves like a conditional language model, generating the target sequence token-by-token from $\mathbf{c}$ and the leftward context of what has already been generated. The same template recurs unchanged in the [[transformer|Transformer]]; only the recurrent backbone is replaced. D2L abstracts this as a `EncoderDecoder` base class with an `init_state` method on the decoder side.
- **Seq2seq is implemented with two RNNs (one encoder, one decoder), connected by initial hidden state and/or context broadcast.** Architectural details from D2L's implementation: (i) both encoder and decoder use a **multi-layer GRU** with an [[Embedding|embedding layer]] in front; (ii) the input is begun with `<bos>` and ends with `<eos>`; (iii) at each decoder time step, the input token's embedding is *concatenated* with the encoder's final hidden state $\mathbf{c}$ so that $\mathbf{c}$ informs *every* decoding step (the [[KyunghyunCho|Cho]] design), not just the first ([[IlyaSutskever|Sutskever]] et al.'s design which uses $\mathbf{c}$ only as the decoder's initial hidden state). [[XavierInitialization|Xavier init]] is applied to all GRU and linear weights via a custom `init_seq2seq` walker.
- **Teacher forcing trains the seq2seq decoder on ground-truth previous targets; the loss is masked over padding.** During training, the decoder input is `<bos>` concatenated with the target sequence minus its last token; the decoder target is the target sequence shifted by one (so position $t$'s target is position $t+1$'s token). This is [[TeacherForcing|teacher forcing]] adapted to seq2seq. The loss is masked: positions beyond each sequence's valid length contribute zero — D2L extends [[CrossEntropyLoss|cross-entropy]] with a `valid_len` argument and zero-weights padding tokens. At inference time, predictions are generated autoregressively (predicted token at $t$ becomes the input at $t+1$).
- **BLEU evaluates translation quality via modified $n$-gram precision with a length penalty.** [[BLEU]] (Bilingual Evaluation Understudy; [[KishorePapineni|Papineni]] et al. 2002) is defined as $\textrm{BLEU} = \exp(\min(0, 1 - \textrm{len}_\textrm{label}/\textrm{len}_\textrm{pred}))\cdot \prod_{n=1}^k p_n^{1/2^n}$, where $p_n$ is the count of $n$-grams in the prediction that also appear in the target, divided by the number of $n$-grams in the prediction. Properties: (i) score is 1 when prediction equals target; (ii) longer $n$-gram matches are weighted more (since $p_n^{1/2^n}$ grows with $n$ for fixed $p_n$); (iii) the leading exponential is a **brevity penalty** that drives BLEU toward 0 when predictions are shorter than targets (e.g. target length 6, prediction length 2, $p_1=p_2=1$ → BLEU = $\exp(1-3)\cdot 1 \approx 0.14$). D2L's implementation uses $k=2$ as a sensible default for short translations.
- **Greedy decoding selects the argmax token at each step; it is locally optimal but globally suboptimal.** [[GreedyDecoding|Greedy search]] picks $y_{t'} = \operatorname{argmax}_{y\in\mathcal{Y}} P(y\mid y_{<t'}, \mathbf{c})$ and halts at `<eos>` or $T'$. Cost: $\mathcal{O}(|\mathcal{Y}|T')$. D2L gives a worked counter-example with a 4-token vocabulary where greedy yields probability-product 0.048 but a slightly off-policy alternative reaches 0.054. **Exhaustive search** ($\mathcal{O}(|\mathcal{Y}|^{T'})$) is provably optimal but computationally infeasible: for $|\mathcal{Y}|=10000$ and $T'=10$, it requires $10^{40}$ evaluations.
- **Beam search is the practical compromise: keep the top $k$ partial sequences at each step.** [[BeamSearch|Beam search]] is parameterized by a single hyperparameter, the **beam size** $k$. At step 1, retain the $k$ highest-probability first tokens. At each subsequent step, extend each of the $k$ candidates by every vocabulary token, score the $k|\mathcal{Y}|$ continuations, and keep the top $k$. Final selection uses a **length-normalized log-likelihood** $\frac{1}{L^\alpha}\sum_{t'=1}^{L}\log P(y_{t'}\mid y_{<t'}, \mathbf{c})$ with $\alpha \approx 0.75$, where $L^\alpha$ penalizes overly long sequences (longer sequences have more negative log-prob terms, so without a penalty short sequences would always win). Cost: $\mathcal{O}(k|\mathcal{Y}|T')$ — sits *between* greedy and exhaustive. Greedy decoding is recovered as beam search with $k=1$; exhaustive is recovered as $k=|\mathcal{Y}|^{T'}$.

## Key Quotes

> "LSTMs are the prototypical latent variable autoregressive model with nontrivial state control." — §lstm, the framing

> "While LSTMs were published in 1997, they rose to great prominence with some victories in prediction competitions in the mid-2000s, and became the dominant models for sequence learning from 2011 until the rise of Transformer models, starting in 2017. Even Transformers owe some of their key ideas to architecture design innovations introduced by the LSTM." — §lstm summary, the historical arc

> "Reset gates help capture short-term dependencies in sequences. Update gates help capture long-term dependencies in sequences." — §gru, the GRU mnemonic

> "Bidirectional RNNs are mostly useful for sequence encoding and the estimation of observations given bidirectional context. Bidirectional RNNs are very costly to train due to long gradient chains." — §bi-rnn summary, the trade-off

> "You could view sequence decoding strategies as lying on a spectrum, with beam search striking a compromise between the efficiency of greedy search and the optimality of exhaustive search." — §beam-search, the framing
