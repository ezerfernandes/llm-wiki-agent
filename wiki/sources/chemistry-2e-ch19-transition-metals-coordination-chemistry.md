## Summary
OpenStax *Chemistry 2e* Chapter 19 covers the d-block (and f-block inner) transition metals: their partially filled d-orbital definition, electron configurations, multiple oxidation states, occurrence and metallurgical isolation (iron blast furnace, copper smelting, silver hydrometallurgy), and the chemistry of their halides, oxides, hydroxides, and carbonates. The second half develops coordination chemistry — complexes built from a central metal Lewis acid and ligand Lewis bases (coordination number, coordination sphere, mono/polydentate ligands and the chelate effect), the IUPAC nomenclature rules, and the full range of isomerism (geometric cis/trans, optical/enantiomers, linkage, ionization). It closes with crystal field theory: octahedral t₂g/eg splitting (Δ_oct), high-spin vs low-spin governed by Δ vs pairing energy P, the spectrochemical series, tetrahedral and square-planar splittings, and how d–d transitions produce color (complementary to absorbed light) and unpaired electrons produce paramagnetism.

## Key Claims
- Transition metals are elements that have (or readily form) **partially filled d orbitals**; the f-block lanthanides/actinides qualify because the d orbital is partially occupied before the f orbitals fill; group 12 is included by convention only.
- When forming ions, transition/inner-transition metals **lose ns electrons before (n−1)d or (n−2)f electrons** (e.g., Ti²⁺ = [Ar]3d², Pd²⁺ = [Kr]4d⁸, Ce³⁺ = [Xe]4f¹).
- Transition metals exhibit **multiple oxidation states**; for Sc→Mn the highest state equals loss of all s + d valence electrons. Second/third-series metals are more stable in higher oxidation states than the first series (Mo, W favor 6+; Cr favors 3+).
- Iron is isolated in a **blast furnace** where CO reduces Fe₂O₃ → Fe₃O₄ → FeO → Fe, with limestone forming CaSiO₃ slag; steel is iron with 0.04–2.5% C plus alloying metals (Mn, Cr, Ni, W, Mo, V).
- A **coordination compound** has a central metal (Lewis acid) bonded to **ligands** (Lewis bases) by **coordinate covalent bonds**; the **coordination sphere** (in brackets) and **coordination number** (count of donor atoms) define its structure.
- **Polydentate (chelating) ligands** (en bidentate, EDTA hexadentate, porphyrin/heme tetradentate) bind through multiple donor atoms, forming especially stable chelate rings.
- IUPAC naming: cation before anion; ligands alphabetical with -o for anions and aqua/ammine/carbonyl/nitrosyl exceptions; di/tri/tetra or bis/tris/tetrakis prefixes; metal name + Roman-numeral oxidation state; **-ate suffix for anionic complexes** (ferrate, plumbate, stannate).
- Coordination compounds show **geometric (cis/trans)**, **optical (enantiomer)**, **linkage** (e.g., SCN⁻ via S or N), and **ionization/coordination** isomerism. Cisplatin works only as the *cis* isomer.
- **Crystal field theory:** octahedral ligand point charges split d orbitals into lower t₂g (dxy, dxz, dyz) and higher eg (dz², dx²−y²) by **Δ_oct**. Filling is **low-spin** when Δ_oct > P (strong field) and **high-spin** when Δ_oct < P (weak field); only d⁴–d⁷ differ.
- The **spectrochemical series** I⁻ < Br⁻ < Cl⁻ < F⁻ < H₂O < NH₃ < en < NO₂⁻ < CN⁻ orders ligands from weak- to strong-field. Tetrahedral splitting is inverted and small (Δ_tet ≈ 4/9 Δ_oct → always high-spin).
- **Color** arises from d–d transitions; the observed color is complementary to the absorbed wavelength, computed via E = hc/λ = Δ. **Magnetism**: unpaired electrons → paramagnetic; all paired → diamagnetic. d⁰ and d¹⁰ complexes are colorless.
