## Summary
OpenStax College Physics 2e, Chapter 25 introduces **geometric optics** — the ray model of light, valid when objects are much larger than light's wavelength. It develops the law of reflection, the law of refraction (Snell's law and the index of refraction), total internal reflection and its use in fiber optics, dispersion (prisms and rainbows), and image formation by lenses and mirrors via ray tracing and the thin-lens/mirror equations. The chapter emphasizes practical optics: vision, cameras, telescopes, fiber-optic communication, gemstone sparkle, and solar concentrators.

## Key Claims
- Light is modeled as straight-line **rays** (geometric optics) when interacting with objects several times larger than its wavelength; wave optics handles the smaller-object regime.
- The **law of reflection** states the angle of reflection equals the angle of incidence (θ_r = θ_i), measured from the normal; smooth surfaces give specular reflection, rough surfaces give diffuse reflection.
- The **index of refraction** n = c/v is always ≥ 1; light slows in matter to v = c/n.
- **Snell's law**, n₁ sin θ₁ = n₂ sin θ₂, governs how rays bend at a boundary; greater index difference produces greater bending.
- **Total internal reflection** occurs above a **critical angle** θc = sin⁻¹(n₂/n₁) when light goes from a higher- to a lower-index medium; it underlies fiber optics and diamond brilliance.
- **Dispersion** arises because the index of refraction varies with wavelength (violet bends more than red), separating white light into a spectrum in prisms and rainbows.
- A **thin lens** obeys 1/d_o + 1/d_i = 1/f with magnification m = h_i/h_o = −d_i/d_o; optical power P = 1/f in diopters.
- Convex (converging) lenses give real inverted images for objects beyond f and virtual upright magnified images within f; concave (diverging) lenses always give virtual, upright, diminished images.
- For spherical mirrors, f = R/2, the mirror equation 1/d_o + 1/d_i = 1/f, and m = −d_i/d_o mirror the lens relations.
- Concave mirrors form real images (object beyond f) or virtual magnified images (object within f); convex mirrors always form virtual, upright, reduced images.
- Light paths are reversible, which is why a source at a focal point produces a parallel outgoing beam (lighthouses, headlights).

## Key Quotes (paraphrased)
> Light behaves like a set of straight-line rays whenever it interacts with things much larger than its wavelength.
> The angle at which light reflects from a surface equals the angle at which it arrives, both measured from the perpendicular.
> The index of refraction tells you how many times slower light moves in a material than in a vacuum.
> Once the incidence angle passes the critical angle, every bit of light reflects back inside the denser medium — the basis of optical fibers.
> Because a material bends short wavelengths more than long ones, white light fans out into a rainbow of colors in a prism.
