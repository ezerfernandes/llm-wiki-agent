## Summary
Chapter 4 of OpenStax College Physics 2e moves from kinematics (describing motion) to dynamics (explaining its causes via forces). It develops the force concept, then states and applies Newton's three laws of motion, introduces normal force and tension, lays out a systematic free-body-diagram problem-solving strategy, and closes with an extended introduction to the four fundamental forces of nature. Classical mechanics is framed as valid for objects moving well below light speed and larger than molecular scales.

## Key Claims
- A force is a push or pull and is a vector; multiple forces combine vectorially into a net (resultant) force.
- Newton's first law (inertia): a body stays at rest or in uniform motion unless acted on by a net external force; mass measures inertia and is location-invariant.
- Newton's second law: acceleration is proportional to net external force and inversely proportional to mass, a = F_net/m, equivalently F_net = ma; the newton is 1 kg·m/s².
- Weight is the gravitational force on mass, w = mg, with g ≈ 9.80 m/s² near Earth; weight varies by location while mass does not.
- Newton's third law: forces occur in equal-and-opposite pairs acting on *different* objects, so they do not cancel on a single chosen system.
- Defining the "system of interest" determines which forces are external (relevant) vs. internal (ignored) — central to all problem solving.
- Normal force is the perpendicular contact force from a surface; tension is a pull transmitted along a flexible connector and can only pull.
- Weight on an incline resolves into w∥ = mg sin θ and w⊥ = mg cos θ; a frictionless incline gives a = g sin θ.
- A nearly horizontal flexible connector under a perpendicular load develops very large tension, T = F⊥/(2 sin θ).
- A free-body diagram showing only external forces, plus applying F_net = ma per axis (or F_net = 0 in equilibrium), is the core solution method.
- Apparent weight differs from true weight under acceleration (e.g., a scale in an accelerating elevator).
- All phenomena derive from four fundamental forces — gravitational, electromagnetic, weak nuclear, strong nuclear — transmitted via fields/carrier particles; everyday forces (friction, tension) are electromagnetic in origin.

## Key Quotes
> Velocity does not change on its own — a net external force is required to alter an object's state of motion. — paraphrase of Newton's first law / inertia
> Forces always come in matched pairs that act on different bodies, which is why action and reaction never cancel. — paraphrase of the third-law discussion
> Nature need not be simple, yet it appears to be: every observed force reduces to just four fundamental interactions. — paraphrase of the four-basic-forces section
