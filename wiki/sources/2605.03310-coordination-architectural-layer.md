## Summary
Nechepurenko & Shuvalov (Devnull FZCO) reframe coordination in LLM multi-agent systems as a **configurable architectural layer** separable from agent logic and information access. They critique prior empirical multi-agent comparisons for confounding architectural effects with information-access effects (Ao et al., 2026), and run an information-controlled study on Polymarket binary prediction markets (n=100, claude-opus-4-6) holding model, tools, prompt template, and per-call output cap constant across five reference coordination configurations. The Murphy decomposition separates calibration error from discriminative power so configurations leave distinguishable signatures even when aggregate scores match.

## Key Claims
- **41–87% of multi-agent LLM systems fail in production**, with most failures attributable to coordination defects rather than base-model capability (cites Cemri et al., 2025).
- **Coordination layer specification** — agents as opaque endpoints, with topology / authority / synchronization / aggregation / termination / failure-handling as a separable, framework-independent layer.
- **Information-controlled methodology** — fixed model, tool stack, output cap, prompt template across configs to avoid the non-identifiability theorem (Ao et al., 2026): observed gains may otherwise reflect added information, not better coordination.
- **Murphy signatures predicted in advance** — pre-specified failure-mode signatures for five configurations; 3 of 5 upheld in predicted direction.
- **Cost–quality Pareto** — two configurations dominate on cost-adjusted accuracy on the testbed; pairwise tests do not survive Bonferroni at n=100.
- **Live on-chain replication** — same five configurations deployed as live agents on Foresight Arena for independent on-chain validation.

## Key Quotes
> "Multi-agent LLM systems fail in production at rates between 41% and 87% ... the majority of these failures attributable to coordination defects rather than to base-model capability."

Tension with the [[2512.04388-conductor|Conductor paper]]: the Conductor learns coordination end-to-end while this paper argues coordination should be specified declaratively to enable architectural reasoning.
