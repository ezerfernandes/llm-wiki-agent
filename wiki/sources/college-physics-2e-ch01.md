## Summary
The opening chapter of OpenStax College Physics 2e frames physics as the natural science describing matter, energy, space, and time from subatomic to cosmic scales, governed by a small set of universal laws. It establishes the vocabulary of science (model, theory, law, principle) and the scientific method, then builds the practical toolkit for doing physics: physical quantities, SI units and metric prefixes, unit conversion via dimensional analysis, accuracy vs. precision, significant figures and uncertainty, and order-of-magnitude approximation.

## Key Claims
- Physics studies the fundamental interactions of energy, matter, space, and time, and its laws apply universally — the same forces govern stars and everyday objects.
- A few unified physical laws explain a vast range of phenomena, revealing an underlying simplicity in nature.
- Scientific terms are precise: a **model** is a simplified representation, a **theory** is a well-tested explanation of a group of phenomena, a **law** is a concise (often mathematical) statement of a pattern, and a **principle** is narrower in scope than a law.
- The scientific method proceeds from observation through hypothesis, experiment, and analysis to conclusions, with reproducibility as its core.
- The SI system rests on fundamental units — meter, kilogram, second, ampere — from which derived units are built; metric prefixes scale by powers of 10.
- SI base units are now defined by physical constants (second by cesium oscillations, meter by the speed of light, kilogram by Planck's constant) rather than artifacts.
- Unit conversion is performed by multiplying by conversion-factor ratios so unwanted units cancel (dimensional analysis).
- Accuracy (closeness to the true value) and precision (consistency of repeated measurements) are independent properties.
- Every measurement carries uncertainty; significant figures communicate the precision of the result, and sig-fig rules differ for multiplication/division versus addition/subtraction.
- Percent uncertainty expresses measurement deviation as a fraction of the measured value.
- Order-of-magnitude approximation lets scientists make useful estimates from limited data and sanity-check results.

## Key Quotes
> A theory is a tested, evidence-supported explanation of a group of related natural phenomena, whereas a law is a concise, often mathematical, statement of a single pattern. — distinguishing scientific terms (1.1)
> The last digit recorded in a measurement is the first digit that carries some uncertainty. — significance of measured digits (1.3)
> Energy changes form but is never created or destroyed. — paraphrase of the conservation-of-energy principle (1.1)
