## Summary
Chapter 14 of OpenStax College Physics 2e treats heat as energy in transit — moving spontaneously
because of a temperature difference — rather than a quantity stored in a body. It quantifies the heat
needed to change temperature (Q = mcΔT) and to drive phase changes at constant temperature
(Q = mL), then surveys the three mechanisms of thermal energy transfer: conduction, convection, and
radiation. The treatment is largely the same physics as the chemistry-2e thermochemistry material but
adds SI/physics conventions (the mechanical equivalent of heat, thermal conductivity, the
Stefan–Boltzmann law).

## Key Claims
- Heat is energy transferred due to a temperature difference; it exists only during transfer and is not stored. Bodies store internal energy, which rises with temperature.
- Work and heat are equivalent ways to change a system's internal energy; the mechanical equivalent of heat is 1.000 kcal = 4186 J (Joule's paddle-wheel experiment).
- The heat to change temperature (no phase change) is Q = mcΔT, where specific heat c depends on substance and phase; water's high c (4186 J/kg·°C) moderates climate.
- In an isolated system, heat lost equals heat gained: Q_cold + Q_hot = 0 — the basis of calorimetry and equilibrium-temperature calculations.
- During a phase change, energy goes into breaking intermolecular bonds, so temperature stays constant; the energy per unit mass is the latent heat: Q = mLf (fusion), Q = mLv (vaporization), Q = mLs (sublimation).
- Latent heats are large (water Lf = 334 kJ/kg; Lv ≈ 2428 kJ/kg at body temperature), so a small mass of melting ice or evaporating sweat moves a lot of energy.
- Conduction transfers heat through stationary matter by molecular collisions at rate Q/t = kA(T2−T1)/d; thermal conductivity k spans many orders of magnitude (metals high, insulators low), and R-value = d/k rates insulation.
- Convection transfers heat by bulk fluid motion (natural convection from buoyancy of warmer, less-dense fluid); it is far faster than conduction and is amplified when coupled to phase change (evaporation/condensation, latent-heat feedback in storms).
- Radiation transfers heat by electromagnetic waves with no medium needed; emitted power follows the Stefan–Boltzmann law Q/t = σeAT⁴ (fourth-power temperature dependence), with emissivity e from 0 (perfect reflector) to 1 (ideal black body).
- Net radiative transfer is Q_net/t = σeA(T2⁴ − T1⁴); human skin has e ≈ 0.97 in the infrared, so a person radiates ~99 W net to a cool room. Greenhouse gases re-radiate infrared back to Earth's surface.

## Key Quotes (paraphrased)
> Heat is best understood as energy on the move because of a temperature difference, not as something an object contains. — 14.1
> Energy added during a phase change breaks bonds between molecules rather than speeding them up, so the temperature does not change until the transition is complete. — 14.3
> Radiated power grows with the fourth power of absolute temperature, making radiation extraordinarily sensitive to how hot a surface is. — 14.7
