## Summary
Chapter 4 of OpenStax [[Prealgebra]] 2e ([[OpenStax]]) develops the **[[Fraction|fraction]]** as a new kind of number — a quotient `a/b` of [[Integer|integers]] with `b ≠ 0` — and builds the full arithmetic of fractions on top of the whole-number and integer work from earlier chapters. It begins by *visualizing* fractions with circle/tile models and the [[NumberLine|number line]], distinguishing proper from improper fractions and introducing the [[MixedNumber|mixed number]]. It then states the [[EquivalentFractions|Equivalent Fractions Property]] and the **Property of One**, which together justify simplifying (reducing) fractions and rewriting them with a common denominator. From there the chapter assembles [[FractionArithmetic|fraction arithmetic]]: multiplication and division (via the [[Reciprocal|reciprocal]]), addition and subtraction with common denominators, then with different denominators using the **least common denominator** (the [[LeastCommonMultiple|LCM]] of the denominators found by [[PrimeFactorization|prime factorization]]). Mixed numbers and [[ComplexFraction|complex fractions]] extend these rules, and the chapter closes by adding the **Multiplication Property of Equality** (multiply both sides by the reciprocal of a fractional coefficient) to the [[Equation|equation]]-solving toolkit.

- **4 Key Concepts** — chapter summary of all definitions, properties, and rules.

## Key Concepts & Definitions
- **[[Fraction]]** — written `a/b` where `a` and `b` are integers and `b ≠ 0`; `a` is the **numerator** (how many parts are counted) and `b` the **denominator** (the number of equal parts the whole is divided into).
- **Proper fraction** — `a/b` with `a < b`; its value is less than 1. **Improper fraction** — `a/b` with `a ≥ b`; its value is greater than or equal to 1.
- **[[MixedNumber|Mixed number]]** — a whole number `a` together with a proper fraction `b/c` (`c ≠ 0`), written `a b/c`; equals a single number greater than 1.
- **[[EquivalentFractions|Equivalent fractions]]** — fractions that have the same value (e.g. `1/2 = 2/4 = 3/6`).
- **Property of One** — any nonzero number divided by itself is 1: `a/a = 1` for `a ≠ 0`.
- **Equivalent Fractions Property** — if `b ≠ 0` and `c ≠ 0`, then `a/b = (a·c)/(b·c)`; multiplying (or dividing) numerator and denominator by the same nonzero number leaves the value unchanged.
- **Simplified fraction** — a fraction with no common factors (other than 1) in numerator and denominator; "simplifying," "reducing," and writing "in lowest terms" all mean the same thing (see [[EquivalentFractions]]).
- **[[Reciprocal]]** — the reciprocal of `a/b` is `b/a` (with `a ≠ 0`, `b ≠ 0`); a number and its reciprocal multiply to 1. Zero has no reciprocal.
- **[[ComplexFraction|Complex fraction]]** — a fraction whose numerator or denominator (or both) itself contains a fraction; it represents a division and is simplified by dividing the top by the bottom.
- **Common denominator** — fractions sharing the same denominator, which can be added or subtracted directly. The **least common denominator (LCD)** of two fractions is the [[LeastCommonMultiple|LCM]] of their denominators.
- **Fraction bar as grouping symbol** — the bar groups its numerator and denominator like parentheses; simplify each fully before dividing.

## Key Procedures / Rules
- **Convert improper fraction → mixed number** — divide the denominator into the numerator; write `quotient remainder/divisor`. **Mixed number → improper fraction** — multiply whole × denominator, add the numerator, write the sum over the original denominator.
- **Simplify a fraction** — rewrite numerator and denominator showing common factors (use [[PrimeFactorization|prime factorization]] if needed), remove the common factors via the Equivalent Fractions Property, then multiply what remains (see [[FractionArithmetic]] / [[EquivalentFractions]]).
- **Fraction Multiplication** — `(a/b)·(c/d) = (a·c)/(b·d)` for `b ≠ 0`, `d ≠ 0`: determine the sign, multiply numerators, multiply denominators, simplify (cancel common factors first when convenient). Write an integer `n` as `n/1`.
- **Fraction Division** — `(a/b) ÷ (c/d) = (a/b)·(d/c)`: multiply the first fraction by the **[[Reciprocal|reciprocal]]** of the second, then simplify.
- **Fraction Addition / Subtraction (common denominator)** — `a/c + b/c = (a+b)/c` and `a/c − b/c = (a−b)/c` for `c ≠ 0`: combine the numerators over the shared denominator, then simplify.
- **Find the LCD** — factor each denominator into primes, line up matching primes in columns, bring down each prime the greatest number of times it appears in any one denominator, and multiply; this is the [[LeastCommonMultiple|LCM]] of the denominators.
- **Add / subtract with different denominators** — find the LCD, rewrite each fraction as an equivalent fraction over the LCD, add or subtract the numerators, then simplify.
- **Multiply / divide mixed numbers** — convert each [[MixedNumber|mixed number]] to an improper fraction, apply the multiplication or division rule, then simplify (often back to a mixed number).
- **Simplify a [[ComplexFraction|complex fraction]]** — rewrite it as a division of its numerator by its denominator and apply the division rule; or simplify the numerator and denominator separately first when the fraction bar is acting as a grouping symbol.
- **Add mixed numbers** — add the whole parts, add the fraction parts, simplify; if the fraction part is improper, convert it and carry into the whole part.
- **Subtract mixed numbers** — work in vertical form; if the top fraction is smaller than the bottom, **regroup (borrow)** one whole as a fraction over the common denominator, then subtract fractions and wholes. (Different denominators: convert to the LCD first.)
- **Sign placement** — `−a/b = a/(−b) = −(a/b)`; a quotient with one negative sign is negative, with two negatives is positive (consistent with [[SignedNumberArithmetic]]).
- **Solve equations with fractions** — use the Addition/Subtraction Properties of Equality to undo added/subtracted fractions; to clear a fractional coefficient, multiply both sides by its **reciprocal** (Multiplication Property of Equality, `a = b ⇒ ac = bc`); check by substitution. Solve `−y = k` by multiplying by `−1` (or recognizing `−y` as "the opposite of `y`").
