## Summary
Chapter 26 of OpenStax College Physics 2e applies geometric optics to the human eye and to engineered optical instruments. It explains how the cornea and lens form a real, inverted image on the retina and how accommodation, vision defects, and corrective lenses work. It then covers trichromatic color vision, compound microscopes, refracting and reflecting telescopes, and the optical aberrations that limit real lenses.

## Key Claims
- The eye focuses by changing its optical power (accommodation) while the lens-to-retina image distance stays fixed at about 2.00 cm; the cornea supplies roughly two-thirds of the total power.
- A relaxed young eye has power near 50 D for distant objects, rising to about 54 D when fully accommodated for near objects; presbyopia is the age-related loss of this accommodation.
- Myopia (over-convergence) is corrected with a diverging negative-power lens; hyperopia (under-convergence) is corrected with a converging positive-power lens; powers of stacked lenses add.
- Color vision is trichromatic: three cone types peaking near 420, 520, and 590 nm yield all perceived hues, and different wavelength mixtures can look identical.
- Rods (~120 million, ~1000× more sensitive, color-blind) handle low-light/peripheral vision; cones (~6 million) cluster in the fovea for sharp color vision.
- A compound microscope's total magnification is the product of objective and eyepiece magnifications, with the objective's real image serving as the eyepiece's object.
- Numerical aperture NA = n sin α sets light gathering and resolution; immersion media push NA above 1.0; optical microscopes reach ~1500× and ~0.2 μm resolution.
- Telescope angular magnification is M = − f_o/f_e, so a long objective focal length and short eyepiece focal length maximize magnification.
- Reflecting telescopes use concave mirrors as objectives to avoid chromatic aberration and to scale to large light-gathering apertures.
- Real lenses suffer aberrations — chromatic, spherical, coma, astigmatism — corrected by achromatic doublets, aspheric/GRIN designs, mirrors, or digital post-processing.

## Key Quotes (paraphrased)
> The cornea and lens behave as one converging system that throws a real, inverted image onto the retina, with the cornea doing most of the bending. — 26.1
> Total magnification of a compound microscope is the product, not the sum, of the objective and eyepiece magnifications. — 26.4
> For the largest angular magnification, pair a long-focal-length objective with a short-focal-length eyepiece. — 26.5
> Because reflection does not depend on wavelength, mirror-based optics escape chromatic aberration. — 26.6
