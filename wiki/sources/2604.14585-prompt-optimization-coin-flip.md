## Summary

[[XingZhang|Zhang]], [[GuanghuiWang|Wang]], [[YanweiCui|Cui]], [[WeiQiu|Qiu]], [[ZiyuanLi|Li]], [[BingZhu|Zhu]] & [[PeiyangHe|He]] ([[AWSGenerativeAIInnovationCenter|AWS Generative AI Innovation Center]] + [[HSBC]] Technology Center China, preprint April 17 2026, arXiv 2604.14585v1) deliver the wiki corpus's **first controlled empirical audit of end-to-end prompt optimization for [[CompoundAISystem|compound AI systems]]**. The paper tests the two assumptions implicit in joint-optimization tools like [[TextGrad]], [[DSPy]]/[[MIPROv2]], and [[GPTSwarm]] — **Assumption A**: individual agent prompts are worth optimizing; **Assumption B**: agent prompts interact, requiring joint rather than independent optimization. Both fail in most conditions: across **72 optimization runs on [[ClaudeHaiku45|Claude Haiku 4.5]] (6 methods × 4 tasks × 3 repeats), 49% score *below* zero-shot** (binomial $p = 0.91$ — statistically indistinguishable from a coin flip); on [[AmazonNovaLite|Amazon Nova Lite]] the failure rate is worse (14/24 method×task means below zero-shot). The $A \times B$ **interaction term is non-significant in every model×task condition** ($p > 0.52$, all $F < 1.0$, only 0.18–2.15% of total variance), demolishing the premise behind joint optimization. The one task where optimization works at all — [[HelpSteer2]] — has **exploitable output structure** (a JSON-formatted rubric the model *can* produce but does not default to), motivating the paper's diagnostic name for this regime: the **[[CanButDoesntPattern|"can but doesn't" pattern]]**.

The constructive contribution is a **two-stage practitioner diagnostic** (Figure 3) that replaces the default joint-optimization assumption: **Stage 1** — a **~$80, 1-day [[ANOVAVarianceDecomposition|ANOVA coupling test]]** over a $10 \times 10$ prompt grid with $n=30$ samples — answers *"do agents interact?"*; **Stage 2** — a **~$5, 10-minute [[HeadroomTest|headroom test]]** generating 10–20 candidate prompts — answers *"is the landscape worth optimizing?"*. The headroom test perfectly separated the one successful task from the three failures in the paper's data. Together they avoid committing $1K–$10K to [[TextGrad]] / [[DSPy]] compilation runs when neither coupling nor headroom exists (3 of 4 tasks in the study).

The paper also introduces **[[PROSEOptimizer|PROSE]]** — *PRompt Optimization via Structured Evolution* — the authors' own risk-aware evolutionary optimizer that decomposes prompts into five semantic components (`role`, `task`, `constraints`, `examples`, `format`) and ranks candidates by a risk-adjusted fitness `0.70 · mean + 0.15 · Sharpe ratio + 0.15 · DRO`. PROSE shows **no measurable advantage** over simpler methods — reinforcing the paper's main finding that optimization gains are fragile and model-specific.

**Methodological contribution**: ANOVA decomposition of $Y_{ijk}$ (Agent A prompt $i$, Agent B prompt $j$, question $k$) into question / Agent-A main / Agent-B main / $A \times B$ interaction / residual is offered as a **falsifiable evaluation protocol for compositional behavior** in multi-agent systems — a structured alternative to the ad hoc aggregate-benchmark reporting that dominates the [[CompoundAISystem|compound-AI]] literature. The protocol is the paper's most generalizable artifact.

This is the wiki's **first paper to question the prompt-optimization research program empirically rather than methodologically**. It sits in deliberate tension with [[2406.11695-mipro|MIPRO]] / [[2407.10930-better-together|BetterTogether]] / [[2507.19457-gepa|GEPA]] — papers whose data the wiki has accepted at face value — by recasting them as **optimization methods looking for tasks with exploitable structure** rather than universally useful tools. Critically, the paper **does not invalidate any of those results**: it argues their gains are conditional on a task property (output-structure latency) that practitioners can now test for cheaply before committing to expensive joint optimization.

## Key Claims

- **Assumption A and Assumption B both fail in most conditions.** The premise of end-to-end joint optimizers like [[TextGrad]], [[DSPy]]/[[MIPROv2]], [[GPTSwarm]], and [[Helix]] is empirically untested. The paper tests both with 18,000 grid evaluations + 144 optimization runs.

- **Agents don't interact (Assumption B fails universally).** The $A \times B$ interaction term is non-significant in all six model×task conditions (HotpotQA / XSum / MBPP × Claude Haiku / Nova Lite): $p > 0.52$, all $F < 1.0$, 0.18–2.15% of total variance. Joint optimization is unnecessary; independent per-agent optimization suffices.

- **Expert predictions about coupling are wrong.** [[hotpotqa|HotpotQA]] — multi-hop QA, *a priori* expected to be tightly coupled because Agent B must build on Agent A's specific decomposition — shows the **smallest** interaction (0.18% on Haiku). Practitioners cannot predict coupling without measurement.

- **Question difficulty dominates everything.** It explains 19–91% of total variance — **far more than any prompt effect** (≤2.15%). Evaluation noise from question sampling overwhelms most real optimization signal at the budgets practitioners face.

- **Visual signature of additivity.** $10 \times 10$ score matrices (Figure 2) show **row/column banding** — good Agent A prompts produce consistently good rows, good Agent B prompts produce consistently good columns — but **no off-diagonal structure**. The joint optimum (best cell) and the independent optimum (best row × best column) are adjacent or identical in every condition, with a gap of 0.0–3.3 points.

- **Budget-equalized simulations confirm: independent search matches joint search at all budget levels** (1,000 trials).

- **The interaction landscape is noise, not gradient.** After removing additive row/column effects from each score matrix, neighbor autocorrelation in the interaction residual is near zero ($\rho = -0.12$ to $+0.05$) — confirming the surface is random noise, not a smooth gradient for [[TextGrad]]-style methods to follow.

- **Assumption A fails on 3 of 4 tasks (single-agent setting).** On [[ClaudeHaiku45|Claude Haiku]], 49% of 72 runs score below zero-shot (binomial $p = 0.91$ — coin flip). Average gains across all 6 methods: [[FeedbackBench]] $-0.20$, [[WildBench]] $-0.82$, [[XSum]] $-0.17$, [[HelpSteer2]] $+4.8$. The aggregate failure rate conceals the one striking exception.

- **The "can but doesn't" pattern explains why HelpSteer2 succeeds.** [[HelpSteer2]] requires **structured rubric-based evaluation with JSON-formatted output** — a format the model *can* produce when prompted correctly (68.0 → 74.8) but does not default to. The other three tasks accept free-form natural language and the model's zero-shot behavior is already near-optimal for that format. Gain ceilings reflect this: HelpSteer2 best $+6.8$ pts (EvoPrompt) vs $+1.1$ / $+0.7$ / $+0.6$ on FB / WB / XSum.

- **Why optimization usually fails.** Two factors compound: (i) **20 training questions is too noisy** for reliable per-candidate selection; (ii) **iterative methods overfit** (train-test gaps up to $+5.6$ pts; [[APE]], being non-iterative, shows none). Aligns with [[Nie2026Iterative|Nie et al. 2026]] reporting only 9% of surveyed agents use any automated optimization.

- **Model specificity dominates prompt-level optimization.** Everything reverses when the model changes. *Which agent matters flips*: on Haiku, Agent B dominates HotpotQA ($p < 0.001$); on Nova, neither agent is significant. On XSum, Agent A matters on Nova but not Haiku. *Which task is optimizable reverses*: HelpSteer2 is highly optimizable on Haiku (6/6 methods beat zero-shot) but barely on Nova (1/6); [[FeedbackBench]] goes from 1/6 on Haiku to 4/6 on Nova — a complete reversal. **Neither coupling structure nor optimization headroom can be determined a priori.**

- **Two-stage diagnostic protocol.** Stage 1: [[ANOVAVarianceDecomposition|ANOVA coupling test]] (~$80, 1 day) — if $F < 1$ for the interaction term, optimize agents **independently**; do not invest in joint optimization. Stage 2: [[HeadroomTest|headroom test]] (~$5, 10 min) — generate 10–20 candidate prompts; if the best gains $<2$ pts over zero-shot, the landscape is flat — **use zero-shot**; if $>2$ pts, look for the [[CanButDoesntPattern|"can but doesn't" pattern]] and optimize with [[APE]]-style generate-and-rank.

- **Cost-benefit verdict.** Two-stage diagnostic ≈ $85 total. [[DSPy]] compilation: $1K–$5K. [[TextGrad]] end-to-end: $5K–$10K. The diagnostic rules out the expensive options in 3 of 4 tasks in the study.

- **PROSE shows no risk-aware advantage.** The paper's own [[PROSEOptimizer|PROSE]] optimizer adds explicit Sharpe-ratio + DRO worst-case penalties on top of evolutionary search. It does not measurably outperform simpler methods — reinforcing the main finding that optimization gains are fragile.

- **Why interactions are weak (mechanistic hypothesis).** [[InstructionTuning|Instruction-tuning]] + [[rlhf|RLHF]] train models to produce consistent outputs across diverse input phrasings — compressing input styles into a narrow output distribution. Agent B's output variance is dominated by the *semantic content* of Agent A's response (set by the question), not by Agent A's *stylistic variation* (what prompt changes affect). The pipeline composes as **independently-robust functions** — coupling would require phrasing sensitivity that RLHF specifically eliminates.

- **When coupling might break independence.** Five predicted regimes the paper does not test: (1) agents share mutable state (database, scratchpad); (2) Agent B's input format depends on Agent A's output schema; (3) feedback loops (B's output → A); (4) deeper pipelines (3+ agents) accumulating interaction opportunities; (5) agents communicate via **structured data** (JSON / code), where format sensitivity may amplify coupling. The ANOVA pretest generalizes to all of these.

- **Model specificity has growing consequences.** Optimization effects are model-specific; frontier models update quarterly. Any prompt optimization strategy has a **shelf life shorter than the model release cycle**. *"Teams that invest $10K in TextGrad-optimized prompts for Model X face re-optimization costs when Model X+1 arrives — and our data shows that which agents matter, which tasks benefit, and which methods work may all reverse."* Base models are absorbing scaffold tricks ([[chainofthought|chain-of-thought]], structured output, [[react|ReAct]]-style tool use) through RL training, shrinking optimization headroom over time.

## Method

**Two studies.**

**Study 1** (Assumption B test): two-agent pipelines (Agent A → Agent B). Generate $K = 10$ diverse system prompts per agent (varying strategy / tone / structure). Exhaustively evaluate all $10 \times 10 = 100$ combinations on $n = 30$ benchmark samples → score tensor $Y_{ijk}$. Two-way ANOVA with question blocking decomposes total variance into 5 sources: question, Agent A main, Agent B main, $A \times B$ interaction, residual.

Variance formulas (Appendix A):
- $\mathrm{SS}_A = n K_B \sum_i (\tilde{\bar Y}_{i\cdot\cdot} - \tilde{\bar Y}_{\cdot\cdot\cdot})^2$
- $\mathrm{SS}_B = n K_A \sum_j (\tilde{\bar Y}_{\cdot j\cdot} - \tilde{\bar Y}_{\cdot\cdot\cdot})^2$
- $\mathrm{SS}_{A \times B} = n \sum_{i,j} (\tilde{\bar Y}_{ij\cdot} - \tilde{\bar Y}_{i\cdot\cdot} - \tilde{\bar Y}_{\cdot j\cdot} + \tilde{\bar Y}_{\cdot\cdot\cdot})^2$

The interaction has $(K_A-1)(K_B-1) = 81$ degrees of freedom vs 9 for each main effect — so 2% interaction variance can still be non-significant ($\mathrm{MS}_{A\times B} = 2\%/81$ per df) while a 0.6% main effect ($\mathrm{MS}_A = 0.6\%/9$ per df) is significant.

**Tasks (Study 1):** chosen to span *a priori* expected coupling — [[hotpotqa|HotpotQA]] (tight), [[MBPP]] (medium), [[XSum]] (loose).

**Study 2** (Assumption A test): four single-agent tasks chosen for output-format diversity — [[FeedbackBench]], [[HelpSteer2]], [[WildBench]], [[XSum]]. Compare six prompt optimizers — [[APE]], [[OPRO]], [[EvoPrompt]], [[PromptBreeder]], DSPy-style bootstrap (after [[BootstrapFewShot|`BootstrapFewShot`]]), and the authors' [[PROSEOptimizer|PROSE]] — against zero-shot and manual baselines. All methods evaluate ~100 candidate prompts (equal compute budget). Train: 20 questions. Test: 100 held-out questions. 3 repeats per condition.

**Models.** Executors: [[ClaudeHaiku45|Claude Haiku 4.5]] (mid-tier) and [[AmazonNovaLite|Amazon Nova Lite]] (budget-tier). Judge: [[ClaudeSonnet46|Claude Sonnet 4.6]].

## Contradictions

- **vs [[2406.11695-mipro|MIPRO]] Lesson 2 (*"joint optimization wins overall"*)**: Study 1's ANOVA finds **no significant interaction** in any model×task condition, contradicting the premise that joint optimization is necessary. The contradiction is **mostly resolved by setting**: MIPRO measures `joint-optimization > demos-only OR instructions-only` on programs MIPRO can already optimize end-to-end; this paper measures *whether interaction exists* on two-agent feed-forward pipelines. Both can be empirically right — MIPRO's wins may come from joint *demos × instructions* per module (a within-module question), not from cross-module coupling.

- **vs [[2407.10930-better-together|BetterTogether]]'s $\Pi \to \Theta \to \Pi$ alternation**: BetterTogether's three-step schedule presumes that prompt-opt before fine-tune produces a higher-quality SFT dataset *because* prompt and weight steps interact. The Study 1 ANOVA does not test this exact claim (BT is single-axis prompt + weight, this paper is two-agent prompt × prompt), but the underlying assumption — *strong coupling between modules / axes* — is the same. Open question: does BT-style bi-axial coupling survive this paper's ANOVA on a two-axis ($\Pi$, $\Theta$) decomposition?

- **vs [[2507.19457-gepa|GEPA]]'s headline gains**: GEPA reports >10% improvements over [[MIPROv2]] across six benchmarks. This paper's coin-flip finding — 49% of runs below zero-shot — does not include GEPA in the optimizer pool. Open question: would GEPA's [[ReflectivePromptMutation|reflective mutation]] cross the 2-pt headroom threshold on [[FeedbackBench]] / [[WildBench]] / [[XSum]]? The paper's framework predicts no — *"no method will reliably help"* when the landscape is flat.

- **vs [[CompoundAISystem|the joint compound-AI-system optimization premise]]** (TextGrad, DSPy, GPTSwarm, Helix): all four frame joint optimization as necessary. This paper provides the first empirical test and finds the premise empirically unsupported in two-agent feed-forward pipelines on mid-tier models.

- **vs the *"PROSE risk-aware design improves robustness"* hypothesis**: PROSE adds explicit Sharpe-ratio + DRO worst-case penalties (Eq. 4) on top of evolutionary search. It does not measurably outperform simpler methods — a negative result on a hypothesis the paper itself was structured to test.

- **vs [[2025-bionlp-archehr-qa-neural|Neural at ArchEHR-QA 2025]]'s *"MIPRO beats few-shot by 10, zero-shot by 20"* claim**: this paper's coin-flip aggregate is a 20-question training budget; Neural at ArchEHR uses 20 dev cases too. The discrepancy is consistent with the [[CanButDoesntPattern|"can but doesn't" framing]]: Neural's task (evidence-grounded clinical QA with strict ≤75-word + citation-format constraints + six-metric composite reward) **has exploitable output structure** — the same property HelpSteer2 has. The 20-point gain over zero-shot in Neural's report falls in the same regime as HelpSteer2's $+6.8$ in this paper (with composite reward replacing JSON rubric).

## Scope Limits

- **Two-agent feed-forward only.** Deeper pipelines (3+ agents) and cyclic / feedback-loop architectures are not tested — the paper predicts coupling may emerge there.
- **Mid-tier models.** Claude Haiku 4.5 + Amazon Nova Lite. Frontier-model behavior (Opus / GPT-4 / Gemini Ultra-class) untested.
- **$K = 10$ whole-prompt substitutions.** Fine-grained edit-level optimization untested.
- **20 training questions.** The paper argues this is the realistic practitioner constraint; iterative methods may shine with hundreds.
- **Natural-language inter-agent communication.** Structured-data communication (JSON schemas, code) may amplify coupling — explicitly listed as the most likely independence-breaker.
