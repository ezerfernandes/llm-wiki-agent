## Summary
Visual Basic for Applications (VBA) is an event-driven, imperative programming language with optional object-oriented features, developed by Microsoft and first released in 1993 as the embedded automation and macro language for Office applications such as Excel, Word, and Access. It is statically typed with a default of dynamic Variant typing when declarations are omitted, derives its syntax from Visual Basic, and runs inside a host application via a built-in editor (the VBE) rather than as a standalone compiled program. VBA is primarily used to automate repetitive tasks, manipulate documents and spreadsheets, build custom forms and dialogs, and integrate Office components through the COM object model. Despite its age and Microsoft's promotion of newer technologies, it remains deeply entrenched in business and finance for spreadsheet automation and legacy tooling.
