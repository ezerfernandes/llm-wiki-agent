## Summary
Io is a pure prototype-based object-oriented language created by Steve Dekorte in 2002, where everything is an object and there are no classes—new objects are produced by cloning existing prototypes and adding slots. It is dynamically typed and draws its design from Smalltalk (all values are objects, message passing), Self and NewtonScript (prototype inheritance), Lisp (code as runtime-manipulable data), and Lua (small embeddable virtual machine). Its uniform message-passing syntax, first-class messages with deferred evaluation, and built-in actor-based concurrency (coroutines, actors, and futures) make it highly malleable and reflective. Io is small, embeddable, and historically used for scripting, teaching language design, and experimenting with metaprogramming and concurrency rather than for large production systems.
