## Summary
Fermat is a computer algebra system and interpreted programming language created by Robert H. Lewis at Fordham University, with development beginning in the 1980s. It specializes in exact, arbitrary-precision arithmetic over the rationals, integers modulo n, finite fields, and especially symbolic computation with multivariate polynomials and matrices, using dynamic typing where values carry their mathematical type at runtime. The language is procedural and array-oriented, offering a terse syntax aimed at interactive symbolic and numeric mathematics. It is typically used for research and teaching in computer algebra, particularly tasks involving polynomial manipulation, modular arithmetic, and dense or sparse matrix algebra where high-performance exact computation matters.
