## Summary
Oberon-2 is a general-purpose, imperative and object-oriented programming language designed in 1991 by Hanspeter Mössenböck and Niklaus Wirth as an extension of Wirth's minimalist Oberon language, itself a descendant of Modula-2 and Pascal. It is statically and strongly typed with type-bound procedures (methods), type extension for single inheritance, runtime type tests, and automatic garbage collection, all delivered in a deliberately small, readable syntax. The language emphasizes simplicity, safety, and efficiency, and was originally tied to the Oberon System operating environment at ETH Zurich. Today it is used mainly in teaching, in systems and compiler research, and in embedded or niche projects where a compact, well-defined object-oriented Pascal-family language is desired.
