## Summary
ALGOL W is an imperative, procedural, block-structured programming language designed in 1966 by Niklaus Wirth and C. A. R. Hoare as a proposed successor to ALGOL 60, and implemented for the IBM System/360 at Stanford University. It is statically and strongly typed, introducing influential features such as records and reference (pointer) types, dynamic arrays, call-by-result and call-by-value-result parameter passing, a case statement, and standardized string handling. As an evolutionary refinement of the ALGOL family, it served as a major stepping stone toward Wirth's later language Pascal. Today it is primarily of historical and academic interest, having been widely used for teaching and as a research vehicle in the late 1960s and 1970s.
