## Summary
Lisaac is a prototype-based, object-oriented programming language created in the early 2000s by Benoît Sonntag and the IsaacOS research team in France, designed to combine the dynamic object model of Self with the low-level performance needed for systems and operating-system programming. It is statically typed with full type inference and compiles to C, relying on aggressive whole-program optimization (notably global inlining) to deliver speed competitive with hand-written C while preserving a pure prototype/cloning model in place of classes. Lisaac introduces contract-based programming (preconditions, postconditions, and invariants) and was used chiefly as the implementation language of the experimental IsaacOS operating system. Today it is largely a research and academic curiosity rather than a mainstream production language.
