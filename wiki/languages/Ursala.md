## Summary
Ursala is a purely functional programming language designed by Dennis Furey in the early 2000s, built on the lambda calculus and centered on the manipulation of lists, trees, and sets through point-free combinator composition. It is dynamically typed and emphasizes treating functions as first-class data, supporting higher-order operators, lazy evaluation, and a distinctive concise notation built around its native list-processing primitives. The language interoperates with C and Fortran and bundles numerous mathematical and computer-algebra libraries, making it well suited to scientific computing, symbolic manipulation, and rapid prototyping of algorithms. Programs are compiled to native code via an intermediate "fun" virtual machine, and its terse, combinator-heavy style places it firmly in the niche, research-oriented end of functional languages.
