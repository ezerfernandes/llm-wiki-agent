## Summary
Objective-C is a general-purpose, object-oriented programming language created in the early 1980s by Brad Cox and Tom Love, layering Smalltalk-style messaging on top of C. It is a strict superset of C with static typing for primitives but dynamic, message-based dispatch for objects, giving it a flexible runtime that supports reflection, late binding, and categories. Adopted by NeXT and later Apple, it became the primary language for macOS and iOS development through the Cocoa and Cocoa Touch frameworks. While largely supplanted by Swift in new Apple projects, it remains widely used for maintaining legacy applications and frameworks.
