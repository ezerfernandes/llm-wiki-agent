## Summary
SETL (SET Language) is a very-high-level, imperative programming language developed by Jacob T. Schwartz at New York University in the late 1960s and 1970s, built around the mathematical theory of finite sets. It is dynamically typed and treats sets, tuples, and maps as first-class primitives, with set-builder comprehensions, quantifiers, and set operations expressed in notation close to formal mathematics, letting programmers work at a high level of abstraction without specifying concrete data structures. Historically significant as a prototyping and specification language, SETL is most famous for having been used to write the first validated Ada compiler (the NYU Ada/Ed translator), and its declarative set-oriented style influenced later languages such as Python's comprehensions and the database/query world.
