## Summary
Egel is an untyped, purely functional toy programming language created by Marco Devillers in the 2010s, built around eager combinator rewriting rather than the more common lambda-calculus evaluation model. Programs are expressed as collections of pattern-matching rewrite rules over symbolic terms, with dynamic typing and no static type checker, and it runs on its own term-graph rewriting interpreter. It draws heavily from the functional and concatenative traditions and is primarily used for experimentation, scripting, and exploring combinator-based evaluation rather than production work.
