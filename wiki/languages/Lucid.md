## Summary
Lucid is a dataflow programming language created by Edward Ashcroft and William Wadge in the mid-1970s, notable as one of the earliest pioneers of the dataflow paradigm. It is purely functional and intensional: programs are sets of equations over infinite streams of values, where variables denote entire histories rather than single mutable cells, and iteration is expressed declaratively through stream operators such as <code>first</code>, <code>next</code>, and <code>fby</code> ("followed by") rather than explicit loops or assignment. The language is implicitly typed and demand-driven, evaluating values lazily as they are requested. Largely a research vehicle, Lucid influenced later work on dataflow, reactive, and synchronous languages and remains of primarily academic and historical interest.
