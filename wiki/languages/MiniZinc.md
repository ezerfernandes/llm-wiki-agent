## Summary
MiniZinc is a high-level, declarative constraint modeling language created around 2007 at Monash University and NICTA for specifying constrained combinatorial optimization and satisfaction problems. It is statically typed over a small set of types (Booleans, integers, floats, strings, sets, and arrays) and follows a constraint-programming paradigm in which the programmer states variables, domains, and constraints rather than an imperative solution procedure. A MiniZinc model is compiled to a low-level solver-independent format called FlatZinc, which can then be dispatched to a wide range of back-end solvers (CP, MIP, SAT, and SMT). It is used primarily in operations research, scheduling, planning, configuration, and academic teaching of constraint and optimization techniques.
