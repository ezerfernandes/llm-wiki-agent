## Summary
Erlang is a functional, concurrent programming language created at Ericsson in the late 1980s by Joe Armstrong, Robert Virding, and Mike Williams to build highly available, fault-tolerant telecom switching systems. It is dynamically and strongly typed, emphasizes immutable data and the actor model of lightweight processes that communicate solely through asynchronous message passing, and runs on the BEAM virtual machine that supports hot code swapping and massive concurrency. Built around a "let it crash" philosophy backed by supervision trees in the OTP framework, Erlang is typically used for distributed, soft real-time, and always-on systems such as messaging platforms, telephony, and large-scale backends.
