## Summary
Isabelle is an interactive theorem prover (proof assistant) originally developed by Lawrence Paulson at the University of Cambridge and Tobias Nipkow at the Technical University of Munich beginning in the late 1980s. It is a generic, logic-independent framework built on a small trusted meta-logic (Isabelle/Pure), with Isabelle/HOL — a strongly, statically typed higher-order logic combining functional programming with classical reasoning — being by far the most widely used object logic. Proofs are written declaratively in the structured Isar language and machine-checked, with heavy automation from tools such as the simplifier, sledgehammer, and decision procedures. Typical uses include formal verification of mathematics and algorithms, certified software and hardware, and programming-language semantics, often exporting verified functional code to Standard ML, Haskell, OCaml, or Scala.
