## Summary
Pascal-P is a portable, imperative and procedural dialect of Pascal developed at ETH Zürich in the 1970s under Niklaus Wirth's group, designed primarily to bootstrap Pascal compilers onto new machines. It is statically and strongly typed in the classic Wirthian tradition, with the compiler emitting P-code, a stack-based intermediate bytecode for an abstract "P-machine" that each target only needs to implement as a small interpreter. Because of this two-stage portability model it was historically used to spread Pascal across diverse early hardware, and its descendants and reconstructions (such as the Pascal-P5/P6 systems) remain in use today mainly for teaching compiler construction, language bootstrapping, and historical or retrocomputing study.
