## Summary
S-BASIC is a structured dialect of BASIC released by Micro-Soft (later Topaz Programming) in the late 1970s and early 1980s, shipped most notably with Kaypro CP/M microcomputers. Unlike the line-numbered, interpreted BASIC of its era, S-BASIC was a compiled, imperative language that emphasized structured programming, offering block-oriented control flow (such as IF/THEN/ELSE blocks, WHILE and REPEAT loops, and CASE statements) and named procedures and functions in place of GOTO-driven code. It supported statically declared variables with explicit types including integers, reals, characters, strings, and user-defined records and arrays. It was used primarily for general-purpose application and business programming on 8-bit CP/M systems before fading from use as the platform was superseded.
