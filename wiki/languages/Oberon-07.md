## Summary
Oberon-07 is a small, imperative, structured procedural programming language designed by Niklaus Wirth, first published in 2007 and revised through the 2010s as the final and most minimal member of the Pascal/Modula-2/Oberon family. It is statically and strongly typed with a deliberately spartan feature set, supporting modules, type-extension-based object orientation, and garbage collection while removing constructs Wirth considered superfluous (such as the FOR loop in early drafts, later reinstated, and most low-level coercions). The language was created chiefly for teaching and for building self-contained systems software, and it serves as the implementation language of the Project Oberon operating system and the educational RISC processor toolchain. Its design philosophy emphasizes simplicity, readability, and compiler implementability over breadth, making programs compact and amenable to formal reasoning.
