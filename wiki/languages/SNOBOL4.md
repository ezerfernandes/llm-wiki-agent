## Summary
SNOBOL4 is a string-oriented procedural language developed at Bell Labs in the early-to-mid 1960s by David Farber, Ralph Griswold, and Ivan Polonsky, and is the most influential member of the SNOBOL (StriNg Oriented and symBOlic Language) family. Its defining feature is a powerful built-in pattern-matching facility, where patterns are first-class data objects that can be composed, named, and matched against strings far more expressively than conventional regular expressions. The language is dynamically typed with automatic storage management, treats strings, patterns, and arrays as core data types, and uses an unusual control-flow model in which statements branch conditionally on the success or failure of pattern matches. SNOBOL4 was widely used for text processing, lexical analysis, document formatting, and symbolic computation, and its ideas later evolved into Griswold's Icon language.
