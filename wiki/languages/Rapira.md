## Summary
Rapira (Рапира) is an educational, imperative procedural programming language developed in the Soviet Union in the early 1980s, notable for using Russian-language keywords and designed as a teaching language for introducing programming concepts to students. It is dynamically typed, with values whose types are checked at runtime, and supports structured control flow, procedures, and high-level data structures such as sequences and tuples. Influenced by ALGOL and the SETL-like emphasis on set/sequence operations, it ran on Soviet school microcomputers (e.g., the Agat and Korvet machines) and saw use primarily in academic and instructional settings rather than industrial software development.
