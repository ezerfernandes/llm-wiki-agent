## Summary
VHDL (VHSIC Hardware Description Language) is a strongly and statically typed hardware description language used to model, simulate, and synthesize digital electronic systems rather than to write conventional software. Originating in the mid-1980s from the U.S. Department of Defense's VHSIC program and standardized as IEEE 1076 (first in 1987, with major revisions through 2008 and 2019), it draws heavily on Ada's Pascal-like syntax and emphasizes concurrent, event-driven dataflow semantics alongside sequential process blocks. Designs are expressed as entities with separate architectures, supporting structural, behavioral, and register-transfer-level (RTL) descriptions. It is used primarily for FPGA and ASIC design, verification, and simulation, where its rich type system and explicit timing model help describe parallel hardware behavior precisely.
