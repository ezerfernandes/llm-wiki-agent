## Summary
Gema is a pattern-matching text-processing language created by David N. Gray in the mid-1990s, designed as a general-purpose macro processor and stream editor in the spirit of tools like awk and sed. Programs consist of rules that pair patterns against template-style replacements, so it follows a declarative, rule-based paradigm rather than imperative control flow, and it is dynamically and weakly typed since virtually everything is treated as text. It reads input as a character stream, matching context-sensitive patterns and emitting transformed output, with support for domains, recursion, and user-defined functions. Its typical use is filtering and rewriting text such as source-code conversion, documentation formatting, and log or data munging.
