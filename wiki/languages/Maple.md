## Summary
Maple is a commercial computer algebra system and multi-paradigm programming language first developed at the University of Waterloo in 1980 and now produced by Maplesoft. Its language is dynamically typed and interpreted, blending imperative, functional, and procedural styles with built-in support for symbolic mathematics, exact arithmetic, and arbitrary-precision computation. Programs typically operate on symbolic expressions through an interactive worksheet environment, and the system ships with an extensive library of routines for algebra, calculus, differential equations, linear algebra, and numerical analysis. It is widely used in engineering, scientific research, and mathematics education for both symbolic and numeric problem solving.
