## Summary
Logo is a dynamically typed, multi-paradigm educational programming language created in 1967 at BBN by Wally Feurzeig, Seymour Papert, and Cynthia Solomon, drawing heavily on Lisp's functional and list-processing roots while replacing parentheses with a friendlier word-based syntax. It is best known for "turtle graphics," in which a cursor (the turtle) is steered with relative movement and turning commands to draw vector figures, making abstract geometric and procedural concepts tangible for beginners. The language is interpreted and interactive, supports recursion, first-class procedures, and symbolic list manipulation, and emphasizes a low floor with a high ceiling rooted in Papert's constructionist learning theory. Historically central to computing education in schools, Logo and its many dialects (such as UCBLogo and the Lisp-flavored variant used here) remain primarily teaching tools rather than general-purpose production languages.
