## Summary
Refal (REcursive Functions Algorithmic Language) is a functional programming language designed by Valentin Turchin in the Soviet Union around 1966, making it one of the oldest functional languages. Its defining feature is pattern matching over arbitrary symbolic expressions driven by a powerful generalized form of unification, with computation proceeding through rewriting rules applied to a structured data field rather than via lambda-calculus reduction. It is dynamically typed and works on symbolic strings and trees, which made it especially well suited to symbolic manipulation, term rewriting, compiler construction, and artificial-intelligence research. Refal is also historically significant for inspiring supercompilation, a metacomputation-based program-transformation technique pioneered by Turchin.
