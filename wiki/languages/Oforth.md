## Summary
Oforth is a stack-based, concatenative programming language in the Forth tradition, created by Franck Bui-Huu around 2013. It blends Forth's reverse-Polish, postfix word composition with object-oriented features, adding classes, methods, closures, and higher-order combinators alongside dynamic typing where values carry their own type at runtime. The language runs on its own virtual machine, supports interactive REPL use as well as compiled programs, and provides a sizable standard library. It is primarily used for exploratory scripting, teaching concatenative and stack-oriented concepts, and small embedded or general-purpose tasks rather than large-scale industrial deployment.
