## Summary
Ratfor (short for "Rational Fortran") is a procedural, imperative programming language created by Brian Kernighan at Bell Labs in 1975 as a preprocessor for Fortran 66. It adds C-style structured control-flow constructs — if/else, while, for, repeat/until, break, and free-form statement layout with braces — that translate down into plain, portable Fortran, letting programmers write clearer structured code without abandoning the Fortran ecosystem. Like its target language it is statically typed with Fortran's numeric and array types, and it was widely used in scientific and numerical computing through the 1970s and 1980s, most famously in the book "Software Tools" by Kernighan and Plauger. Today it survives mainly in legacy scientific codebases and as a historical milestone in the spread of structured programming.
