## Summary
Inform 7 is a domain-specific, rule-based declarative language designed by Graham Nelson and first released in 2006 for authoring interactive fiction (text adventures), compiling down to the earlier Inform 6 and ultimately to Z-machine or Glulx story files. Its most distinctive trait is a syntax modeled on natural-language English, where programs read as ordinary descriptive prose ("The kitchen is a room. The table is in the kitchen.") and behavior is expressed through rulebooks, relations, and assertions rather than conventional imperative statements. It is statically typed over a world model of kinds, objects, and properties, and emphasizes pattern-matched rules that fire in response to actions. Though general computation is possible, it is almost exclusively used by writers and hobbyists to build narrative parser-based games, making it more accessible to non-programmers than typical programming languages.
