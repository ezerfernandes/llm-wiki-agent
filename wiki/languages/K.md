## Summary
K is a terse, array-oriented programming language created by Arthur Whitney in 1993 as a descendant of APL and a precursor to its query language Q and the kdb+ database. It is dynamically typed and interpreted, treating vectors and nested lists as primitive values and favoring a dense, functional style built from single-character primitive operators and higher-order adverbs (map, reduce, scan) rather than explicit loops. K is prized for extreme expressiveness and runtime performance, and is used predominantly in high-frequency trading and financial time-series analytics, where its kdb+/Q ecosystem dominates ingestion and querying of massive columnar datasets.
