## Summary
Rocq is an interactive theorem prover and dependently typed functional language, formerly known as Coq, which was renamed to Rocq in 2025 after decades of development originating at INRIA in France in 1989. Built on the Calculus of Inductive Constructions, it features an extremely expressive, purely functional type system in which propositions are types and proofs are programs (the Curry-Howard correspondence), enabling machine-checked mathematical proofs alongside extractable, verified executable code. Programs and proofs are constructed using the Gallina specification language together with the Ltac tactic language for interactive proof scripting. It is primarily used for formal verification of software and hardware, certified compilers such as CompCert, and the formalization of advanced mathematics like the Feit-Thompson theorem.
