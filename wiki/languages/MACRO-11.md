## Summary
MACRO-11 is the assembly language and macro assembler for Digital Equipment Corporation's PDP-11 family of 16-bit minicomputers, introduced in the 1970s and used heavily under the RT-11, RSX-11, and RSTS/E operating systems. As an imperative, low-level language it offers no data typing beyond the raw bytes, words, and addressing modes of the underlying machine, with values manipulated directly through registers and memory; its defining feature is a powerful macro facility supporting conditional assembly and repeat blocks. It was the primary tool for systems programming, device drivers, and performance-critical code on the PDP-11, and remained influential because much of early Unix and DEC's own operating systems were bootstrapped on PDP-11 hardware.
