## Summary
Icon is a high-level, imperative, structured programming language created by Ralph Griswold and colleagues at the University of Arizona in the late 1970s as a successor to SNOBOL4. It is dynamically typed and is best known for its distinctive goal-directed evaluation model, in which expressions are generators that can produce sequences of results and either succeed or fail, with automatic backtracking driving control flow instead of conventional boolean conditions. The language excels at string scanning, pattern matching, and text processing, and its design ideas were later carried forward into Unicon, an object-oriented extension. Icon is primarily used for text manipulation, rapid prototyping, and as a teaching vehicle for novel control-flow concepts.
