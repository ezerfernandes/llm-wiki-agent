## Summary
LFE (Lisp Flavored Erlang) is a functional, concurrent Lisp dialect that compiles to BEAM bytecode and runs on the Erlang virtual machine, created by Robert Virding (a co-inventor of Erlang) starting around 2008. It marries Lisp's homoiconic syntax and macro-based metaprogramming with Erlang's dynamically typed, immutable, actor-model concurrency, giving full interoperability with Erlang/OTP and other BEAM languages such as Elixir. Like its host platform it favors pattern matching, lightweight processes, and message passing over shared mutable state. It is used primarily for building highly concurrent, fault-tolerant, distributed systems by developers who want Lisp ergonomics on top of the battle-tested OTP runtime.
