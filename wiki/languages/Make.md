## Summary
Make is a declarative, rule-based build automation language and tool originally created by Stuart Feldman at Bell Labs in 1976 to manage the compilation of software projects. Programs (called makefiles) consist of targets, prerequisites, and recipes, where dependency relationships form a directed acyclic graph that Make traverses to rebuild only the files whose dependencies have changed. It is dynamically and untyped, treating nearly everything as strings, and combines declarative dependency rules with embedded shell commands and a layer of macro/variable expansion. Although designed for build orchestration rather than general-purpose programming, its macro engine is Turing-complete, and Make remains ubiquitous in C/C++ and Unix toolchains, with widely used variants such as GNU Make and BSD Make.
