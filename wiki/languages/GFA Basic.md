## Summary
GFA Basic is a structured, imperative dialect of the BASIC programming language developed by the German company GFA Systemtechnik, first released in 1986 for the Atari ST and later ported to Amiga, MS-DOS, and Microsoft Windows. It departed from traditional line-numbered BASIC by emphasizing procedures, named subroutines, structured control-flow constructs (such as multi-line IF/ENDIF, WHILE/WEND, and REPEAT/UNTIL loops), and a built-in editor that enforced clean formatting. The language used statically typed variables distinguished largely by type-suffix conventions (for example, integers, floats, and strings) and was prized for its fast compiled output and direct access to the host system's hardware and operating-system calls. It saw heavy use in the late 1980s and 1990s for rapid application and game development on home computers, particularly the Atari ST, where it became one of the platform's most popular programming environments.
