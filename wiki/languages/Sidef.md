## Summary
Sidef is a high-level, dynamically typed, multi-paradigm programming language created by Daniel Șuteu around 2013, drawing heavy inspiration from Ruby, Perl, and Raku (Perl 6). It is object-oriented to the core—every value is an object with methods—while also supporting functional and imperative styles, and it offers expressive features such as lazy sequences, big-number arithmetic by default, and operator overloading. Implemented in Perl, Sidef emphasizes mathematical and number-theoretic computation, with built-in arbitrary-precision integers, rationals, and complex numbers. It is used chiefly as a concise scripting and exploratory language, and is especially popular for solving algorithmic and recreational-math challenges such as those on Rosetta Code, Project Euler, and the OEIS.
