## Summary
App Inventor is a visual, block-based programming environment for building Android (and later cross-platform) mobile applications, originally created at Google in 2010 and now maintained by MIT as MIT App Inventor. Programs are composed by dragging together interlocking puzzle-piece blocks—built on the Blockly framework and conceptually descended from Scratch—rather than writing textual code, following an event-driven paradigm where behavior is attached to component events. It is dynamically typed, with values such as numbers, text, lists, and booleans handled at runtime, and offers no manual memory management. App Inventor is aimed primarily at education and beginners, letting non-programmers and students rapidly prototype functional smartphone apps with sensors, UI components, and data without prior coding experience.
