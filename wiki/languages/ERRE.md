## Summary
ERRE is a structured, imperative procedural programming language created in Italy in the late 1980s by Mario Pluchino and Antonio Coppola, originally for the Commodore 64 and later ported to MS-DOS and Windows; its name derives from the Italian pronunciation of the letter "R". Statically typed with a small set of primitive types (integer, real, and string, distinguished by variable-name suffixes in the BASIC tradition), it was conceived as a teaching language that imposes Pascal-like block structure and named procedures on a BASIC-style syntax, eliminating unstructured GOTO-driven flow. It compiles to bytecode run by a virtual machine and is used chiefly in education and by retrocomputing hobbyists rather than in commercial software.
