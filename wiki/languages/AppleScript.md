## Summary
AppleScript is a dynamically typed scripting language created by Apple and released in 1993 as part of System 7, designed around an English-like, natural-language syntax intended to be approachable for non-programmers. It is primarily imperative and procedural with object-oriented and event-driven facets, and its defining feature is interapplication communication: scripts send Apple Events to "scriptable" applications to drive and automate them through application-defined dictionaries of commands and objects. It is used chiefly on macOS for desktop automation, gluing applications together, and orchestrating repetitive GUI and document workflows, often alongside the Automator tool, AppleScriptObjC for Cocoa access, and Script Editor.
