## Summary
PILOT (Programmed Inquiry, Learning, Or Teaching) is an imperative procedural language designed in the late 1960s by John Amsden Starkweather for authoring computer-assisted instruction. Programs are built from terse single-letter statements—T for Type (output), A for Accept (input), M for Match, J for Jump, C for Compute—each optionally guarded by a yes/no condition derived from the most recent match, giving it a distinctive line-oriented, label-driven control flow. It is dynamically typed with a small repertoire of string and numeric variables and was standardized by IEEE in 1991. Once popular on early microcomputers and minicomputers for building interactive quizzes and tutorials, PILOT is now largely of historical and educational interest.
