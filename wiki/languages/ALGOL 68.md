## Summary
ALGOL 68 is an imperative, procedural, block-structured programming language defined by IFIP Working Group 2.1 in 1968 as the successor to ALGOL 60. It is statically and strongly typed with an unusually rigorous orthogonal design, introducing influential concepts such as user-defined modes (types), unions, references, coercions, expression-oriented syntax, and structured concurrency. Famous for its formal definition via a two-level van Wijngaarden grammar, the language was considerably more complex than its predecessor, which limited its adoption but deeply influenced later languages like C, Pascal, and Ada. Today it is primarily of historical and academic interest, surviving mainly through implementations such as ALGOL 68 Genie.
