## Summary
Chef is an esoteric, stack-oriented programming language created by David Morgan-Mar in 2002 whose defining gimmick is that every program must read as a plausible cooking recipe, complete with a title, an ingredients list, and numbered method steps. Numeric and character data are stored in "ingredients" whose values are manipulated through mixing bowls and baking dishes that behave as stacks, and operations such as "put", "fold", "add", and "liquefy" double as both culinary instructions and arithmetic or I/O primitives. It is dynamically typed in the loosest sense, with ingredients treated as either dry (numeric) or liquid (character) quantities, and supports recursion through auxiliary recipes invoked via the "Serves" directive. Built purely for amusement and as a design challenge rather than practical use, Chef is a staple of recreational programming and code-golf-style puzzles where the goal is writing working programs that also read as edible recipes.
