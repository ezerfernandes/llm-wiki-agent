## Summary
Joy is a purely functional, concatenative programming language designed by Manfred von Thun around 2001, building on ideas from FP, Forth, and the lambda calculus. Rather than applying functions to named arguments, Joy programs are written as compositions of functions that operate on a single shared stack, making composition the fundamental operation and eliminating the need for variables. It is dynamically typed and treats programs themselves as data — quoted programs are first-class values manipulated by higher-order combinators such as map, fold, and the recursion-defining combinators that replace explicit recursion. Joy is primarily an academic and research language, valued for its elegant algebraic semantics and as the canonical example of concatenative, point-free functional programming.
