## Summary
QB64 is an imperative, procedural BASIC dialect first released in 2007 as a modern, open-source successor to Microsoft QBasic and QuickBASIC, designed to be largely backward-compatible with their syntax. Rather than interpreting code, it transpiles BASIC source to C++ and compiles it through a GCC toolchain to produce native, self-contained executables for Windows, macOS, and Linux. It is statically typed with the familiar BASIC type-sigil conventions (such as % for integers, ! for singles, and $ for strings) alongside explicit DIM declarations, while extending the language with built-in graphics, sound, file, and networking facilities. QB64 is typically used for retro-style game development, hobbyist and educational programming, and modernizing or running legacy QBasic/QuickBASIC programs on contemporary systems.
