## Summary
ActionScript is an object-oriented, multi-paradigm programming language derived from ECMAScript, introduced by Macromedia in 1998 and later developed by Adobe for the Flash Player and Adobe AIR runtimes. Its dominant version, ActionScript 3.0 (2006), runs on the AVM2 virtual machine and offers optional static typing layered over a dynamic, prototype-influenced core, along with class-based inheritance, packages, and event-driven programming. Historically it powered interactive web animations, browser games, rich internet applications, and multimedia content embedded in Flash. With the deprecation and end-of-life of Flash Player in 2020, its use has sharply declined, surviving mainly in legacy systems and the open-source Apache Royale and Adobe AIR ecosystems.
