## Summary
S-Lang is a dynamically typed, interpreted multi-paradigm scripting language created by John E. Davis in the early 1990s, distributed primarily as an embeddable C library that provides an interpreter, terminal/screen management, and array-oriented numerical facilities. Its syntax borrows from C while supporting imperative, functional, and object-oriented styles, with first-class support for vectorized array operations reminiscent of MATLAB or IDL. It is best known as the extension and macro language embedded in widely used applications such as the JED text editor, the Mutt mail client, and the slrn newsreader, where it serves as a lightweight, portable means of scripting and customization.
