## Summary
APL (A Programming Language) is an array-oriented, functional language created by Kenneth Iverson in the 1960s, growing out of the mathematical notation he developed at Harvard and IBM. It is dynamically typed and treats multidimensional arrays as first-class values, applying primitive operators that work on whole arrays at once, expressed through a famously terse set of special symbols (such as ⍳, ⍴, and ⌿) rather than keywords. The language emphasizes concise, expression-based programming and pioneered concepts like higher-order operators and implicit iteration that influenced later array and functional languages. Historically used for interactive mathematical exploration, statistics, and financial and actuarial modeling, APL survives today in commercial dialects like Dyalog APL and open-source descendants such as J, K, and GNU APL.
