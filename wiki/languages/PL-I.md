## Summary
PL/I (Programming Language One) is an imperative, procedural, structured language developed by IBM in the mid-1960s with the ambition of unifying the scientific computing of Fortran and the business data processing of COBOL into a single general-purpose language. It is statically typed with rich data declarations, supporting fixed- and floating-point arithmetic, bit and character strings, structures, arrays, and pointer-based storage, along with notable features for its era such as exception handling via ON-conditions, multitasking, and recursion. The language was designed for mainframe environments and remains in use chiefly for legacy enterprise and systems software on IBM platforms (z/OS), with the PL/M and PL/S subsets historically used for systems programming. Its free-form syntax, optional keywords, and default attribute rules made it expressive but complex, contributing to its decline relative to more focused languages.
