## Summary
Wart is an experimental, dynamically typed Lisp dialect created by Kartik Agaram in the early 2010s as a research vehicle for exploring more readable and malleable language design. It blends functional and procedural styles, replacing Lisp's heavy parenthesization with significant indentation and infix-friendly syntax while retaining macros, first-class functions, and a small extensible core. Its semantics emphasize generic functions and dispatch on argument structure, letting programmers redefine and reshape the language from within. Wart is primarily a personal and academic exploration of programmer-friendly design rather than a tool intended for production software.
