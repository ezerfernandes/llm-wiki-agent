## Summary
IDL (Interactive Data Language) is a dynamically typed, array-oriented language created by David Stern in 1977 (commercialized through Research Systems Inc., now owned by NV5 Geospatial/L3Harris) for interactive numerical analysis and data visualization. It is primarily imperative and procedural with optional object-oriented features, and uses vectorized whole-array operations in a style reminiscent of APL and MATLAB. IDL is heavily used in scientific and engineering domains such as astronomy, remote sensing, atmospheric physics, and medical imaging, where it excels at processing and plotting large multidimensional datasets. The open-source GDL (GNU Data Language) provides a largely compatible free implementation.
