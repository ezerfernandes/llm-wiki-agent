## Summary
BCPL (Basic Combined Programming Language) is an imperative, procedural language designed by Martin Richards at the University of Cambridge in 1967 as a simplified successor to CPL, intended primarily for writing compilers and systems software. It is famously typeless: the single data type is a fixed-length machine word, and the programmer decides through operators whether to treat a word as an integer, a bit pattern, a character, or a pointer (memory is addressed in word-sized cells via the `!` and `%` operators). Compiled to a portable intermediate code called O-code (later INTCODE), BCPL was highly portable for its era and ran the first "Hello, World" program in a tutorial by Richards; it directly inspired Ken Thompson's B language, which in turn led to C. Today it survives mainly in historical and educational contexts, though Richards has maintained a modern distribution.
