## Summary
Sed (stream editor) is a non-interactive, line-oriented text-processing tool created by Lee E. McMahon at Bell Labs around 1973-1974 as part of early Unix, derived from the line editor ed. Its programs are declarative scripts built from single-letter commands that operate on a pattern space and hold space, with regular-expression addressing driving substitution, deletion, insertion, and branching; it has no typed variables, treating all data as untyped text streams. Though Turing-complete (it offers conditional branches and a loop construct), sed is overwhelmingly used in practice for stream filtering and editing in shell pipelines—find-and-replace, extraction, and transformation of text—rather than for general-purpose programming.
