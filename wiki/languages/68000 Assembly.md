## Summary
68000 Assembly is the low-level, imperative assembly language for Motorola's 68000 (m68k) family of CISC microprocessors, introduced in 1979 and assembled directly into machine code with a one-to-one correspondence between mnemonics and CPU instructions. It is untyped, operating on raw bytes, words, and longwords held in a clean, largely orthogonal register set of eight data registers and eight address registers, with rich addressing modes and explicit programmer control over memory and flags. The 68000 powered iconic systems of the 1980s and early 1990s such as the original Apple Macintosh, Commodore Amiga, Atari ST, and the Sega Genesis. Today it is used chiefly in retrocomputing, embedded systems, demoscene work, and education on computer architecture, where its readable instruction set makes it a popular teaching assembly language.
