## Summary
Maxima is a computer algebra system (CAS) and the dynamically typed, interpreted language that drives it, descended from MIT's Macsyma — one of the earliest symbolic mathematics systems, begun in the late 1960s — and maintained as free software since the 1998 release of a Common Lisp branch (Maxima itself is implemented in Lisp). It supports symbolic and arbitrary-precision exact numeric computation, blending a functional and term-rewriting style with imperative control flow and pattern-matching rules, where expressions are first-class data that can be manipulated, simplified, and evaluated. It is typically used for interactive and scripted mathematics: algebraic manipulation, calculus (differentiation, integration, limits, series), solving equations, linear algebra, and generating plots, often via interfaces such as wxMaxima.
