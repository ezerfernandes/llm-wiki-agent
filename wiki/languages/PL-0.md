## Summary
PL/0 is a small imperative, procedural programming language designed by Niklaus Wirth and introduced in his 1976 book "Algorithms + Data Structures = Programs" as a minimal teaching subset of Pascal. It is statically typed but offers only a single data type, the integer, and supports constants, variables, nested procedures, arithmetic and comparison operators, assignment, conditional (if) and loop (while) statements, and procedure calls. Lacking arrays, real numbers, parameters, and most of Pascal's richness, PL/0 is deliberately spartan and is used almost exclusively in compiler-construction courses, where its compact recursive-descent compiler targeting a simple stack machine illustrates lexical analysis, parsing, and code generation. It remains a canonical pedagogical example rather than a language for production software.
