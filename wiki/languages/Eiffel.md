## Summary
Eiffel is a statically typed, purely object-oriented programming language designed by Bertrand Meyer and first released in 1986. Its defining feature is Design by Contract, a methodology built into the language whereby routines specify preconditions, postconditions, and class invariants that are checked at runtime to enforce correctness. Eiffel emphasizes software reliability, reusability, and maintainability, and contributed influential ideas such as command-query separation, multiple inheritance with renaming and feature adaptation, and uniform access. It is used primarily in education and in enterprise and mission-critical systems where rigorous, contract-based reasoning about correctness is valued.
