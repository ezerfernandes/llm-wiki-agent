## Summary
Yorick is an interpreted, dynamically typed scripting language created by David H. Munro at Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory in the mid-1990s for numerical analysis and the postprocessing and visualization of large scientific datasets. It is primarily imperative and array-oriented, offering MATLAB- or APL-style whole-array operations with terse C-like syntax, so vectorized computations on multidimensional arrays can be expressed compactly and run efficiently. The language is designed for easy embedding and extension with compiled C and Fortran code, and ships with built-in interactive 2D and 3D plotting facilities. It is most commonly used in physics, astronomy, and engineering for simulation analysis, adaptive optics, and exploratory data crunching.
