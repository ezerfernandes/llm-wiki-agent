## Summary
ABC is an imperative, structured programming language developed at CWI in the Netherlands during the 1980s as an interactive teaching and prototyping language intended to replace BASIC. It is dynamically typed yet strongly typed, offering high-level built-in data structures such as arbitrary-precision numbers, lists, tables, and compounds, and it famously uses indentation rather than braces or keywords to delimit blocks. Designed for clarity and ease of learning, ABC heavily influenced Python, whose creator Guido van Rossum worked on the ABC implementation. It is mainly of historical and educational interest today, valued for its concise, readable syntax and its role as a direct ancestor of modern scripting languages.
