## Summary
Frink is a general-purpose, dynamically typed programming language created by Alan Eliasen in 2001, designed around the principle that all numeric quantities carry physical units of measure that are tracked and dimensionally checked throughout every calculation. It blends imperative, functional, and object-oriented styles, and its number type transparently supports arbitrary-precision integers, exact rational arithmetic, intervals, and a vast built-in database of units and physical constants. Frink runs on the Java Virtual Machine (with a separate Android implementation) and is typically used for unit-aware scientific and engineering computation, quick numerical and date/time calculations, and exploratory "back of the envelope" problem solving where dimensional correctness matters.
