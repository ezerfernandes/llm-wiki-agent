## Summary
VAX Assembly is the low-level, imperative assembly language for Digital Equipment Corporation's VAX architecture, a 32-bit CISC family introduced in 1977 with the VAX-11/780 and programmed predominantly under the VMS operating system. As an assembly language it is untyped, operating directly on machine registers, memory, and a famously rich orthogonal instruction set with elaborate addressing modes and complex instructions for queues, strings, decimal arithmetic, and polynomial evaluation. The MACRO-32 assembler translates these mnemonics one-to-one into machine code while providing macros and directives for structured source. Today it is essentially historical, encountered chiefly in legacy VMS/OpenVMS systems, retrocomputing, and as a teaching example of mature CISC design.
