## Summary
360 Assembly is the low-level assembly language for the IBM System/360 mainframe architecture, introduced by IBM in 1964 and carried forward through the System/370, System/390, and modern z/Architecture lines. It follows an imperative, register-based machine model in which the programmer manipulates 16 general-purpose registers and main storage directly, with no inherent type system beyond the byte-, halfword-, fullword-, and packed-decimal data formats interpreted by individual instructions. Programs are written for IBM's High Level Assembler (HLASM), making heavy use of macros, base-displacement addressing, and EBCDIC character data. It remains in use for performance-critical and legacy system software, operating system internals (such as z/OS), and financial and enterprise batch processing on IBM mainframes.
