## Summary
Aikido is a general-purpose, dynamically typed scripting language created by David Allison at Sun Microsystems in the early 2000s, with a C- and Java-influenced syntax that supports multiple paradigms including procedural, object-oriented, and functional programming. It offers built-in concurrency through native threads, monitors, and coroutines, along with first-class support for system-level scripting, sockets, and operating-system integration. The language was originally designed and used for debugging, test automation, and tooling within Sun, and was later released as open source. In practice it is most often applied to general scripting, prototyping, and lightweight system administration tasks rather than large-scale production software.
