## Summary
Mercury is a purely declarative logic/functional programming language developed at the University of Melbourne in the mid-1990s, syntactically descended from Prolog but with a strict static type, mode, and determinism system enforced at compile time. It is purely declarative: side effects are threaded through the program via unique-mode "io" state arguments rather than implicit global state, and its strong static analysis enables aggressive optimization and compilation to efficient C, Java, C#, or Erlang code. Mercury is used primarily for large, reliability-critical applications and research where the combination of logic programming expressiveness and ahead-of-time correctness guarantees is valued, with its own compiler and parts of the Prince/PrinceXML HTML-to-PDF engine being notable real-world deployments.
