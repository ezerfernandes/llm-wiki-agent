## Summary
Acornsoft Lisp is a dialect of the Lisp programming language released by Acornsoft in the early 1980s for Acorn's 8-bit microcomputers, principally the BBC Micro and the Acorn Atom. Like other Lisps it is a dynamically typed, functional language built around symbolic expressions (s-expressions) and list processing, with code and data sharing the same parenthesized representation and evaluation driven by recursion. Constrained by the limited memory and 6502 hardware of its era, it offered a compact interpreter aimed at hobbyists, students, and educators exploring artificial intelligence and symbolic computation on home machines. It was used chiefly for teaching the concepts of Lisp and functional programming rather than for large-scale production software.
