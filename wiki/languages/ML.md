## Summary
ML (Meta Language) is a family of functional programming languages that originated in the early 1970s at the University of Edinburgh, where Robin Milner designed it as the metalanguage for the LCF theorem prover. It is statically typed with a powerful Hindley-Milner type system that supports automatic type inference, parametric polymorphism, and algebraic data types with pattern matching, while remaining impure by permitting controlled side effects and mutable references. Its design pioneered features now standard across modern functional languages, and its descendants -- notably Standard ML and OCaml -- form a long-lived dialect family. ML is most commonly used in compiler construction, theorem proving and formal verification, programming-language research, and education.
