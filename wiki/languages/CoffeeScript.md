## Summary
CoffeeScript is a multi-paradigm scripting language created by Jeremy Ashkenas in 2009 that compiles directly to JavaScript, layering a terse, Python- and Ruby-inspired syntax over JavaScript's semantics. It is dynamically and weakly typed, leans on significant whitespace and implicit returns to eliminate boilerplate, and embraces both object-oriented (with first-class class syntax) and functional styles, along with conveniences like list comprehensions, destructuring, and the existential operator. Released in the early 2010s, it gained substantial popularity in the Rails and Node.js communities before its influence waned as ES6/ES2015 absorbed many of its best ideas (arrow functions, classes, destructuring). Today it is used mainly for maintaining legacy web and Node codebases and as a more expressive alternative for developers who prefer its concise syntax.
