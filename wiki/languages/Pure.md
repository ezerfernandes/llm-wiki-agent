## Summary
Pure is a dynamically typed, functional programming language built on term rewriting, created by Albert Gräf around 2008 as the successor to his earlier Q language. Programs are expressed as collections of equations that the interpreter evaluates by rewriting expressions to normal form, with eager (call-by-value) evaluation, support for lazy lists and higher-order functions, and seamless interoperability with C and the LLVM compiler infrastructure used for JIT compilation. Its blend of symbolic computation and native-code performance makes it well suited to mathematical and scientific computing, computer algebra, signal processing, and scripting tasks, and it ships with bindings to libraries such as GSL and Octave.
