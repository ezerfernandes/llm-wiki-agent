## Summary
Applesoft BASIC is an imperative, line-numbered dialect of the BASIC programming language that shipped in ROM on the Apple II family of computers from 1978 onward, having been licensed and adapted by Microsoft from its 6502 floating-point BASIC. It is an interpreted language with weak, dynamic typing: variables are implicitly real (floating-point) unless suffixed with `%` for 16-bit integers or `$` for strings, and only the first two characters of a variable name are significant. Control flow relies on GOTO, GOSUB, and FOR/NEXT loops, while platform-specific features expose low-resolution and high-resolution graphics, sound, and direct memory access through PEEK, POKE, and CALL. Historically it served as the primary programming environment and educational language for hobbyists and early home-computer users throughout the late 1970s and 1980s.
