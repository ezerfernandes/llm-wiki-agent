## Summary
Miranda is a purely functional, lazily evaluated programming language designed by David Turner and first released in 1985 by Research Software Ltd. It features a statically typed, polymorphic type system with Hindley-Milner type inference, uses non-strict (lazy) evaluation by default, and relies on a clean, equation-based syntax with significant indentation (the offside rule) and pattern matching. As a commercial successor to Turner's earlier languages SASL and KRC, Miranda was highly influential on the design of Haskell and was used primarily in academia for teaching functional programming and in research, though it has since been largely supplanted by Haskell.
