## Summary
Sather is a statically typed, object-oriented programming language originally developed at the International Computer Science Institute at UC Berkeley in the early 1990s, initially inspired by Eiffel but later diverging significantly. It emphasizes a clean separation between subtyping (abstract classes) and code reuse (implementation inheritance via inclusion), and incorporates functional-style features such as iterators, closures (bound routines), and higher-order constructs alongside parametric polymorphism through generics. Designed with efficiency, safety, and expressiveness in mind, it offered garbage collection, strong type checking, and support for design-by-contract style pre/post conditions. Sather saw use chiefly in academic and research settings and was succeeded by GNU Sather; it is largely of historical interest today, having influenced later language designs.
