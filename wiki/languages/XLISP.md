## Summary
XLISP is a small, lightweight dialect of Lisp created by David Betz in the early-to-mid 1980s, originally written in C to be portable and easy to embed in other programs. It is primarily a functional and dynamically typed language in the Lisp tradition, but it pioneered an object-oriented extension built around classes and message passing, making it one of the earliest Lisps to integrate objects directly into the core. XLISP was notable for its compact implementation and educational value, and it served as the foundation for XLISP-STAT (statistical computing) and Autodesk's AutoLISP. Today it is mainly of historical and pedagogical interest, surviving in derivatives such as the Nyquist sound-synthesis language used by the Audacity audio editor.
