## Summary
LiveCode is a high-level, event-driven programming language and rapid application development environment built around an English-like, natural-language syntax descended from Apple's HyperTalk and the MetaCard/Revolution lineage. First released in 2001 (and open-sourced as LiveCode Community in 2013), it is dynamically and weakly typed, treating most values as untyped strings that are coerced contextually, and follows a message-passing, procedural model where handlers respond to events flowing through a hierarchy of stacks, cards, and controls. It is primarily used for cross-platform GUI application development, deploying the same codebase to desktop, mobile, and web with minimal change. Its readable, low-barrier syntax makes it especially popular for education, prototyping, and citizen-developer or business productivity tools.
