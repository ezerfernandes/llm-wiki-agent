## Summary
UnixPipes is not a conventional programming language but a dataflow-style approach to composing programs from the small command-line utilities of a Unix shell, connected by the pipe operator (`|`) so that the standard output of one process is streamed into the standard input of the next. Rooted in the Unix philosophy articulated at Bell Labs in the early 1970s by Ken Thompson, Dennis Ritchie, and Doug McIlroy (who introduced pipes in 1973), it favors building solutions by chaining filters such as grep, sed, awk, sort, and tr rather than writing monolithic code. It is essentially untyped, operating on untyped byte and line streams of text, and execution is concurrent since piped processes run in parallel with the kernel handling buffering and synchronization. It remains in everyday use for text processing, log analysis, system administration, and ad hoc data transformation across Unix, Linux, and macOS systems.
