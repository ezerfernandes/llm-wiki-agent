## Summary
Draco is an imperative, structured, procedural programming language designed by Chris Gray in the early 1980s, drawing heavily on ALGOL, Pascal, and C while introducing its own concise syntax. It is statically and strongly typed, offering rich support for records, unions, pointers, and bit-level operations, and it compiles to native code on platforms such as CP/M and the Commodore Amiga, where it saw its most notable use. Intended as a systems and general-purpose language, Draco favored compact, efficient programs and gave programmers low-level control reminiscent of C alongside the readability of the ALGOL family. Today it survives largely as a retrocomputing and Rosetta Code curiosity rather than in active mainstream development.
