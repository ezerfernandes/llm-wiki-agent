## Summary
Fortran (from "Formula Translation") is a statically and strongly typed, compiled, imperative and procedural language originally developed by John Backus and his team at IBM in 1957, making it the oldest high-level programming language still in widespread use. Designed from the outset for numerical and scientific computation, it offers first-class support for multidimensional arrays, complex numbers, and high-performance floating-point arithmetic, and successive standards (FORTRAN 77, Fortran 90/95, and Fortran 2003/2008/2018) have added free-form source, modules, array notation, object-oriented features, and coarray-based parallelism. It remains dominant in high-performance and scientific computing domains such as computational physics, climate and weather modeling, computational fluid dynamics, and large-scale numerical libraries like BLAS and LAPACK, where its mature optimizing compilers and decades-old legacy codebases keep it entrenched.
