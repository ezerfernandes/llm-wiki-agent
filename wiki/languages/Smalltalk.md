## Summary
Smalltalk is a purely object-oriented, dynamically and strongly typed language created at Xerox PARC in the 1970s by Alan Kay, Dan Ingalls, Adele Goldberg, and others, with Smalltalk-80 becoming its definitive standardized form. Everything in the language is an object, computation proceeds entirely by sending messages to objects, and even control flow constructs like conditionals and loops are expressed as messages to objects rather than as built-in syntax. It pioneered many ideas now central to modern computing, including the integrated graphical IDE, live image-based development, just-in-time compilation, and reflection. Today it survives mainly through implementations such as Pharo, Squeak, GNU Smalltalk, and VisualWorks, and remains influential in research, education, and enterprise systems while also being the direct ancestor of object-oriented features in languages like Ruby, Objective-C, and Java.
