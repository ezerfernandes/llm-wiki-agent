## Summary
Nascom BASIC is an imperative, line-numbered dialect of BASIC developed by Microsoft (as a variant of its 8080 BASIC) and supplied for the Nascom 1 and Nascom 2 single-board microcomputers, which appeared in the United Kingdom from 1977 onward. Like other microcomputer BASICs of the era, it is an interpreted language with weak, dynamic typing built around numeric and string variables, GOTO/GOSUB control flow, and direct PEEK/POKE access to the Z80 machine's memory. It was used primarily for hobbyist programming, education, and small utility and game programs on these kit-built home computers.
