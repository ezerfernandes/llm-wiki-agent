## Summary
Agda is a purely functional, dependently typed programming language and interactive proof assistant developed at Chalmers University of Technology, with its modern incarnation (Agda 2) created by Ulf Norell in the mid-2000s as a descendant of earlier 1990s systems. It features a powerful dependent type system based on intuitionistic (Martin-Löf) type theory, where types can depend on values, enabling programmers to encode precise specifications and machine-checked correctness proofs directly within types under the Curry-Howard correspondence. With Haskell-like syntax, support for Unicode identifiers, inductive families, pattern matching, and a totality checker, it emphasizes total functional programming. Agda is used primarily in academic research and education for formalizing mathematics, verifying software, and exploring the foundations of type theory rather than for general-purpose application development.
