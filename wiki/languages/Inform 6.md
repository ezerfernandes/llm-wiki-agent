## Summary
Inform 6 is a domain-specific, object-oriented programming language created by Graham Nelson in 1993 for authoring interactive fiction (text adventures). Programs are compiled to story files for the Z-machine (and later the Glulx virtual machine), the same bytecode format pioneered by Infocom. The language is weakly and dynamically typed, treating most values as untyped 16-bit (or 32-bit on Glulx) words, and it blends imperative procedural code with a rich object/class system featuring properties and attributes well suited to modeling rooms, items, and characters. While Inform 7 introduced a natural-language syntax layer on top, Inform 6 remains in use both as a lower-level target and directly by authors who prefer its concise C-like notation.
