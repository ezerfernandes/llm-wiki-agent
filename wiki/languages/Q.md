## Summary
Q is a concise, array-oriented programming language created by Arthur Whitney at Kx Systems in 2003 as a more readable layer atop the terse APL-derived k language. It is a dynamically typed, functional language built around vectors and tables as first-class data structures, with deep integration into the kdb+ in-memory column-store database and its embedded query dialect q-sql. Q emphasizes vectorized, expression-oriented computation rather than explicit looping, favoring higher-order functions like each, over, and scan for iteration. Its primary use is high-performance time-series and analytic workloads, most prominently in quantitative finance for processing large volumes of streaming and historical market data.
