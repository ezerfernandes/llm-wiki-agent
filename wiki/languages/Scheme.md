## Summary
Scheme is a minimalist dialect of Lisp, created by Guy L. Steele and Gerald Jay Sussman at MIT in 1975, that emphasizes a small, clean core with powerful general-purpose abstraction facilities. It is a multi-paradigm language with strong support for functional programming, featuring first-class procedures, proper lexical scoping, guaranteed tail-call optimization, and first-class continuations (call/cc); it is dynamically typed and uses fully parenthesized prefix (S-expression) syntax along with hygienic macros for metaprogramming. Standardized through successive Revised^n Reports on Scheme (RnRS, e.g. R5RS, R6RS, R7RS), it is widely used for teaching programming concepts, language research, and as an extension and scripting language (notably GNU Guile as the official extension language of the GNU project).
