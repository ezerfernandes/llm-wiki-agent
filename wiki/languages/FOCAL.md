## Summary
FOCAL (Formulating On-Line Calculations in Algebraic Language) is an imperative, line-numbered procedural language created by Richard Merrill at Digital Equipment Corporation in 1968 for the PDP-8 and other DEC minicomputers. Closely resembling JOSS and a contemporary of early BASIC, it is dynamically typed around a single numeric (floating-point) data type, with terse single-letter commands such as SET, TYPE, ASK, DO, FOR, and IF abbreviated to their first letter. Programs run under an interactive interpreter resident in only a few kilobytes of core memory, making FOCAL well suited to teaching, laboratory calculation, and time-sharing on resource-constrained 1960s-70s hardware. Today it survives mainly through PDP-8 emulators and retrocomputing enthusiasts.
