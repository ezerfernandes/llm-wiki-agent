## Summary
Asymptote is a descriptive vector graphics language created by Andy Hammerlindl, John Bowman, and Tom Prince around 2004, designed as a modern successor to MetaPost for producing publication-quality technical figures and 2D/3D scientific illustrations. It is statically typed and imperative with C/C++-like syntax, supporting functions as first-class values, operator overloading, and high-level data types, while integrating tightly with LaTeX so that labels and equations are typeset natively. Its coordinate system is fully programmable through mathematical expressions and Bezier path constructions, and output is rendered to PostScript, PDF, SVG, or 3D PRC formats. It is used primarily by mathematicians, physicists, and engineers to script precise diagrams, plots, and geometric figures for academic papers and textbooks.
