## Summary
Simula is a statically typed, ALGOL-derived language developed at the Norwegian Computing Center by Ole-Johan Dahl and Kristen Nygaard, first as Simula I (1962-1965) for discrete-event simulation and then generalized as Simula 67 (1967). It is historically significant as the first language to introduce objects, classes, inheritance (subclassing), virtual procedures, and coroutines, making it the original object-oriented programming language and a direct ancestor of C++, Smalltalk, and most later OOP languages. Imperative and block-structured in the ALGOL tradition, Simula combines compile-time type checking with garbage collection and a built-in SIMSET/SIMULATION framework for process-based modeling. Though largely superseded for production work, it remains influential in programming-language history and is still used for teaching simulation and OOP fundamentals.
