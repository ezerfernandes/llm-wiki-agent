## Summary
RLaB is an interpreted, dynamically typed scripting language and interactive environment for numerical computation, created by Ian Searle in the early 1990s as an open-source alternative to MATLAB. It follows an imperative, array-oriented paradigm built around matrices and higher-dimensional numeric arrays as its core data types, with values stored primarily as double-precision floating point and complex numbers. RLaB integrates established numerical libraries such as BLAS, LAPACK, FFTPACK, and RANLIB, and supports graphics through Gnuplot or PlPlot. It is typically used for scientific and engineering prototyping, linear algebra, signal processing, and other matrix-heavy numerical analysis tasks.
