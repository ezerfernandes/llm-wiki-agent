## Summary
Salmon is a general-purpose, multi-paradigm programming language created by David A. Wheeler in the 2000s, designed around an extensible grammar that lets programmers add new syntax and constructs through its built-in macro and language-extension facilities. It supports imperative, object-oriented, and functional styles with static typing, while emphasizing flexibility so that domain-specific notations can be embedded directly in source code. The language is largely experimental and academic in nature, used primarily to demonstrate ideas about syntactic extensibility and expressive power rather than seeing widespread production deployment.
