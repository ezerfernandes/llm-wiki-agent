## Summary
МК-61/52 is the imperative, low-level keystroke-programming language of the Soviet Elektronika MK-61 and MK-52 programmable RPN pocket calculators, produced from the early-to-mid 1980s. Programs are entered as a linear sequence of numbered instruction steps that manipulate a four-register operational stack (X, Y, Z, T) and a bank of addressable memory registers, using reverse Polish notation, conditional and unconditional jumps, subroutine calls, and indirect addressing rather than named variables or structured control flow. It is dynamically and weakly typed in practice, operating on a single floating-point numeric domain, and is famous among Russian-speaking enthusiasts both for tightly optimized numerical routines and for the calculators' undocumented "Eggogology" quirks. Today it survives mainly as a retro-computing and educational curiosity, exercised through emulators.
