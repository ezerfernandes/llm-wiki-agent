## Summary
ECL (Enterprise Control Language) is a declarative, data-centric programming language created in the early 2000s by Seisint (later acquired by LexisNexis Risk Solutions) for the HPCC Systems big-data platform. It is statically typed and built around an implicit dataflow model: programmers describe transformations over datasets rather than imperative step-by-step control flow, and the ECL compiler optimizes and distributes the resulting computation across a cluster. The language favors immutability and lazy evaluation, with operations composed through definitions that the engine schedules in parallel. It is used primarily for large-scale ETL, data integration, and analytics workloads on HPCC's Thor (batch) and Roxie (query-serving) clusters.
