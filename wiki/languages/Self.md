## Summary
Self is a pure object-oriented, dynamically typed programming language developed at Xerox PARC and Sun Microsystems beginning in 1986, designed by David Ungar and Randall Smith as a successor to Smalltalk. It is prototype-based rather than class-based: there are no classes, and new objects are created by cloning existing prototypes, with all computation expressed through message passing and even local variable access modeled as slot access. Pioneering implementation research on Self produced foundational techniques such as polymorphic inline caching, type feedback, and adaptive just-in-time compilation that later shaped high-performance virtual machines like Java's HotSpot and modern JavaScript engines. Today it is primarily of academic and historical interest, valued for its live, exploratory programming environment and its influence on dynamic-language runtime design.
