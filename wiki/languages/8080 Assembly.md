## Summary
8080 Assembly is the low-level assembly language for the Intel 8080, an 8-bit microprocessor introduced in 1974 that became foundational to early personal computing and the CP/M operating system. It follows an imperative, register-and-memory model with no type system: programmers manipulate the accumulator, register pairs (BC, DE, HL), the stack pointer, and flags directly through mnemonic instructions that map one-to-one to machine opcodes. Typical uses include systems programming, firmware, embedded control, and performance-critical routines on 8080-family hardware and its binary-compatible successors such as the 8085 and Zilog Z80. Programs are hand-assembled or processed by an assembler into raw machine code, demanding explicit management of memory layout, subroutine calls, and I/O ports.
