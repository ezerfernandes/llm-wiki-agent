## Summary
AutoHotkey is an imperative, procedural scripting language for Windows, created by Chris Mallett in 2003 as a fork of AutoIt and now maintained as open source. It is dynamically and weakly typed, with values treated primarily as strings that are coerced to numbers as needed, and its later v1.1 and v2 dialects added object-oriented features such as classes, maps, and arrays. Although general-purpose, it is most widely used for desktop automation on Windows: defining global hotkeys and hotstrings, remapping keys, sending simulated keystrokes and mouse input, manipulating windows, and building small GUI utilities and text-expansion tools.
