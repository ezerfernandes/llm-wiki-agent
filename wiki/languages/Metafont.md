## Summary
Metafont is a domain-specific, declarative programming language created by Donald Knuth in 1977 as the companion to TeX, designed for describing vector fonts and the shapes of glyphs mathematically rather than as fixed bitmaps. Programs define letterforms through equations over numeric and pair (point) variables, where the system solves systems of linear equations to position pen strokes and Bezier-like curves, making it as much a constraint-solving environment as a procedural macro language. Its typing centers on a small set of numeric, pair, path, pen, and boolean types, and the language emphasizes interpolation and parametric control so a single description can generate an entire family of fonts at different sizes and weights. Today it survives mainly through the Computer Modern font family and its descendant METAPOST, and it is rarely used for general-purpose computation, though its arithmetic and macro facilities make it Turing-capable enough for the small algorithmic tasks collected here.
