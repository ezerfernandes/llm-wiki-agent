## Summary
AmigaBASIC is an interpreted, imperative procedural dialect of BASIC written by Microsoft and bundled with Commodore's Amiga personal computers from 1985 through the late 1980s. Like other early BASICs it is dynamically typed with a small set of scalar types (numeric and string, the latter denoted by a trailing dollar sign) and originally relied on line-oriented program structure, though it also supported labeled subroutines and access to the Amiga's graphics, sound, and windowing facilities. It was primarily used for hobbyist programming, learning, and quick application prototyping on the Amiga, and was later effectively superseded by faster compiled alternatives such as AMOS and Blitz BASIC.
