## Summary
COBOL (Common Business-Oriented Language) is a compiled, imperative, statically typed programming language designed in 1959 by the CODASYL committee, with Grace Hopper's earlier FLOW-MATIC as a key influence. It is known for its verbose, English-like syntax organized into four divisions (Identification, Environment, Data, and Procedure) and its fixed-point decimal arithmetic with detailed record and field definitions via the PICTURE clause, which made it well suited to precise financial and administrative data processing. Standardized repeatedly (notably COBOL-85, COBOL 2002, and COBOL 2014, which added object-oriented features), it remains heavily used in mainframe-based banking, insurance, and government batch and transaction systems where enormous volumes of legacy code are still in production. Despite its age, billions of lines of COBOL continue to run mission-critical enterprise workloads.
