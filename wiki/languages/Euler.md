## Summary
Euler is an imperative, procedural programming language designed in 1965-1966 by Niklaus Wirth and Helmut Weber as a generalization and extension of ALGOL 60, intended to demonstrate a clean, formally defined language that could be implemented efficiently. It is dynamically typed, with values carrying their type at runtime, and treats arrays, procedures, references, and symbols as first-class values that can be assigned to variables and passed around freely. The language pioneered ideas such as the use of a precise EBNF-style grammar paired with a precedence-based parser and a compact stack-based runtime. Euler was primarily an academic and research vehicle for studying language design, formal definition, and compiler construction rather than a tool for production software, and its concepts influenced Wirth's later languages such as Pascal.
