## Summary
Slate is a prototype-based, dynamically typed object-oriented programming language created by Lee Salzman and Brian Rice in the early 2000s, blending the prototype object model of Self with the multiple dispatch of CLOS. Its distinctive feature is "multiple prototype-based dispatch," combining prototype-based inheritance with multimethods, allowing methods to be selected on the runtime types of several arguments at once. Syntactically influenced by Smalltalk, it runs on a bytecode virtual machine and was conceived as a research and general-purpose language exploring expressive object-oriented and metaprogramming techniques. It remained largely experimental and is primarily of academic and historical interest rather than mainstream production use.
