## Summary
Prolog is a logic programming language created in 1972 by Alain Colmerauer and Robert Kowalski, rooted in first-order predicate logic and Horn clauses. It is declarative: programs are expressed as facts and rules, and computation proceeds through unification and chronological backtracking driven by an SLD-resolution query engine rather than explicit control flow. Prolog is dynamically typed and untyped at the term level, working with logic variables, atoms, numbers, and compound terms (including lists), and its order of clause and goal evaluation is significant in practice. It has historically been central to artificial intelligence, expert systems, natural-language processing, theorem proving, and symbolic reasoning, with widely used implementations such as SWI-Prolog and GNU Prolog.
