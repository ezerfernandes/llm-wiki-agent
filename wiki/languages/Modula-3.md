## Summary
Modula-3 is a structured, imperative, and modular systems programming language with statically typed, strongly typed semantics, designed in the late 1980s at DEC's Systems Research Center and Olivetti as a successor to Niklaus Wirth's Modula-2 and Mesa. It combines low-level systems capabilities with safety features such as garbage collection, exceptions, generics, lightweight object-oriented support via single-inheritance objects, and first-class threading, while keeping the language deliberately small and orthogonal. A hallmark is its explicit distinction between safe and UNSAFE modules and its rigorous interface/implementation separation. Though never widely adopted commercially, it was influential in academia and language research—notably shaping aspects of Java and C#—and was used for operating systems and compiler work.
