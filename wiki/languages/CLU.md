## Summary
CLU is an imperative, procedural programming language developed by Barbara Liskov and her students at MIT in the mid-to-late 1970s. It is statically typed and is historically significant as one of the first languages to fully support data abstraction through encapsulated abstract data types called "clusters" (the source of its name), along with pioneering features such as iterators, parametric polymorphism, exception handling, and type-safe variant unions. Though primarily a research and teaching language that never saw wide commercial adoption, CLU was deeply influential on later languages like C++, Java, Python, and Ruby, especially in their treatment of iterators and abstraction. It is typically used today for studying programming-language design and the foundations of object-oriented and modular programming.
