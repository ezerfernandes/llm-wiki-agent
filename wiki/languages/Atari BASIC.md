## Summary
Atari BASIC is an imperative, procedural dialect of the BASIC programming language built into the ROM of the Atari 8-bit family of home computers (the 400 and 800), introduced in 1979 and developed for Atari by Shepardson Microsystems. Like most early microcomputer BASICs it is an interpreted, line-numbered language with weak, dynamic typing centered on floating-point numbers and string variables, distinguished by its unusual handling of strings (fixed-length, DIM-allocated, with no string arrays) and its tokenized program storage. It was used primarily for hobbyist programming, education, and games on the Atari home computers throughout the 1980s, typically driving the machine's distinctive graphics and sound hardware through PEEK, POKE, and dedicated graphics and sound statements.
