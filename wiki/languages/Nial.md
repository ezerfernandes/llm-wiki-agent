## Summary
Nial (Nested Interactive Array Language) is a high-level, array-oriented functional programming language developed by Mike Jenkins and Trenchard More at Queen's University in the early 1980s, grounded in More's mathematical "array theory." Like APL and J, it treats nested rectangular arrays as the fundamental data structure and applies operations across whole arrays, but it uses readable English-like keywords and conventional ASCII syntax instead of special symbols. It is dynamically typed and interpreted, supporting both functional and imperative styles. Historically it was used for rapid prototyping, data analysis, and applications in artificial intelligence and decision-support systems, and it survives today chiefly through the open-source Q'Nial interpreter.
