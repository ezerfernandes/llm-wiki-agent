## Summary
QBasic is an imperative, procedural dialect of BASIC released by Microsoft in 1991, bundled with MS-DOS 5.0 and later as a stripped-down sibling of QuickBASIC (it dropped the standalone compiler and kept an interpreter and integrated IDE). It is statically typed with a small set of built-in types (INTEGER, LONG, SINGLE, DOUBLE, STRING) and type-suffix sigils, while supporting structured control flow, SUB/FUNCTION procedures, and user-defined TYPE records, moving away from the older line-numbered, GOTO-heavy BASIC style. Targeting 16-bit DOS on x86, it was widely used in the 1990s as an introductory teaching language and for small utilities, games, and quick scripts; today it survives mainly through compatible reimplementations such as QB64 and FreeBASIC.
