## Summary
HicEst (High Speed Tools) is a proprietary imperative, procedural programming language and integrated development environment developed by the Swiss firm Christian Schöll (HICEST) beginning in the 1980s and refined through the 1990s and 2000s for Microsoft Windows. It uses dynamic, weakly typed numeric and string variables that auto-allocate, and its syntax centers on built-in functions and a tightly coupled GUI, plotting, and dialog system invoked through subroutine-style calls. Designed primarily for scientific, engineering, and technical computing, HicEst targets rapid numerical analysis, data visualization, and instrument control where interactive computation and built-in mathematical routines (interpolation, optimization, statistics) matter more than general-purpose software construction.
