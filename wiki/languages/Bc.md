## Summary
bc is an imperative, procedural language for arbitrary-precision arithmetic, originally developed in the early 1970s at Bell Labs by Lorinda Cherry and Robert Morris and later standardized by POSIX. Its single numeric type is a signed decimal of arbitrary precision whose number of fractional digits is governed by a settable `scale` variable, and it offers C-like control flow, user-defined functions, and a handful of string operations layered on top. Historically it acted as a preprocessor front end to the dc reverse-Polish calculator, and it remains widely available as a standard Unix utility. It is typically used for interactive command-line calculation and scripting tasks that demand exact or very high precision, such as financial computations and number-theoretic experiments.
