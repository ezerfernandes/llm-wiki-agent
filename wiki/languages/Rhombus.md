## Summary
Rhombus is a general-purpose, multi-paradigm programming language built on the Racket platform, emerging in the early 2020s as the next-generation successor to Racket's traditional Lisp-style surface syntax. It pairs a modern, expression-oriented infix notation with Racket's powerful macro and language-extension machinery, so that programmers can define new syntactic forms and embedded domain-specific languages while retaining hygienic macros and a rich module system. The language is dynamically typed by default but supports gradual annotations and pattern-based binding and matching, and it inherits Racket's functional core augmented with object, class, and imperative facilities. It is primarily used for language-oriented programming, building DSLs, scripting, and as a research and teaching vehicle for extensible language design.
