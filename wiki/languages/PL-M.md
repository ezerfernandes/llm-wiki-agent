## Summary
PL/M (Programming Language for Microcomputers) is an imperative, procedural high-level language designed by Gary Kildall at Intel in 1973 — one of the earliest such languages created specifically for microprocessors, debuting on the Intel 8008 and 8080. It is statically and weakly typed, offering a small set of unsigned scalar types (BYTE, WORD, DWORD), explicit pointer-based memory access, and a block structure influenced by ALGOL and PL/I, while deliberately omitting floating-point and recursion to stay close to the hardware. PL/M was the implementation language for CP/M and much early Intel firmware, and it remained the dominant systems-programming language for embedded and microcontroller development (notably on the 8051 and 8086 families) before C displaced it. Today it survives mainly in legacy embedded and industrial control codebases.
