## Summary
Pascal is an imperative, procedural, statically and strongly typed programming language designed by Niklaus Wirth around 1970 to encourage disciplined structured programming and to serve as a clear teaching language. It features strong typing with rich user-defined types (enumerations, subranges, records, sets, and pointers), explicit block structure with begin/end, and a clean, readable syntax that influenced many later languages. Historically it dominated computer-science education and powered widely used implementations such as UCSD Pascal and Borland's Turbo Pascal, the latter of which evolved into the object-oriented Object Pascal and the Delphi line. Today it survives mainly through Free Pascal and Delphi, used for desktop applications, embedded and legacy systems, and continued instructional use.
