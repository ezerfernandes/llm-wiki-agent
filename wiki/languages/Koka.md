## Summary
Koka is a strongly and statically typed functional programming language created by Daan Leijen at Microsoft Research around 2012, with research releases continuing through the 2020s. Its defining feature is a row-based effect type system that automatically infers and tracks the side effects of every function — such as exceptions, divergence, state, and user-defined effects — making effects part of the type signature while keeping the language largely pure. Koka offers first-class algebraic effect handlers, automatic reference-counting memory management via its Perceus optimization (no tracing garbage collector), and compiles through C to produce efficient native executables. It is primarily a research and educational language used to explore effect typing, structured control flow, and high-performance functional programming.
