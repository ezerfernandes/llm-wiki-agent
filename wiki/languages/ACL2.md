## Summary
ACL2 (A Computational Logic for Applicative Common Lisp) is a purely functional, applicative subset of Common Lisp coupled with a first-order automated theorem prover, developed by Matt Kaufmann and J Strother Moore at the University of Texas at Austin beginning in the early 1990s as the successor to the Boyer-Moore prover Nqthm. It is dynamically typed and side-effect-free, so the same definitions serve both as an executable Lisp program and as a logical theory about which the system can mechanically prove theorems by induction and rewriting. ACL2 is used primarily in industrial and academic formal verification, most famously for verifying microprocessor floating-point hardware, microcode, and other safety- and correctness-critical systems. Its 2005 ACM Software System Award reflects its standing as one of the most widely used industrial-strength theorem provers.
