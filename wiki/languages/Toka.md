## Summary
Toka is a small, stack-based, concatenative programming language created by Charles Childers in the mid-2000s as an outgrowth of the Forth tradition, sharing Forth's interactive word-defining model and reverse-Polish syntax. It is dynamically and weakly typed, manipulating untyped cells on a data stack, and is extensible by defining new words that compose existing ones. Implemented as a compact, portable virtual machine, Toka was designed primarily for embedding, scripting, and minimalist systems programming where a lightweight, easily hackable interpreter is preferable to a large runtime.
