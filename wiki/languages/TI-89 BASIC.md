## Summary
TI-89 BASIC is the built-in, interpreted programming language of Texas Instruments' TI-89, TI-89 Titanium, TI-92 Plus, and Voyage 200 graphing calculators, introduced in 1998 atop the Motorola 68000-based hardware and its computer-algebra system. It is an imperative, procedural dialect of BASIC with dynamic typing, where variables hold numbers, symbolic expressions, strings, lists, or matrices, and the language tightly integrates the calculator's symbolic-math engine so expressions can remain unevaluated. Programs and user functions are entered on-device or via TI's connectivity software, with control flow built from If/Then/Else, While/EndWhile, For/EndFor, and Loop constructs and explicit subroutine prefixes like Local, Prgm, and Func. It is used chiefly for classroom math and science routines, small games, and on-the-fly calculations rather than large-scale software, and runs noticeably slower than the device's assembly or C alternatives.
