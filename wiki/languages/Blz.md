## Summary
Blz is a small, dynamically typed scripting language built around a single core data structure, the "blaze map," an ordered key-value collection that doubles as array, object, and namespace. It is an imperative, expression-oriented language with first-class functions and concise C-style syntax, designed in the modern Rosetta Code era as a compact, embeddable interpreter rather than a general-purpose production toolchain. In practice it appears mainly in code-task and puzzle settings, where its terse map-centric idioms let short programs cover a broad range of algorithmic exercises.
