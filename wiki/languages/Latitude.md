## Summary
Latitude is a dynamically typed, object-oriented scripting language created in the mid-2010s, distinguished by its use of prototype-based inheritance rather than classes, in the spirit of languages like Self, Io, and JavaScript. Everything in Latitude is an object built by cloning and extending other objects, and the language places heavy emphasis on first-class dynamically scoped variables and reflective metaprogramming, allowing slots, methods, and even control flow to be manipulated at runtime. It supports a flexible, expressive syntax with message-passing semantics and features such as continuations and first-class lexical scopes. Latitude is primarily a hobbyist and experimental language used to explore prototype-oriented design and reflective programming techniques rather than for mainstream production work.
