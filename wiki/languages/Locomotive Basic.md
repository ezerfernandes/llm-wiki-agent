## Summary
Locomotive Basic is a dialect of the BASIC programming language developed by Locomotive Software and shipped in the ROM of the Amstrad CPC range of 8-bit home computers introduced in 1984. It is an imperative, line-numbered, interpreted language with weak dynamic typing distinguished only between numeric values (single-precision floating point and integers) and strings, where string variables are denoted by a trailing dollar sign. The dialect was notable in its era for relatively fast execution and rich hardware-oriented commands for graphics, sound, and windowing on the CPC, including structured constructs like WHILE/WEND alongside the usual GOTO and GOSUB. It was used primarily for hobbyist programming, education, and games development on the Amstrad CPC home computers throughout the mid-to-late 1980s.
