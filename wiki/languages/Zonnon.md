## Summary
Zonnon is a general-purpose, statically and strongly typed programming language in the Pascal, Modula-2, and Oberon family, designed at ETH Zurich in the early 2000s under the influence of Niklaus Wirth's tradition. It is multi-paradigm, blending structured and object-oriented programming with a distinctive module-and-object composition model, and it adds concurrency support through active objects that communicate via dialogs and a built-in exception-handling mechanism. Zonnon targets the .NET Common Language Infrastructure, compiling to managed code and integrating with the .NET Framework, and it was used primarily in academic teaching and research on language design rather than in mainstream industrial development.
