## Summary
QuickBASIC is a procedural, imperative dialect of BASIC released by Microsoft in 1985 for MS-DOS, evolving the language beyond line-numbered GW-BASIC into a structured form with named subroutines, functions, user-defined types, and a true compiler alongside an integrated IDE. It is statically typed with a small set of intrinsic types (INTEGER, LONG, SINGLE, DOUBLE, STRING) distinguished by type-suffix characters or DIM declarations, while remaining beginner-friendly and loosely strict by default. The language was widely used through the late 1980s and early 1990s for DOS application development, education, and rapid prototyping, with a reduced interpreter variant (QBasic) shipping with MS-DOS and Windows. Its lineage continues today through compatible open-source compilers such as QB64 and FreeBASIC.
