## Summary
The Vedit macro language is the built-in scripting language of VEDIT, a fast text and binary editor for DOS, Windows, and OS/2 that dates back to the early 1980s. It is an imperative, procedural language tightly coupled to the editor's text-buffer model: rather than working with abstract variables, most operations move a cursor and read, search, insert, or delete characters directly within edit buffers, with a small set of numeric and text registers for state. Typing is dynamic and weak, centered on integer registers and named text registers rather than rich data structures. It is used chiefly to automate editing chores, perform programmable search-and-replace and reformatting, process and convert files, and build custom editor commands and macros.
