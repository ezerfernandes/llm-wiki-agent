## Summary
COMAL (Common Algorithmic Language) is a structured, imperative procedural programming language created in Denmark around 1973-1975 by Benedict Løfstedt and Børge Christensen, designed to combine the approachable interactive nature of BASIC with the structured control flow and named procedures of Pascal. It is statically yet loosely typed with simple numeric and string types, supports named PROC/FUNC procedures with parameters, block-structured IF/WHILE/REPEAT/CASE constructs, and unlike contemporary BASIC dialects encourages programming without line numbers or GOTO. Originating in the late-1970s and 1980s home-computer and education era, it was widely adopted as a teaching language in Scandinavian and British schools and on machines such as the Commodore 64 and BBC Micro. Today it survives mainly in retrocomputing and educational contexts.
