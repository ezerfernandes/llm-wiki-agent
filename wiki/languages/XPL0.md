## Summary
XPL0 is an imperative, procedural programming language created by Peter J. R. Boyle in 1976, designed as a portable, simplified descendant of PL/I and ALGOL in the tradition of structured systems languages. It is statically typed with a small set of primitive types (integer, real, character) and uses a compact, terse syntax featuring single-character intrinsic codes for I/O and runtime operations, with code historically compiled to an intermediate form run by a small virtual machine for portability across microcomputers. It saw most use as a hobbyist and educational systems language on early personal computers such as the Apple II and DOS/Windows PCs, and remains maintained primarily for recreational and Rosetta Code-style programming.
