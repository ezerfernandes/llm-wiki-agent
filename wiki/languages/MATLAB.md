## Summary
MATLAB (short for "Matrix Laboratory") is a high-level, dynamically typed language and interactive numerical computing environment developed by MathWorks, originating from work by Cleve Moler in the late 1970s and released commercially in 1984. It is primarily imperative and array-oriented, treating matrices as its fundamental data type so that vectorized operations replace explicit loops, while also supporting functional, object-oriented, and procedural styles. The language is weakly and dynamically typed, with most values defaulting to double-precision floating-point matrices, and it relies on an extensive library of built-in functions and domain toolboxes. It is widely used in engineering, scientific research, signal and image processing, control systems, and numerical simulation, often for prototyping algorithms and visualizing data.
