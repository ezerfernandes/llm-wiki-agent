## Summary
Pop-11 is a multi-paradigm, reflective programming language created in the late 1970s at the University of Sussex, descended from the earlier POP-2 and forming the core of the Poplog development environment. It blends functional, imperative, and object-oriented styles, is dynamically typed, and is distinguished by an open-stack-based evaluation model, an incremental compiler accessible at runtime, and powerful pattern-matching facilities. Its syntax resembles ALGOL while its dynamic, list-oriented features echo Lisp, making it well suited to symbolic computation. Historically it has been used primarily in artificial intelligence research, cognitive science, and teaching, where its support for rapid prototyping and rule-based systems is valued.
