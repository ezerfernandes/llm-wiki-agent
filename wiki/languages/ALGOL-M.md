## Summary
ALGOL-M is an imperative, procedural, block-structured language in the ALGOL family, developed in the mid-1970s by Mark Moranville and Steve Sutphen as a student-oriented dialect of ALGOL 60 designed to run on small machines, originally targeting the Intel 8080 microprocessor under CP/M. It is statically typed with a small set of primitive types (notably INTEGER, DECIMAL, and STRING) and follows ALGOL's classic syntax of BEGIN/END blocks, explicit declarations, and lexically scoped procedures. Lacking the full numeric and array facilities of mainframe ALGOL implementations, it was intended chiefly as an educational and hobbyist language for learning structured programming on early microcomputers, and survives today mainly through preservation efforts and a modern compiler running on CP/M emulators.
