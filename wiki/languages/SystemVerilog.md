## Summary
SystemVerilog is a hardware description and verification language (HDVL) standardized as IEEE 1800, first released in 2005 as a major superset of Verilog and later unified with it under a single standard in 2009. It is a strongly, statically typed language whose design blends event-driven, concurrent register-transfer-level (RTL) and gate-level description with an object-oriented, constrained-random verification methodology, adding constructs such as classes, interfaces, assertions (SVA), and functional coverage on top of Verilog's procedural and structural roots. It is used primarily for designing, modeling, and verifying digital integrated circuits and systems-on-chip, both for synthesis into real silicon and for building reusable testbenches (commonly via the UVM framework). Although its describe-and-verify nature makes it unusual as a general-purpose language, its procedural subset can express ordinary algorithms, which is what Rosetta Code tasks exercise.
