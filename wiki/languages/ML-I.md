## Summary
ML/I is a general-purpose macro processor developed by Peter J. Brown at the University of Cambridge in the mid-1960s. Rather than being a conventional programming language, it operates as a free-form text-substitution engine: it scans an input stream for user-defined macro names and replaces them according to programmer-supplied definitions, with support for nested calls, recursion, and conditional and iterative constructs built from macros themselves. It is essentially untyped, treating all data as character text, and was designed to be highly portable across machines via a small, machine-described bootstrap. Historically it was used for preprocessing source code, language translation, and general text manipulation tasks in an era before such facilities were commonly built into compilers.
