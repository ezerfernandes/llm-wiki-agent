## Summary
PostScript is a stack-based, concatenative programming language created by Adobe (John Warnock and Charles Geschke) in 1982, primarily as a page description language for driving printers and typesetting devices. It is dynamically typed and uses Reverse Polish (postfix) notation with an explicit operand stack, while also supporting dictionaries, procedures as first-class objects, and graphics-state operators for vector rendering. Though designed chiefly to describe the appearance of printed and displayed pages, it is Turing-complete and can express general computation. Today it underpins the PDF document format and remains common in professional printing workflows, with interpreters such as Ghostscript providing wide tooling support.
