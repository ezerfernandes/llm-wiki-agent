## Summary
BASIC (Beginner's All-purpose Symbolic Instruction Code) is a family of imperative, procedural programming languages designed by John Kemeny and Thomas Kurtz at Dartmouth College in 1964 to make computing accessible to non-specialist students. Traditional dialects are dynamically typed and were typically interpreted, line-numbered, and oriented around simple numeric and string variables, with GOTO/GOSUB control flow; later structured and object-oriented variants such as QuickBASIC and Visual Basic added procedures, modules, and event-driven GUI programming. Its heyday came with the microcomputer era of the late 1970s and 1980s, when interpreters shipped in ROM on home machines, making it the first language for a generation of programmers. Today it survives mainly in education, hobbyist retro-computing, legacy business applications, and Microsoft's VB.NET and VBA scripting within Office.
