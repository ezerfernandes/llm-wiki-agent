# Chemical Equilibrium

A **chemical equilibrium** is the state of a [[ReversibleReaction|reversible reaction]] in which the forward and reverse [[ReactionRate|reaction rates]] are equal, so the concentrations of all reactant and product species remain constant over time. Equilibrium is written with a double arrow:

$$\mathrm{N_2O_4(g) \rightleftharpoons 2NO_2(g)}$$

> Disambiguation: this is the *chemistry* sense of equilibrium. For the mechanics sense (balanced forces/torques on a body) see [[StaticEquilibrium]]; the game-theory/economics senses (Nash equilibrium etc.) live on their own pages.

## Equilibrium is dynamic, not static

A reaction at equilibrium has **not stopped** — it proceeds in both directions at the same rate. The macroscopic composition is constant, but individual molecules continue to react and re-form. This is why it is called *dynamic equilibrium*.

For the elementary reaction N₂O₄(g) ⇌ 2NO₂(g) the opposing [[RateLaw|rate laws]] are:

$$\text{rate}_f = k_f[\mathrm{N_2O_4}] \qquad \text{rate}_r = k_r[\mathrm{NO_2}]^2$$

Starting from pure N₂O₄ (t = 0): the forward rate is finite and the reverse rate is zero. As N₂O₄ is consumed the forward rate falls; as NO₂ accumulates the reverse rate rises. The two rates converge until **rate_f = rate_r**, at which point concentrations stop changing — equilibrium is established.

## Equilibrium composition varies

The relative amounts of reactant and product at equilibrium differ greatly between reactions: some systems are almost all product, some almost all reactant, and some hold appreciable amounts of both. The position is summarized quantitatively by the [[EquilibriumConstant|equilibrium constant]] K.

## Physical equilibria

Reversible *physical* changes also reach equilibrium. For a liquid in a closed container:

$$\mathrm{Br_2(l) \rightleftharpoons Br_2(g)}$$

equilibrium is reached when the rates of vaporization and condensation are equal (the gas then sits at its [[VaporPressureChemistry|vapor pressure]]).
