## Definition
**Gauge pressure** is pressure measured relative to atmospheric pressure: positive when above atmospheric, negative when below. **Absolute pressure** is the total — gauge pressure plus atmospheric pressure: P_abs = P_g + P_atm. Most everyday gauges (tire, blood-pressure) read gauge pressure, showing zero when the contents are at atmospheric pressure.

## Key Points
- Because a fluid can push but never pull, the minimum possible **absolute** pressure is zero, so the minimum **gauge** pressure is −P_atm.
- **Open-tube manometers** read gauge pressure as the height difference of a liquid column: P_g = hρg (see [[FluidPressure]]). **Barometers** use a mercury column to read atmospheric pressure and double as altimeters.
- **Aneroid gauges** convert pressure into mechanical motion via flexible bellows and springs.
- Relies on [[PascalsPrinciple]] for transmitting pressure to a remote sensor.
- Blood pressure is reported as systolic/diastolic gauge pressure in mm Hg (1.0 mm Hg = 133 Pa).

## Equations
- P_abs = P_g + P_atm
- P_g = hρg  (manometer / fluid column)
- 1.0 mm Hg = 133 Pa;  1 atm = 1.01×10⁵ Pa
