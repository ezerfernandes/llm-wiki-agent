# Self-Bias (AI Judge)

**Self-bias** is the AI-judge bias toward its own outputs. Per [[ai-engineering-ch03-evaluation-methodology|*AI Engineering* Ch 3]]:

> "AI judges tend to have self-bias, where a model favors its own responses over the responses generated by other models. The same mechanism that helps a model compute the most likely response to generate will also give this response a high score."

## Magnitude (Zheng et al. 2023)

Ch 3 cites a striking pair of numbers:
- **GPT-4** favors itself with a **10% higher win rate**.
- **Claude-v1** favors itself with a **25% higher win rate**.

## The mechanism

Self-bias is mechanistic, not gratuitous: an LLM-as-judge that uses the same scoring distribution it would use for generation will rate its own outputs higher, because *its own outputs are by construction more probable under its own distribution*. This is the same gradient that drives generation aligned to the model's biases.

## Implications

- **For comparative evaluation**: do not use a model to judge itself in head-to-head settings. Use a cross-family judge (as the wiki's [[LLMAsAJudge]] page notes for [[2603.19247-prompt-optimization-jailbreaking]] — choose a judge model that is NOT in the family of any candidate).
- **For self-evaluation**: [[SelfEvaluation]] / [[SelfCritique]] still works for **sanity checks** (if a model thinks its own answer is wrong, that's strong signal), but self-rating is unreliable for ranking.
- **For reward modeling**: if the reward model and the policy share architecture or training data, reward hacking is more likely.

## Mitigation strategies

- Use a judge from a different model family than any candidate.
- For pairwise self-evaluation, swap the position of A and B and average — partially cancels self-bias if it correlates with position (though [[FirstPositionBias]] is a separate axis).
