## Definition
**Electrical resistance** (R) is the opposition a component offers to [[ElectricCurrent|current]], defined through [[OhmsLaw|Ohm's law]] as R = V/I. Its SI unit is the **ohm** (Ω), where 1 Ω = 1 V/A. Resistance depends both on the object's geometry and on the material's intrinsic [[Resistivity|resistivity]].

## Key Points
- For a uniform conductor, R = ρL/A: resistance rises with length (more collisions) and falls with cross-sectional area (more parallel paths).
- Any object with simple resistance is a **resistor**; even good conductors have small but nonzero R.
- Resistance is temperature-dependent: R = R₀(1 + αΔT). A light-bulb filament's hot (operating) resistance can far exceed its cold resistance.
- Typical magnitudes: copper wire ~10⁻⁵ Ω, the human heart ~10³ Ω, good insulators ~10¹² Ω and up.
- A [[PullUpResistor|pull-up resistor]] is one circuit application; resistance dissipates [[ElectricPower|power]] as heat.

## Equations
- R = V / I  (definition)
- R = ρ L / A  (geometry + material)
- R = R₀ (1 + α ΔT)  (temperature dependence)
- 1 Ω = 1 V/A
