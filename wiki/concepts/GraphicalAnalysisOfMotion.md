## Definition
Graphical analysis of one-dimensional motion extracts physical quantities from graphs of position, velocity, and acceleration versus time, using the slope and intercept of those graphs.

## Key Points
- A straight-line graph follows y = mx + b, where m is slope (rise/run) and b is the y-intercept.
- **Position–time (x–t):** slope = [[Velocity]] (average), y-intercept = initial position x₀.
- **Velocity–time (v–t):** slope = [[Acceleration]], y-intercept = initial velocity v₀.
- The slope of a tangent line at a point gives the *instantaneous* value (instantaneous velocity from x–t, instantaneous acceleration from v–t).
- Zero acceleration → straight x–t line (constant velocity); a curved x–t graph means changing velocity; a curved v–t graph means changing acceleration.

## Equations
- y = mx + b
- x = x₀ + v̄t  (slope = v̄)
- v = v₀ + at  (slope = a)
- slope of x–t = Δx/Δt = v
- slope of v–t = Δv/Δt = a
