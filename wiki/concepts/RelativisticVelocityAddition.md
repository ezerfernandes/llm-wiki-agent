## Definition
Relativistic velocity addition is the rule for combining velocities at high speed so that no result exceeds the [[SpeedOfLightPhysics|speed of light]] c. If an object moves at u′ relative to a frame that moves at v relative to an observer, the observer measures u = (v + u′)/(1 + vu′/c²), not the classical sum v + u′.

## Key Points
- Required by the [[EinsteinPostulates|second postulate]]: light always travels at c for every observer, so velocities cannot simply add.
- At low speeds the denominator ≈ 1 and the formula reduces to the classical u = v + u′ ([[RelativeVelocity]]).
- Combining any speed with c still gives c; the result can never exceed c.
- Unlike classical addition, it is asymmetric: e.g. a ship at 0.500c firing a canister at 0.750c yields 0.909c toward Earth but 0.400c away.
- The **relativistic Doppler shift** is a related consequence: receding light sources are redshifted (longer λ), approaching sources blueshifted — the basis of measuring stellar and galactic velocities.

## Equations
- Classical: u = v + u′
- Relativistic: u = (v + u′) / (1 + v u′ / c²)
- Doppler (wavelength): λ_obs = λ_s √[(1 + u/c)/(1 − u/c)]   (u > 0 = receding)
- Doppler (frequency): f_obs = f_s √[(1 − u/c)/(1 + u/c)]
