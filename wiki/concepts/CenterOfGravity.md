## Definition
The center of gravity (cg) is the point at which the total weight of an object effectively acts. When summing torques, an extended body's weight can be replaced by a single downward force `w = mg` applied at this point.

## Key Points
- In a uniform gravitational field the center of gravity coincides with the [[CenterOfMass]].
- It is critical for determining the type of equilibrium and overall [[Stability]]: a body remains stable only while its cg stays directly above its [[Stability|base of support]].
- Lowering the cg or widening the base of support increases stability (e.g., bending the knees, spreading the feet, using a cane).
- In biomechanics, aligning the upper-body cg over the hip pivot minimizes the muscle force needed to maintain posture.
- The cg lets a distributed weight be treated as one force when applying the conditions for [[StaticEquilibrium]].

## Equations
- Weight acting at the cg: `w = mg`
