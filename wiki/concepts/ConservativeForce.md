## Definition
A **conservative force** is one whose [[Work]] depends only on the initial and final positions, not on the path traveled between them. Because of this path-independence, a conservative force can be associated with a [[PotentialEnergy]], and the work it does is fully recoverable. Gravity and ideal spring (elastic) forces are the standard examples.

## Key Points
- Path-independent work ⇔ a well-defined potential energy can be assigned.
- Under conservative forces only, [[MechanicalEnergy]] (KE + PE) is conserved.
- Contrast with a [[NonconservativeForce]] (e.g., friction), whose work is path-dependent and dissipates mechanical energy.
- Spring force follows [[HookesLaw]] (F = kx) and stores elastic potential energy ½kx².

## Equations
- KE_i + PE_i = KE_f + PE_f  (mechanical energy conserved)
- ΔKE + ΔPE = 0
- PE_s = ½ k x²  (elastic/spring PE)
- ½mv² + mgh + ½kx² = ½mv_f² + mgh_f + ½kx_f²
