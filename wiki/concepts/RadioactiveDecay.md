## Definition
**Radioactive decay** is the spontaneous transformation of an unstable nuclide (the **parent**) into a more stable one (the **daughter**) through emission of a particle or photon. Successive decays form a **decay series** (e.g., ²³⁸U → … → ²⁰⁶Pb). The decay modes are [[AlphaDecay]], [[BetaDecay]], and [[GammaDecay]].

## Key Points
- **Conservation laws** govern every decay: charge, nucleon number A, linear and angular momentum, mass–energy, and electron family number.
- **Decay energy** comes from the mass deficit: Q = (Δm)c², where Δm is the parent mass minus total product mass.
- Decay is **statistical** — each nucleus has a fixed probability of decaying per unit time, leading to exponential decay (see [[HalfLife]]).
- Stability rule: neutron-rich nuclei tend to β⁻ decay; proton-rich nuclei tend to β⁺ decay or electron capture, each moving products toward the line of stability.

## Equations
- Decay energy: Q = (Δm)c²; conversion 1 u = 931.5 MeV/c²
- Exponential decay: N = N₀e^(−λt) (see [[HalfLife]])
