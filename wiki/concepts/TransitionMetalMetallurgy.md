# Transition Metal Metallurgy (Occurrence & Preparation)

Isolation of the **[[TransitionMetals|transition metals]]** from their ores. (Companion to [[Metallurgy]], which covers the *representative* metals from Ch 18.) Three general steps: **(1) preliminary treatment** — crush/grind, concentrate, and convert to a more easily reducible compound; **(2) smelting** — extract the molten metal, reducing it and removing impurities as slag; **(3) refining** — final purification by distillation (Zn, Hg), gravity separation (Sn), or electrolysis.

## Iron — the blast furnace

Iron occurs almost exclusively oxidized (rust, Fe₂O₃). Roasted ore, coke, and limestone are fed continuously into an 80–100 ft furnace; CO is the reducing agent, stepping the oxide down:

> 3Fe₂O₃ + CO → 2Fe₃O₄ + CO₂ (230 °C)
> Fe₃O₄ + CO → 3FeO + CO₂ (410 °C)
> FeO + CO → Fe + CO₂ (525 °C)

CO is regenerated low in the furnace: CO₂(g) + C(s) → 2CO(g). Limestone forms slag: CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂, then CaO(s) + SiO₂(s) → CaSiO₃(l). The less-dense slag floats on molten iron, protecting it from re-oxidation; both are tapped several times daily.

**Steel:** iron refined by removing impurities and adding Mn, Cr, Ni, W, Mo, V; most steels contain **0.04–2.5% carbon** (excess C makes it brittle).

## Copper — smelting

Chief ores are sulfides (covellite, CuS), with oxides (tenorite, CuO) and hydroxycarbonates [malachite, Cu₂(OH)₂CO₃]. Roast to drive off some S as SO₂, then flux with limestone — SiO₂ (Lewis acid) takes oxide ions (Lewis bases) to form slag:

> CaCO₃(s) + SiO₂(s) → CaSiO₃(l) + CO₂(g)
> FeO(s) + SiO₂(s) → FeSiO₃(l)

Then blow air through the melt:

> 2Cu₂S(l) + 3O₂(g) → 2Cu₂O(l) + 2SO₂(g)
> 2Cu₂O(l) + Cu₂S(l) → 6Cu(l) + SO₂(g)

The product is **blister copper**, cast as anodes for electrolytic refining.

## Silver — hydrometallurgy

Silver is converted to a soluble [[ComplexIon|complex ion]], dicyanoargentate(I), then reduced out:

> 4Ag(s) + 8CN⁻(aq) + O₂(g) + 2H₂O(l) → 4[Ag(CN)₂]⁻(aq) + 4OH⁻(aq)
> 2Ag₂S(s) + 8CN⁻ + O₂ + 2H₂O → 4[Ag(CN)₂]⁻ + 2S(s) + 4OH⁻
> AgCl(s) + 2CN⁻(aq) → [Ag(CN)₂]⁻(aq) + Cl⁻(aq)

Then precipitate the metal with a reducing agent:

> 2[Ag(CN)₂]⁻(aq) + Zn(s) → 2Ag(s) + [Zn(CN)₄]²⁻(aq)

O₂ is required to balance the redox: Ag oxidizes 0→1+, O₂ is reduced 0→2−.
