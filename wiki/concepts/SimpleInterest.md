# Simple Interest

**Simple interest** is the interest earned (or owed) computed only on the original sum of money, by the formula

> **I = Prt**

where `P` is the **principal** (the amount invested or borrowed), `r` is the annual **rate of interest** (a [[Percent|percent]] **written as a decimal**), `t` is the **time in years**, and `I` is the **interest**. OpenStax [[Prealgebra]] 2e (Chapter 6, [[prealgebra-2e-ch06-percents]], §6.4) introduces it. The formula is a [[Percent|percent-equation]]-style "rate × base" relationship extended over time, and it is solved with the same algebra ([[Equation|Properties of Equality]]) used throughout the chapter.

## Vocabulary
- **Principal (P)** — the amount of money originally deposited or borrowed.
- **Interest (I)** — money the bank pays you on a deposit, or money you owe on a loan.
- **Rate (r)** — the interest rate per year, *almost always given as a percent*; **convert it to a decimal** before substituting (`6% = 0.06`).
- **Time (t)** — the number of **years** the money is invested or borrowed.

## Procedure
Organize the four quantities `I, P, r, t` in a list, substitute the known values into `I = Prt`, and solve algebraically for the remaining unknown:
- **Find interest** — given `P, r, t`, multiply.
- **Find principal** — given `I, r, t`, solve `P = I / (rt)`.
- **Find rate** — given `I, P, t`, solve `r = I / (Pt)`, then express as a percent.
- **Find time** — given `I, P, r`, solve `t = I / (Pr)`.

**Unit conversions:** convert the rate **percent → decimal** (multiply by 0.01) and convert any time given in **months → years** by dividing by 12 (e.g. `10 months = 10/12 year`).

Worked-example types: finding each of the four variables in turn; problems with time given in months; and real-world scenarios (bank deposits, loans, lending between family members).
