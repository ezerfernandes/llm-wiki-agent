# Multiplication Property of Equality

The **Multiplication Property of Equality** states that multiplying **both sides** of an [[Equation|equation]] by the **same number** preserves the equality:

> For all real numbers `a`, `b`, `c`: **if `a = b`, then `ac = bc`.**

It is the inverse partner of the [[DivisionPropertyOfEquality|Division Property of Equality]] and the fourth of the [[PropertiesOfEquality|Properties of Equality]]. OpenStax [[Prealgebra]] 2e introduces it in Chapter 8.2 ([[prealgebra-2e-ch08-solving-linear-equations]]). Like the others it rests on the balance idea: doing the *same thing to both sides* keeps an equation balanced.

## When to use it
Use the Multiplication Property to **undo a division** — i.e. to solve equations where the [[Variable|variable]] is **divided** by a number:

- `x/a = b` ⇒ multiply both sides by `a` ⇒ `x = ab`. Example: `a/−7 = −42` ⇒ multiply by `−7` ⇒ `a = 294`.
- **Fractional coefficient** `(p/q)x = b`: multiply both sides by the [[Reciprocal|reciprocal]] `q/p`, which is the most efficient form of the property because a number times its reciprocal is 1. Example: `(2/3)x = 18` ⇒ multiply by `3/2` ⇒ `x = 27`.

It is also the property invoked when **clearing fractions or decimals** ([[SolvingLinearEquations|Ch 8.4]]): multiplying both sides by the [[LeastCommonMultiple|LCD]] (or by a power of 10) is a single application of the Multiplication Property that turns a fractional equation into an all-integer one.

## Where it fits among the properties of equality
| Property | Statement (`a = b ⟹`) | Undoes |
|---|---|---|
| Addition | `a + c = b + c` | a subtraction |
| Subtraction | `a − c = b − c` | an addition |
| **Multiplication** | `ac = bc` | a **division** |
| Division (`c ≠ 0`) | `a/c = b/c` | a multiplication |

The Multiplication and [[DivisionPropertyOfEquality|Division]] Properties together let you make the variable's coefficient equal 1 — step 4 of the [[SolvingLinearEquations|General Strategy]].

## Solving procedure
1. Multiply both sides by the number (or reciprocal) that makes the variable's coefficient 1.
2. Simplify both sides using [[FractionArithmetic|fraction]] / [[SignedNumberArithmetic|signed-number]] arithmetic.
3. **Check** by substituting the result into the original equation.
