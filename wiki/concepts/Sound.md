## Definition
Sound is a mechanical disturbance that propagates outward from a source through matter as a [[LongitudinalWave]] of pressure variations. At the atomic scale it is organized motion beyond random thermal agitation — often periodic, with particles executing [[SimpleHarmonicMotion]]. **Hearing** is the biological perception of sound, distinct from the physical wave itself.

## Key Points
- Sound consists of **compressions** (high-pressure zones) and **rarefactions** (low-pressure zones) traveling outward at the source's vibration frequency.
- Fluids carry primarily longitudinal sound because they have negligible shear strength; solids can also carry transverse sound.
- A vibrating object transfers energy to the surrounding medium; most becomes heat (turbulence, viscosity), while a portion propagates as audible pressure fluctuations.
- Wave energy spreads over an expanding area with distance, reducing amplitude, and is also lost to absorption and thermal exchange (entropy increase) between compressions and rarefactions.
- The eardrum vibrates from the pressure difference across it, beginning the conversion of mechanical energy to neural signals.
