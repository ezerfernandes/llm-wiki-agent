# Henry's Law

**Henry's law** states that "the quantity of an ideal gas that dissolves in a definite volume of liquid is directly proportional to the pressure of the gas." It governs the pressure dependence of gas [[Solubility|solubility]] in liquids:

**Cg = k · Pg**

- **Cg** — concentration of dissolved gas (mol/L)
- **k** — the Henry's law constant (depends on the solute identity, solvent identity, and temperature)
- **Pg** — partial pressure of the gas (atm or kPa)

## Worked Example
At 20 °C, water exposed to O₂ at 101.3 kPa holds Cg = 1.38 × 10⁻³ mol/L.
- Solve for the constant: k = Cg/Pg = (1.38 × 10⁻³ mol/L)/(101.3 kPa) = **1.36 × 10⁻⁵ mol/(L·kPa)**.
- Predict solubility at Pg = 20.7 kPa: Cg = k·Pg = (1.36 × 10⁻⁵)(20.7) = **2.82 × 10⁻⁴ mol/L**.

## Deviations
Henry's law assumes the gas does not react with the solvent. When it does, solubility rises faster than predicted — e.g., ammonia: NH₃ + H₂O ⇌ NH₄⁺ + OH⁻.

## Consequences
- **Carbonation** — drinks are bottled with CO₂ at high pressure; opening lowers Pg, supersaturating the liquid so CO₂ bubbles out (see [[SupersaturatedSolution]]).
- **Decompression sickness ("the bends")** — pressure rises ~1 atm per 10 m of depth, so more gas dissolves in a diver's blood; ascending too fast lowers solubility and forms bubbles in the blood. Prevention: slow ascent / decompression stops; treatment: hyperbaric oxygen therapy.
- **Lake Nyos (1986)** — CO₂ supersaturated at depth was released catastrophically when the water rose.
