# Percent Yield (Theoretical and Actual Yield)

When a reaction is run, the amount of product actually obtained is usually less than what the [[ChemicalEquation|balanced equation]] predicts. Three quantities describe this:

- **Theoretical yield** — the amount of product that *should* form under the specified conditions, computed by [[ReactionStoichiometry|reaction stoichiometry]] based on the [[LimitingReactant|limiting reactant]].
- **Actual yield** — the amount of product actually recovered. It is typically lower because of side reactions, incomplete reactions, and losses during collection/purification.
- **Percent yield** — actual as a fraction of theoretical:

**percent yield = (actual yield / theoretical yield) × 100%**

Actual and theoretical yields may be expressed as masses, molar amounts, or other appropriate properties (e.g. gas volume), but the **units must match** between numerator and denominator.

## Worked Example

CuSO₄(aq) + Zn(s) → Cu(s) + ZnSO₄(aq), starting from 1.274 g CuSO₄ with excess Zn (so CuSO₄ is limiting); actual yield = 0.392 g Cu.

Theoretical yield:
1.274 g CuSO₄ × (1 mol / 159.62 g) × (1 mol Cu / 1 mol CuSO₄) × (63.55 g/mol) = **0.5072 g Cu**.

Percent yield: (0.392 / 0.5072) × 100 = **77.3%**.
