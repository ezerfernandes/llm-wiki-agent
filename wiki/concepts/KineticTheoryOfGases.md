## Definition
The **kinetic theory of gases** explains macroscopic gas properties — [[Pressure|pressure]] and
[[ThermodynamicTemperature|temperature]] — in terms of the motion of huge numbers of molecules.
Pressure is the cumulative effect of countless elastic molecular collisions with the container
walls; temperature measures the average molecular kinetic energy.

## Key Points
- More molecules or higher average speed → greater pressure. In an elastic wall collision the
  perpendicular momentum component reverses while speed is unchanged.
- Molecular motion is **isotropic**: the mean squared velocity components are equal
  (v̄ₓ² = v̄_y² = v̄_z²), so v̄² = 3·v̄ₓ².
- **Temperature is kinetic energy**: average translational KE = (3/2)kT, so it depends only on
  absolute temperature — not on the gas's identity.
- **rms speed** is a representative molecular speed; nitrogen at 20 °C moves at ~511 m/s even though
  the bulk air appears still. Lighter atoms (helium, hydrogen) can reach escape velocity and leak
  from Earth's atmosphere; heavier ones cannot.
- The **Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution** describes the spread of molecular speeds — peaked at lower
  speeds with a long high-speed tail. The **mean free path** is the average distance between
  collisions.
- Collisions drive **Brownian motion** of small suspended particles (e.g. pollen).

## Equations
- Pressure from molecular motion: PV = (1/3)·N·m·v̄²
- Temperature–KE link: KE̅ = (1/2)·m·v̄² = (3/2)·k·T
- rms speed: v_rms = √(3kT/m)
- Single-molecule wall force: F = m·vₓ²/l
