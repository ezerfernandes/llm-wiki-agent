## Definition
**Uncertainty (δA)** is a quantitative expression of how much a measurement may deviate from the true value. A measured quantity is written as **A ± δA**, signaling the range within which the true value is expected to lie.

## Key Points
- Every measurement carries uncertainty arising from equipment limits, user skill, irregularities in the object, and situational factors.
- **Percent uncertainty** rescales δA relative to the measured value, making uncertainties comparable across different quantities.
- Uncertainty is communicated through [[SignificantFigures]]: the last recorded digit is the first one carrying uncertainty.
- Distinct from but related to [[AccuracyAndPrecision]] — uncertainty quantifies the spread; accuracy and precision describe its relationship to the true value and its repeatability.
- Example: a thermometer with ±3.0°C uncertainty is clinically useless because the error band overwhelms the meaningful signal.

## Equations
- Measured value: A ± δA.
- Percent uncertainty: % unc = (δA / A) × 100%.
