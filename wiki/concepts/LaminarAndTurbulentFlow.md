## Definition
**Laminar flow** is smooth, orderly fluid motion in parallel layers with continuous streamlines. **Turbulent flow** is chaotic motion with eddies and swirls that mix the layers and break up streamlines. The transition between them is predicted by the [[ReynoldsNumber]].

## Key Points
- Streamlines are the paths traced by small fluid volumes — smooth in laminar flow, broken in turbulent flow.
- [[Viscosity]] damps disturbances and favors laminar flow; higher speed or density favors turbulence.
- Laminar flow obeys [[PoiseuilleLaw|Poiseuille's law]] and (when nearly frictionless) [[BernoulliPrinciple|Bernoulli's equation]]; turbulence invalidates both.
- In tubes, N_R < 2000 is laminar, 2000–3000 unstable, > 3000 turbulent.
- Medically important: blood flow is normally laminar; turbulence produces audible sounds (murmurs, Korotkoff sounds) signaling narrowed vessels, valve problems, or aneurysms.

## Equations
- Reynolds number (tube): N_R = 2ρvr/η  (see [[ReynoldsNumber]])
