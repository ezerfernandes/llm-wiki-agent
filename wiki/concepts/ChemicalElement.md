# Chemical Element

A **chemical element** is a pure substance that cannot be broken down into simpler substances by a chemical change. Over 100 elements are known; about 90 occur naturally on Earth (familiar examples: iron, silver, gold, aluminum, sulfur, oxygen, copper).

Just eleven elements make up roughly 99% of Earth's crust and atmosphere by mass — oxygen (O, 49.20%), silicon (Si, 25.67%), aluminum (Al, 7.50%), iron (Fe, 4.71%), calcium (Ca, 3.39%), sodium (Na, 2.63%), potassium (K, 2.40%), magnesium (Mg, 1.93%), hydrogen (H, 0.87%), titanium (Ti, 0.58%), and chlorine (Cl, 0.19%).

By their ability to conduct heat and electricity, elements are grouped as **metals** (good conductors), **[[Nonmetals|nonmetals]]** (poor conductors), and **[[Metalloids|metalloids]]** (intermediate). The [[PeriodicTable|periodic table]] arranges elements so those with similar properties are near one another; the main-group families' descriptive chemistry is surveyed under [[RepresentativeElements]].

The smallest unit of an element retaining its properties is the [[Atom]]. Elements are one kind of pure substance in [[MatterClassification]]; the other is the [[ChemicalCompound]].

## Element Identity and Symbolism (Ch 2)

Each element is defined by its [[AtomicNumberAndMassNumber|atomic number]] Z — the number of [[Proton|protons]] in its atoms' nuclei — not by atomic mass. Atoms of one element sharing Z but differing in neutron count are [[Isotope|isotopes]]; the [[AtomicMass|atomic mass]] tabulated for each element is an abundance-weighted average over its isotopes. Elements are denoted by one- or two-letter chemical symbols (e.g., Co for cobalt), and the modern [[PeriodicTable]] orders all 118 elements by increasing atomic number.
