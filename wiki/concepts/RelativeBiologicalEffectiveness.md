## Definition
Relative biological effectiveness (RBE), also called the quality factor (QF), is a dimensionless number that scales [[IonizingRadiation]] absorbed dose to its biological harm. It captures how densely a radiation type packs its ionization along its track: more concentrated ionization produces clustered, harder-to-repair damage.

## Key Points
- RBE = 1 for X-rays and gamma rays (the reference).
- RBE ≈ 10–20 for alpha particles, because they deposit energy in short, dense tracks; fast neutrons and protons are intermediate.
- Equal *energy* deposition can mean very different biological damage — this is why internal alpha emitters (e.g. inhaled ²³⁹Pu) are far more dangerous than the same energy delivered as beta/gamma.
- RBE converts physical [[RadiationDose]] (gray) into dose equivalent (sievert).

## Equations
- Dose (Sv) = Dose (Gy) × RBE
- rem = rad × RBE
