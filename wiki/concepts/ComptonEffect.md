# Compton Effect (Photon Momentum)

The **Compton effect** is the scattering of X-ray photons by electrons, in which the scattered photon loses energy (its wavelength increases). Observed by Arthur H. Compton (~1923), it provided direct evidence that **photons carry momentum** despite being massless — confirming the particle side of [[WaveParticleDuality|wave-particle duality]].

## Key Points
- A photon has momentum p = h/λ, derivable from the relativistic energy-momentum relation with rest mass zero.
- Momentum is conserved in classical, relativistic, and quantum interactions, including photon emission, absorption, and reflection.
- A visible photon's momentum is tiny by macroscopic standards (e.g., a 500 nm photon: p ≈ 1.33 × 10⁻²⁷ kg·m/s), yet its energy can far exceed that of a slow electron with the same momentum.

## Equations
- Photon momentum: p = h/λ
- Relativistic energy-momentum: E² = (pc)² + (mc²)²
- For massless photons (m = 0): E = pc, so p = E/c
- E = hc/λ = hf

## Applications
- **Comet tails** — dust pushed away from the Sun by reflected photons.
- **Solar sails** — accumulated sunlight momentum for propulsion in vacuum.
- Photon detectors (eyes, film, photomultipliers, CCDs) rely on particle-like photon interactions.
