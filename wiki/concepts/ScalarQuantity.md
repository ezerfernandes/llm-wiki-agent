## Definition
In physics, a scalar quantity is a physical measurement characterized by magnitude only, with no direction. Examples include temperature, energy, [[Speed]], height, and [[Distance]]. Contrast with a [[VectorQuantity]], which also has direction.

> Note: this is the physics sense of "scalar." For the linear-algebra / ML sense (a 0th-order tensor, a single real number), see [[Scalar]].

## Key Points
- A scalar can be negative (e.g., −20 °C); here the minus sign marks a point on a scale, not a direction.
- Speed is a scalar (magnitude of velocity); distance is a scalar (magnitude of displacement).
- Scalars are combined with ordinary arithmetic, unlike vectors.
