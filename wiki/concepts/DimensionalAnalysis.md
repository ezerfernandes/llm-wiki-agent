# Dimensional Analysis

**Dimensional analysis** (the *factor-label method*) is a general approach to quantitative calculations based on one principle: *the units of quantities must be subjected to the same mathematical operations as their associated numbers.* Identical units that divide cancel to 1, which lets you track and verify units through any computation.

## Conversion factors
A **unit conversion factor** expresses two equivalent quantities in different units; from 2.54 cm = 1 in you get (2.54 cm / 1 in) or (1 in / 2.54 cm). You pick the orientation that cancels the unwanted unit. Common factors: 1 m = 1.0936 yd, 1 km = 0.62137 mi, 1 kg = 2.2046 lb, 1 lb = 453.59 g, 1 L = 1.0567 qt.

## Worked examples (Ch 1)
- 34 in × (2.54 cm / 1 in) = 86 cm.
- 125 g × (1 oz / 28.349 g) = 4.41 oz.
- Antifreeze density: 9.26 lb → 4.20 × 10³ g; 4.00 qt → 3.78 × 10³ mL; density = 1.11 g/mL.
- Fuel economy: 1250 km, 213 L → 777 mi / 56.3 gal = 13.8 mpg.

Dimensional analysis is the engine behind [[Density|density]] calculations and [[TemperatureMeasurement|temperature conversions]], and it relies on [[SIUnits]] and [[SignificantFigures]] for correct units and precision.

## Stoichiometric Conversions (Ch 3)

The factor-label method is also the engine behind composition and solution calculations: grams ↔ moles via [[MolarMass|molar mass]] (g/mol), moles ↔ particles via [[AvogadrosNumber|Avogadro's number]], and solute mass ↔ volume via [[Molarity|molarity]] (mol/L). Each step is a conversion factor whose units cancel.
