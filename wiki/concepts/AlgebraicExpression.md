# Algebraic Expression

An **algebraic expression** is a number, a [[Variable|variable]], or any combination of numbers, variables, and operation symbols — for example `3 + 5`, `x + 7`, `2x − 4`, or `5x² + 3x`. The defining feature is what it *lacks*: an expression has **no equals sign**. The moment two expressions are joined by `=`, the result is an [[Equation|equation]], not an expression. In OpenStax [[Prealgebra]] 2e (Chapter 2, [[prealgebra-2e-ch02-language-of-algebra]]), distinguishing an expression from an equation is one of the first skills, because the two are handled very differently: you *simplify or evaluate* an expression, but you *solve* an equation.

Every expression is built from **terms**. A **term** is a constant or the product of a constant and one or more variables — in `5x² + 3x − 7`, the terms are `5x²`, `3x`, and `7`. The constant factor in a term is its **coefficient** (the coefficient of `5x²` is 5; a lone variable like `x` has an understood coefficient of 1). **Like terms** are terms that are either both constants or have the same variables raised to the same exponents; `3x` and `5x` are like terms, but `3x` and `3x²` are not.

To **evaluate** an expression, substitute a given number for each variable and then simplify using the [[OrderOfOperations|order of operations]]. For example, `2x + 7` evaluated at `x = 3` is `2(3) + 7 = 13`. Care is needed with exponents: `x²` means `x · x`, so it is *not* the same as `2x` — at `x = 5`, `x² = 25` while `2x = 10`.

To **simplify** an expression you **combine like terms**: identify the like terms, use the Commutative Property of Addition to move them next to each other, and add their coefficients while keeping the variable part. So `3x + 7 + 4x` simplifies to `7x + 7`. Combining like terms shortens an expression without changing its value for any input.

Expressions are also the bridge between English and algebra. **Translating** a word phrase produces an expression: "the sum of a number and 8" → `n + 8`; "5 less than twice a number" → `2n − 5`. Watch how grouping changes meaning — "the sum of 5 times m and n" is `5m + n`, whereas "5 times the sum of m and n" is `5(m + n)`. These translated expressions become the two sides of an [[Equation|equation]] when a word like *is* or *equals* appears.
