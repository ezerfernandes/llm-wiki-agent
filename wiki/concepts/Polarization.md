## Definition
Polarization is the property that a transverse [[Wave]]'s oscillations have a definite directional orientation perpendicular to its propagation. For an [[ElectromagneticWave]], the polarization direction is that of the electric field. Unpolarized light contains random orientations; polarized light oscillates in one plane.

## Key Points
- A polarizing filter transmits only the E-field component along its transmission axis.
- Light becomes partially polarized by reflection (reflected light is preferentially horizontally polarized) and by scattering (scattered light is polarized perpendicular to the incident direction).
- At Brewster's angle, reflected light is completely polarized — the basis of polarizing sunglasses for cutting glare.
- Optically active substances rotate the plane of polarization; birefringent crystals split light into ordinary and extraordinary rays.
- Applications: polarized sunglasses, LCD displays (voltage-controlled rotation), photoelastic stress analysis, sugar/protein concentration measurement, and sky-darkening photographic filters.

## Equations
- Malus's law: `I = I₀ cos²θ` (θ = angle between incoming polarization and filter axis)
- Brewster's angle: `tan θ_b = n₂ / n₁`
