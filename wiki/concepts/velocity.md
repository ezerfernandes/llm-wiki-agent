# Velocity

## Definition
Velocity is a [[VectorQuantity]] equal to [[Displacement]] divided by the time interval. Because displacement is a vector, velocity has both magnitude and direction. Its magnitude is [[Speed]] (a scalar).

## Key Points
- **Average velocity** = displacement / elapsed time.
- **Instantaneous velocity** is the velocity at a specific instant (over an infinitesimally small interval).
- Velocity differs from speed: speed uses distance traveled, velocity uses displacement.
- Average velocity hides intermediate detail; smaller time intervals reveal more.
- A round trip gives zero average velocity (displacement = 0) even though average speed is nonzero.
- On a position–time graph the slope is velocity (see [[GraphicalAnalysisOfMotion]]).

## Equations
- Δt = t_f − t₀
- v̄ = Δx/Δt = (x_f − x₀)/(t_f − t₀)
