## Definition
Two-dimensional kinematics is the description of motion confined to a plane (e.g. a ball on a pool table), extending one-dimensional [[Kinematics]] by analyzing motion along two perpendicular axes. Three-dimensional kinematics generalizes this to motion not restricted to a single plane.

## Key Points
- Its central principle is the **independence of perpendicular motions**: horizontal and vertical components are independent and can be analyzed separately, then recombined.
- Most real motion follows curved paths; representing position and velocity as vectors (see [[VectorQuantity]]) makes this tractable.
- The straight-line resultant of perpendicular displacements obeys the Pythagorean theorem, so total displacement is generally shorter than the distance traveled along the path.
- Tools: graphical and analytical [[VectorAddition]], [[VectorComponents]], applied to [[ProjectileMotion]] and [[RelativeVelocity]].

## Equations
- Resultant of perpendicular displacements: c = √(a² + b²)
- Components: Ax = A cos θ ; Ay = A sin θ
