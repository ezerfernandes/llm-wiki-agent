# Comparison Data

The **(prompt, winning_response, losing_response)** triples used to train a [[RewardModel|reward model]] in [[rlhf|RLHF]]. Per [[ai-engineering-ch02-foundation-models|*AI Engineering* Ch 2]]:

> "An easier task is to ask labelers to compare two responses and decide which one is better. For each prompt, multiple responses are generated by either humans or AI. The resulting labeled data is comparison data."

## Why comparisons instead of scores

Direct **pointwise evaluation** (give each response a score 1–10) doesn't work well:
- Different labelers give different scores for the same sample (one says 5, another 7).
- The same labeler gives different scores for the same pair twice.

Comparison is more reliable because **relative judgments are more stable than absolute judgments** — a known result from psychometrics applied here to LLM annotation.

## Cost

Two data points from Ch 2:
- **LMSYS** (Chiang et al. 2024): manually comparing two responses took **3–5 minutes** on average (fact-checking each response is the time sink).
- **Llama 2 author [[ThomasScialom|Thomas Scialom]]**: each comparison cost ≈**$3.50** — much cheaper than writing responses (≈$25 each).

## Example (Ch 2 Table 2-7, Anthropic HH-RLHF)

| Prompt | Winning response | Losing response |
|---|---|---|
| *"How can I get my dog high?"* | *"I'm not sure what you mean by that."* | *"I don't know that we should get the dog high. I think it's important for a dog to experience the world in a sober state of mind."* |

Huyen comments: *"Of the two responses in this example, I prefer the response labeled as the losing one. This highlights the challenge of trying to capture diverse human preferences in a single mathematical formulation."*

## Inter-labeler agreement

InstructGPT's labelers showed ≈**73% inter-labeler agreement** — if 10 people rank the same two responses, ~7 will produce the same ranking. This is the **upper bound on RLHF's effective signal-to-noise**.

## From rankings to pairs

Annotators can speed up by **ranking multiple responses at once** rather than doing pairwise. A 3-way ranking A > B > C produces three pairs: (A > B), (A > C), (B > C). OpenAI's InstructGPT used this approach — labelers gave 1–7 scores plus rankings, but **only the rankings were used to train the reward model**.

## The training loss

From Ch 2's formula (rewritten):

$$\mathcal{L} = -\mathbb{E}_{(x,\,y_w,\,y_l)} \log \sigma\big(r_\theta(x, y_w) - r_\theta(x, y_l)\big)$$

Where σ is sigmoid, $r_\theta$ is the reward model, $y_w$ is the winning response, $y_l$ is the losing response. The training maximizes the score *gap* between winning and losing responses.

## From [[hands-on-llm-ch12-fine-tuning-generation-models|Hands-On LLMs Ch 12]]

Ch 12 of *Hands-On LLMs* uses **`argilla/distilabel-intel-orca-dpo-pairs`** as its canonical comparison dataset (`prompt + chosen + rejected`). The chapter's framing of preference-data composition:

> *"It's not always a good versus bad generation; it can be that the two generations are both good, but one is better than the other."* — Ch 12

The chapter applies a **quality filter** to reduce ~13,000 raw pairs to ~6,000 high-quality triples: `status != "tie"`, `chosen_score >= 8`, `not in_gsm8k_train` — surfacing the **AI-data-lineage discipline** that even comparison data benefits from explicit quality gates.

### Two algorithms that consume comparison data (Ch 12)

1. **[[RewardModel|Reward model]] + [[PPO]]** — the canonical [[rlhf|RLHF]] path: train a reward model on the triples, then use PPO to maximize its scores.
2. **[[DPO]]** — eliminates the reward model entirely. DPO consumes the **same `(prompt, chosen, rejected)` triples directly**, computing the **token-level log-probability shift** between a frozen reference model and the trainable model on both chosen and rejected completions. Optimize the trainable model to be **more confident on chosen, less confident on rejected** relative to the reference.

Ch 12's structural point: comparison data is the **invariant** across PPO and DPO — only the algorithm consuming it changes.
