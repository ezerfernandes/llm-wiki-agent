# Band of Stability

The **band of stability** (also belt, zone, or valley of stability) is the narrow region on a plot of neutron number versus proton number into which the roughly **250 stable nuclides** fall. A [[NuclideNotation|nuclide]] lying outside this band is unstable and undergoes [[RadioactiveDecayChemistry|radioactive decay]] toward it.

### Neutron-to-proton (n:p) ratio
- **Light** stable nuclei have roughly equal protons and neutrons (n:p ≈ 1); e.g. nitrogen-14 (7p, 7n).
- **Heavier** stable nuclei need progressively more neutrons: iron-56 (26p, 30n; n:p = 1.15), lead-207 (82p, 125n; n:p = 1.52). The extra neutrons add [[StrongNuclearForce|strong-force]] attraction to offset growing proton-proton Coulomb repulsion.

The position of a nuclide relative to the band predicts its [[RadioactiveDecayChemistry|decay mode]]:
- **Above** the band (too many neutrons, high n:p) → **beta decay**
- **Below** the band (too few neutrons, low n:p) → **positron emission** or **electron capture**
- **Heavy** nuclei beyond the band (A > 200) → **alpha decay**

### Extra-stable patterns
Even counts and "magic numbers" enhance stability:

| Protons | Neutrons | Stable isotopes |
|---|---|---|
| Even | Even | 157 |
| Even | Odd | 53 |
| Odd | Even | 50 |
| Odd | Odd | 5 |

**Magic numbers** — 2, 8, 20, 28, 50, 82, 126 nucleons — correspond to complete nuclear shells (analogous to noble-gas electron shells). **Doubly magic** nuclei (magic in both protons and neutrons), such as $^{4}_{2}\text{He}$, $^{16}_{8}\text{O}$, $^{40}_{20}\text{Ca}$, and $^{208}_{82}\text{Pb}$, are especially stable.

All nuclides with **Z > 83** are unstable.
