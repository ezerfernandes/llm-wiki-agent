# Foundation Model Use Cases

The eight-category taxonomy [[ChipHuyen|Chip Huyen]] develops in [[ai-engineering-ch01-intro|*AI Engineering* Ch 1]] for surveying real-world applications of [[FoundationModel|foundation models]]. Drawn from interviewing 50 enterprises, reading 100+ case studies, and analyzing 205 open-source AI applications with 500+ GitHub stars.

## The eight categories

| Category | Consumer examples | Enterprise examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Coding** | Coding | Coding |
| **Image and video production** | Photo / video editing, design | Presentation, ad generation |
| **Writing** | Email, social media, blog posts | Copywriting, SEO, reports, memos, design docs |
| **Education** | Tutoring, essay grading | Employee onboarding, upskill training |
| **Conversational bots** | General chatbot, AI companion | Customer support, product copilots |
| **[[InformationAggregation\|Information aggregation]]** | Summarization, talk-to-your-docs | Summarization, market research |
| **[[DataOrganization\|Data organization]]** | Image search, Memex | Knowledge management, document processing |
| **[[WorkflowAutomation\|Workflow automation]]** | Travel / event planning | Data extraction, lead generation |

## Other taxonomies Huyen surveyed

- **AWS** — three buckets: customer experience, employee productivity, process optimization.
- **2024 [[OReilly|O'Reilly]] survey** — eight: programming, data analysis, customer support, marketing copy, other copy, research, web design, art.
- **Deloitte** — by value capture: cost reduction, process efficiency, growth, accelerating innovation.
- **Gartner** — adds *business continuity* (companies that go out of business without AI): 7% of executives in 2023.

## Key empirical findings

- **Coding is the most popular use case across multiple surveys** — partly because AI is good at coding, partly because early AI engineers are themselves coders.
- **Enterprises prefer internal-facing applications** (e.g., knowledge management) over external-facing (e.g., customer-support chatbots). a16z Growth (2024) data shows companies deploy internal applications faster — *"internal applications help companies develop their AI engineering expertise while minimizing the risks associated with data privacy, compliance, and potential catastrophic failures."*
- **Close-ended tasks dominate enterprise** — classification is easier to evaluate than open-ended generation, so risks are easier to estimate.
- **Categories overlap** — *"an application can belong to more than one category"* (e.g., a bot that provides companionship and aggregates information).

## Eloundou et al. (2023) on occupational exposure

> *"GPTs are GPTs"* (Eloundou et al., 2023) studies which occupations are most exposed to AI. They define a task as "exposed" if AI/AI-software can cut task time by ≥50%. **100% exposed**: interpreters/translators, tax preparers, web designers, writers, mathematicians. **No exposure**: cooks, stonemasons, athletes.
