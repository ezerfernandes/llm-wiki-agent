# Percent Composition

The **percent composition** of a compound is the percentage by mass contributed by each of its elements:

$$\%\,\text{element} = \frac{\text{mass of element}}{\text{mass of compound}} \times 100\%$$

It can be obtained two ways: from **experimental mass data** for a sample, or **from the formula** using [[MolarMass|molar masses]].

## From Experimental Data

Divide each element's measured mass by the total sample mass.
- A 12.04 g sample with 7.34 g C, 1.85 g H, 2.85 g N → 61.0% C, 15.4% H, 23.7% N.

## From a Chemical Formula

Multiply each element's count by its molar mass, divide by the compound's molar mass.
- Aspirin (C₉H₈O₄, 180.159 g/mol): %C = 9(12.01)/180.159 = 60.00%; %H = 8(1.008)/180.159 = 4.476%; %O = 4(16.00)/180.159 = 35.52%.

## Why It Matters

Percent composition is the link between a measurable bulk property (mass fractions) and atomic-level structure: dividing each mass percent by the element's molar mass converts composition into mole ratios, the first step in deriving an [[EmpiricalFormula|empirical formula]] (a convenient trick is to assume a 100 g sample so each percent becomes grams directly).
