## Definition
Loudness is the subjective perception of a sound's strength. Unlike [[SoundIntensity]] (a physical W/m² quantity) or the [[Decibel]] level, loudness depends jointly on intensity **and** frequency, and is measured in **phons**.

## Key Points
- Ear sensitivity peaks at 2,000–5,000 Hz; sounds at very low or very high frequencies need greater intensity to seem equally loud.
- **Equal-loudness curves** map the intensity required at each frequency to produce a constant perceived loudness.
- By definition, at 1000 Hz the loudness in phons equals the sound level in decibels.
- Hearing spans a ~10¹² range from the threshold (~10⁻¹² W/m², 0 dB) to the pain threshold (~1 W/m²).
- Audiograms chart hearing thresholds across frequencies; noise-induced loss often dips near 4000 Hz, and age-related presbycusis erodes high frequencies first.
