## Definition
Relativistic energy is the energy framework of [[SpecialRelativity|special relativity]], in which a moving object's **total energy** is E = γmc² — its **rest energy** E₀ = mc² plus its **relativistic kinetic energy** KE = (γ−1)mc². It generalizes classical [[KineticEnergy|kinetic energy]] and extends [[ConservationOfEnergy|energy conservation]] to include [[MassEnergyEquivalence|mass-energy conversion]].

## Key Points
- **Rest energy** E₀ = mc² is the energy a mass has even at rest (see [[MassEnergyEquivalence]]).
- **Total energy** E = γmc² grows with speed and diverges as v → c.
- **Relativistic kinetic energy** KE = (γ−1)mc² reduces to the classical ½mv² at low speed (binomial expansion) but greatly exceeds it near c — an electron at 0.990c has ~12.4× the classical KE.
- The **energy–momentum relation** E² = (pc)² + (mc²)² ties total energy to [[RelativisticMomentum|relativistic momentum]]; for massless or ultra-relativistic particles, E ≈ pc.
- The divergence of E as v → c is another reason no massive object can reach light speed.
- Energy is conserved in all inertial frames once mass-energy interconversion is counted.

## Equations
- Rest energy: E₀ = mc²
- Total energy: E = γ mc²
- Kinetic energy: KE_rel = (γ − 1) mc²
- Energy–momentum: E² = (pc)² + (mc²)²
- Low-speed limit: KE → ½ m v²
