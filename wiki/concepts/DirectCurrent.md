## Definition
**Direct current (DC)** is [[ElectricCurrent|current]] that flows in a single direction, usually at constant [[Voltage|voltage]]. Batteries and DC power supplies deliver it; it is the simplest case for applying [[OhmsLaw|Ohm's law]] (V = IR) and power relations directly with steady values.

## Key Points
- Contrasts with [[AlternatingCurrent|alternating current]], whose direction periodically reverses.
- DC is produced by batteries, solar cells, and rectified supplies; many electronic devices run internally on DC even when plugged into AC mains.
- For DC, instantaneous and average values coincide, so no rms conversion is needed.
- DC dominated early power systems (Edison) but lost to AC for distribution because DC voltage was hard to transform efficiently; modern high-voltage DC transmission revisits DC for specific long-distance links.

## Equations
- V = I R  ([[OhmsLaw|Ohm's law]], steady values)
- P = I V = I² R = V² / R  ([[ElectricPower|power]])
