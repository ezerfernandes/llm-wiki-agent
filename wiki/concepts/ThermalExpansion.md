## Definition
**Thermal expansion** is the change in a material's size or volume when its temperature changes.
Higher temperature means more vigorous atomic vibration, which pushes neighboring atoms farther
apart on average, so the material grows; cooling reverses this.

## Key Points
- **Linear expansion** uses the coefficient of linear expansion **α** (units 1/°C or 1/K), a
  material property. Area expansion goes roughly as 2α and volume as **β ≈ 3α**.
- **Thermal stress**: a material that is heated or cooled but constrained from expanding/contracting
  develops large internal forces (and [[Pressure|pressure]]), which can rupture containers or
  buckle structures.
- Engineering responses: expansion joints in bridges and railroads, slack in power lines, low-α
  glass (Pyrex) to resist cracking, and matched expansion coefficients for dental fillings and
  implant coatings.
- **Water anomaly**: between 0 °C and 4 °C water expands as it *cools*, reaching maximum
  [[Density|density]] at 4 °C. Ice is therefore less dense and floats, insulating the liquid below.
- Sea-level rise is partly the thermal expansion of warming seawater.

## Equations
- Linear: ΔL = α·L·ΔT
- Area: ΔA = 2α·A·ΔT
- Volume: ΔV = β·V·ΔT, with β ≈ 3α
- Pressure from constrained volume change: P = (ΔV / V₀)·B (B = bulk modulus)
