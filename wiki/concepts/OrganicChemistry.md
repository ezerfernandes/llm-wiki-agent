# Organic Chemistry

**Organic chemistry** is the chemistry of **carbon compounds**. An **organic compound** is a natural or synthetic substance whose principal element is **carbon**, bonded to hydrogen and to other carbon atoms. (A few carbon-containing species — carbonates, cyanides, CO, and CO₂ — are conventionally treated as *inorganic* and excluded.) Because carbon can form **up to four strong covalent bonds to other carbon atoms**, it builds chains and rings of essentially unlimited size and shape: about 10 million organic compounds are known, with an estimated **10⁶⁰** possible. (Named *OrganicChemistry* to distinguish the discipline from the inorganic [[CarbonGroupChemistry|carbon-group descriptive chemistry]].)

## Vitalism and Wöhler

Early chemists called substances extracted from organisms "organic" and held to **vitalism** — the belief that only living things possessed the vital force to make such molecules. In **1828**, [[FriedrichWohler|Friedrich Wöhler]] synthesized **urea** (a body-fluid compound) from non-living materials, demonstrating that organic molecules obey the same natural laws as inorganic ones and founding organic chemistry as a science.

## How the field is organized

Organic compounds are classified by their **[[OrganicFunctionalGroup|functional groups]]** — the parts of a molecule that impart specific reactivity. The OpenStax chapter is structured around this idea:

- **[[Hydrocarbon|Hydrocarbons]]** (C and H only): [[Alkane|alkanes]], [[Alkene|alkenes]], [[Alkyne|alkynes]], and [[AromaticHydrocarbon|aromatic]] rings.
- Oxygen-containing groups: [[AlcoholsAndEthers|alcohols and ethers]]; the carbonyl family [[AldehydesAndKetones|aldehydes and ketones]] and [[CarboxylicAcidsAndEsters|carboxylic acids and esters]].
- Nitrogen-containing groups: [[AminesAndAmides|amines and amides]].

Recurring themes include **[[OrganicIsomerism|isomerism]]**, the three reaction patterns (substitution, addition, oxidation), and **[[OrganicPolymer|polymerization]]**.
