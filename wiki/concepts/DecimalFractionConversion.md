# Decimal–Fraction Conversion

A [[Decimal|decimal]] and a [[Fraction|fraction]] are two notations for the same kind of number, and each converts to the other. OpenStax [[Prealgebra]] 2e (Chapter 5, [[prealgebra-2e-ch05-decimals]], §5.1 and §5.3) gives both directions.

## Decimal → fraction
A decimal already *is* a fraction with a power-of-ten denominator. Check the digit left of the point (zero → a proper fraction; nonzero → a [[MixedNumber|mixed number]]), take the place value of the rightmost digit as the denominator and the fractional digits as the numerator, then simplify. The number of zeros in the denominator equals the number of decimal places: `0.68 = 68/100 = 17/25`.

## Fraction → decimal
**Divide the numerator by the denominator** — the fraction bar *is* a division sign. `3/4 = 3 ÷ 4 = 0.75`; `−7/2 = −3.5`. Sign rules from [[SignedNumberArithmetic]] carry over.

## Terminating vs. repeating decimals
The division may **terminate** (`3/4 = 0.75`) or it may produce a **repeating decimal** — a decimal in which a digit or block of digits repeats endlessly (`4/3 = 1.333…`). A repeating block is marked with an **overline (bar)** over the repeating digits:
- `1.333… = 1.3̄`
- `4.1666… = 4.16̄` (only the 6 repeats)
- `4.161616… = 4.1̄6̄` (the block "16" repeats)
- `0.271271271… = 0.2̄7̄1̄`

## Comparing and ordering across forms
To compare a fraction with a decimal (or to order a mixed list), **convert the fractions to decimals first**, then compare digit by digit as with any decimals — `3/8 = 0.375 < 0.4`. For negatives, the value farther to the right on the [[NumberLine|number line]] is greater. Mixed expressions that contain both decimals and fractions (and circle formulas using `π`, where `C = 2πr` and `A = πr²` with `π ≈ 3.14`) are evaluated by first converting everything to one form, then applying the [[OrderOfOperations|order of operations]].
