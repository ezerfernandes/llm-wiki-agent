# Standard Enthalpy of Formation (ΔHf°)

The **standard enthalpy of formation (ΔHf°)** is the [[EnthalpyChemistry|enthalpy change]] for a reaction in which exactly **1 mole** of a pure substance is formed from its constituent elements in their **most stable states** under standard-state conditions.

## Standard State
A **standard state** is a reference set of conditions: pressure of **1 bar** (solutions at **1 M**), conventionally at **298.15 K (25 °C)** unless stated otherwise. The superscript ° denotes standard-state values (ΔH° vs. nonstandard ΔH).

## Key Rule
> The standard enthalpy of formation of an element in its most stable form is **zero**.

So the formation reaction begins with elements in their standard states (e.g., carbon as graphite, oxygen as O₂(g), phosphorus as P₄) and produces exactly 1 mol of product:
- CO₂(g): C(s, graphite) + O₂(g) → CO₂(g)   ΔHf° = −393.5 kJ/mol
- NO₂(g): ½N₂(g) + O₂(g) → NO₂(g)   ΔHf° = +33.2 kJ/mol (note the ½ N₂, so exactly 1 mol product forms)
- C₂H₅OH(l): 2C(s, graphite) + 3H₂(g) + ½O₂(g) → C₂H₅OH(l)

## Computing Reaction Enthalpy
Tabulated ΔHf° values give any reaction's enthalpy via (an application of [[HessLaw|Hess's law]]):

**ΔH°_reaction = Σ n · ΔHf°(products) − Σ n · ΔHf°(reactants)**

where n are the stoichiometric coefficients. Example — 3NO₂(g) + H₂O(l) → 2HNO₃(aq) + NO(g) gives ΔH°_rxn = [2(−207.4) + (+90.25)] − [3(+33.2) + (−285.83)] = −138.4 kJ.

The **standard enthalpy of combustion (ΔHc°)** is the analogous quantity for burning 1 mol of a substance in oxygen under standard conditions (e.g., methane −890.8 kJ/mol, ethanol −1366.8 kJ/mol).
