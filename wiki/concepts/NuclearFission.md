## Definition
Nuclear fission is a nuclear reaction in which a heavy nucleus splits into lighter **fission fragments**, releasing energy. Energy is liberated because the binding energy per nucleon (see [[NuclearBindingEnergy]]) is higher in the medium-mass products than in the heavy parent, so the mass deficit converts to kinetic energy via [[MassEnergyEquivalence]].

## Key Points
- **Neutron-induced fission:** a free neutron is absorbed, deforming the nucleus until Coulomb repulsion overcomes the nuclear force and it splits (liquid-drop model), emitting more neutrons.
- Fissile fuels ²³⁵U (0.72% of natural U) and ²³⁹Pu have odd neutron counts; absorbing a neutron adds ~2 MeV of binding energy that promotes fission. ²³⁸U (99.27%) is hard to fission.
- The emitted neutrons enable a [[NuclearChainReaction]].
- 1 kg of ²³⁵U releases ~8.21 × 10¹³ J (≈14,000 barrels of oil) — enormous power density, though per kg below [[NuclearFusion]] fuel.
- ²³⁵U is enriched from ²³⁸U by gaseous diffusion of UF₆, exploiting mass-dependent molecular speeds.

## Equations
- Energy released: E = Δm·c²
- General: n + ᴬX → FF₁ + FF₂ + x·n
- Example: n + ²³⁵U → ¹⁴²Ba + ⁹¹Kr + 3n
- Spontaneous: ²³⁸U → ⁹⁵Sr + ¹⁴⁰Xe + 3n (yields ~171 MeV, below the ~240 MeV ideal because fragments are unequal and neutrons are emitted)
- Breeding ²³⁹Pu: ²³⁸U + n → ²³⁹U + γ; ²³⁹U → ²³⁹Np + β⁻ + ν̄ₑ; ²³⁹Np → ²³⁹Pu + β⁻ + ν̄ₑ
