# Representative (Main-Group) Elements

The **representative elements** (also called main-group elements) are those in which the *s* and *p* orbitals are filling — the groups 1, 2, and 13–18 of the [[PeriodicTable|periodic table]], plus group 12 (its last electron enters an *s* orbital). They are distinguished from the **transition elements** (filling *d* orbitals, groups 3–11) and the **inner transition metals** (filling *f* orbitals). This is the organizing scheme for the whole of [[chemistry-2e-ch18-representative-metals-metalloids-nonmetals|Chemistry 2e Chapter 18]].

## Metal / Metalloid / Nonmetal Classification
- **Representative metals** — found in groups 1, 2, 3, 12, 13, 14, 15 (twenty nonradioactive ones). **Metallic character** is an element's ability to lose its outer valence electrons, giving high thermal and electrical conductivity; it increases down a group and decreases across a period (see [[PeriodicTrends]]).
- **[[Metalloids]]** — properties between metals and nonmetals; typically semiconductors (B, Si, Ge, As, Sb, Te).
- **[[Nonmetals]]** — tend to form anions or molecular compounds.

A **salt** is an ionic compound consisting of cations and anions (other than hydroxide or oxide). **Passivation** is the formation of a protective, nonreactive oxide (or other) film when a reactive metal meets air, which is why many representative metals resist further attack despite high inherent reactivity.

## The Inert Pair Effect
A recurring theme for the heavier p-block elements: they form a stable ion with an oxidation state **two lower than the group value**, because the valence *s* electron pair stays bound. Examples: Tl⁺ (vs Tl³⁺), Sn²⁺/Pb²⁺ (vs 4+), Bi³⁺ (vs 5+).

The chapter treats each family in turn: [[AlkaliMetals]] (group 1), [[AlkalineEarthMetals]] (group 2), [[MainGroupMetals|the group 12–15 representative metals]], [[Metalloids]], and the nonmetals — [[HydrogenChemistry]], [[CarbonGroupChemistry]], [[NitrogenGroupChemistry]], [[OxygenChemistry]], [[SulfurChemistry]], [[HalogensChemistry]], and [[NobleGasesChemistry]].
