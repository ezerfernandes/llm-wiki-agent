## Definition
Mass is the quantity of matter in an object — a measure of its [[Inertia]] — and is invariant with location. Weight is the gravitational force exerted on that mass by a nearby massive body, and therefore varies with location (e.g., the Moon's surface gravity is about 1/6 of Earth's).

## Key Points
- Mass (kg) is constant on Earth, in orbit, or on the Moon; weight (N) changes with the local gravitational field g.
- Weight is computed via [[NewtonsSecondLaw]] applied to free-fall under gravity.
- **Free-fall** is the state in which gravity alone acts (air resistance negligible).
- Bathroom scales actually measure force (the [[NormalForce]] or spring force), which equals weight only when not accelerating; apparent weight changes under acceleration (e.g., in an elevator).

## Equations
- Weight: w = mg, with g ≈ 9.80 m/s² near Earth's surface
- 1 N = 1 kg·m/s²
