# Limiting Reactant

The **limiting reactant** (limiting reagent) is the substance that is **entirely consumed** in a reaction, thereby capping the amount of product that can form. Any reactant present in more than the stoichiometric amount is an **excess reactant**.

The "stoichiometric amounts" are the relative reactant/product amounts written in the balanced [[ChemicalEquation|equation]]; real reactions are often run with one reactant in excess, so identifying the limiting reactant is the first step in computing [[PercentYield|theoretical yield]].

## Two Ways to Identify It

**Method 1 — compare ratios.** Compute moles of each reactant; compare the *provided* mole ratio to the *stoichiometric* ratio. The reactant present in less-than-stoichiometric proportion is limiting.

**Method 2 — compute product from each.** Using [[ReactionStoichiometry|reaction stoichiometry]], calculate the product each reactant would yield if fully consumed. The reactant giving the **smaller** amount of product is limiting.

## Worked Examples

**H₂(g) + Cl₂(g) → 2HCl(g), with 3 mol H₂ and 2 mol Cl₂.** Provided ratio 1.5:1 exceeds the 1:1 stoichiometric ratio, so H₂ is in excess and **Cl₂ is limiting**. By Method 2: H₂ → 6 mol HCl, Cl₂ → 4 mol HCl; Cl₂ gives less, confirming it limits.

**3Si(s) + 2N₂(g) → Si₃N₄(s), with 2.00 g Si and 1.50 g N₂.** mol Si = 0.0712, mol N₂ = 0.0535; provided ratio 1.33 < stoichiometric 1.5, so **Si is limiting**. By Method 2: Si → 0.0237 mol Si₃N₄ vs N₂ → 0.0268 mol; Si gives less.
