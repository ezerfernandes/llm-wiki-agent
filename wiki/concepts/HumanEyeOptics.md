## Definition
The human eye is a converging optical system in which the cornea and lens together form a real, inverted image on the retina. Focus is maintained not by moving the image plane (the lens-to-retina distance is fixed at about 2.00 cm) but by changing the eye's optical power, a process called **accommodation**.

## Key Points
- The cornea provides roughly two-thirds of the focusing power because of the large air-to-cornea refractive-index change; the lens fine-tunes the rest.
- The ciliary muscle changes lens curvature/thickness to vary power: a relaxed young eye is ~50 D (distant objects) and rises to ~54 D when fully accommodated for near objects.
- The **near point** (closest sharply focused distance) is conventionally 25 cm; the **far point** is normally infinity.
- **Presbyopia** is age-related loss of accommodation as the lens stiffens.
- Refractive indices: aqueous/vitreous humor ~1.34, cornea ~1.38, lens ~1.41 (densest at center).
- The **fovea** has the densest cone packing and sharpest acuity; peripheral retina (mostly rods) is less sharp and color-insensitive.

## Equations
- Power: P = 1/f
- Thin lens (power form): P = 1/d_o + 1/d_i
- Magnification: m = h_i/h_o = − d_i/d_o
