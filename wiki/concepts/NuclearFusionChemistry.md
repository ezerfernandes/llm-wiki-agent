# Nuclear Fusion (Chemistry)

> Disambiguation: this is the OpenStax *Chemistry 2e* (Ch 21) treatment. For the College Physics 2e treatment see [[NuclearFusion]].

**Fusion** combines very light nuclei into a heavier nucleus, releasing large amounts of energy because the product has higher [[NuclearBindingEnergyChemistry|binding energy per nucleon]]. The lost mass is converted to energy via E = mc².

### Examples
**Solar fusion** (net): $4\,^{1}_{1}\text{H} \rightarrow ^{4}_{2}\text{He} + 2\,^{0}_{+1}\text{e}$. The helium nucleus has 0.7% less mass than four protons, yielding ~**3.6 × 10¹¹ kJ per mole of ⁴He** — this powers the Sun and other stars.

**Deuterium-tritium fusion**: $^{2}_{1}\text{H} + ^{3}_{1}\text{H} \rightarrow ^{4}_{2}\text{He} + ^{1}_{0}\text{n}$; mass loss 0.0188 amu → **1.69 × 10⁹ kJ per mole of ⁴He**.

### Energy comparison

| Process | Energy |
|---|---|
| Solar fusion | 3.6 × 10¹¹ kJ/mol of ⁴He |
| U-235 [[NuclearFissionChemistry|fission]] | 1.8 × 10¹⁰ kJ/mol |
| ²H–³H fusion | 1.69 × 10⁹ kJ/mol of ⁴He |
| Octane combustion (chemical) | 5,471 kJ/mol |

Per unit mass, fusion releases even more energy than fission, and both vastly exceed chemical combustion.

### Why it is so hard
Fusion requires temperatures of ~**15,000,000 K or higher** (thermonuclear) to force positively charged nuclei together against their mutual repulsion; at such temperatures matter is fully ionized **plasma**. A **fusion reactor** would control fusion of light nuclei, but no solid material can hold a 15-million-kelvin plasma, so two confinement schemes are researched: **magnetic-field confinement** and **focused-laser confinement**. The goal is net energy gain. As of publication no self-sustaining fusion reactor exists; ITER (France) targets ~2027, and the National Ignition Facility briefly produced >500 TW peak power, delivering 1.85 MJ and reaching ignition conditions for billionths of a second.
