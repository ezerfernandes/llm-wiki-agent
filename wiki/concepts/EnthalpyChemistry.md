# Enthalpy (H) and Enthalpy Change (ΔH)

**Enthalpy (H)** is a thermodynamic state function defined from [[InternalEnergyThermodynamics|internal energy]], pressure, and volume:

**H = U + PV**

Absolute enthalpy values cannot be measured directly — only the **enthalpy change (ΔH)** of a process can be determined.

## ΔH Equals Heat of Reaction at Constant Pressure
Using the [[FirstLawOfThermodynamics|first law]] (ΔU = q + w) and expansion work (PΔV = −w), at constant pressure with only expansion work:

**ΔH = ΔU + PΔV = (q_p + w) − w = q_p**

So for a constant-pressure process the **enthalpy change equals the heat of reaction (q_p)** — which is why constant-pressure ([[Calorimetry|coffee-cup]]) calorimetry measures ΔH directly. ΔH is the **enthalpy of reaction** when the process is a chemical reaction.

## Sign Convention
- **ΔH < 0** — [[ExothermicEndothermic|exothermic]] (releases heat).
- **ΔH > 0** — endothermic (absorbs heat).
- Reversing the reaction reverses the sign of ΔH.

## State Function
Enthalpy is a state function: ΔH depends only on the initial and final states, not the path — the basis of [[HessLaw|Hess's law]].

## Enthalpies of Phase Change (Ch 10)
[[PhaseTransitionChemistry|Phase transitions]] have characteristic enthalpy changes: the **enthalpy of vaporization** (ΔH_vap), **fusion** (ΔH_fus), and **sublimation** (ΔH_sub). Because forming a gas fully overcomes intermolecular attractions while forming a liquid only partly does, **ΔH_fus < ΔH_vap**, and (as a state function consequence) **ΔH_sub = ΔH_fus + ΔH_vap**. For water, ΔH_fus = 6.01 kJ/mol and ΔH_vap = 44.01 kJ/mol.
