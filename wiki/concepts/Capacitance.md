## Definition
**Capacitance** (C) is the amount of [[ElectricCharge|charge]] a [[Capacitor]] stores per unit [[Voltage]]: C = Q/V. It is measured in **farads** (F = C/V) and is a fixed property of the capacitor's geometry and [[Dielectric|dielectric]], independent of the applied voltage.

## Key Points
- Charge stored is proportional to voltage: Q = CV.
- For a parallel-plate capacitor, capacitance grows with plate area and dielectric constant κ and shrinks with separation: C = κε₀A/d.
- Combinations: in **series** the total is below the smallest member; in **parallel** it is above the largest.
- One farad is very large; real capacitors are typically pF–µF, achieved with thin foils and dielectrics.

## Equations
- C = Q / V
- Parallel plate: C = κ ε₀ A / d  (κ = 1 for vacuum/air)
- ε₀ = 8.85 × 10⁻¹² F/m
- Series: 1/C_S = 1/C₁ + 1/C₂ + …
- Parallel: C_P = C₁ + C₂ + …
