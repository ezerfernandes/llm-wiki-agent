## Definition
**Unit conversion** is the process of expressing a measured quantity in different units by multiplying it by one or more **conversion factors** — ratios that equal 1 because their numerator and denominator are equivalent (e.g., 1 km / 1000 m).

## Key Points
- Conversion factors are arranged so that unwanted units cancel and the desired units remain — this is [[DimensionalAnalysis]].
- Within the metric/[[SIUnits|SI]] system, conversions are simply powers of 10 via prefixes (kilo 10³, centi 10⁻², milli 10⁻³, micro 10⁻⁶, nano 10⁻⁹).
- The English system is harder because relationships are non-decimal (12 in/ft, 5280 ft/mile).
- Multiple valid conversion pathways can give the same answer; checking **dimensional consistency** and **reasonableness** validates the result.
- Worked Example 1.1 illustrates converting an average speed between unit systems while preserving [[SignificantFigures]].

## Equations
- Conversion-factor form: quantity in new units = quantity in old units × (new unit / old unit).
- Example: distance (km) = distance (m) × (1 km / 1000 m).
