# Collision

## Definition
A collision is a short, strong interaction between bodies in which [[ConservationOfMomentum]] holds (the net external force is negligible during contact). Collisions are classified by what happens to internal kinetic energy: [[ElasticCollision|elastic]] (conserved) or [[InelasticCollision|inelastic]] (not conserved), with the perfectly inelastic case being the bodies sticking together.

## Key Points
- **Internal kinetic energy** is the sum of the kinetic energies of the bodies; whether it is conserved distinguishes collision types.
- Momentum is always conserved (for negligible external force); kinetic energy is conserved only in elastic collisions.
- **Two dimensions**: momentum conserves independently along perpendicular axes. Treating bodies as **point masses** (structureless, non-rotating) simplifies the analysis.
- For an equal-mass elastic 2D collision with one body initially at rest, the two bodies move off 90° apart (cos(θ₁ − θ₂) = 0) — visible in billiards.
- Scattering experiments exploit collision conservation laws to deduce properties of an unseen target (Rutherford's nucleus, particle physics).

## Equations
- 1D momentum: m₁v₁ + m₂v₂ = m₁v'₁ + m₂v'₂
- 2D x-axis: m₁v₁ = m₁v'₁cosθ₁ + m₂v'₂cosθ₂
- 2D y-axis: 0 = m₁v'₁sinθ₁ + m₂v'₂sinθ₂
- Equal-mass elastic angle: θ₁ − θ₂ = 90°
