## Definition
**Molecular diffusion** is the transport of a substance caused by the random thermal motion (random walk) of its molecules, occurring even without any bulk fluid flow. Net movement is from regions of high concentration to low concentration.

> Disambiguation: distinct from the machine-learning [[DiffusionModel]] / [[StableDiffusion]] generative process. This page is the physical-transport phenomenon from College Physics 2e Ch. 12.

## Key Points
- Net diffusion proceeds down a concentration gradient and stops when concentrations equalize; rate is proportional to the concentration difference.
- The typical distance traveled in time t is the root-mean-square distance x_rms = √(2Dt), where D is the diffusion constant (m²/s).
- At fixed temperature, lighter molecules move faster (speed inversely related to mass), so they diffuse more quickly.
- D rises with temperature because mean molecular kinetic energy (½mv²) increases with absolute temperature.
- Diffusion is extremely slow over macroscopic distances (glucose takes ~21 hours to diffuse 1 cm in water) — which is why organisms evolved circulatory systems and why mixing requires bulk flow.
- Related membrane processes: [[Osmosis]] (water), dialysis (solutes), and active transport (against a gradient, using energy).

## Equations
- x_rms = √(2Dt)
