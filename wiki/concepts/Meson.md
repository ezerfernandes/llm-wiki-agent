## Definition
A **meson** is a [[Hadron|hadron]] composed of one [[Quark|quark]] and one antiquark. Mesons have intermediate mass (between the electron and the nucleon), baryon number **B = 0**, and integer spin (bosons).

## Key Points
- Historically introduced via the **[[CarrierParticle|Yukawa particle]]** (the pion), proposed as the exchange particle that transmits the [[StrongNuclearForce|strong nuclear force]] between [[Nucleon|nucleons]]. Pions (π⁺, π⁻, π⁰) were first seen in cosmic rays in 1947.
- Mesons decay to [[Lepton|leptons]] and photons without producing other hadrons, so meson number is not conserved. Rest masses ~135–550 MeV/c² (e.g. kaons carry strangeness).
- Composition example: π⁺ = ud̄, giving charge (+2/3 + 1/3)qₑ = +qₑ. The J/ψ (cc̄) and Upsilon (bb̄) are heavy-quark mesons that confirmed the charm and bottom flavors.
- The pion's force-carrier role is the *residual* strong force; the more fundamental carriers between quarks are gluons (see [[StandardModel]]).

## Equations
- π⁺ charge: (+2/3 + 1/3)qₑ = +qₑ (ud̄)
- Yukawa range estimate: d ≈ cΔt with ΔE·Δt ≥ h/(4π), giving pion mass ~100 MeV/c²
