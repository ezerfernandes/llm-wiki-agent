# Conjugate Acid-Base Pair

A **conjugate acid-base pair** is two species that differ by a single proton (H⁺). When a [[BronstedLowryAcidsAndBases|Brønsted-Lowry acid]] donates H⁺, the remaining species is its **conjugate base** (it can accept the proton back in the reverse reaction). When a base accepts H⁺, the result is its **conjugate acid**.

For the generic ionization

$$\mathrm{HA + H_2O \rightleftharpoons H_3O^+ + A^-}$$

HA/A⁻ is one conjugate pair (acid/its conjugate base) and H₂O/H₃O⁺ is the other (base/its conjugate acid). Example: in HF + H₂O ⇌ H₃O⁺ + F⁻, the pairs are HF/F⁻ and H₂O/H₃O⁺.

## The Ka·Kb relationship

For a conjugate pair, the [[AcidIonizationConstant|acid ionization constant]] of the acid and the [[BaseIonizationConstant|base ionization constant]] of its conjugate base are linked through the [[WaterAutoionization|ion-product of water]]:

$$K_a \times K_b = [\mathrm{H_3O^+}][\mathrm{OH^-}] = K_w = 1.0\times10^{-14}\ (25\,^\circ\mathrm{C})$$

so $K_a = K_w/K_b$ and $K_b = K_w/K_a$. The immediate consequence: **the stronger the acid, the weaker its conjugate base, and vice versa.** A strong acid (large Ka) has a conjugate base of negligible base strength (tiny Kb).

This is why [[SaltHydrolysis|salt hydrolysis]] works the way it does: the conjugate base of a *weak* acid is itself a meaningful weak base, while the conjugate base of a *strong* acid is essentially inert.
