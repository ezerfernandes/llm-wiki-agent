## Definition
In mechanics, stress is the intensity of an internal force within a material, defined as force per unit area (F/A), with SI units of N/m² (pascals). Applied stress causes [[Strain]] (deformation); their ratio in the elastic region is an elastic modulus (see [[HookesLaw]], [[Elasticity]]).

## Key Points
- A normalized measure: it removes the dependence on the object's size so that material behavior can be compared directly.
- Linked to strain by stress = modulus × strain — Young's modulus (tension/compression), shear modulus (shear), or bulk modulus (volume).
- Tensile strength is the breaking stress at which a material permanently deforms or fractures.
- Distinct from the machine-learning/statistics senses of "stress test" — this page is the physical force-per-area quantity.

## Equations
- Stress: σ = F / A
- Longitudinal: F/A = Y (ΔL/L₀)
- Shear: F/A = S (Δx/L₀)
- Volumetric: F/A = B (ΔV/V₀)
