## Definition
**Capillary action** is the rise (or depression) of a liquid inside a narrow tube, driven by the competition between [[CohesionAndAdhesion|adhesive and cohesive forces]] and mediated by [[SurfaceTension]]. When adhesion to the tube wall dominates (e.g., water in glass), the liquid wets the wall and rises with a concave meniscus; when cohesion dominates (e.g., mercury in glass), the liquid is depressed with a convex meniscus.

## Key Points
- The **contact angle** θ encodes the force balance: small θ → adhesion dominates (rise); large θ → cohesion dominates (depression).
- Capillary rise height is **inversely proportional to tube radius** — narrower tubes lift liquid higher, because less mass must be supported.
- Rise also increases with surface tension γ and decreases with liquid [[Density]] ρ.
- Capillary action alone cannot raise tree sap to great heights (the required radius is implausibly small); transpiration-driven negative [[Pressure]] does most of the work.
- Applications: capillary blood sampling, wicking in fabrics and soils, plant water transport, and water beading on waxed vs. unwaxed surfaces.

## Equations
- h = 2γ cos θ / (ρ g r)  (capillary rise height; r = tube radius)
