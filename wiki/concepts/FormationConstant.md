# Formation Constant (Kf)

The **formation constant, Kf** (also called the **stability constant**), is the [[EquilibriumConstant|equilibrium constant]] for the reaction of a metal ion with [[Ligand|ligands]] to form a [[ComplexIon|complex ion]]. A large Kf indicates a very stable complex (the equilibrium lies far toward the complex).

For Cu⁺ binding cyanide:

$$\mathrm{Cu^+(aq) + 2\,CN^-(aq) \rightleftharpoons Cu(CN)_2^-(aq)} \qquad K_f = \frac{[\mathrm{Cu(CN)_2^-}]}{[\mathrm{Cu^+}][\mathrm{CN^-}]^2}$$

The **dissociation constant** for the reverse reaction is the reciprocal: Kd = 1/Kf.

## Worked example — dissociation of Ag(NH₃)₂⁺

For Ag⁺ + 2 NH₃ ⇌ Ag(NH₃)₂⁺, Kf = 1.7 × 10⁷. Starting from 0.10 M complex, an [[ICETable|ICE table]] gives:

$$K_f = \frac{0.10 - x}{(x)(2x)^2} = 1.7 \times 10^7 \;\Rightarrow\; x^3 = \frac{0.10}{4(1.7\times 10^7)} = 1.5 \times 10^{-9} \;\Rightarrow\; x = 1.1 \times 10^{-3}$$

So [Ag⁺] = 1.1 × 10⁻³ M and only ~1.1% of the complex dissociates — the large Kf keeps the complex intact.

## Role in coupled equilibria

Because complex formation consumes free metal ion, multiplying a [[SolubilityProduct|Ksp]] by a large Kf gives a much larger overall constant for the coupled dissolution (e.g. Al(OH)₃: K = Ksp × Kf = 22; AgBr in thiosulfate: K = Ksp × Kf = 24). See [[CoupledEquilibria]].
