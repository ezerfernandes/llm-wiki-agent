# Rutherford Scattering

**Rutherford scattering** is the deflection of energetic alpha particles ([[AtomicNucleus|helium nuclei]], ~5 MeV) fired at a thin metal (gold) foil, the experiment ([[ErnestRutherford|Rutherford]], with Geiger and Marsden) that revealed the atom's small, dense, positively charged **nucleus**.

## Key Points
- Most alpha particles passed through the foil nearly undeflected, but a small fraction scattered at very large angles — even backward.
- Large-angle scattering can only occur if the atom's positive charge and nearly all its mass are concentrated in a tiny core, not spread out as in [[JJThomson|Thomson's]] plum-pudding model.
- The deduced **nucleus** is ~10⁻¹⁵ m across — roughly 10⁵ times smaller than the ~10⁻¹⁰ m atom — yet holds almost the entire atomic mass (nuclear density ~10¹⁵ g/cm³).
- This established the **planetary model**: light [[Electron|electrons]] orbiting a massive central nucleus, the picture the [[BohrModel|Bohr model]] then quantized.

## Equations
- The scattering interaction is governed by the Coulomb repulsion between the alpha particle (charge +2e) and the nuclear charge +Ze; closest-approach distance follows from equating kinetic energy to electrostatic potential energy.
