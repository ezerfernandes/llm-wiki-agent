# Resonance Structure

When two or more valid [[LewisStructure|Lewis structures]] with the **same arrangement of atoms** can be written for a molecule or ion, the actual electron distribution is an **average** of them. (Named *ResonanceStructure* to avoid collision with a generic `Resonance` page covering oscillatory physics.)

## Resonance Forms and the Resonance Hybrid

- Each individual structure is a **resonance form**; a **double-headed arrow (↔)** connects them.
- The true structure is the **resonance hybrid** — the average of all resonance forms.
- A resonance hybrid *never* matches any single form and does *not* flip back and forth between forms; it is *always* the average.

## Evidence

- **Nitrite ion (NO₂⁻)**: two resonance forms with the double bond on either N–O bond; experimentally both N–O bonds have identical length and strength (intermediate between single and double).
- **Carbonate ion (CO₃²⁻)**: three resonance forms; all three C–O bonds are equivalent.

[[FormalCharge|Formal charge]] is used to judge which resonance forms contribute most.
