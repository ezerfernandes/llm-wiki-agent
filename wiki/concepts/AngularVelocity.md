## Definition
Angular velocity (ω) is the rate of change of rotation angle with time, ω = Δθ/Δt, measured in radians per second. The rotation angle Δθ itself is arc length divided by radius (Δθ = Δs/r), so angle is dimensionless (radians).

## Key Points
- Every point on a rigid rotating body sweeps the same Δθ in the same time, regardless of its distance from the axis.
- One full revolution is 2π rad = 360°, so 1 rad ≈ 57.3°.
- Linear (tangential) speed and angular velocity are coupled by the radius: v = rω.
- At a given linear speed, a larger radius means a smaller angular velocity (e.g., a bigger wheel spins slower than a small one at the same road speed).
- ω is the rotational analog of linear [[Velocity]]; its time rate of change is [[AngularAcceleration]] (α = Δω/Δt).
- ω is one of the core variables of [[RotationalKinematics]] and appears in [[RotationalKineticEnergy]] (½Iω²) and [[AngularMomentum]] (L = Iω).

## Equations
- Δθ = Δs / r
- ω = Δθ / Δt
- v = r ω  (equivalently ω = v / r)
- 1 rad = 360° / 2π ≈ 57.3°
