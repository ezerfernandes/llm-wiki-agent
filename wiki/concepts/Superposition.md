## Definition
The superposition principle states that when two or more [[Wave]]s occupy the same location, the resultant disturbance is the algebraic sum of the individual disturbances (because the underlying forces add).

## Key Points
- Superposition is the basis of [[WaveInterference]], [[StandingWave]]s, and [[Beats]].
- In-phase waves reinforce (constructive interference); out-of-phase waves cancel (destructive interference).
- Real situations usually mix constructive and destructive interference at different locations and times.
- Two slightly different frequencies superimpose into a slow amplitude envelope (beating) over a carrier at the average frequency.

## Equations
- Wave at a point: x = X cos(2π f t)
- Two-wave sum: x = X cos(2π f₁ t) + X cos(2π f₂ t)
- Beat resultant: x = 2X cos(π f_B t) cos(2π f_ave t), with f_ave = (f₁ + f₂)/2
