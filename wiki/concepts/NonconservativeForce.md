## Definition
A **nonconservative force** is one whose [[Work]] depends on the path taken between two points, not just the endpoints. Such a force cannot be assigned a [[PotentialEnergy]]. Friction is the canonical example: it converts [[MechanicalEnergy]] into thermal energy that is not fully recoverable. Nonconservative forces add or remove mechanical energy from a system.

## Key Points
- Total mechanical energy changes by exactly the nonconservative work done, W_nc.
- The general work-energy relation splits net work into conservative and nonconservative parts.
- Friction acting over a distance d does W_nc = −f d, dissipating kinetic energy as heat.
- Contrast with a [[ConservativeForce]], whose work is path-independent and recoverable.

## Equations
- W_net = W_nc + W_c
- W_nc + W_c = ΔKE
- W_nc = ΔKE + ΔPE
- KE_i + PE_i + W_nc = KE_f + PE_f
