## Definition
Mass-energy equivalence is the [[SpecialRelativity|relativistic]] principle that mass is a concentrated form of energy, expressed by the **rest energy** equation E₀ = mc². A small mass corresponds to an enormous energy because c² is very large, and mass and energy can interconvert.

## Key Points
- E = mc² is named in the chapter as a direct consequence of [[EinsteinPostulates|Einstein's postulates]].
- 1 gram of matter holds ~9 × 10¹³ J of rest energy — comparable to a nuclear weapon — yet this is imperceptible in daily life.
- Adding energy adds mass: a charged car battery gains ~10⁻¹⁰ kg, far too small to detect, which is why mass-energy conversion shows up only in nuclear processes with large fractional mass changes.
- Underlies nuclear fission and fusion (mass deficits release energy), solar fusion, and geothermal heat from radioactive decay.
- Forms the rest-energy term of [[RelativisticEnergy|total relativistic energy]] E = γmc² and of the energy–momentum relation E² = (pc)² + (mc²)².
- [[ParticleAccelerator|Particle accelerators]] convert kinetic energy into the mass of new particles — matter created from energy, m = ΔE/c², the basis of [[ParticlePhysics]] discovery; it also sets the maximum mass of [[VirtualParticle|virtual carrier particles]] in the Yukawa range estimate.

## Equations
- E₀ = mc²
- (within total energy) E = γ mc² = E₀ + KE_rel
