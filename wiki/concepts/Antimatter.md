## Definition
**Antimatter** is matter made of **antiparticles**. Every particle has an antiparticle with the **same mass, spin, and lifetime** but **opposite charge** (and opposite baryon/lepton number, strangeness, etc.).

## Key Points
- A particle and its antiparticle **annihilate** on contact, converting their rest mass into energy (usually photons), per [[MassEnergyEquivalence|E = mc²]].
- Antimatter was predicted by P.A.M. Dirac's relativistic quantum theory (1928); the positron (anti-electron) was confirmed experimentally in 1932.
- Particle creation from energy always yields particle–antiparticle pairs, so total charge (and other quantum numbers) is conserved — consistent with [[ConservationOfCharge]] and the other [[ConservationLawsParticlePhysics|conservation laws]].
- [[ParticleAccelerator|Colliders]] exploit antimatter: circulating particle–antiparticle pairs (opposite charge) in one beam pipe lets opposing beams collide head-on (e.g. proton–antiproton at the Tevatron).
- In [[Quark|quark]] terms, antiparticles are built from antiquarks (antibaryons) or quark–antiquark pairs ([[Meson|mesons]]).

## Equations
- Pair annihilation/creation energy: E = mc² (or Δm = E/c²)
