## Definition
Wave interference is the result of [[Superposition]] when waves overlap: their amplitudes add, producing reinforcement or cancellation depending on relative phase.

## Key Points
- **Constructive interference:** waves arrive in phase (crest on crest); amplitudes add, giving a larger combined amplitude.
- **Destructive interference:** waves arrive fully out of phase (crest on trough); they cancel, reaching zero amplitude in the pure case.
- Because energy scales with amplitude squared, constructive interference of two equal waves (amplitude 2X) quadruples [[WaveIntensity]]; total energy is conserved through spatial redistribution.
- Mixed interference in a room (e.g., stereo speakers) creates spatially varying loudness.
- Counter-propagating identical waves interfere into a [[StandingWave]]; close frequencies interfere into [[Beats]].
- **Light/optics case:** the same superposition governs [[WaveOptics]] — the path-length difference between two routes sets the phase. Bright/dark fringes appear in the [[DoubleSlitExperiment]] and [[DiffractionGrating]] (`d sin θ = m λ` for maxima), in [[Diffraction]], and in [[ThinFilmInterference]] (where a 180° phase flip on reflection off a higher-index medium also matters).

## Equations
- Constructive: amplitudes add (up to 2X for two equal waves) → intensity up to 4× (I ∝ X²)
- Destructive: amplitudes subtract (to zero for equal waves)
