# Enzyme

An **enzyme** is a biological catalyst — typically a protein — that accelerates a biologically important reaction, especially in cellular metabolism. Enzymes are a form of [[Catalysis|catalysis]]: they provide a lower-[[ActivationEnergy|activation-energy]] pathway and are not consumed.

## Active site and substrate

An enzyme has an **active site** whose shape is complementary to a specific **substrate**. Binding forms an **enzyme–substrate complex**, a reaction intermediate, before products are released and the enzyme is regenerated. Two models of the fit:
- **Lock-and-key** — active site and substrate have rigidly complementary shapes.
- **Induced fit** — the flexible enzyme adjusts its shape around the substrate while keeping reaction specificity.

## Enzyme classes

| Class | Function |
|---|---|
| Oxidoreductases | [[OxidationReduction|redox]] reactions |
| Transferases | functional-group transfer |
| Hydrolases | hydrolysis |
| Lyases | group elimination forming double bonds |
| Isomerases | isomerization |
| Ligases | bond formation with ATP hydrolysis |

## Example

Glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase (G6PD) is the rate-limiting enzyme of the pentose phosphate pathway that supplies NADPH and regulates glutathione (an antioxidant) in red blood cells. G6PD deficiency — the most common human enzyme deficiency — weakens protection against oxidative damage and can cause jaundice.
