# Raoult's Law

**Raoult's law** describes the **vapor pressure lowering** of a solution: "the partial pressure exerted by any component of an [[IdealSolution|ideal solution]] is equal to the vapor pressure of the pure component multiplied by its mole fraction in the solution."

$$P_A = X_A \, P_A^{\circ}$$

- P_A — partial vapor pressure of component A above the solution
- P°_A — vapor pressure of pure A
- X_A — mole fraction of A in the solution

## Mechanism
A nonvolatile solute occupies surface area, reducing the rate at which solvent molecules vaporize while their condensation rate is unchanged, so equilibrium vapor pressure drops. With a nonvolatile solute, only the solvent contributes:

$$P_{\text{solution}} = X_{\text{solvent}} \, P_{\text{solvent}}^{\circ}$$

For multi-component (volatile) solutions, total vapor pressure sums each component's Raoult contribution:

$$P_{\text{solution}} = \sum_i P_i = \sum_i X_i \, P_i^{\circ}$$

## Consequences
Vapor pressure lowering is the root [[ColligativeProperties|colligative property]] from which [[BoilingPointElevation|boiling point elevation]] and [[FreezingPointDepression|freezing point depression]] follow. Differing component vapor pressures also enable **distillation** — the more volatile component is enriched in the vapor (e.g., fractional distillation of petroleum into diesel, kerosene, gasoline).
