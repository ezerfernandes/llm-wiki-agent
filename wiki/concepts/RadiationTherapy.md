## Definition
Radiation therapy (radiotherapy) is the deliberate therapeutic use of [[IonizingRadiation]], chiefly to kill malignant tumors. It works because rapidly dividing cancer cells are more radiosensitive than normal tissue, so a well-aimed [[RadiationDose]] can destroy a tumor while sparing surrounding cells.

## Key Points
- **Therapeutic ratio** — the fraction of cancer cells killed relative to normal cells damaged; every technique tries to maximize it.
- **External-beam radiotherapy** — narrow beams from multiple angles converge on the tumor, concentrating dose at the target while spreading exposure over healthy tissue; sources include X-rays, ⁶⁰Co gamma rays, and accelerator particle beams.
- **Brachytherapy** — radioactive seeds (e.g. ¹²⁵I, ¹⁰³Pd) implanted in the tumor; short-range emitters with high local RBE.
- **Targeted radiopharmaceuticals** — a [[RadiopharmaceuticalTracer]] (e.g. radioactive iodine, radiolabeled antibodies) homes to cancer cells.
- Typical schedule: ~3–5 doses of ~200 rem per week over several weeks; intervals let normal tissue recover. Totals vary (lung ~10–20 Sv; brain 80+ Sv).

## Equations
- Dose accounting uses [[RadiationDose]]: Dose (Gy) = energy / mass; Dose (Sv) = Dose (Gy) × RBE.
