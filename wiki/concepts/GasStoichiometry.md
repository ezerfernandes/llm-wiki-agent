# Gas Stoichiometry

**Gas stoichiometry** applies the quantitative relationships of [[ReactionStoichiometry|reaction stoichiometry]] to gaseous reactants and products, using the [[IdealGasLawChemistry|ideal gas law]] to connect measurable gas properties (P, V, T) to moles.

## Gas Density and Molar Mass

Because density d = m/V and V = nRT/P, the ideal gas law gives the density of a gas directly from its [[MolarMass|molar mass]] (ℳ):

$$d = \frac{\mathcal{M}P}{RT}$$

Rearranged, gas measurements determine an unknown gas's molar mass:

$$\mathcal{M} = \frac{mRT}{PV}$$

## Volume Ratios = Mole Ratios

The key simplification: by [[AvogadrosLaw|Avogadro's law]], equal volumes of ideal gases at the same T and P contain equal numbers of molecules. Therefore, when all gas volumes are measured at the same temperature and pressure, **the volume ratios equal the stoichiometric coefficients** of the balanced equation. For:

$$\text{N}_2(g) + 3\,\text{H}_2(g) \rightarrow 2\,\text{NH}_3(g)$$

one volume of N₂ reacts with three volumes of H₂ to produce two volumes of NH₃ — no conversion to moles is needed when conditions match.

## General Workflow

1. Use PV = nRT to convert a measured volume/pressure/temperature to **moles**.
2. Apply the **mole ratio** from the balanced equation (the stoichiometric factor).
3. Convert the resulting moles back to **volume** (or mass) at the specified conditions.

For gas mixtures, combine with [[DaltonsLawOfPartialPressures|Dalton's law]] (e.g., gases collected over water need a vapor-pressure correction). All of it is [[DimensionalAnalysis|dimensional analysis]].
