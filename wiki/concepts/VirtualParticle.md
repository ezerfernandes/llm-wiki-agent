## Definition
A **virtual particle** is a particle that exists only briefly, its fleeting existence permitted by the [[HeisenbergUncertaintyPrinciple|Heisenberg uncertainty principle]]. Its effects are detectable, but observing it directly would permanently violate energy conservation.

## Key Points
- The energy–time uncertainty relation ΔE·Δt ≥ h/(4π) allows a temporary "borrowing" of energy ΔE for a time Δt, enough to create a [[CarrierParticle|carrier particle]] of mass m = ΔE/c².
- This is how forces are transmitted in [[ParticlePhysics|particle physics]]: e.g. two charges repel by exchanging virtual [[PhotonQuantum|photons]]; nucleons interact by exchanging virtual pions ([[Meson|mesons]]).
- The maximum distance a virtual carrier can travel sets the force range: d ≈ cΔt — a massless carrier can reach infinitely far, a heavy one only a short distance.
- Virtual particles underpin the Yukawa estimate of the pion mass and the explanation for why the weak force is short-ranged.

## Equations
- ΔE·Δt ≥ h/(4π)
- d ≈ cΔt; ΔE = mc²
