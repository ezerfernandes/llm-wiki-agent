# Lewis Base

A **Lewis base** is an **electron-pair donor** — a species that donates a lone pair to a [[LewisAcid|Lewis acid]], forming a [[CoordinateCovalentBond|coordinate covalent bond]] and an acid–base adduct. This is the base half of the [[LewisAcidsAndBases|Lewis acid–base model]].

A Lewis base must have an available **lone pair** (visible in its [[LewisStructure|Lewis structure]]). Examples:

- **H₂O** and **NH₃** donating to H⁺ → H₃O⁺ and NH₄⁺ (the [[BronstedLowryAcidsAndBases|Brønsted-Lowry]] proton-acceptor case).
- **F⁻** donating to BF₃ → [BF₄]⁻.
- **[[Ligand|Ligands]]** — neutral molecules (H₂O, NH₃) or ions (CN⁻, OH⁻, S₂O₃²⁻) that donate lone pairs to a central metal cation to build a [[ComplexIon|complex ion]].
- **O²⁻** donating to SO₂ → [SO₃]²⁻.

Every Lewis base is a potential ligand in coordination chemistry; ligands are simply Lewis bases bound to a metal center.
