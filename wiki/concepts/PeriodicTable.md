# Periodic Table

The **periodic table** organizes all 118 known [[ChemicalElement|elements]] by increasing [[AtomicNumberAndMassNumber|atomic number]] so that elements with similar properties fall into the same vertical column.

## History

Dmitri [[DmitriMendeleev|Mendeleev]] (Russia, 1869) and Lothar Meyer (Germany, 1870) independently noticed periodic relationships when elements were arranged by increasing atomic mass. Mendeleev went further, **predicting undiscovered elements**; the later discovery of gallium (1875) and germanium (1886) confirmed his predictions.

## The Periodic Law

The modern **periodic law**: *the properties of the elements are periodic functions of their atomic numbers.* (The organizing variable is atomic number Z, not atomic mass.)

## Structure

- **7 horizontal rows** = **periods** (or series).
- **18 vertical columns** = **groups** (or families), numbered 1–18 per IUPAC ([[IUPAC]]) recommendation.
- Each cell shows atomic number, symbol, average [[AtomicMass|atomic mass]], and often the name.

## Element Classes (by property)

- **Metals** — shiny, malleable, ductile, good conductors of heat and electricity.
- **Nonmetals** — dull, poor conductors.
- **Metalloids** — intermediate conductivity; both metallic and nonmetallic traits.

## Element Categories (by position)

- **Main-group / [[RepresentativeElements|representative]] elements** — columns 1, 2, and 13–18 (plus group 12, whose last electron is an *s* electron).
- **Transition metals** — columns 3–12 (variable [[ChemicalIon|ion]] charges).
- **Inner transition metals** — the two bottom rows: **lanthanides** (Z 57–71) and **actinides** (Z 89–103).

## Named Groups

- Group 1 — **[[AlkaliMetals|alkali metals]]**
- Group 2 — **[[AlkalineEarthMetals|alkaline earth metals]]**
- Group 15 — **pnictogens** ([[NitrogenGroupChemistry|N, P, …]])
- Group 16 — **chalcogens** / oxygen family ([[OxygenChemistry|O]], [[SulfurChemistry|S]], …)
- Group 17 — **[[HalogensChemistry|halogens]]** (F, Cl, Br, I)
- Group 18 — **[[NobleGasesChemistry|noble (inert) gases]]** — once thought unreactive

**Hydrogen** is a unique nonmetal with traits of both group 1 and group 17.

Periodic position predicts the charges of monatomic [[ChemicalIon|ions]] for main-group elements, underpinning [[IonicCompound|ionic-compound]] formulas. Radioactive-only elements (e.g., Tc-43, Pm-61, and most with Z ≥ 84) show their most-stable mass number in square brackets.

## Electronic Basis of Periodicity (Ch 6)

The table's structure follows directly from [[ElectronConfiguration|electron configurations]]: the [[AufbauPrinciple|Aufbau]] filling order partitions the table into **blocks** — the **s block** (groups 1–2), **p block** (groups 13–18), **d block** (transition metals, groups 3–12), and **f block** (inner transition metals, the lanthanides and actinides). Elements in the same group share the same number of **valence electrons**, which is why their chemistry recurs periodically. The trends in [[PeriodicTrends|atomic radius]], [[IonizationEnergy|ionization energy]], and [[ElectronAffinity|electron affinity]] are explained by effective nuclear charge (Z_eff) acting on those valence electrons.

College Physics 2e (Ch.30) reaches the same conclusion from quantum mechanics: the [[PauliExclusionPrinciple|Pauli exclusion principle]] caps subshells at 2(2l+1) and shells at 2n², and orbital penetration sets the filling order — so the once-mysterious periodicity is a direct consequence of quantization plus Pauli.
