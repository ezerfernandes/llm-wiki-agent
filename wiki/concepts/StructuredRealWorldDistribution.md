# Structured Real-World Distribution

A data-generating model introduced as Definition 2.1 in [[2605.12966-agentic-ai-to-agi]] (Liao et al., ICML 2026). It refines the [[NoFreeLunchTheorem]]'s "no prior" assumption with a structural prior aligned with how real-world tasks actually distribute over input space.

## Definition

Let input space $\mathcal{X} \subseteq \mathbb{R}^D$ and output space $\mathcal{Y} \subseteq \mathbb{R}$. The **Structured Real-World Distribution** $\mathcal{D}_{\text{real}}$ on $\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}$ is generated by a latent task variable $z\in\{1,\dots,K\}$ with $\alpha_k = P(z=k)$, and is characterized by:

1. **Union of Manifolds.** The marginal $P(x)$ is supported on a union of $K$ compact Riemannian manifolds:
$$\text{supp}(P(x)) \subseteq \bigcup_{k=1}^K \mathcal{M}_k, \qquad \mathcal{M}_k \subset \mathbb{R}^D, \qquad \dim \mathcal{M}_k = d_k \ll D.$$
2. **Local Functional Consistency.** For each task $k$ there is a distinct labeling function $f_k: \mathcal{M}_k\to\mathcal{Y}$ such that $y = f_k(\text{Proj}_{\mathcal{M}_k}(x)) + \xi$ with noise $\xi$.
3. **Task Divergence.** Optima are heterogeneous: $\inf_\theta \mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mathcal{M}_k}[\ell(h_\theta(x), f_k(x))] \neq \inf_\theta \mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mathcal{M}_j}[\ell(h_\theta(x), f_j(x))]$ for any $j\neq k$.

The joint density is therefore *piecewise-smooth on a manifold union*:
$$\mathcal{D}_{\text{real}}(x,y) = \sum_{k=1}^K \alpha_k \cdot \mathbb{I}_{\mathcal{M}_k}(x) \cdot P(y \mid f_k(x)).$$

## Why this concept matters

Without a structural prior the [[CurseOfDimensionality]] bites hard: Proposition 2.2 (Stone 1982) gives a minimax lower bound $\inf_{\hat f_N}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}_L}\mathbb{E}[\int|\hat f_N(x)-f(x)|\,dP(x)] \geq C\cdot N^{-1/(2+D)}$ on Lipschitz functions over a compact $D$-cube — exponential sample complexity.

But under the Structured Real-World Distribution, learning each $f_k$ requires only $N^{-1/d_k}$ samples on its low-dimensional manifold. This is what powers the exponential gap between [[RoutingBasedAgenticAI]] ($\mathcal{O}(K\cdot N^{-1/d_{\max}})$) and a monolithic learner ($\mathcal{O}(N^{-1/D})$).

## Empirical realism (Remark B.7)

Pure orthogonality of task subspaces (Assumption 3.4) is an idealization. The paper formalizes partial overlap with parameter $\gamma \in [0,1]$ (cosine fraction of shared subspace): each $S_k = S_{\text{shared}} \oplus S_k^{\text{res}}$ with $\dim S_{\text{shared}} = \gamma\cdot d_{\max}$. The Average Trap penalty rescales to $\epsilon(\gamma) \approx (1-\gamma)\cdot\epsilon_{\text{full}}$, the sample-complexity exponent shrinks linearly with $\gamma$ but stays strictly negative for any $\gamma < 1$ — i.e. the exponential routing advantage degrades gracefully and survives partial overlap.
