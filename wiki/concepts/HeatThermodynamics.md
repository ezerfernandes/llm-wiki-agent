# Heat (q)

**Heat (q)** is the transfer of [[ThermalEnergy|thermal energy]] between two bodies at different temperatures. Heat is a process/transfer quantity, not a property stored in a body, and it is *not* a state function — its value depends on the path of the process.

## Direction and Sign Convention
Heat flows spontaneously from the higher-temperature body to the lower-temperature body until thermal equilibrium is reached. The sign of q is taken from the perspective of the substance or system of interest:
- **Positive q**: the substance/system *gains* thermal energy (temperature rises; heat flows in).
- **Negative q**: the substance/system *loses* thermal energy (temperature falls; heat flows out).

## Quantifying Heat
For a substance undergoing no reaction or phase change, the heat exchanged is

**q = c · m · ΔT = c · m · (T_final − T_initial)**

where c is the [[SpecificHeatCapacity|specific heat]], m the mass, and ΔT the temperature change.

## Units
The SI unit is the **joule (J)**: 1 J = 1 kg·m²/s² = 1 N·m. The **calorie (cal)** is the energy to raise 1 g of water by 1 °C; **1 cal = 4.184 J**. The food **Calorie (Cal)** equals one kilocalorie.

## Physics View (College Physics 2e, Ch.14)
Physics frames heat as **energy in transit** — it exists only during transfer, never as a stored property; what a body stores is **internal energy**. Heat (Q) and **work** transfer energy interchangeably (Joule's paddle-wheel experiment established the **mechanical equivalent of heat**), and the final state depends only on the total energy change, not the path — the basis of [[ConservationOfEnergy]]. The physics-convention calorie gives **1 kcal = 4186 J** (vs. the thermochemical 4184 J/kcal); both are accepted definitions, not a contradiction. Heat moves by [[HeatConduction]], [[Convection]], and [[ThermalRadiation]] (see [[HeatTransfer]]).
