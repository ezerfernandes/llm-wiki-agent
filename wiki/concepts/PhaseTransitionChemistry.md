# Phase Transitions (Chemistry)

> Chemistry-domain page (OpenStax *Chemistry 2e* Ch 10). The physics treatment (latent heat) is on [[PhaseChange]].

A **phase transition** is a change between the solid, liquid, and gas [[StatesOfMatter|states of matter]]. Each transition has a characteristic temperature and an associated [[EnthalpyChemistry|enthalpy]] change, set by the [[IntermolecularForces|intermolecular forces]] that must be overcome (or that re-form).

## Vaporization / Condensation

The **enthalpy of vaporization** (ΔH_vap) is the heat to convert one mole of liquid to gas; vaporization is endothermic and condensation exothermic:

- H₂O(l) → H₂O(g)  ΔH_vap = +44.01 kJ/mol
- H₂O(g) → H₂O(l)  ΔH_con = −44.01 kJ/mol

In a closed container, vaporization and condensation reach a **dynamic equilibrium** (equal opposing rates), establishing the [[VaporPressureChemistry|vapor pressure]].

## Melting / Freezing

The **melting (freezing) point** is "the temperature at which the solid and liquid phases of a given substance are in equilibrium." The **enthalpy of fusion** (ΔH_fus) is the heat to melt one mole; fusion is endothermic, freezing exothermic:

- H₂O(s) → H₂O(l)  ΔH_fus = +6.01 kJ/mol
- H₂O(l) → H₂O(s)  ΔH_frz = −6.01 kJ/mol

## Sublimation / Deposition

**Sublimation** is solid → gas directly (dry ice); **deposition** is gas → solid (frost). The **enthalpy of sublimation**:

- CO₂(s) → CO₂(g)  ΔH_sub = +26.1 kJ/mol

## Enthalpy Relationships

Forming a gas requires fully overcoming attractions, while forming a liquid only partly does, so **ΔH_fus < ΔH_vap**. Because sublimation = melting then vaporizing:

$$\Delta H_{sub} = \Delta H_{fus} + \Delta H_{vap}$$

## Heating and Cooling Curves

Within a single phase, heat changes temperature: **q = m·c·ΔT**. During a transition, temperature is constant and heat drives the phase change: **q = n·ΔH**. A full heating curve alternates sloped (heating) and flat (transition) segments. Example: warming 135 g of ice at −15 °C to steam at 120 °C requires q_total = (mcΔT)_ice + nΔH_fus + (mcΔT)_water + nΔH_vap + (mcΔT)_steam ≈ 416 kJ.
