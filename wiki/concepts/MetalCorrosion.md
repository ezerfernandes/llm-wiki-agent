# Corrosion (of Metals)

> Disambiguation: this is the *chemistry* concept — electrochemical degradation of metals. Named `MetalCorrosion` to avoid collision with any generic/physics "corrosion" usage.

**Corrosion** is usually defined as the **degradation of metals by a naturally occurring electrochemical process**. Familiar examples include rust on iron, tarnish on silver, and the blue-green patina on copper. It is a spontaneous [[GalvanicCell|galvanic]] [[OxidationReduction|redox]] process in which the metal acts as an anode.

## Rusting of Iron
Iron exposed to O₂ and water sets up tiny galvanic cells:
- **Anode:** Fe(s) → Fe²⁺(aq) + 2e⁻ (E° = −0.44 V)
- **Cathode:** O₂(g) + 4H⁺(aq) + 4e⁻ → 2H₂O(l) (E° = +1.23 V)
- **Overall:** 2Fe(s) + O₂(g) + 4H⁺(aq) → 2Fe²⁺(aq) + 2H₂O(l) (E°cell = +1.67 V)
- Further oxidation in humid air: 4Fe²⁺(aq) + O₂(g) + (4+2x)H₂O(l) → 2Fe₂O₃·xH₂O(s) + 8H⁺(aq)

Rust (hydrated iron(III) oxide) **flakes off**, exposing fresh metal, so it offers no protection.

## Passivation (Self-Protecting Oxides)
Some metals form an adherent oxide/carbonate layer that **stops further attack** — **passivation**. Copper's blue-green **patina** (the Statue of Liberty) forms in steps:
- 2Cu + ½O₂ → Cu₂O (red); Cu₂O + ½O₂ → 2CuO (black)
- then carbonate/sulfate species: Cu₂CO₃(OH)₂ (green), Cu₃(CO₃)₂(OH)₂ (blue), Cu₄SO₄(OH)₆ (green)

## Prevention
- **Paint:** blocks water/oxygen.
- **Alloying:** stainless steel's chromium forms a passivating oxide.
- **Galvanization:** zinc coating both blocks air and gives [[CathodicProtection|cathodic protection]] if breached.
- **[[CathodicProtection|Cathodic protection]]:** a sacrificial anode (Zn, Mg) oxidizes in place of the protected metal.
