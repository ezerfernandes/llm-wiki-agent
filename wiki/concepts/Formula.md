# Formula (Solving for a Specific Variable)

A **formula** is an [[Equation|equation]] relating two or more variables (and constants) that recurs across math, science, and everyday applications — e.g. the distance formula `d = rt`, the area formula `A = ½bh`, or the simple-interest formula `I = Prt`. OpenStax [[Prealgebra]] 2e (Chapter 9, [[prealgebra-2e-ch09-math-models-and-geometry]], §9.7) covers **solving a formula for a specific variable**.

## What "solve for a variable" means
> To **solve a formula for a specific variable** is to get that variable by itself, with a coefficient of 1, on one side of the equation and every other variable and constant on the other side.

Because the result is again a formula made only of variables, this is also called solving a **literal equation**. The mechanics are exactly the [[SolvingLinearEquations|general solving strategy]]: apply the [[PropertiesOfEquality|Properties of Equality]] to both sides, treating the target variable as the unknown and every other letter as if it were a known number. Clear fractions by multiplying both sides by the denominator first.

## Worked patterns
- **Distance** `d = rt` ⇒ r = d/t,  t = d/r
- **Triangle area** `A = ½bh` ⇒ h = 2A/b,  b = 2A/h (clear the ½ by multiplying by 2)
- **Simple interest** `I = Prt` ⇒ P = I/(rt),  t = I/(Pr)
- **Two-variable linear** `3x + 2y = 18` ⇒ y = (18 − 3x)/2; `6x + 5y = 13` ⇒ y = (13 − 6x)/5

The chapter presents numeric and general (variable-only) solutions in parallel, so the same isolation steps work whether the other quantities are numbers or letters.

## Why it matters
Rearranging a formula lets you reuse it for whatever quantity is unknown — finding a missing dimension from an [[PerimeterAndArea|area or perimeter]], a missing radius from a [[Circle|circumference]], or a [[VolumeAndSurfaceArea|solid's]] dimension — rather than memorizing a separate formula per unknown. Solving for `y` also converts a two-variable linear equation into a form ready to graph.
