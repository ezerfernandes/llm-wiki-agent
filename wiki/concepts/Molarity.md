# Molarity

**Molarity (M)** is the most common way to express the concentration of a solution: the number of moles of solute per liter of solution.

$$M = \frac{\text{mol solute}}{\text{L solution}}$$

A solution is a homogeneous mixture; its **solvent** is the component present in much greater amount and its **solute** is the dissolved component present in smaller amount (when the solvent is water, it is an *aqueous* solution). Molarity quantifies how much solute occupies a given volume of solution — note the denominator is the volume of the *whole solution*, not of the solvent alone.

## Rearrangements

Because M, moles, and volume are linked, any one can be found from the other two:
- **Moles of solute:** mol = M × L. (e.g., 0.375 M × 0.010 L = 0.004 mol)
- **Volume:** L = mol ÷ M.
- **Concentration from solute mass:** convert the mass to moles with [[MolarMass|molar mass]] first. e.g., 25.2 g acetic acid (60.052 g/mol) in 0.500 L → (25.2/60.052)/0.500 = 0.839 M.
- **Mass of solute in a volume:** mol = M × L, then × molar mass. e.g., 0.250 L of 5.30 M NaCl → 1.325 mol → ×58.44 = 77.4 g.

Retain extra guard digits in intermediate steps to avoid rounding errors.
