# Triangle (Angles, Similarity, and Properties)

A **triangle** is a three-sided polygon with three angles, named by its vertices (e.g. ΔABC). Each side is labeled with the lowercase letter of the **opposite** vertex (side `a` is opposite vertex `A`). OpenStax [[Prealgebra]] 2e (Chapter 9, [[prealgebra-2e-ch09-math-models-and-geometry]], §9.3–9.4) develops triangle angle relationships, similarity, and measures, applying the [[ProblemSolvingStrategy|seven-step strategy]] throughout.

> **Angle context.** This page covers the *plane-geometry* angle. The separate [[Angle]] page is the inner-product (vector) angle from linear algebra — a different domain.

## Angle relationships (geometry)
An **angle** is formed by two rays sharing a common endpoint (the **vertex**) and is measured in degrees (°), written m∠A.
- **Complementary angles** — two angles whose measures sum to **90°**: m∠A + m∠B = 90°.
- **Supplementary angles** — two angles whose measures sum to **180°**: m∠A + m∠B = 180°.

(Mnemonic: *c*omplementary/*s*upplementary mirror the alphabetical/numerical order of 90 before 180.)

## Triangle angle-sum property
> For **any** triangle, the measures of the three interior angles sum to 180°:
> **m∠A + m∠B + m∠C = 180°**

This holds for every triangle, including right triangles, and lets you find a third angle from the other two — or set up an equation when angles are described in relation to one another ("one angle is 30° more than another").

## Right triangles
A **right triangle** contains one **90°** angle (marked with a small square). The side opposite the right angle is the **hypotenuse** (always the longest side); the two sides forming the right angle are the **legs**. Right triangles are governed by the [[PythagoreanTheorem|Pythagorean Theorem]].

## Similar triangles
Two figures are **similar** when they have the same shape but possibly different sizes. For similar triangles ΔABC ~ ΔXYZ:
- **corresponding angles are equal**, and
- **corresponding sides are in the same ratio**:
  > AB/XY = BC/YZ = AC/XZ

An unknown side length is therefore found by setting up and solving a [[Proportion|proportion]] from the matching ratios.

## Special triangles and measures
- **Isosceles triangle** — two sides equal.
- **Equilateral triangle** — all three sides equal.
- **Perimeter**: P = a + b + c (sum of the three sides).
- **Area**: A = ½bh, where `b` is the base and `h` is the perpendicular **height** to that base. (See [[PerimeterAndArea]].)

## Worked-example types
Finding complements/supplements; finding a missing angle by the angle-sum property; solving for angles described relationally; finding unknown sides via similar-triangle proportions; computing perimeter/area; real-world right-triangle problems (ladders, bracing) via the [[PythagoreanTheorem|Pythagorean Theorem]].
