## Definition
A **physical quantity** is a measurable property of a physical system, defined either by specifying how it is measured or by stating how it is calculated from other measurements. Every physical quantity is expressed as a numerical value together with a [[SIUnits|unit]].

## Key Points
- **Fundamental quantities** are measured in fundamental (base) units — length (meter), mass (kilogram), time (second), electric current (ampere) in the four bases emphasized for mechanics and electricity.
- **Derived quantities** are combinations of fundamental ones, expressed in **derived units** (e.g., speed = distance/time, in m/s).
- A value without its unit is meaningless or dangerous (the unit is part of the quantity).
- The scale of a quantity is often described by its [[OrderOfMagnitude]] (its nearest power of 10).
- Comparing or combining quantities requires consistent units, achieved via [[UnitConversion]] and [[DimensionalAnalysis]].

## Equations
- Example derived quantity: speed v = distance / time.
