## Definition
**Poiseuille's law** gives the rate of laminar, incompressible flow through a uniform horizontal tube: Q = (P₂ − P₁)πr⁴/(8ηl). Equivalently, flow equals the pressure difference divided by a flow resistance R = 8ηl/(πr⁴).

## Key Points
- Flow rate is proportional to the **fourth power of the radius** — the dominant factor. Doubling the radius increases flow 16-fold; halving it cuts flow to 1/16.
- Higher [[Viscosity]] η or longer tube l increases resistance and reduces flow.
- A ~16% reduction in a blood-vessel radius roughly halves flow, so the heart must raise pressure to compensate — central to understanding atherosclerosis.
- Holds only for laminar flow (low [[ReynoldsNumber]]); turbulence invalidates it.
- The Q = ΔP/R form is analogous to Ohm's law for current.

## Equations
- Resistance: R = 8ηl/(πr⁴)
- Flow vs. resistance: Q = (P₂ − P₁)/R
- Poiseuille's law: Q = (P₂ − P₁)πr⁴/(8ηl)
