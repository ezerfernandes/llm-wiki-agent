# Selective Precipitation

**Selective precipitation** separates two or more ions that share a common counter-ion by exploiting their different [[SolubilityProduct|Ksp]] values. By gradually controlling the counter-ion concentration, the ions are precipitated one at a time; the **least soluble compound precipitates first** (it reaches Q = Ksp at the lowest counter-ion concentration).

## Worked example (silver halides)

A solution is 0.10 M in Cl⁻ and 0.00010 M in Br⁻, and Ag⁺ is added slowly. Each halide begins to precipitate once [Ag⁺] reaches Ksp/[halide]:

$$\mathrm{AgBr:}\ [\mathrm{Ag^+}] = \frac{5.0 \times 10^{-13}}{0.00010} = 5.0 \times 10^{-9}\ \mathrm{M}$$
$$\mathrm{AgCl:}\ [\mathrm{Ag^+}] = \frac{1.6 \times 10^{-10}}{0.10} = 1.6 \times 10^{-9}\ \mathrm{M}$$

AgCl requires the *lower* [Ag⁺] under these particular concentrations, so **AgCl precipitates first**. (Which salt precipitates first depends not only on Ksp but on the actual ion concentrations — the comparison is between the threshold [Ag⁺] each needs.) This principle underlies qualitative-analysis schemes that separate metal cations into groups.
