## Definition
Rotational kinetic energy is the energy a body has because it is spinning, KE_rot = ½ I ω². It is the rotational analog of translational [[KineticEnergy]] (½mv²), with [[MomentOfInertia]] I replacing mass and [[AngularVelocity]] ω replacing speed. Like all energy, it is a scalar.

## Key Points
- Rotational work is done when a [[Torque]] acts through an angular displacement: W = τθ.
- The rotational work-energy theorem mirrors the translational one: net work equals the change in rotational kinetic energy.
- For a rolling object, total kinetic energy splits between translational (½mv²) and rotational (½Iω²) parts, with the rolling-without-slipping constraint ω = v/R linking them.
- Energy conservation on an incline (mgh = ½mv² + ½Iω²) shows that *how mass is distributed* (the I term), not total mass or radius, determines which object rolls fastest.
- Spinning components (flywheels, helicopter rotors) can store large amounts of rotational KE — often exceeding their translational KE.

## Equations
- W_net = τ θ
- KE_rot = ½ I ω²
- Work-energy theorem: W_net = ½Iω² − ½Iω₀²
- Rolling without slipping: ω = v / R
- Rolling energy conservation: m g h = ½ m v² + ½ I ω²
