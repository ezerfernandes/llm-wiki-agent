## Definition
The law of conservation of angular momentum states that when the net external [[Torque]] on a system is zero, its total [[AngularMomentum]] L remains constant: Iω = I′ω′. It follows directly from net τ = ΔL/Δt — no net torque means no change in L.

## Key Points
- It is the rotational counterpart of conservation of [[LinearMomentum]].
- Changing the [[MomentOfInertia]] forces a compensating change in [[AngularVelocity]]: reducing I increases ω and vice versa.
- Signature example — the spinning ice skater: pulling arms in lowers I, so ω rises sharply while L stays fixed. Kinetic energy *increases* because the skater does internal muscular work; no external torque is needed to speed up.
- Explains why tornadoes intensify as their radius shrinks, why collapsing dust/gas clouds spin up into stars and planets, and why Earth keeps rotating.
- Holds even in some collisions about a pivot — see [[CollisionsOfExtendedBodies]].

## Equations
- If net τ = 0:  ΔL/Δt = 0  ⇒  L = constant
- I ω = I′ ω′
- KE_rot = ½ I ω²  (changes when internal work is done, even though L is fixed)
