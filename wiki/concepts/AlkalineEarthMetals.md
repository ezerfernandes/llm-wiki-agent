# Alkaline Earth Metals (Group 2)

The **alkaline earth metals** — beryllium, magnesium, calcium, strontium, barium, and radium — are the group 2 [[RepresentativeElements|representative metals]]. They are named because the oxides of the heavier members react with water to give alkaline solutions. Smaller and with higher first ionization energies than the [[AlkaliMetals|alkali metals]] of the same period (see [[PeriodicTrends]]), they are still very reactive, losing **both** valence electrons to form **2+ ions**. Be and Mg (highest ionization energies) form some compounds with covalent character. Flame colors: Ca and Sr red shades, Ba green.

## Reactions
Mg, Ca, Sr, and Ba react with water and air (Ba most vigorous at room temperature), giving H₂ + metal hydroxide — so the heavier members are better reducing agents than hydrogen. They react with acids and nonmetals to form ionic compounds; many salts are water-insoluble due to high lattice energies.

Hot magnesium is a powerful reducing agent — it even reduces CO₂:

> 2Mg(s) + CO₂(g) → 2MgO(s) + C(s)

so a CO₂ extinguisher cannot put out a magnesium fire. Burning Mg emits brilliant white light (flares, fireworks). Magnesium is the lightest widely used structural metal and is passivated by a magnesium oxycarbonate film.

## Occurrence and Preparation
Occur as silicates, carbonates, and sulfates; Be as beryl Be₃Al₂Si₆O₁₈ (emerald, aquamarine); Mg from seawater. Prepared by [[ElectrolysisChemistry|electrolysis]], or for Mg by chemical reduction (**Pidgeon process**) — see [[Metallurgy]].
