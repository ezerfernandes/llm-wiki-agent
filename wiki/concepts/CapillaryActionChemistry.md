# Capillary Action (Chemistry)

> Chemistry-domain page (OpenStax *Chemistry 2e* Ch 10). For the physics treatment, see [[CapillaryAction]].

"Capillary action—when a liquid flows within a porous material due to the attraction of the liquid molecules to the surface of the material and to other liquid molecules." It happens when **adhesive** forces (liquid–wall) plus **cohesive** forces (liquid–liquid) are strong enough to draw liquid upward against gravity. Examples: wine wicking up a paper towel, water in cloth and in plant xylem, and thin-layer chromatography.

## Capillary Rise Equation

$$h = \frac{2T\cos\theta}{r\rho g}$$

- *h* — height of liquid in the tube relative to the outside surface
- *T* — [[LiquidSurfaceTension|surface tension]] of the liquid
- *θ* — contact angle between liquid and tube (water in glass: θ = 0°, so cos θ = 1)
- *r* — tube radius (narrower tube → greater rise)
- *ρ* — liquid density
- *g* — 9.8 m/s²

**Worked example:** at 25 °C in a glass capillary of inner diameter 0.25 mm (r = 1.25 × 10⁻⁴ m), with T = 71.99 mN/m and ρ = 1000 kg/m³, water rises h ≈ 0.12 m = 12 cm. (A rise of 8.4 cm implies a tube diameter of 0.36 mm.)

Capillary blood collection relies on this: surface tension holds the droplet together, adhesion to glass pulls blood up, and rise stops when the liquid's weight balances the upward capillary force.
