# Surface Tension (Liquids)

> Chemistry-domain page (OpenStax *Chemistry 2e* Ch 10). For the physics treatment (γ = F/L, bubble pressure P = 4γ/r, lung surfactant), see [[SurfaceTension]].

"Surface tension is defined as the energy required to increase the surface area of a liquid, or the force required to increase the length of a liquid surface by a given amount." It arises because a molecule in the bulk is attracted equally in all directions, while a surface molecule has neighbors on roughly half its sides and feels a net inward pull. The liquid therefore contracts to minimize surface area — small drops become spheres (lowest surface-area-to-volume ratio).

| Substance | Formula | Surface Tension (mN/m) |
|-----------|---------|------------------------|
| Octane | C₈H₁₈ | 21.14 |
| Ethanol | C₂H₅OH | 21.97 |
| Ethylene glycol | CH₂(OH)CH₂(OH) | 47.99 |
| Water | H₂O | 71.99 |
| Mercury | Hg | 458.48 |

Water's high surface tension comes from strong [[HydrogenBonding|hydrogen bonding]], letting it support a carefully placed steel needle or a walking insect.
