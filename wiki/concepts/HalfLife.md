## Definition
The **half-life** (t₁/₂) of a radioactive nuclide is the time for half of the original nuclei in a sample to decay. Because [[RadioactiveDecay|decay]] is purely statistical, each individual nucleus has a 50% chance of surviving any one half-life, independent of how long it has already existed.

> Note: this is the **nuclear-physics** half-life. The chemistry-kinetics version (decay of a reactant concentration) is the separate page [[ReactionHalfLife]].

## Key Points
- **Decay constant (λ)** sets the decay rate; the number of nuclei decays exponentially.
- **Activity (R)** is the number of decays per unit time; it is proportional to the number of nuclei present and inversely proportional to t₁/₂.
- Units of activity: **becquerel (Bq)** = 1 decay/s (SI); **curie (Ci)** = 3.70 × 10¹⁰ Bq (≈ activity of 1 g of ²²⁶Ra).
- Half-lives span an enormous range (fractions of a second to billions of years), enabling [[RadiometricDating|radiometric dating]].

## Equations
- N = N₀e^(−λt)
- λ = ln(2)/t₁/₂ ≈ 0.693/t₁/₂
- R = ΔN/Δt = λN = 0.693N/t₁/₂
- R = R₀e^(−λt)
