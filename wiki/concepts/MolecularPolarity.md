# Molecular Polarity

**Molecular polarity** describes whether a whole molecule has a net separation of charge. A molecule with a nonzero net [[DipoleMoment|dipole moment]] is **polar** (a dipole); one whose bond dipoles cancel is **nonpolar**. (Named *MolecularPolarity* to avoid a generic `Polarity` page.)

## Criteria for a Polar Molecule

A molecule is polar only if **both** hold:

1. It contains at least one **[[BondPolarity|polar covalent bond]]**.
2. Its **[[MolecularGeometry|molecular geometry]]** is such that the vector sum of the individual bond [[DipoleMoment|dipole moments]] does **not** cancel.

Thus geometry, not just bond polarity, determines molecular polarity.

## Examples

- **Diatomics**: homonuclear (Br₂, N₂) are nonpolar; heteronuclear (CO, HF) are polar.
- **CO₂** (linear): the two C=O bond dipoles point opposite and cancel → **nonpolar** despite polar bonds.
- **H₂O** (bent, two lone pairs on O): the O–H dipoles do not cancel → **polar**.
- **CH₃Cl** (tetrahedral): dipoles sum toward Cl → **polar**.
- **Symmetric** molecules (BF₃, CH₄, PF₅, SF₆): identical bond dipoles in symmetric arrangements cancel → **nonpolar**.
- **Asymmetric** molecules (H₂S, NH₃): incomplete cancellation → **polar**.

## Consequences

Polar molecules align in an electric field (δ+ end toward the negative plate). [[LikeDissolvesLike|"Like dissolves like"]]: polar solvents dissolve polar solutes; nonpolar solvents dissolve nonpolar solutes — the basis of liquid miscibility and the [[Solvation|solvation]] of ions by polar water (ion-dipole attraction). Molecular polarity also dictates which [[IntermolecularForces|intermolecular forces]] act between molecules: only polar molecules experience [[DipoleDipoleForces|dipole-dipole attractions]], so for similar molar masses a polar molecule boils higher than a nonpolar one (CO vs N₂; HCl vs F₂).
