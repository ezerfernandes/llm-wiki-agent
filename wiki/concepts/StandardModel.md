## Definition
The **Standard Model** is the unified theoretical framework of [[ParticlePhysics|particle physics]] that combines **quantum chromodynamics (QCD)** — the theory of the strong force between [[Quark|quarks]] via color charge — with **[[ElectroweakForce|electroweak theory]]** — the unification of the electromagnetic and weak forces. It describes the known fundamental particles and how three of the four forces are transmitted.

> Note: this page is the *particle-physics* Standard Model, not a statistical/ML "model." Disambiguated as title "Standard Model (Particle Physics)."

## Key Points
- **Matter content**: two families of [[FundamentalParticle|fundamental]] fermions — six [[Lepton|leptons]] and six [[Quark|quarks]], each in three generations.
- **Force carriers** ([[CarrierParticle|gauge bosons]]): [[PhotonQuantum|photon]] (electromagnetic), W⁺/W⁻/Z⁰ (weak), and eight **gluons** (strong; six carry color/anticolor, two are colorless). Gravity (graviton) is *not* included.
- **QCD**: gluons mediate the strong force by exchanging color charge between quarks; observable [[Hadron|hadrons]] are always colorless, and quarks are confined.
- **[[ElectroweakForce|Electroweak unification]]**: above ~100 GeV the electromagnetic and weak forces become a single interaction; the **Higgs boson** (tentative LHC evidence ~125 GeV) is tied to the symmetry breaking that gives the W and Z their mass.
- Limits: it omits gravity and does not by itself unify the strong with the electroweak force — motivating [[GrandUnifiedTheory|GUTs]].

## Equations
- Predicted carrier masses: W ≈ 81 GeV/c², Z⁰ ≈ 90 GeV/c²
- Electroweak unification scale ≈ 100 GeV (↔ ~10⁻¹⁸ m)
