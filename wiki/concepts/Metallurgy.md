# Metallurgy: Occurrence and Preparation of the Representative Metals

**Metallurgy** here means the isolation of the [[RepresentativeElements|representative metals]] from their natural sources. These metals are too reactive to occur free, so they are recovered from minerals either by [[ElectrolysisChemistry|electrolysis]] (for ions too hard to reduce chemically) or by cheaper chemical reduction.

## Occurrence
- [[AlkaliMetals|Alkali metals]]: Li in silicate/phosphate minerals; Na, K in evaporite salt deposits and silicates.
- [[AlkalineEarthMetals|Alkaline earth metals]]: silicates, carbonates, sulfates; Be as beryl.
- Magnesium: seawater (~0.5% MgCl₂).
- Aluminum: clay and **bauxite** (impure aluminum oxide hydroxide).
- Tin: cassiterite, SnO₂. Lead and thallium: sulfides.

## Electrolysis (ions too difficult to reduce chemically: groups 1, 2, Al)
**Sodium — Downs cell:** electrolysis of molten NaCl at 600 °C (CaCl₂ lowers the 801 °C melting point):

> 2NaCl(l) → 2Na(l) + Cl₂(g)   (electrolysis, 600 °C)
> Cathode: 2Na⁺ + 2e⁻ → 2Na(l)    Anode: 2Cl⁻ → Cl₂(g) + 2e⁻

Aqueous electrolysis cannot make Na because H⁺ reduces first.

**Aluminum — Hall–Héroult cell** (Hall and Héroult, independently 1886): bauxite is dissolved in hot NaOH (Na[Al(OH)₄]), reprecipitated as Al(OH)₃, heated to Al₂O₃, then dissolved in molten **cryolite** (Na₃AlF₆) + CaF₂ and electrolyzed (cathode reduces Al³⁺; anode gives O₂/CO/CO₂).

**Magnesium (electrolytic):** from seawater — Mg(OH)₂ precipitated with Ca(OH)₂, dissolved in HCl, then molten MgCl₂ electrolyzed.

## Chemical Reduction (cheaper, preferred where possible)
**Magnesium — Pidgeon process** (>75% of world production, mostly China):

> Si(s) + 2MgO(s) → SiO₂(s) + 2Mg(g)   (Mg-vapor removal shifts the unfavorable equilibrium)

**Zinc:** roast (2ZnS + 3O₂ → 2ZnO + 2SO₂) or decompose ZnCO₃, then reduce with carbon (ZnO + C → Zn + CO) and purify by distillation (bp 907 °C).

**Tin:** roast and acid-treat the ore, then SnO₂(s) + 2C(s) → Sn(s) + 2CO(g) (>1000 °C).
