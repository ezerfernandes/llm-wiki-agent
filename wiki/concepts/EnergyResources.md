## Definition
**Energy resources** are the sources from which usable energy is drawn, split into **renewable** (effectively inexhaustible — hydroelectric, wind, solar, biomass) and **nonrenewable** (finite fossil fuels: oil, natural gas, coal). About 85% of current world energy comes from nonrenewable fossil fuels, which emit CO₂ when burned.

## Key Points
- Although energy is conserved ([[ConservationOfEnergy]]), most use degrades it into waste heat that can no longer do work — the core argument for conservation.
- **Energy conservation** is achieved by behavior change (lower thermostats) or by improving [[EnergyEfficiency]] (efficient vehicles, appliances).
- Higher GDP per capita generally correlates with higher energy use per capita.
- Snapshot data: oil ~35% of world energy; the U.S. (~4.25% of population) uses ~21% of global oil; Germany targets 65% renewable power by 2030; China leads in solar/hydro; India runs the largest solar-cooking program; diversified renewables speed grid recovery after disasters.
