# Atomic Orbital

An **atomic orbital** is a three-dimensional region around the [[AtomicNucleus|nucleus]] where an [[Electron|electron]] is likely to be found — specifically, the spatial distribution of electron probability density given by **|ψ|²**, the square of the wavefunction from the Schrödinger equation. It replaces the [[BohrModel|Bohr model]]'s definite orbits, consistent with the [[HeisenbergUncertaintyPrinciple|uncertainty principle]].

> Disambiguation: this is the *chemistry* orbital (electron probability cloud), distinct from [[OrbitalMechanics]] (satellites/gravity).

## Shapes
Determined by the angular momentum quantum number l (see [[QuantumNumbers]]):
- **s (l=0)** — spherical.
- **p (l=1)** — dumbbell-shaped; 3 orientations (px, py, pz).
- **d (l=2)** — more complex; 5 orientations.
- **f (l=3)** — more complex still; 7 orientations.

The number of orbitals in a subshell is **2l + 1**, and each orbital holds at most two electrons (opposite spins) by the [[PauliExclusionPrinciple|Pauli principle]]; College Physics 2e (Ch.30) frames the same picture as the wave-mechanical replacement for Bohr's orbits, where the [[DeBroglieWavelength|de Broglie]] standing-wave condition forces quantization.

## Nodes
A **radial node** is a distance from the nucleus where the probability density is zero (ψ = 0). The number of radial nodes is **n − l − 1** (e.g., 1s: 0, 2s: 1, 3s: 2). Penetration toward the nucleus decreases as l increases, which affects orbital energies in multi-electron atoms (s < p < d < f within a shell).

## Role in Bonding

Atomic orbitals are the building blocks of the two quantum bonding models. In [[ValenceBondTheory|valence bond theory]] half-filled orbitals **overlap** to form [[SigmaAndPiBonds|σ and π bonds]], and they combine into [[OrbitalHybridization|hybrid orbitals]] (sp, sp², sp³, sp³d, sp³d²) that match molecular geometry. In [[MolecularOrbitalTheory|MO theory]] they combine by LCAO into delocalized [[BondingAndAntibondingOrbitals|bonding and antibonding molecular orbitals]].
