# Chemical Ion

A **chemical ion** is an electrically charged atom or group of atoms that results when the number of [[Electron|electrons]] does not equal the number of [[Proton|protons]]. (Named *ChemicalIon* because the bare name `Ion` collides with unrelated wiki pages.)

$$\text{charge} = (\text{number of protons}) - (\text{number of electrons})$$

The [[AtomicNucleus|nucleus]] and element identity are unchanged when an ion forms — only electrons move.

## Cations and Anions

- **Cation** (positive): an atom that has **lost** electrons. Main-group metals lose electrons to reach the electron count of the preceding noble gas — Group 1 → 1+ (Na⁺), Group 2 → 2+ (Ca²⁺). Example: Ca (20 p, 20 e) → Ca²⁺ (20 p, 18 e, like argon).
- **Anion** (negative): an atom that has **gained** electrons. Nonmetals gain electrons to reach the next noble gas — Group 17 → 1−, Group 16 → 2−. Example: Br (35 p, 35 e) → Br⁻ (35 p, 36 e, like krypton).

## Predicting Charge from the Periodic Table

For main-group elements, cation charge tracks the group number; moving left from the noble gases, anion charge equals the number of groups moved left. [[PeriodicTable|Transition metals]] are exceptions — they show variable charges (Cu⁺/Cu²⁺, Fe²⁺/Fe³⁺), specified by Roman numerals in [[ChemicalNomenclature|nomenclature]].

## Monatomic vs Polyatomic Ions

- **Monatomic ions** form from a single atom (Na⁺, Cl⁻).
- **Polyatomic ions** are charged groups of bonded atoms acting as a unit: ammonium NH₄⁺, hydroxide OH⁻, nitrate NO₃⁻, sulfate SO₄²⁻, carbonate CO₃²⁻, phosphate PO₄³⁻, etc.

Ions of opposite charge combine in charge-balanced ratios to form electrically neutral [[IonicCompound|ionic compounds]] through [[IonicBonding|ionic bonding]]. Forming a cation strips valence electrons (transition metals lose outermost *s* before *d*: Fe → Fe²⁺ → Fe³⁺); forming an anion fills the *s*/*p* shell to the next noble gas (O²⁻ = [Ne], Se²⁻ = [Ar]3d¹⁰4s²4p⁶).
