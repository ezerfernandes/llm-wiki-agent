## Definition
Damped harmonic motion is oscillation in which non-conservative forces (friction, drag) continuously remove mechanical energy, usually dissipating it as heat. Unlike ideal [[SimpleHarmonicMotion]], the amplitude decays over time.

## Key Points
- The work done by damping forces equals the (negative) change in total mechanical energy (see [[NonconservativeForce]]).
- **Underdamped:** light damping; the system overshoots and crosses equilibrium repeatedly with shrinking amplitude.
- **Critically damped:** returns to equilibrium as fast as possible without oscillating — the optimum for many instruments.
- **Overdamped:** heavy damping; returns slowly without oscillating, slower than critical.
- Damping force models vary: constant Coulomb friction (f = μ_k mg) or velocity-proportional viscous drag.

## Equations
- Work-energy (non-conservative): W_nc = Δ(KE + PE)
- Frictional damping force: f = μ_k m g
- Rest offset when motion ceases: x = μ_k m g / k
- Distance traveled before stopping: d = (k / 2 μ_k m g) [ X² - (μ_k m g / k)² ]
