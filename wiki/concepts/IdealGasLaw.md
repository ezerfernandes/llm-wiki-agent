## Definition
The **ideal gas law** is the equation of state relating a gas's pressure, volume, temperature, and
amount. An **ideal gas** is a model whose particles have negligible volume and no intermolecular
forces except during elastic collisions; real gases approach this under most everyday conditions.

## Key Points
- It unifies the experimental gas laws: **Boyle's law** (PV constant at fixed T) and **Charles'
  law** (V ∝ T at fixed P).
- Two equivalent forms: a **molecular** form in terms of particle count N and the Boltzmann
  constant k, and a **molar** form in terms of moles n and the universal gas constant R.
- Pressure and temperature must be **absolute** (Pa and K — see [[TemperatureScale]]).
- Because molecules are far apart relative to their size, behavior depends on particle density and
  temperature, not on the gas's chemical identity (~2.68×10²⁵ molecules/m³ at 0 °C and 1 atm for
  any gas).
- [[AvogadrosNumber]] converts between the molecular and molar forms (N = n·Nₐ).
- Energy link: PV has units of work while NkT is proportional to total translational kinetic
  energy, tying the law to [[ConservationOfEnergy]].

## Equations
- Molecular form: PV = NkT  (k = 1.38×10⁻²³ J/K)
- Molar form: PV = nRT  (R = 8.31 J/(mol·K) = 0.0821 L·atm/(mol·K))
- Constant V, N: P_f/P₀ = T_f/T₀
- N = PV/(kT);  n = PV/(RT)
