# Vapor Pressure and Boiling Point (Chemistry)

> Chemistry-domain page (OpenStax *Chemistry 2e* Ch 10). For the physics treatment (saturation vapor pressure, humidity), see [[VaporPressure]].

The **vapor pressure** (equilibrium vapor pressure) is the pressure of the vapor in [[PhaseTransitionChemistry|dynamic equilibrium]] with its liquid in a closed container at a given temperature. It depends only on the substance and the temperature — **not** on surface area or container size (those affect only the time to reach equilibrium).

## What Sets Vapor Pressure

- **[[IntermolecularForces|IMFs]]:** strong attractions impede escape and favor recapture → **low** vapor pressure; weak attractions → **high** vapor pressure. (Diethyl ether > ethanol > water > ethylene glycol in vapor pressure, inversely with IMF strength.)
- **Temperature:** higher T gives more molecules enough kinetic energy to escape, so vapor pressure rises (the basis of the [[ClausiusClapeyronEquation|Clausius-Clapeyron]] relation).
- Among alcohols at 20 °C, vapor pressure falls with size: methanol 11.9 > ethanol 5.95 > propanol 2.67 > butanol 0.56 kPa.

## Boiling Point

The **boiling point** is "the temperature at which its equilibrium vapor pressure is equal to the pressure exerted on the liquid by its gaseous surroundings." The **normal boiling point** is the boiling point at 1 atm (101.3 kPa). Lower ambient pressure lowers the boiling point — water boils at ~90 °C at Leadville, CO (~68 kPa).
