## Definition
The **Reynolds number (N_R)** is a dimensionless quantity that predicts whether fluid flow is [[LaminarAndTurbulentFlow|laminar or turbulent]]. It combines fluid density, speed, a characteristic length, and [[Viscosity]].

## Key Points
- For flow in a uniform tube: N_R = 2ρvr/η.
- Classification (tube flow): N_R < 2000 laminar; 2000–3000 unstable/transitional; > 3000 turbulent.
- For an object moving through a fluid, N_R′ = ρvL/η (L = characteristic length, e.g. diameter), with its own thresholds (laminar below ~1, fully turbulent above ~10⁶).
- High density or speed raises N_R (toward turbulence); high viscosity lowers it (toward laminar).
- Used to verify that medical infusion flow is laminar and to explain turbulence in the aorta, around thrown balls, and behind moving objects (turbulent wakes).

## Equations
- Tube flow: N_R = 2ρvr/η
- Object in fluid: N_R′ = ρvL/η
