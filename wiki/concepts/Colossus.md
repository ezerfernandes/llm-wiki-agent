# Colossus

The **world's first programmable electronic digital computer**, built in 1943 by [[TommyFlowers|Tommy Flowers]] at the General Post Office Research Station, Dollis Hill, and deployed at [[BletchleyPark]] to break the German **Lorenz cipher** (the higher-grade machine cipher distinct from Enigma).

## Architecture

- ~1,500 [[VacuumTube|vacuum tubes]] (later Mark 2 variants ~2,400)
- **Binary** internal representation
- Programmed via plugboards and switches — **not** a [[StoredProgram|stored-program]] machine
- Operated by personnel from the **[[WomensRoyalNavalService|Women's Royal Naval Service (WRNS)]]** — *"Wrens"* in period parlance

## Why most histories omit Colossus

Per [[dis-5-1-history|*Dive into Systems* Ch 5.1]], Colossus predates [[ENIAC]] (1945) by two years as a fully electronic digital computer — but **its existence remained classified until the 1970s**, by which time the public narrative crediting ENIAC as the *first* electronic computer had solidified. Ch 5.1 names Colossus to correct this distortion and reinforce its theme that historical credit in computing has been **systematically misallocated**.

Operationally, Colossus was the technological backbone of Allied SIGINT against the Lorenz traffic; its existence is widely credited with shortening WWII.
