# BF16 — Brain Float 16

A 16-bit floating-point format designed by [[google|Google]] to **match FP32's range while using half the bits**, at the cost of reduced precision. Per [[ai-engineering-ch07-finetuning|*AI Engineering* Ch 7]], BF16 was *"designed by Google to optimize AI performance on TPUs"* — Google promoted it as *"the secret to high performance on Cloud TPUs"*.

## Bit allocation

- **Sign**: 1 bit.
- **Range** (exponent): **8 bits** (same as [[FP32]]).
- **Precision** (mantissa): **7 bits** (fewer than [[FP16]]'s 10 bits).

Total: 16 bits — same as FP16 but with **more range, less precision**.

## BF16 vs FP16 — the headline trade

| Property | [[FP16]] | BF16 |
|---|---|---|
| Bits | 16 | 16 |
| Range | ±65,504 (~10⁴) | ±3.4 × 10³⁸ (same as FP32) |
| Precision | ~3.5 decimal digits | ~2 decimal digits |
| Behavior of `1234.56789` | → 1235.0 (0.035% error) | → 1232.0 (0.208% error) |
| Behavior of out-of-range | INF | Stays representable |

**BF16 trades precision for range.** This makes BF16 more robust for training (no INF blowups from rare large activations) but less precise per-value than FP16.

## Why this matters: the Llama 2 confusion

Llama 2 was released in BF16. Many teams loaded it in FP16 and reported *"the model's quality much worse than advertised."* Per [[ai-engineering-ch07-finetuning|Ch 7]]:

> "While this misunderstanding wasted a lot of people's time, the upside is that it forced many people to learn about numerical representations."

The Llama 3.1 release saw the same confusion repeat — see Bloke / Raschka writeups, llama.cpp benchmarks.

**Always load a model in the format it was trained in.**

## When BF16 is the right choice

- **Training foundation-scale models** — range matters more than per-value precision because activations and gradients span many orders of magnitude.
- **[[NVIDIA]] Ampere+ / Hopper / Blackwell** — native BF16 support, often faster than FP16 throughput.
- **TPUs** — designed around BF16.
- **[[QLoRA]] LoRA adapters** — Dettmers et al. chose BF16 for the LoRA weights on top of NF4-quantized base.

## When FP16 wins

- **Inference workloads where range is bounded** and you want the extra precision.
- **Older NVIDIA hardware** (V100 lineage) — FP16 has wider tooling support.
- **When you need to compare to an FP16-trained baseline** for reproducibility.
