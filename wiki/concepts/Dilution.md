# Dilution

**Dilution** is the process of lowering a solution's concentration by adding more solvent. The defining insight is that adding solvent does **not change the amount of solute** present — only the volume it is spread through. Because the moles of solute are conserved (n₁ = n₂) and moles = concentration × volume (n = M·L), the concentration and volume before and after dilution are related by:

$$M_1 V_1 = M_2 V_2 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad C_1 V_1 = C_2 V_2$$

This **dilution equation** works with any concentration unit and any volume unit, as long as the units are consistent on both sides (subscript 1 = before, 2 = after).

## Solving for Each Variable

- **New concentration C₂:** C₂ = C₁V₁ / V₂. e.g., 0.850 L of 5.00 M Cu(NO₃)₂ diluted to 1.80 L → (5.00 × 0.850)/1.80 = 2.36 M.
- **Final volume V₂:** V₂ = C₁V₁ / C₂. e.g., 0.011 L of 0.45 M HBr to reach 0.12 M → (0.45 × 0.011)/0.12 = 0.041 L.
- **Concentrate volume V₁:** V₁ = C₂V₂ / C₁. e.g., to make 5.00 L of 0.100 M KOH from 1.59 M stock → (0.100 × 5.00)/1.59 = 0.314 L.
