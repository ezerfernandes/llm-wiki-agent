# de Broglie Wavelength (Matter Waves)

The **de Broglie wavelength** is the wavelength associated with any moving particle of momentum p. Proposed by [[LouisDeBroglie|de Broglie]] in his 1923 thesis from the symmetry argument that, since light has particle properties, matter should have wave properties.

## Key Points
- Significant only for tiny particles; for macroscopic objects the wavelength is unmeasurably small, so they behave purely as particles (the [[WaveParticleDuality|correspondence principle]]).
- **Davisson-Germer experiment** (1925–26): electrons scattered from a crystal produced diffraction patterns obeying the Bragg condition — direct experimental proof of matter waves.
- Underpins the wave description of electrons in atoms and the design of electron microscopes.

## Equations
- de Broglie wavelength: λ = h/p
- Nonrelativistic speed from wavelength: v = h/(mλ)
- Bragg diffraction condition: nλ = 2d sin θ, n = 1, 2, 3, …
- Kinetic energy: KE = ½mv²

## Applications
- **Electron microscopes** — TEM (electrons through a thin sample; ~0.1 nm resolution, ~100 million× magnification) and SEM (surface scan, 3D imaging, ~10× lower resolution, no thin specimen required), both exploiting the short electron wavelength.
- **Crystal structure analysis** via electron Bragg reflection.
