## Definition
Ultraviolet (UV) radiation is the [[ElectromagneticSpectrum]] band just above visible light in frequency ("ultra" = above violet), spanning roughly 400 nm down to ~10 nm. It is produced by atomic/molecular electronic transitions and thermal motion.

## Key Points
- Subdivided into **UV-A** (320–400 nm; ~99% of solar UV reaching the surface), **UV-B** (290–320 nm; mostly absorbed by ozone), and **UV-C** (220–290 nm; fully absorbed by ozone).
- Damages collagen and DNA; UV-B is the primary cause of skin cancer and may cause cataracts.
- Tanning (melanin pigment) is a protective response; UV-B exposure (~10 min/day) drives vitamin-D synthesis in skin.
- Ozone depletion from CFC breakdown lets more dangerous UV-B/C reach the surface.

## Equations
- `c = f λ` (UV lies between visible light and X-rays).

## Applications
Sterilization of tools/workspaces, treatment of skin conditions and jaundice, fluorescence-based substance analysis, and imaging finer detail than visible-light microscopy.
