## Definition
Nuclear medical imaging detects radiation emitted by a [[RadiopharmaceuticalTracer]] inside the body to map physiological function (not just anatomy). Because gamma rays penetrate tissue, an externally placed detector can reconstruct where a gamma-emitting tracer has localized.

## Key Points
- **Anger (gamma) camera** — a lead collimator with bored apertures admits only gamma rays traveling in known directions onto a detector array, forming a 2-D activity map.
- **SPECT** (Single-Photon Emission Computed Tomography) — a rotating gamma-camera array reconstructs a 3-D tracer distribution at roughly 1 cm resolution.
- **PET** (Positron Emission Tomography) — uses positron-emitting tracers; each positron annihilates an electron into two 0.511-MeV photons emitted ~180° apart, and coincidence detection localizes the event to ~0.5 cm.
- **Radioimmunoassay (RIA)** — quantifies a substance via competitive antibody binding, measuring unbound antibody.
