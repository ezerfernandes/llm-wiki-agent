# Titration Curve

A **titration curve** plots a solution property against the amount of titrant added. For an [[AcidBaseTitration|acid-base titration]] the property is [[pHScale|pH]], because pH varies predictably with composition; the curve lets one follow the titration's progress and locate the **equivalence point** at the steep, near-vertical rise in pH.

## Shape

- **Strong acid / strong base:** the curve starts low (or high), is fairly flat, then rises almost vertically through a large pH range centered on **pH 7** at the equivalence point, and flattens again.
- **Weak acid / strong base:** the curve starts at a higher initial pH, has a gently sloped **buffer region** before equivalence (where pH = pKa at the half-equivalence point, via [[HendersonHasselbalch|Henderson-Hasselbalch]]), a *smaller* steep jump, and an equivalence point **above pH 7** (the conjugate base hydrolyzes — see [[SaltHydrolysis|salt hydrolysis]]).

## Use

The position and width of the vertical region determine which [[AcidBaseIndicator|indicator]] is appropriate: the indicator's color-change interval must fall within the steep portion so its end point matches the equivalence point. Detailed worked stages (initial, before-, at-, and after-equivalence pH) are given under [[AcidBaseTitration]].
