# Reaction Rate

The **rate of a chemical reaction** measures how much reactant is consumed, or how much product is produced, in a given amount of time. For species in solution it is expressed as the change in molar [[Molarity|concentration]] per unit time. For the decomposition of hydrogen peroxide:

$$\text{rate} = -\frac{\Delta[\mathrm{H_2O_2}]}{\Delta t}$$

The negative sign converts the (negative) change in a reactant's concentration into a positive rate; product terms take a positive sign. Rates are measured by tracking a time-dependent property — gas volume/pressure, light absorption of a colored species, conductivity of an electrolyte, or concentration directly.

## Three kinds of rate

- **Average rate** — over a finite time interval, Δ[concentration]/Δt.
- **Instantaneous rate** — at a single moment; equals the slope of the tangent to the concentration-vs-time curve at that time. Because reactant concentration falls as the reaction proceeds, the curve flattens and the reaction slows.
- **Initial rate** — the instantaneous rate at t = 0, used heavily in the [[ReactionOrder|method of initial rates]].

## Relative rates (stoichiometry)

For $aA + bB \rightarrow cC + dD$ the species rates are coupled by the [[ChemicalEquation|balanced equation]]'s stoichiometric coefficients:

$$-\frac{1}{a}\frac{\Delta[A]}{\Delta t} = -\frac{1}{b}\frac{\Delta[B]}{\Delta t} = \frac{1}{c}\frac{\Delta[C]}{\Delta t} = \frac{1}{d}\frac{\Delta[D]}{\Delta t}$$

Example: for $2\mathrm{H_2O_2}\rightarrow 2\mathrm{H_2O}+\mathrm{O_2}$, O₂ forms at half the rate H₂O₂ is consumed, and H₂O forms at the same rate H₂O₂ is consumed.
