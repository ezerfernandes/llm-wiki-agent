# Polyprotic Acid

A **polyprotic acid** can donate more than one proton per molecule, ionizing in successive steps.
- **Monoprotic** — one ionizable H (HCl, HNO₃, HCN). Acetic acid CH₃CO₂H is monoprotic despite four H atoms: only the carboxyl (–COOH) hydrogen ionizes.
- **Diprotic** — two ionizable H (H₂SO₄, H₂CO₃).
- **Triprotic** — three ionizable H (H₃PO₄).

## Stepwise ionization: Ka1 > Ka2 > Ka3

Each successive proton is harder to remove (a positive/more negative species holds H⁺ more tightly), so the [[AcidIonizationConstant|ionization constants]] decrease, usually by a factor of **~10⁵ to 10⁶** per step.

**Carbonic acid (weak diprotic):**
$$\mathrm{H_2CO_3 + H_2O \rightleftharpoons H_3O^+ + HCO_3^-}\quad K_{a1}=4.3\times10^{-7}$$
$$\mathrm{HCO_3^- + H_2O \rightleftharpoons H_3O^+ + CO_3^{2-}}\quad K_{a2}=4.7\times10^{-11}$$

**Phosphoric acid (triprotic):** Ka1 = 7.5×10⁻³, Ka2 = 6.2×10⁻⁸, Ka3 = 4.2×10⁻¹³.

**Sulfuric acid:** Ka1 > 10² (strong, complete first step); Ka2 = 1.2×10⁻².

## First ionization dominates

Because Ka1 ≫ Ka2, the **first ionization produces essentially all the H₃O⁺**, and [H₃O⁺] (hence pH) can be computed from the first step alone whenever Ka1 exceeds Ka2 by at least ~20×. The conjugate base of the second step has a concentration approximately equal to Ka2 and is largely independent of the first step (e.g. for H₂CO₃, [CO₃²⁻] ≈ 4.7×10⁻¹¹ M).

## Polyprotic bases

Bases can likewise accept multiple protons. Carbonate is a diprotic base:
$$\mathrm{CO_3^{2-} + H_2O \rightleftharpoons HCO_3^- + OH^-}\quad K_{b1}=2.1\times10^{-4}$$
$$\mathrm{HCO_3^- + H_2O \rightleftharpoons H_2CO_3 + OH^-}\quad K_{b2}=2.3\times10^{-8}$$
with constants decreasing per step. The intermediate ions (HCO₃⁻, H₂PO₄⁻, HPO₄²⁻) are [[Amphoteric|amphiprotic]].
