# Classifying Chemical Reactions

Many common reactions fall into three broad classes, recognized by characteristic reactivity patterns. A single reaction may belong to more than one class.

## The Three Classes

- **[[PrecipitationReaction|Precipitation reactions]]** — dissolved substances react to form one or more **insoluble solid** products. Also called double-displacement, double-replacement, or metathesis reactions; governed by solubility rules.
- **[[AcidBaseReaction|Acid-base reactions]]** — a **hydrogen ion (H⁺)** is transferred from one species to another. Neutralization (acid + base → salt + water) is the canonical case.
- **[[OxidationReduction|Oxidation-reduction (redox) reactions]]** — one or more elements undergo a **change in [[OxidationNumber|oxidation number]]** (electron transfer). Combustion, single-displacement, and disproportionation are subclasses.

## How to Classify

- Form a solid precipitate from mixed solutions → precipitation (check solubility rules).
- Transfer H⁺, or produce salt + water from an acid and a base → acid-base.
- Any change in oxidation number of an element → redox.

A combustion reaction is simultaneously a redox reaction; a single-displacement reaction is both a redox and a "replacement" reaction. Classification is about recognizing the dominant pattern, not assigning a unique label.
