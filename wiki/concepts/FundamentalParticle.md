## Definition
A **fundamental** (or elementary) **particle** has no internal structure and no finite size beyond its associated de Broglie wavelength — it cannot be broken into smaller constituents. Some are stable; others decay.

## Key Points
- The known fundamental matter particles are the two fermion families: **[[Lepton|leptons]]** (electron, muon, tau and their neutrinos) and **[[Quark|quarks]]** (up, down, strange, charm, bottom, top). All have half-integer spin.
- Forces are carried by fundamental **[[CarrierParticle|gauge bosons]]**: [[PhotonQuantum|photon]] (EM), W⁺/W⁻/Z⁰ (weak), gluons (strong), and the conjectured graviton.
- Leptons and quarks each split into **three generations/families** of increasing mass; ordinary matter uses only the first (up, down, electron, electron-neutrino). Heavier generations decay to lighter ones.
- Composite particles ([[Hadron|hadrons]] such as protons, neutrons, pions) are *not* fundamental — they are built from quarks. Probing for substructure (e.g. SLAC electron scattering, 1967) is how quark composites were distinguished from elementary leptons.
- The full inventory and their interactions are the content of the [[StandardModel]].
