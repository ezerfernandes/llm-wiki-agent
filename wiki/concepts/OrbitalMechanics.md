## Definition
Orbital mechanics describes the motion of a satellite — a smaller body gravitationally bound to a much larger parent. Gravity supplies the [[CentripetalForce]] that continually bends the satellite's path into an orbit instead of a straight line.

## Key Points
- Setting gravity equal to the centripetal force for a circular orbit (GmM/r² = mv²/r) gives orbital speed v² = GM/r.
- Substituting v = 2πr/T yields [[KeplersLaws|Kepler's third law]]: T² = (4π²/GM)r³.
- Orbital period at a given radius is independent of the satellite's mass (mass cancels).
- Orbital data lets physicists determine the parent body's mass: r³/T² = GM/4π².
- Apparent weightlessness in orbit is continuous free fall — craft and occupants accelerate together under gravity.
- Real orbits are ellipses ([[KeplersLaws|first law]]); small deviations (e.g., Mercury's precession) need general relativity.

## Equations
- Gravity as centripetal force: G m M / r² = m v² / r  →  v² = G M / r
- With v = 2πr/T:  T² = (4π² / (G M)) r³
- Parent mass: r³ / T² = G M / (4π²)
