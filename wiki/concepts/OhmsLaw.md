## Definition
**Ohm's law** states that the [[ElectricCurrent|current]] through a conductor is directly proportional to the [[Voltage|voltage]] across it and inversely proportional to its [[ElectricalResistance|resistance]]: I = V/R, equivalently V = IR. It is an empirical relationship, named for [[GeorgOhm]], that holds for **ohmic** materials whose resistance is constant over a wide range of voltage and current.

## Key Points
- Doubling the voltage doubles the current; doubling the resistance halves it.
- Ohmic materials (e.g., copper, aluminum) have voltage-independent resistance; many devices (diodes, filaments) are **non-ohmic**, so Ohm's law applies only over limited ranges.
- The voltage across a resistor carrying current is the **voltage drop** V = IR.
- In a simple single-resistor circuit, energy conservation requires the source's energy to be fully dissipated in the resistor (usually as heat).
- Resistance is mechanically analogous to friction opposing current flow.

## Equations
- I = V / R
- V = I R
- R = V / I
- 1 Ω = 1 V/A
