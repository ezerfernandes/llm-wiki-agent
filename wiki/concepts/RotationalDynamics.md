## Definition
Rotational dynamics is the study of what causes rotational motion — the relationship between [[Torque]], [[MomentOfInertia]], and [[AngularAcceleration]]. Its central result is the rotational form of [[NewtonsSecondLaw]]: net torque equals moment of inertia times angular acceleration.

## Key Points
- net τ = Iα is the rotational analog of F = ma, with τ↔F, I↔m (mass), α↔a.
- A torque applied to a body produces angular acceleration inversely proportional to its [[MomentOfInertia]].
- Because I depends on mass distribution, the same applied torque gives different α for the same total mass arranged differently (e.g., a rider near a merry-go-round's edge slows its angular acceleration).
- Derivation: a tangential force gives F = mrα, and torque τ = Fr = mr²α = Iα for a point mass; summing over a body gives net τ = Iα.

## Equations
- net τ = I α  (so α = net τ / I)
- τ = F r  (force perpendicular to radius)
- a = r α,  F = m r α
- I = Σ m r²
