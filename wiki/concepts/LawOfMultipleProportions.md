# Law of Multiple Proportions

The **law of multiple proportions** states that *when two elements react to form more than one [[ChemicalCompound|compound]], a fixed mass of one element will react with masses of the other element in a ratio of small, whole numbers*. Like the [[LawOfDefiniteProportions|law of definite proportions]], it is explained by [[AtomicTheory|Dalton's atomic theory]].

## Worked Example — Copper and Chlorine

Copper and chlorine form two compounds:
- Green solid: 0.558 g Cl per 1 g Cu
- Brown solid: 1.116 g Cl per 1 g Cu

The ratio of the two mass ratios is:

$$\frac{1.116\ \text{g Cl/g Cu}}{0.558\ \text{g Cl/g Cu}} = 2:1$$

This 2:1 reflects atomic composition: the brown compound is **CuCl₂** (1 Cu : 2 Cl) and the green compound is **CuCl** (1 Cu : 1 Cl).

## Worked Example — Carbon and Oxygen

(Example 2.2) Compound A: 4.27 g C, 5.69 g O (O:C = 1.33:1). Compound B: 5.19 g C, 13.84 g O (O:C = 2.67:1). The ratio 1.33 : 2.67 = 1:2 shows A and B are different compounds — consistent with CO and CO₂.
