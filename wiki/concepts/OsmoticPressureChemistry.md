# Osmotic Pressure (Chemistry)

> Chemistry-domain page (OpenStax *Chemistry 2e*, Ch 11). For the physics/fluid-dynamics and biology treatment of membrane transport, see [[Osmosis]] (College Physics 2e).

**Osmosis** is the "diffusion-driven transfer of solvent molecules through a semipermeable membrane" — a membrane that passes solvent but blocks solute (e.g., cell membranes, dialysis tubing). Pure solvent diffuses into a solution faster than it returns because the solvent concentration is higher on the pure-solvent side.

**Osmotic pressure (Π)** is the pressure that must be applied to the solution to **stop** osmosis. As a [[ColligativeProperties|colligative property]] it depends only on the dissolved particle count:

$$\Pi = i \, M R T$$

- Π — osmotic pressure (atm)
- M — molar concentration ([[Molarity|molarity]], mol/L)
- R — universal gas constant = 0.08206 L·atm·mol⁻¹·K⁻¹
- T — absolute temperature (K)
- i — [[VantHoffFactor|van 't Hoff factor]]

## Tonicity
Relative to blood serum (osmotic pressure ≈ 7.7 atm):
- **Isotonic** — equal osmotic pressure; injected solutions must be isotonic to avoid cell damage.
- **Hypotonic** — more dilute; water enters cells, swelling and bursting them (**hemolysis**).
- **Hypertonic** — more concentrated; cells lose water and shrivel (**crenation**).

## Reverse Osmosis
Applying pressure greater than the solution's osmotic pressure forces solvent *out* of the solution — used to desalinate seawater and produce high-purity water.
