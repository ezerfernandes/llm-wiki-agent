# Precipitation Reaction

A **precipitation reaction** is one in which dissolved substances react to form one or more **insoluble solid products** (a precipitate). These are also called **double-displacement**, **double-replacement**, or **metathesis** reactions. They are one of the three classes in [[ClassifyingChemicalReactions]].

## Solubility

**Solubility** is the maximum concentration of a substance attainable under specified conditions. A substance with relatively large solubility is **soluble**; one with relatively low solubility is **insoluble** and readily precipitates. A precipitate forms when the would-be product's concentration would exceed its solubility.

## Solubility Guidelines (selected)

**Generally soluble:**
- NH₄⁺ salts and Group 1 cations (Li⁺, Na⁺, K⁺, Rb⁺, Cs⁺) — no exceptions
- NO₃⁻, ClO₃⁻, C₂H₃O₂⁻ (acetate), HCO₃⁻ — no exceptions
- Cl⁻, Br⁻, I⁻ — except with Ag⁺, Hg₂²⁺, Pb²⁺
- SO₄²⁻ — except with Ag⁺, Ba²⁺, Ca²⁺, Hg₂²⁺, Pb²⁺, Sr²⁺

**Generally insoluble:**
- CO₃²⁻, CrO₄²⁻, PO₄³⁻, S²⁻ — except with Group 1 cations and NH₄⁺
- OH⁻ — except with Group 1 cations and Ba²⁺

## Worked Example

Mixing potassium iodide and lead nitrate solutions precipitates bright-yellow lead iodide:

- **Molecular:** 2KI(aq) + Pb(NO₃)₂(aq) → PbI₂(s) + 2KNO₃(aq)
- **Net ionic:** Pb²⁺(aq) + 2I⁻(aq) → PbI₂(s)

(K⁺ and NO₃⁻ are spectator ions — see [[ChemicalEquation]].)

## Quantitative treatment (Ch 15)

Chapter 15 makes precipitation quantitative via the [[SolubilityProduct|solubility product Ksp]]: a precipitate forms when the [[ReactionQuotient|reaction quotient]] Qsp exceeds Ksp (and dissolution proceeds when Qsp < Ksp). This underpins the [[CommonIonEffect|common-ion effect]] and [[SelectivePrecipitation|selective precipitation]] (separating ions by their differing Ksp — the least soluble precipitates first).
