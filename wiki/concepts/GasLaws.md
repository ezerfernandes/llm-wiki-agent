# Gas Laws (Simple / Empirical)

The **simple gas laws** are four experimentally derived relationships among a gas's pressure (P), volume (V), temperature (T, always absolute/kelvin), and amount in moles (n). Each holds two variables constant and relates the other two. Together they combine into the [[IdealGasLawChemistry|ideal gas law]] PV = nRT. They are the hub for the four focused pages [[BoylesLaw]], [[CharlesLaw]], [[AvogadrosLaw]], and Amontons's law below.

## The Four Laws

| Law | Statement | Relationship | Held constant |
|---|---|---|---|
| **[[BoylesLaw|Boyle's law]]** | V inversely proportional to P | PV = k, so P₁V₁ = P₂V₂ | T, n |
| **[[CharlesLaw|Charles's law]]** | V directly proportional to T | V/T = k, so V₁/T₁ = V₂/T₂ | P, n |
| **[[AvogadrosLaw|Avogadro's law]]** | V directly proportional to n | V/n = k, so V₁/n₁ = V₂/n₂ | T, P |
| **Amontons's / Gay-Lussac's law** | P directly proportional to T | P/T = k, so P₁/T₁ = P₂/T₂ | V, n |

Amontons's law (P ∝ T at constant V, n) was established by Guillaume Amontons (~1700) and refined by Joseph Louis Gay-Lussac (~1800). Example: a sealed isobutane can at 24 °C (297 K) and 360 kPa reaches 390 kPa when warmed to 50 °C (323 K).

## Combined Gas Law

For a fixed amount of gas between two states, the three variable laws merge:

$$\frac{P_1 V_1}{T_1} = \frac{P_2 V_2}{T_2}$$

Each side equals nR. Adding Avogadro's law (the dependence on n) yields the full ideal gas law.

## Why the Laws Hold (Microscopic View)

The [[KineticMolecularTheory|kinetic-molecular theory]] explains each law in terms of the frequency and force of molecular collisions with the container walls — e.g., higher T raises molecular speed (more forceful/frequent collisions → higher P), and smaller V crowds molecules (more frequent collisions → higher P).
