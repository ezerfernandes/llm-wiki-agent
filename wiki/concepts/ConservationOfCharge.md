## Definition
The law of conservation of charge states that the total [[ElectricCharge]] in any closed process is constant. Charge is never created or destroyed — it is only transferred or redistributed between objects and regions.

## Key Points
- When materials are rubbed together, electrons move from one to the other, leaving equal and opposite net charges — no new charge is made.
- In particle creation (e.g., from energy), particles always appear in oppositely charged pairs (such as an electron and a positron), so net charge change is zero.
- A conductor charged by induction-with-grounding gains charge that flows in from Earth; the total charge of the whole system (object + Earth) is unchanged.
- It is one of the exact conservation laws of physics, on the same footing as conservation of energy and momentum.
- In [[ParticlePhysics|particle physics]] it is one of several quantum-number conservation laws — alongside **baryon number (B)** and **lepton family numbers** — used to decide which particle reactions and decays are allowed (see [[ConservationLawsParticlePhysics]]). [[Antimatter|Antiparticles]] carry opposite charge, so pair creation/annihilation conserves total charge.
