## Definition
A transverse wave is a [[Wave]] in which the disturbance (particle displacement) is perpendicular to the direction of propagation.

## Key Points
- Examples: a vibrating guitar string, ripples on a string, and electromagnetic radiation.
- Contrast with a [[LongitudinalWave]], where displacement is parallel to travel.
- Some media carry both types simultaneously: ocean surface waves are a mix, and seismic waves include transverse (S) and longitudinal (P) components that travel at different speeds.
- Like all waves, it obeys v_w = f λ and combines by [[Superposition]].

## Equations
- Wave speed: v_w = f λ
