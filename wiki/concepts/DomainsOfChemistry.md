# Domains of Chemistry

Chemists study matter and energy across three interrelated **domains**:

- **Macroscopic** — the realm of everyday things large enough to be sensed directly by sight or touch. Here chemists observe and measure properties such as density, solubility, and flammability.
- **Microscopic** — entities too small to observe directly but essential to explanation: [[Molecule|molecules]] and [[Atom|atoms]], ions and electrons, protons and neutrons, and chemical bonds. Most are too small to see even with advanced microscopes and must be pictured in the mind.
- **Symbolic** — the specialized language that represents the other two domains: chemical symbols (periodic table), chemical formulas, chemical equations, graphs, drawings, and calculations.

**Water as an example:** macroscopically it freezes, melts, and boils; microscopically it is two H atoms and one O atom whose intermolecular attractions explain its behavior; symbolically it is written H₂O, with state labels (g), (l), (s). A recurring challenge for learners is that *the same symbol can mean different things* in the macroscopic vs. microscopic domains.
