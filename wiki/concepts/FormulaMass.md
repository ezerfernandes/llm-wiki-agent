# Formula Mass

The **formula mass** of a substance is the sum of the average [[AtomicMass|atomic masses]] of all atoms represented in its [[ChemicalFormula|chemical formula]], expressed in **atomic mass units (amu)**.

## Molecular Mass (Covalent Substances)

For a covalent compound the formula represents a single molecule, so its formula mass is often called the **molecular mass**.
- Chloroform (CHCl₃): 12.01 + 1.008 + 3(35.45) = **119.37 amu**
- Ibuprofen (C₁₃H₁₈O₂): 13(12.01) + 18(1.008) + 2(16.00) = **206.27 amu**

## Formula Mass for Ionic Compounds

[[IonicCompound|Ionic compounds]] are not made of discrete molecules, so "molecular mass" does not apply — but the arithmetic is identical. Use the average atomic masses of the neutral atoms; the tiny mass change from gaining or losing electrons during ion formation is negligible.
- Sodium chloride (NaCl): 22.99 + 35.45 = **58.44 amu**
- Aluminum sulfate (Al₂(SO₄)₃): 2(26.98) + 3(32.06) + 12(16.00) = **342.14 amu**

## Relationship to Molar Mass

Formula mass (in amu) is **numerically equal** to [[MolarMass|molar mass]] (in g/mol). This is the link that lets a formula mass computed atom-by-atom be used directly to weigh out one [[TheMoleConcept|mole]] of the substance.
