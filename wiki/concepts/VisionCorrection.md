## Definition
Vision correction compensates for an eye's refractive error by placing a lens (spectacle or contact) that shifts the image of an object into the range the eye can focus, or by surgically reshaping the cornea.

## Key Points
- **Myopia (nearsightedness)**: the eye over-converges and focuses distant objects in front of the retina; corrected with a **diverging, negative-power** lens that forms a virtual image within the wearer's far point (lens thinnest at center).
- **Hyperopia (farsightedness)**: the eye under-converges and would focus near objects behind the retina; corrected with a **converging, positive-power** lens that forms an image beyond the wearer's near point (lens thickest at center).
- **Astigmatism**: uneven focus (often corneal) giving direction-dependent blur; corrected with cylindrical (asymmetric) lens components.
- Powers of lenses placed close together add: P = P_1 + P_2.
- Contact lenses sit fixed relative to the eye and can mask corneal surface irregularities via the tear layer.
- **LASIK** uses a 193 nm excimer laser to ablate corneal tissue in ~0.3 μm layers, reshaping the cornea to correct myopia, hyperopia, and astigmatism.

## Equations
- P = 1/d_o + 1/d_i (P in diopters, distances in meters)
