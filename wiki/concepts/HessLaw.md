# Hess's Law

**Hess's law** states that if a process can be written as the sum of several stepwise processes, the [[EnthalpyChemistry|enthalpy change]] of the overall process equals the sum of the enthalpy changes of the steps. It is named for [[GermainHess]].

Hess's law is valid because **enthalpy is a state function**: ΔH depends only on the initial and final states, not on the path. It lets chemists compute ΔH for reactions that are hard or impossible to measure directly, by adding known reactions.

## Worked Illustration
Direct: C(s) + O₂(g) → CO₂(g)   ΔH° = −394 kJ
Two-step:
- C(s) + ½O₂(g) → CO(g)   ΔH° = −111 kJ
- CO(g) + ½O₂(g) → CO₂(g)   ΔH° = −283 kJ
- Sum: −111 + (−283) = −394 kJ ✓ (the intermediate CO cancels)

## Manipulation Rules
When combining reactions to reach a target, two properties of ΔH are used (see [[ThermochemicalEquation]]):
1. **Direct proportionality** — multiply a reaction's coefficients by n ⇒ multiply its ΔH by n.
2. **Reversibility** — reverse a reaction ⇒ reverse the sign of its ΔH.

## Formation-Enthalpy Form
A common application uses tabulated [[StandardEnthalpyOfFormation|standard enthalpies of formation]]:

**ΔH°_reaction = Σ n · ΔHf°(products) − Σ n · ΔHf°(reactants)**
