## Definition
The center of mass is the single point at which the mass of a system can be treated as concentrated for the purpose of analyzing its translational motion. In a uniform gravitational field it coincides with the [[CenterOfGravity]], the point where the total weight effectively acts.

## Key Points
- For an object near Earth's surface, the center of mass and center of gravity are the same point because gravity is essentially uniform.
- Treating weight as acting entirely at this point lets you reduce a distributed body to a single force when summing forces and torques.
- Its location is central to [[Stability]]: a body stays stable only while this point lies above its base of support.
- For a uniform object the point sits at the geometric center; for non-uniform objects it shifts toward the heavier region.

## Equations
- Weight acting at the center of mass: `w = mg`
