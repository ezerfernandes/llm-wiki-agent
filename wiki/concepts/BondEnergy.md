# Bond Energy

**Bond energy** (or **bond dissociation energy**, D) is the energy required to break one mole of a specific [[CovalentBonding|covalent bond]] in gaseous molecules. For a diatomic XY: XY(g) → X(g) + Y(g), with D(X–Y) = ΔH°. Example: D(H–H) = 436 kJ/mol. It measures covalent **bond strength**, the analogue of [[LatticeEnergy|lattice energy]] for ionic solids.

## Bond Order, Strength, and Length

Bond strength increases with the number of shared electron pairs (bond order), and as strength increases, **bond length decreases**:

| Bond | Length (Å) | Energy (kJ/mol) |
|---|---|---|
| C–C | 1.54 | 345 |
| C=C | 1.34 | 611 |
| C≡C | 1.20 | 837 |
| C–O | 1.43 | 350 |
| C=O | 1.23 | 741 |
| C≡O | 1.13 | 1080 |

Bond strength typically decreases down a group: C–F 439 > C–Cl 330 > C–Br 275 kJ/mol. Other values (kJ/mol): H–F 569, H–Cl 432, C–H 415, N≡N 946, O=O 498.

## Estimating Reaction Enthalpy

Average bond energies estimate the enthalpy of reaction:

$$\Delta H = \sum D(\text{bonds broken}) - \sum D(\text{bonds formed})$$

All D values are positive. ΔH < 0 (exothermic) means products have stronger bonds; ΔH > 0 (endothermic) means weaker. Example: H₂ + Cl₂ → 2HCl, ΔH = (436 + 243) − 2(432) = −185 kJ. Because average D values vary by molecule, this gives only an estimate (CO + 2H₂ → CH₃OH yields −107 kJ vs. the actual [[StandardEnthalpyOfFormation|ΔHf°]]-based −90.5 kJ).

## Orbital View: σ vs. π Contributions

[[ValenceBondTheory|Valence bond theory]] explains bond strength as orbital overlap: more overlap → stronger bond, and at the bond distance the system sits at its energy minimum. Because a [[SigmaAndPiBonds|σ bond]] overlaps more than a π bond, each π bond is weaker. Multiple-bond energies are roughly additive: C–C 347 (σ); C=C 614 = 347 + 267 (σ+π); C≡C 839 = 614 + 225 (σ+2π) kJ/mol. In [[MolecularOrbitalTheory|MO theory]] the same trend appears as rising [[BondOrder|bond order]].

## Magnitude vs. Lattice Energy

Single-bond dissociation energies (~150–400 kJ/mol) are not directly comparable to [[LatticeEnergy|lattice energies]] (~600–4000 kJ/mol): bond energy is a two-atom interaction, lattice energy the sum of many electrostatic interactions in a crystal.
