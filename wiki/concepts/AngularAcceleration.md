## Definition
Angular acceleration (α) is the rate of change of [[AngularVelocity]] with time, α = Δω/Δt, measured in rad/s². It is the rotational analog of linear [[Acceleration]]: it tells how quickly a rotating object's spin rate changes.

## Key Points
- α is positive when ω is increasing and negative when ω is decreasing (spin-down).
- Tangential (linear) acceleration aₜ changes the *magnitude* of speed along a circular path and is linked to α through the radius: aₜ = rα.
- This is distinct from [[CentripetalAcceleration]], which changes only the *direction* of velocity (points toward the center).
- A larger radius converts the same α into larger tangential acceleration; small wheels accelerate the road surface less for the same α.
- The analogy holds across rotation: θ↔x, ω↔v, α↔a.

## Equations
- α = Δω / Δt
- aₜ = Δv / Δt
- aₜ = r α  (equivalently α = aₜ / r)
