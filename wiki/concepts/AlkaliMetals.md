# Alkali Metals (Group 1)

The **alkali metals** — lithium, sodium, potassium, rubidium, cesium, and francium — are the group 1 [[RepresentativeElements|representative metals]]. They have the largest atomic radii and the lowest first ionization energies in their periods (see [[PeriodicTrends]]), so the single valence electron is removed very easily, giving stable **1+ cations**. Reactivity increases down the group. The solid metals are very soft; lithium has the lowest density of any metal (0.5 g/cm³).

## Reactions
All react vigorously with water to form H₂ and a basic metal hydroxide (they are easier to oxidize than hydrogen):

> 2Li(s) + 2H₂O(l) → 2LiOH(aq) + H₂(g)

They react directly with all nonmetals except the noble gases to form binary ionic compounds with 1+ ions. Because of their reactivity toward moisture and O₂, they are stored under mineral oil and never occur free in nature.

## Occurrence, Preparation, Uses
- Occurrence: Li in silicate/phosphate minerals; Na and K in salt deposits and silicates.
- Preparation: only by [[ElectrolysisChemistry|electrolysis]] of molten compounds — e.g., sodium from the **Downs cell** (see [[Metallurgy]]).
- Uses: Na reduces compounds of Ti and Zr in their production; Li/Na are reducing agents in organic synthesis (dyes, drugs, perfumes).
- Flame colors: Na bright yellow, Li crimson, the others pale violet.
