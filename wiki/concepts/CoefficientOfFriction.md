## Definition
The coefficient of friction is a dimensionless proportionality constant relating the [[Friction]] force to the [[NormalForce]] between two surfaces. Separate values exist for the two regimes: μ_s for [[StaticFriction]] and μ_k for [[KineticFriction]].

## Key Points
- Depends on the materials and condition of the contacting surfaces, not on contact area.
- μ_s ≥ μ_k for a given pair of surfaces, so starting motion takes more force than sustaining it.
- Lubrication lowers it dramatically — e.g. synovial fluid in joints gives μ ≈ 0.015.
- Can be measured directly: on an incline where an object slides at constant velocity, μ_k = tan θ.

## Equations
- Static: μ_s = f_s(max) / N
- Kinetic: μ_k = f_k / N
- From an incline (constant velocity): μ_k = tan θ
