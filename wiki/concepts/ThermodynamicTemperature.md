## Definition
**Temperature** is, operationally, the quantity a thermometer measures — a scientific notion
distinct from human sensation. Microscopically (see [[KineticTheoryOfGases]]) it is a direct
measure of the **average translational kinetic energy** of a substance's particles. It is an
**intensive** property: independent of how much substance is present.

## Key Points
- **Thermal equilibrium**: two systems in contact exchange energy until they reach the same
  temperature, after which no net heat flows.
- **Zeroth law of thermodynamics**: if A is in thermal equilibrium with B and B with C, then A is
  in equilibrium with C. This transitivity makes temperature well defined and justifies thermometry.
- **Absolute zero** is 0 K = −273.15 °C, the lowest possible temperature; a constant-volume gas's
  pressure extrapolates to zero there.
- Thermometers exploit temperature-dependent properties: liquid [[ThermalExpansion|expansion]],
  bimetallic-strip curvature, electrical resistance, liquid-crystal color, and emitted infrared.
- Human perception is unreliable — metal and wood at the same temperature feel different because of
  conductivity, not temperature.

## Equations
- Microscopic link: KE̅ = (3/2)·k·T (k = Boltzmann constant 1.38×10⁻²³ J/K)
