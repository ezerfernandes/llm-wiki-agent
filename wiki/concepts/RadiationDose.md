## Definition
Radiation dose quantifies energy deposited in matter by [[IonizingRadiation]] and its biological hazard. **Absorbed dose** measures energy per unit mass; **dose equivalent** weights that by the radiation's [[RelativeBiologicalEffectiveness]] to reflect biological harm.

## Key Points
- Units: **rad** (0.01 J/kg) and SI **gray (Gy)** for absorbed dose; **rem** and SI **sievert (Sv)** for dose equivalent.
- Biological damage stems from ionization disrupting molecules, especially DNA; overwhelmed repair leads to senescence, cell death, or cancer.
- **Linear no-threshold hypothesis:** low-dose risk is assumed proportional to high-dose risk (debated; hormesis is a competing low-dose idea).
- **LD50/32 ≈ 4.5 Sv** — acute whole-body dose killing 50% within 32 days.
- Typical background ~2.8 mSv/yr; chest X-ray ~0.02 mSv; abdominal CT ~10 mSv. Occupational limits ~20–50 mSv/yr.
- Protection relies on time, distance, and shielding.

## Equations
- Absorbed dose: Dose (Gy) = E_absorbed (J) / mass (kg); 1 Gy = 1 J/kg = 100 rad
- Dose equivalent: Dose (Sv) = Dose (Gy) × RBE; rem = rad × RBE; 1 Sv = 100 rem
- Energy from activity: N = R · t, then E = N × (energy per decay)
