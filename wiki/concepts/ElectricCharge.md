## Definition
Electric charge is a fundamental property of matter that produces electromagnetic interactions. It comes in two types, conventionally called positive and negative. Charge is the source of the [[ElectricField]] and the quantity that appears in [[CoulombsLaw]].

## Key Points
- Like charges repel; unlike charges attract; the force weakens with distance.
- Charge is carried by subatomic particles: the [[Electron]] is negative and the proton is positive, with equal magnitude.
- Charge is **quantized** — it occurs in whole-number multiples of the elementary charge |q_e|.
- The electromagnetic force from charge is one of the [[FundamentalForces]] and is the origin of nearly all everyday [[Force|forces]] (friction, cohesion, adhesion).
- Charge obeys [[ConservationOfCharge]]: it can be transferred but not created or destroyed.

## Equations
- Elementary charge: |q_e| = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ C
- Charges per coulomb: 1.00 C / (1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ C) = 6.25 × 10¹⁸
