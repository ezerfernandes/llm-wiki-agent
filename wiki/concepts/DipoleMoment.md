# Dipole Moment

A **dipole moment** quantifies the separation of charge across a polar bond or molecule. (Named *DipoleMoment* explicitly for chemistry context.)

## Bond Dipole Moment

A polar covalent bond between atoms of differing [[Electronegativity|electronegativity]] has a **bond dipole moment** of magnitude

$$\mu = Q\,r$$

where Q is the magnitude of the partial charges and r is the distance between them. It is drawn as a vector arrow pointing from the **less** electronegative atom toward the **more** electronegative atom, with a small plus sign marking the δ+ end. See [[BondPolarity]].

## Molecular Dipole Moment

The molecular dipole moment is the **vector sum** of all bond dipole moments, measuring the net charge separation of the whole molecule. Whether it is zero or nonzero — and hence whether the molecule is [[MolecularPolarity|polar or nonpolar]] — depends on [[MolecularGeometry|molecular geometry]]:

- Homonuclear diatomics (Br₂, N₂): μ = 0 (no electronegativity difference).
- Heteronuclear diatomics (CO, HF): μ ≠ 0.
- CO₂ (linear): bond dipoles cancel → μ = 0 (nonpolar) even though the bonds are polar.
- H₂O (bent): bond dipoles do not cancel → μ ≠ 0 (polar).
