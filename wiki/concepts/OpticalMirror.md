## Definition
An optical mirror forms images by [[LightReflection]]. Plane mirrors give virtual upright images; spherical (concave and convex) mirrors focus or spread light to form real or virtual images.

(Distinct from data "mirroring"/replication — this is the physical optics element.)

## Key Points
- A **plane mirror** produces a virtual, upright, same-size image that appears to lie behind the mirror.
- **Concave mirror:** converges parallel rays to a focal point; can form real images.
  - Object beyond `f`: real, inverted, magnified or reduced (telescope, solar concentrator).
  - Object within `f`: virtual, upright, magnified (makeup/shaving mirror).
- **Convex mirror:** always forms a virtual, upright, reduced image with a wide field of view (security/shop, passenger-side car mirrors).
- For a spherical mirror, the focal length is half the radius of curvature, `f = R/2`.
- Same imaging relation as lenses: the [[ThinLensEquation]] form `1/d_o + 1/d_i = 1/f`.
- Applications: reflecting telescopes, headlight/flashlight reflectors, parabolic-trough solar thermal systems, keratometers (corneal curvature).

## Equations
- Focal length / radius: `f = R / 2`
- Mirror equation: `1/d_o + 1/d_i = 1/f`
- Power: `P = 1 / f`
- Magnification: `m = − d_i / d_o`
