## Definition
**Hadrons** are composite particles that feel the [[StrongNuclearForce|strong nuclear force]]. They are built from [[Quark|quarks]] and are *not* [[FundamentalParticle|fundamental]]. They divide into **baryons** and **[[Meson|mesons]]**.

## Key Points
- **Baryons**: three-quark systems (antibaryons = three antiquarks). They carry **baryon number B = +1** and always decay into other baryons. Includes nucleons (proton, neutron) and the hyperons (Λ, Σ, Ξ, Ω); rest masses ~939–1673 MeV/c².
- **[[Meson|Mesons]]**: quark–antiquark pairs, baryon number B = 0; they decay to [[Lepton|leptons]] and photons without producing other hadrons. Includes pions and kaons; rest masses ~135–550 MeV/c².
- Hadrons obey [[ConservationLawsParticlePhysics|conservation laws]]: charge, baryon number, and (in strong-force reactions) strangeness. The proliferation of hadrons (the "particle zoo") revealed patterns that led to the [[Quark|quark]] model.
- Quark substructure was confirmed by deep-inelastic electron scattering off protons (SLAC, 1967) and three-jet events in colliders.

## Equations
- Baryon number: B = +1 (3 quarks), B = 0 (meson, quark + antiquark)
- Proton (uud): charge (+2/3 + 2/3 − 1/3)qₑ = +qₑ; neutron (udd): charge 0
- Example decay: Ξ⁻ → Λ⁰ + π⁻ (weak, strangeness changes by 1)
