## Definition
Kinematics is the study of motion without considering its causes (the forces that produce it). The term derives from the Greek *kinema*, meaning motion. One-dimensional kinematics describes motion along a straight line; [[TwoDimensionalKinematics]] extends this to motion in a plane by decomposing it into independent perpendicular components.

## Key Points
- Describes motion quantitatively using position, [[Displacement]], [[Velocity]], and [[Acceleration]].
- Requires a chosen [[ReferenceFrame]] / coordinate system; direction in 1D is encoded by positive/negative signs.
- Distinguishes [[ScalarQuantity]] (magnitude only) from [[VectorQuantity]] (magnitude and direction).
- The constant-acceleration model predicts future motion from initial conditions (see [[KinematicEquations]]).
- Motion can be analyzed graphically (see [[GraphicalAnalysisOfMotion]]).
- Forces and causes of motion are treated separately, in dynamics.
