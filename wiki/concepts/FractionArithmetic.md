# Fraction Arithmetic

**Fraction arithmetic** is the set of rules for the four operations on [[Fraction|fractions]]. A useful organizing fact, developed in OpenStax [[Prealgebra]] 2e (Chapter 4, [[prealgebra-2e-ch04-fractions]], sections 4.2, 4.4–4.5), is that **multiplication and division do *not* need a common denominator, while addition and subtraction *do*.** Signs follow the [[SignedNumberArithmetic|integer sign rules]] throughout, and every result is simplified to lowest terms (see [[EquivalentFractions]]).

**Multiplication.** `(a/b)·(c/d) = (a·c)/(b·d)` for `b ≠ 0`, `d ≠ 0`: determine the sign, multiply the numerators, multiply the denominators, and simplify. It is usually easier to **cancel common factors before multiplying** rather than reducing a large product afterward. An integer `n` is written `n/1` first (e.g. `1/7 · 56 = 56/7 = 8`).

**Division.** `(a/b) ÷ (c/d) = (a/b)·(d/c)` for `b, c, d ≠ 0`: "multiply the first fraction by the **[[Reciprocal|reciprocal]]** of the second," then simplify. This is the rule that also simplifies a [[ComplexFraction|complex fraction]], which is just a division written as a stacked fraction.

**Addition and subtraction with a common denominator.** For `c ≠ 0`: `a/c + b/c = (a+b)/c` and `a/c − b/c = (a−b)/c` — combine the numerators over the shared denominator and simplify. The denominators are *not* added; only the numerators are.

**Addition and subtraction with different denominators.** First make the denominators match using the **least common denominator (LCD)** — the [[LeastCommonMultiple|least common multiple]] of the denominators, found by [[PrimeFactorization|prime factorization]] (factor each denominator, line up matching primes in columns, take each prime the greatest number of times it appears, and multiply). Then rewrite each fraction as an equivalent fraction over the LCD (Equivalent Fractions Property), add or subtract the numerators, and simplify. Comparing/ordering unlike fractions uses the same LCD step and then compares numerators.

These four rules are the foundation for arithmetic on [[MixedNumber|mixed numbers]] (convert to improper fractions, operate, convert back) and for clearing fractions when solving an [[Equation|equation]]. The fraction bar also acts as a **grouping symbol**: in an expression, fully simplify above and below the bar (respecting [[OrderOfOperations|order of operations]] and any [[Exponent|exponents]]) before dividing.
