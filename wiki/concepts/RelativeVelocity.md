## Definition
Relative velocity is the velocity of an object as measured from a particular observer's [[ReferenceFrame]]. Because [[Velocity]] is a [[VectorQuantity]], velocities measured in different frames combine by vector addition. Velocity has no meaning until a reference frame is specified.

## Key Points
- The same physical event yields different observed velocities and trajectories in different frames, yet the underlying physics is consistent (classical relativity).
- Velocities add by vector addition, not scalar arithmetic (except in one dimension) — an application of [[VectorAddition]] and [[VectorComponents]].
- Perpendicular velocity components are independent (e.g. a boat's heading velocity vs. a river's crosswise current).
- Classical (Galilean) velocity addition is valid for speeds well below ~1% of the speed of light (< ~3,000 km/s); beyond that, relativistic addition is needed.

## Equations
- Components: vx = v cos θ ; vy = v sin θ
- Magnitude: v = √(vx² + vy²)
- Direction: θ = tan⁻¹(vy / vx)
