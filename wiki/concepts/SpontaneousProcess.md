# Spontaneous and Nonspontaneous Processes

A **spontaneous process** occurs naturally under given conditions without requiring continual input of energy from an external source. A **nonspontaneous process** will not proceed unless it is "driven" by such external input. Spontaneity is directional: a process that is spontaneous one way under given conditions is nonspontaneous in reverse under those same conditions (ice melts spontaneously at room temperature; water does not spontaneously freeze there).

## Spontaneity Is Not Speed
Spontaneity is a **thermodynamic** property and is *not* correlated with the **kinetic** rate of a process. A reaction can be thermodynamically spontaneous yet effectively unobservable in practice:
- C(s, diamond) ⟶ C(s, graphite) is spontaneous at ambient pressure but immeasurably slow — diamond is *thermodynamically unstable* but *kinetically stable*.
- ⁹⁹ᵐTc decays in hours; ²³⁸U over billions of years; both are spontaneous.

## Driving Force: Dispersal of Matter and Energy
Spontaneity correlates with the extent to which a process increases the **dispersal (distribution) of matter and/or energy**, producing a more uniform distribution.
- **Matter dispersal** — ideal gas expanding into an evacuated flask is spontaneous even though, for the isolated system, w = −PΔV = 0 (P = 0 in vacuum), q = 0, and so ΔU = q + w = 0. The driver is purely the more uniform spreading of matter.
- **Energy dispersal** — heat spontaneously flows from a hotter object to a colder one (q_X < 0, q_Y = −q_X > 0), redistributing thermal energy more uniformly with no net energy change for the system.

This qualitative idea is made quantitative by [[EntropyThermodynamics|entropy]] and the [[SecondLawOfThermodynamics|second law]]: spontaneous processes increase the entropy of the universe.
