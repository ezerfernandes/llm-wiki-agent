## Definition
Time dilation is the relativistic effect in which a clock moving relative to an observer is measured to tick more slowly than a clock at rest. The elapsed time Δt an observer measures for a process is longer than the **proper time** Δt₀ — the interval measured in the frame where the events occur at the same location — by the [[LorentzFactor|Lorentz factor]] γ.

## Key Points
- **Proper time (Δt₀)** is the shortest possible interval for a process; it is measured by an observer at rest relative to the events.
- A direct consequence of [[EinsteinPostulates|Einstein's postulates]] and the invariant [[SpeedOfLightPhysics|speed of light]] (derived via a "light clock").
- **Relativity of simultaneity**: two events simultaneous in one [[ReferenceFrame|frame]] need not be in another, though the order of causally connected events is preserved.
- Verified experimentally: a muon at 0.950c (γ = 3.20) with proper lifetime 1.52 μs is measured by Earth observers to live 4.87 μs.
- **GPS** must apply time-dilation corrections or positioning errors accumulate within minutes.
- **Twin paradox**: a traveler at γ = 30 ages 2 years while her Earth twin ages 60; the asymmetry is real because the traveler accelerates (her frame is non-inertial), so she genuinely ages less.

## Equations
- Δt = Δt₀ / √(1 − v²/c²) = γ Δt₀
- γ = 1 / √(1 − v²/c²)
