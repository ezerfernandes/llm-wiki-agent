## Definition
Young's double slit experiment passes coherent, monochromatic light through two closely spaced slits, producing a pattern of alternating bright and dark interference fringes on a screen. It was historical proof that light behaves as a [[Wave]]. (Thomas Young — [[ThomasYoung]].)

## Key Points
- Requires coherent light (definite phase relationship); the slits act as two in-phase [[HuygensPrinciple]] sources.
- The path-length difference between the two slits to a screen point determines constructive vs. destructive [[WaveInterference]] ([[Superposition]]).
- Bright fringes (maxima) occur where the path difference is an integer number of wavelengths; dark fringes where it is a half-integer number.
- The order m labels successive fringes; since sin θ ≤ 1, only a finite number of orders is observable (depends on λ/d).
- Measuring fringe angles yields the wavelength — the basis of early spectroscopy.

## Equations
- Constructive (bright): `d sin θ = m λ`, m = 0, ±1, ±2, …
- Destructive (dark): `d sin θ = (m + ½) λ`, m = 0, ±1, ±2, …
- d = slit separation, θ = angle from incident beam, λ = wavelength
