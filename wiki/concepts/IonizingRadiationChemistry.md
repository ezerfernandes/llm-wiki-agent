# Ionizing Radiation and Biological Effects (Chemistry)

> Disambiguation: this is the OpenStax *Chemistry 2e* (Ch 21) treatment of radiation units and biological effects. For the College Physics 2e treatment see [[IonizingRadiation]].

**Nonionizing radiation** (light, microwaves) merely speeds up atomic/molecular motion (heating). **Ionizing radiation** is energetic enough to knock electrons out of molecules: α and β particles, γ rays, X-rays, and high-energy ultraviolet. It damages tissue two ways:
- **Direct** — breaking chemical bonds or ionizing biomolecules.
- **Indirect** — ionizing water to form hydronium (H₃O⁺) and highly reactive **hydroxyl radicals** that attack DNA, proteins, and enzymes.

Damage is **somatic** (whole body) or **genetic** (eggs/sperm) and is worst in rapidly dividing cells (stomach lining, hair follicles, bone marrow, embryos).

### Penetration vs. ionizing power
Penetration, least to most: **α** (stopped by paper/skin — but dangerous if inhaled or ingested) < **β** (stopped by thin metal) < **neutrons** (stopped by water) < **γ** (very penetrating; needs thick, dense lead). Ionizing power runs the other way: α ≈ 10× β and ≈ 20× γ/X-rays; neutrons ≈ 5× β.

### Units of radiation
| Quantity | SI unit | Traditional unit | Conversion |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Activity** (decay rate) | becquerel (Bq) = 1 decay/s | curie (Ci) | 1 Ci = 3.7 × 10¹⁰ Bq |
| **Absorbed dose** | gray (Gy) = 1 J/kg | rad | 1 Gy = 100 rad |
| **Dose equivalent** | sievert (Sv) | rem | 1 Sv = 100 rem |

The **dose equivalent** weights absorbed dose by the **relative biological effectiveness (RBE)**, a measure of the relative damage of a radiation type: Sv = RBE × Gy and rem = RBE × rad. RBE ≈ 10 for α, ≈ 2+ for protons/neutrons, ≈ 1 for β and γ.

### Exposure and health effects
**Radon-222** (α emitter, t₁/₂ = 3.82 d, from the U-238 chain) is ~8× denser than air, pools in basements (US average ~1.25 pCi/L), needs remediation in ~1 in 6 homes, and is the leading cause of lung cancer in nonsmokers (>20,000 US deaths/yr).

Average US exposure is ~**620 mrem/yr** (~310 from natural background — cosmic rays, radon, ingested K-40/C-14 — plus medical and other). NRC limits: 5,000 mrem/yr (workers), 100 mrem/yr (public); a whole-body CT ≈ 1,000 mrem. Exposure is **cumulative** over a lifetime.

Acute effects of a single large dose: blood-chemistry changes (5–10 rem), nausea (50), hair loss (75), hemorrhage (100), and death (≥400; LD₅₀ ≈ **500 rem**, ~50% death within 30 days). Minimize exposure by **shielding, distance, and limiting duration**.

**Detection:** Geiger (Geiger-Müller) counter (gas ionization), scintillation counter (luminescent scintillator + sensor), and dosimeters (film badge, thermoluminescent, electronic, quartz-fiber) for personal dose.
