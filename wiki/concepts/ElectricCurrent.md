## Definition
**Electric current** is the rate at which [[ElectricCharge|electric charge]] passes through a cross-section of a conductor: I = ΔQ/Δt. Its SI unit is the **ampere** (A), where 1 A = 1 C/s. By convention, **conventional current** points in the direction positive charge would move — the opposite of electron [[DriftVelocity|drift]] in metals.

## Key Points
- Microscopically, current depends on carrier density, charge, area, and drift speed: I = nqAv_d.
- Charge carriers drift slowly (~10⁻⁴ m/s) yet the electrical signal travels near light speed (~10⁸ m/s), because each charge pushes its neighbors almost instantly.
- Charge does not pile up in a conductor — carriers entering force an equal number out, so current is continuous around a circuit.
- Collisions of carriers with atoms transfer energy to the conductor as heat ([[ElectricPower]]).
- Current ratings set fuse, circuit-breaker, and wire-gauge specifications.

## Equations
- I = ΔQ / Δt
- 1 A = 1 C/s
- I = n q A v_d  (n = carrier number density, q = carrier charge, A = cross-sectional area)
