## Definition
Angular magnification M is the ratio of the angle an object subtends when viewed through an optical instrument (θ') to the angle it subtends to the unaided eye (θ). It describes how much larger an object *appears*, which is the relevant quantity for distant objects whose linear image size is not meaningful.

## Key Points
- M = θ'/θ; a value greater than 1 means the object appears larger through the instrument.
- For a telescope, M = − f_o/f_e: a long objective focal length and short eyepiece focal length give the largest magnification (negative sign indicates inversion).
- Angular magnification is distinct from linear magnification m = − d_i/d_o, which compares image and object heights; the two are related through the instrument geometry.
- Magnifying glasses and microscopes also use angular magnification when the final image is viewed by the eye.

## Equations
- M = θ'/θ
- Telescope: M = − f_o/f_e
