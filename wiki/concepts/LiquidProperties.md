# Properties of Liquids

Several bulk properties of liquids are direct consequences of their [[IntermolecularForces|intermolecular forces]]: viscosity, surface tension, and capillary action. This page is the chemistry-domain hub for these; College Physics 2e treats them on the separate [[Viscosity]], [[SurfaceTension]], [[CapillaryAction]], and [[CohesionAndAdhesion]] pages.

## Cohesive vs. Adhesive Forces

- **Cohesive forces:** "The various IMFs between identical molecules of a substance are examples of cohesive forces." They pull a liquid together.
- **Adhesive forces:** "The IMFs of attraction between two different molecules are called adhesive forces" — e.g., between a liquid and a container wall.

The competition between cohesion and adhesion governs wetting and meniscus shape: water wets glass (adhesion > cohesion) forming a concave meniscus; mercury does not (cohesion > adhesion) forming a convex meniscus.

## Viscosity

See [[LiquidViscosity]]. Resistance to flow; increases with IMF strength and molecular complexity, decreases with temperature.

## Surface Tension

See [[LiquidSurfaceTension]]. The energy required to increase a liquid's surface area; surface molecules feel a net inward pull, so liquids minimize surface area (drops are spherical).

## Capillary Action

See [[CapillaryActionChemistry]]. Liquid rising in a narrow tube/porous material, with rise height h = 2T cos θ / (rρg).
