## Definition
Stability describes how a system in equilibrium responds to a small displacement. There are three categories: stable, unstable, and neutral equilibrium, distinguished by whether the resulting net force or [[Torque]] restores, amplifies, or is indifferent to the displacement.

> Note: this is the mechanics concept of equilibrium stability, distinct from numerical/ML stability or data-drift stability indices.

## Key Points
- **Stable equilibrium**: a displacement produces a restoring force/torque that returns the system toward equilibrium (e.g., a marble at the bottom of a bowl, a pencil balanced on its eraser).
- **Unstable equilibrium**: a displacement produces a force/torque in the same direction, driving the system further away (e.g., a pencil balanced on its point).
- **Neutral equilibrium**: the system is indifferent to displacement (e.g., a ball on a flat surface).
- **Stability criterion**: a body is stable only while its [[CenterOfGravity]] remains directly above its **base of support** (the area bounded by its contact points); once the cg passes the boundary, it tips.
- Stability can be increased by widening the base of support, lowering the center of gravity, or using assistive devices.
- Different stable systems tolerate different displacement sizes before becoming unstable (degrees of stability).
- In humans, posture relies on rapid nervous-system control of torques about the hips; a lower cg and wider stance improve balance.

## Equations
- Qualitative: judged by the sign of the net restoring torque after displacement (no single closed-form equation in this chapter).
