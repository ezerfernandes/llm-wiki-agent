# Alkyne

An **alkyne** is an **unsaturated** [[Hydrocarbon|hydrocarbon]] containing one or more **carbon–carbon triple bonds (C≡C)**. The triple bond is **one [[SigmaAndPiBonds|σ bond plus two π bonds]]**; the triply bonded carbons are **sp hybridized** ([[OrbitalHybridization]]) with **180°** bond angles, giving a **linear, rod-like** shape. The simplest alkyne is **ethyne** (acetylene, C₂H₂): H–C≡C–H.

## Nomenclature

Like [[Alkene|alkene]] naming, but the triple bond takes the suffix **"-yne"**. Example: CH₃CH₂C≡CH is **1-butyne**.

## Reactivity

Alkynes are chemically similar to alkenes, but because the C≡C [[OrganicFunctionalGroup|functional group]] has **two π bonds**, alkynes typically react **even more readily** and consume **twice as much reagent** in **[[AdditionReaction|addition reactions]]**:

HC≡CH + 2 Br₂ → Br₂CHCHBr₂  (ethyne + 2 bromine → 1,1,2,2-tetrabromoethane)

Alkynes burn readily — the oxy-acetylene torch exploits acetylene's high heat of combustion.
