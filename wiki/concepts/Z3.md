# Z3

The **first programmable electromechanical binary computer**, completed in 1941 in Berlin by [[KonradZuse|Konrad Zuse]]. Used **electromechanical relays** (not [[VacuumTube|vacuum tubes]]) and operated on **binary floating-point** numbers. Programmed via punched film tape.

## Significance

Per [[dis-5-1-history|*Dive into Systems* Ch 5.1]], the Z3 is named to demonstrate that the 1930s–1940s computing revolution was **not** a single Anglo-American project. Zuse worked in wartime isolation from the [[BletchleyPark]] / [[UniversityOfPennsylvania|Moore School]] / [[HarvardUniversity|Harvard]] efforts, yet converged on the same fundamental design choices: **binary representation**, **programmability**, and **automatic sequential execution** — supporting the section's *convergence-not-linear-progression* thesis.

The original Z3 was destroyed in a 1943 Allied bombing of Berlin. A working replica was built in the 1960s.

Zuse later proved (1998, posthumously confirmed) that the Z3 was **Turing-complete** in principle, although it was not designed with that property in mind.
