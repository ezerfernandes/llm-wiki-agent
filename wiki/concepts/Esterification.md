# Esterification

**Esterification** is the reaction in which a **[[CarboxylicAcidsAndEsters|carboxylic acid]] reacts with an [[AlcoholsAndEthers|alcohol]] to form an ester**, with the elimination of a **water** molecule. The ester functional group (**–CO₂R**) is assembled from the carbonyl/–OH part of the acid and the carbon part of the alcohol.

Example — **ethyl acetate** from acetic acid + ethanol:

CH₃COOH + HOCH₂CH₃ → CH₃CO₂CH₂CH₃ + H₂O

The acid contributes its –C(=O)–O– and the alcohol contributes the –R that replaces the acid's hydroxyl hydrogen; the leaving –OH (from the acid) and –H (from the alcohol) combine as water.

## Why it matters

- Esters lack intermolecular hydrogen bonds, so they are **volatile and fragrant** — esterification underlies the **odors and flavors of fruits, flowers, and perfumes**.
- The same water-eliminating, "condensation" logic recurs in [[AminesAndAmides|amidation]] (acid + amine → amide + water), which links amino acids into proteins, and in ether formation (two alcohols → ether + water).
- **Fats and oils** are esters of glycerine with long-chain fatty acids.
