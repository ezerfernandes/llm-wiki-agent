## Definition
Refraction is the change in direction ("bending") of a light ray as it passes between media in which light travels at different speeds. The amount of bending is governed quantitatively by [[SnellsLaw]] and the [[IndexOfRefraction]].

## Key Points
- Light slows in a denser medium; the change in speed at a boundary bends the ray.
- A ray bends toward the normal when entering a higher-index medium and away from the normal when entering a lower-index one.
- Greater difference in index of refraction produces greater bending (air→diamond bends more than air→water).
- Refraction explains why a fish in a tank can appear displaced, how lenses focus light, prism color separation ([[OpticalDispersion]]), and gemstone sparkle.
- At sufficiently steep angles into a lower-index medium, refraction gives way to [[TotalInternalReflection]].

## Equations
- Snell's law: `n₁ sin θ₁ = n₂ sin θ₂`
- Speed in a medium: `v = c / n`
