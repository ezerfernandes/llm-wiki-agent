# Bonding and Antibonding Molecular Orbitals

In [[MolecularOrbitalTheory|molecular orbital theory]], combining two [[AtomicOrbital|atomic orbitals]] by the **linear combination of atomic orbitals (LCAO)** always yields **two** molecular orbitals: a lower-energy **bonding** orbital and a higher-energy **antibonding** orbital (marked with an asterisk, *). The bonding orbital concentrates electron density between the nuclei (stabilizing); the antibonding orbital has a node between the nuclei (destabilizing). (Named explicitly to keep the chemistry sense distinct.)

## σ and σ* (from s orbitals, or end-to-end p)

Two *s* atomic orbitals combine into:

- **σₛ (bonding)**: in-phase combination, most density directly between the nuclei; electrons here are attracted by both nuclei and are lower in energy than in the isolated atoms.
- **σₛ\* (antibonding)**: out-of-phase combination with a node between the nuclei; electrons sit well away from the internuclear region.

End-to-end overlap of *p* orbitals likewise gives σₚ and σₚ* orbitals.

## π and π* (from side-by-side p orbitals)

Side-by-side *p*-orbital overlap gives:

- **π (bonding)**: electrons interact with both nuclei, holding the atoms together.
- **π\* (antibonding)**: two nodal planes — one along the internuclear axis and one perpendicular between the nuclei.

A diatomic has two perpendicular sets of side-by-side *p* orbitals (*p_y*, *p_z*), so it has **two degenerate** π bonding orbitals (π_py, π_pz, equal energy) and two degenerate π* orbitals.

## Filling and Consequences

Electrons fill bonding orbitals before antibonding ones (Aufbau). The balance of bonding vs. antibonding electrons sets the [[BondOrder|bond order]]: when an antibonding level is only half-filled across **degenerate** orbitals, Hund's rule leaves unpaired electrons — exactly the situation that makes O₂ [[Paramagnetism|paramagnetic]].
