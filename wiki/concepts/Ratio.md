# Ratio (Ratios, Rates, Unit Rate & Unit Price)

A **ratio** compares two numbers or two quantities **measured in the same unit**. It can be written three equivalent ways — "`a` to `b`", `a/b`, or `a:b` — and as a [[Fraction|fraction]] it is simplified by removing common factors, *but* an [[Fraction|improper]] result is **kept as an improper fraction (not rewritten as a [[MixedNumber|mixed number]])** so that both quantities being compared stay visible. OpenStax [[Prealgebra]] 2e (Chapter 5, [[prealgebra-2e-ch05-decimals]], §5.6) introduces ratios and rates.

A **rate** also compares two quantities, but with **different units**; it is written as a fraction with the first quantity in the numerator and the second in the denominator, and the **units are kept** through the simplification (e.g. `240 miles / 8 gallons`).

A **unit rate** is a rate whose **denominator is 1 unit** — found by dividing the numerator by the denominator. "68 miles in 1 hour" → **68 miles/hour** (68 mph); "36 miles per gallon" (36 mpg); "$12.50/hour". A **unit price** is the special unit rate giving the **cost of one item**, found by dividing the total price by the quantity (rounding to the nearest cent as needed); comparing unit prices identifies the **better buy**.

## Writing and simplifying ratios
- **Whole numbers** — write as a fraction, divide out common factors.
- **Decimals** — multiply numerator and denominator by the same power of ten to clear the [[Decimal|decimals]], then simplify (this is the same trick as in decimal [[DecimalArithmetic|division]]).
- **Fractions / mixed numbers** — write the comparison as a [[ComplexFraction|complex fraction]], convert any [[MixedNumber|mixed numbers]] to improper fractions, then divide using the [[Reciprocal|reciprocal]] (see [[FractionArithmetic]]).

Worked example types: simplifying ratios of whole numbers, decimals, and mixed numbers; ratios needing a common unit; interpreting a health/cholesterol ratio; rate (distance/time) problems; computing unit rates (wages, fuel economy); comparing unit prices for the better buy; and translating ratio/rate phrases into expressions. A ratio with denominator 100 is a [[Percent|percent]], and an equation between two ratios is a [[Proportion|proportion]] — both developed in Chapter 6.
