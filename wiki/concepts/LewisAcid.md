# Lewis Acid

A **Lewis acid** is an **electron-pair acceptor** — a species that accepts a lone pair from a [[LewisBase|Lewis base]] to form a [[CoordinateCovalentBond|coordinate covalent bond]] and an acid–base adduct. This is the acid half of the [[LewisAcidsAndBases|Lewis acid–base model]]. (Page named *LewisAcid* to stay within chemistry; the unrelated person pages such as PatrickLewis/LewisTunstall are distinct.)

Typical Lewis acids have an empty or expandable valence orbital:

- **The proton H⁺** — the special case that makes every [[BronstedLowryAcidsAndBases|Brønsted-Lowry]] acid a Lewis acid (e.g. H⁺ + H₂O → H₃O⁺).
- **Electron-deficient molecules** such as BF₃ (only six valence electrons on boron): BF₃ + F⁻ → [BF₄]⁻.
- **Metal cations** such as Ag⁺, Cu⁺, Al³⁺ — central atoms in [[ComplexIon|complex ions]] that accept lone pairs from [[Ligand|ligands]] (Ag⁺ + 2 NH₃ → [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺).
- **Nonmetal oxides** such as SO₂ accepting an oxide ion: SO₂ + O²⁻ → [SO₃]²⁻.
