## Definition
The **order of magnitude** of a quantity is its scale expressed as a power of 10. Two values share the same order of magnitude when they have the same power of 10; differing by an order of magnitude means differing by roughly a factor of 10.

## Key Points
- Provides a fast way to compare quantities spanning enormous ranges — e.g., an atomic diameter (~10⁻¹⁰ m) versus the Sun's diameter (~10⁹ m), about 19 orders of magnitude apart.
- Closely tied to scientific notation and to the metric prefixes of [[SIUnits]].
- Underlies order-of-magnitude [[Approximation]], where a rough power-of-10 estimate is enough to reason about feasibility or sanity-check a result.

## Equations
- A value's order of magnitude is the exponent n in its scientific-notation form a × 10ⁿ (with 1 ≤ a < 10).
