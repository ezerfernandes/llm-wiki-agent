# Colloid

A **colloid** (colloidal dispersion) is a mixture with properties intermediate between a [[SolutionConcentration|solution]] and a suspension. Its particles are larger than simple molecules but small enough that they **do not settle** on standing — unlike a **suspension**, whose relatively large, visible particles make it cloudy and settle out after mixing.

Colloids scatter light (the [[TyndallEffect|Tyndall effect]]), which makes them appear cloudy or opaque (clouds are colloidal water droplets).

## Dispersed Phase and Dispersion Medium
The minor particulate component is the **dispersed phase**; the substance throughout which it is dispersed is the **dispersion medium**. Almost any combination of physical states is possible:

| Dispersed | Medium | Examples | Name |
|---|---|---|---|
| solid | gas | smoke, dust | — |
| solid | liquid | starch in water, inks, paints, milk of magnesia | **sol** |
| solid | solid | colored gems, alloys | — |
| liquid | gas | clouds, fogs, mists, sprays | **aerosol** |
| liquid | liquid | milk, mayonnaise, butter | **emulsion** |
| liquid | solid | jellies, gels, pearl, opal | **gel** |
| gas | liquid | foams, whipped cream, beaten egg whites | **foam** |
| gas | solid | pumice, floating soaps | — |

## Preparation
- **Dispersion methods** — break large particles down (grinding paint pigment in mills).
- **Condensation methods** — grow particles from molecules/ions (clouds from condensing water; a red Fe(OH)₃ sol from FeCl₃ + hot water: Fe³⁺ + 3Cl⁻ + 6H₂O → Fe(OH)₃ + H₃O⁺ + 3Cl⁻).
- Some substances (gelatin, glue, starch, milk powder) are already colloidal-sized and disperse spontaneously in water.

## Emulsions and Emulsifying Agents
An **emulsion** forms by shaking/blending two immiscible liquids into colloidal droplets. The droplets tend to coalesce and separate, so emulsions are stabilized by an **emulsifying agent**: casein protein stabilizes butterfat in water (milk); egg-yolk components stabilize oil in vinegar (mayonnaise); soap stabilizes kerosene in water. Soaps and detergents are **amphiphilic** (a nonpolar hydrocarbon tail plus an ionic head), which lets them suspend grease as colloidal particles.

## Electrical Stabilization and Removal
Colloidal particles are often electrically charged, and all particles in one system carry the **same-sign charge** (e.g., positive Fe(OH)₃ particles), so they repel and stay dispersed. They can be removed by **electrostatic precipitation** — attracting the charged particles to highly charged electrodes (Frederick Cottrell's precipitator cleans industrial smoke).

## Gels
A **gel** is a colloidal dispersion of a liquid throughout a solid: the solid fibers form a 3-D network whose interstices hold the liquid. Examples: gelatin desserts, pectin (jelly-making), silica gel (from dilute HCl + sodium silicate).
