## Definition
A nuclear chain reaction is a self-perpetuating sequence of [[NuclearFission]] events: neutrons released by one fission go on to trigger further fissions. The reaction is self-sustaining when the average number of neutrons that cause a subsequent fission, x, satisfies x > 1.

## Key Points
- **Critical mass** — the minimum amount of fissile material needed for the chain reaction to sustain itself.
- **Criticality** — steady, self-sustaining reaction (x ≈ 1 in a controlled reactor).
- **Supercriticality** — x substantially greater than 1, giving exponential growth of fissions (the basis of weapons; a hazard in reactors).
- Whether neutrons cause new fissions depends on their speed, the fuel enrichment, geometry, and the presence of absorbers or a moderator.

## Equations
- Chain-reaction condition: x > 1 (neutrons per fission that cause further fission)
- Per-fission energy comes from [[NuclearFission]]: E = Δm·c²
