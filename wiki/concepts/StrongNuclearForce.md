## Definition
The **strong nuclear force** is the short-range, attractive interaction that binds [[Nucleon|nucleons]] (protons and neutrons) together in the [[AtomicNucleus|nucleus]], overcoming the electromagnetic ([[CoulombsLaw|Coulomb]]) repulsion between protons. It is one of the four [[FundamentalForces|fundamental forces]].

## Key Points
- Roughly **100× stronger** than the Coulomb force at nuclear distances, but acts only over ~10⁻¹⁵ m (a few femtometers).
- Its short range vs. the long-range Coulomb repulsion shapes nuclear stability: light nuclei bind more tightly as they grow, but in heavy nuclei accumulated proton repulsion eventually wins — the origin of the [[NuclearBindingEnergy|binding-energy-per-nucleon]] curve peaking near iron-56.
- The energy released in [[RadioactiveDecay|nuclear decay]] comes from work done by the strong force as nucleons rearrange.
- The repulsive Coulomb barrier it competes with is what an alpha particle must [[QuantumTunneling|tunnel]] through during [[AlphaDecay|alpha decay]].
- In the [[StandardModel|Standard Model]] it is mediated by **gluons** between [[Quark|quarks]] (quantum chromodynamics); the internucleon binding seen here is its residual effect, historically modeled as pion ([[Meson|meson]]) exchange — the [[CarrierParticle|Yukawa carrier-particle]] picture (see [[FundamentalForces]] and [[ParticlePhysics]]).
- It conserves quark flavor and color, and acts only between particles carrying **color charge**, so it acts on [[Hadron|hadrons]] but not [[Lepton|leptons]].
