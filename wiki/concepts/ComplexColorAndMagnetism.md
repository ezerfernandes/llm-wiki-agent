# Color and Magnetism of Coordination Complexes

Two observable properties of [[CoordinationCompound|coordination compounds]] follow directly from the d-orbital splitting of [[CrystalFieldTheory|crystal field theory]]: **color** (from d–d electronic transitions) and **magnetism** (from the number of unpaired d electrons).

## Color — d–d transitions and complementary color

A complex **absorbs a visible photon** when its energy matches the splitting Δ, promoting an electron from a lower to a higher d set (e.g. t₂g → eg). The eye sees the **complement** of the absorbed color (opposite on the color wheel): red absorbed → green observed; violet absorbed → lemon yellow observed. [Cu(NH₃)₄]²⁺ looks blue because it absorbs orange/red, leaving blue and green.

**Computing Δ from the absorption (E = hν, ν = c/λ):** for [Ti(H₂O)₆]³⁺ with an absorbance maximum at **499 nm**:
- ν = (3.00×10⁸ m/s)/(499×10⁻⁹ m) = **6.01×10¹⁴ Hz**
- Δ_oct = E = (6.63×10⁻³⁴ J·s)(6.01×10¹⁴ Hz) = **3.99×10⁻¹⁹ J/ion**

It absorbs ~450–600 nm (blue–orange), transmits indigo/violet/red, and **appears purple**. A green-looking complex instead absorbs red (620–800 nm).

**Field strength sets the color** (via [[SpectrochemicalSeries]]): strong-field ligands → large Δ → absorb short-wavelength violet/blue → complex appears yellow/orange/red. Weak-field ligands → small Δ → absorb long-wavelength red/orange → complex appears blue-green/blue/indigo.

**d⁰ and d¹⁰ are colorless:** Cu⁺ (d¹⁰) has filled eg; promotion needs UV-energy photons, so no visible light is absorbed ([Cu(CN)₂]⁻ is colorless). **d⁹** (Cu²⁺) has a vacant eg orbital so it absorbs in the visible — Cu²⁺ complexes are almost always colored.

## Magnetism — unpaired electrons

A complex with **unpaired electrons is [[Paramagnetism|paramagnetic]]** (attracted to a magnetic field); one with all electrons paired is **diamagnetic** (slightly repelled). The **magnetic moment grows with the number of unpaired electrons**. Because high-spin vs low-spin (set by Δ vs pairing energy P) fixes that count, the same metal can be either: low-spin **[Fe(CN)₆]⁴⁻ is diamagnetic** while high-spin **[Fe(H₂O)₆]²⁺ is paramagnetic with four unpaired electrons**.
