## Definition
The **electron volt** (eV) is a unit of energy equal to the [[ElectricPotentialEnergy|electric potential energy]] gained by a single fundamental [[ElectricCharge|charge]] (e = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ C) accelerated through a [[PotentialDifference|potential difference]] of 1 [[Voltage|volt]]: 1 eV = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ J.

## Key Points
- It is the natural energy scale for **submicroscopic** processes — atomic, molecular, and [[ParticlePhysics|particle physics]] — where the joule is inconveniently large. Particle masses are quoted in MeV/c² or GeV/c²; [[ParticleAccelerator|accelerators]] reach GeV–TeV beam energies (a proton gains PE = qV per gap), and via m = ΔE/c² that energy can become new matter.
- Follows directly from ΔPE = qΔV with q = e and ΔV = 1 V.
- Used to judge whether a particle has enough energy to ionize atoms or break molecular bonds (relevant to radiation damage in biology).

## Equations
- 1 eV = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ J
- ΔPE = q ΔV  (with q = e, ΔV = 1 V ⇒ 1 eV)
