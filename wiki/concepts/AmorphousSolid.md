# Amorphous Solid

An **amorphous (noncrystalline) solid**, or **glass**, is one whose particles "lack an ordered internal structure and are randomly arranged." It forms when a liquid freezes before its molecules can settle into an ordered pattern — common for substances with large molecules or restricted molecular movement, such as candle waxes. Some materials, like silicon dioxide, can form either a crystalline or an amorphous solid depending on conditions, and an amorphous solid may crystallize under appropriate conditions.

## Melting Behavior

Unlike a [[CrystallineSolid|crystalline solid]] (which has a sharp melting point because all its attractions are of equal strength), an amorphous solid **softens gradually over a temperature range**: its structural nonequivalence means some attractions are weaker than others, so the weakest break first as temperature rises, followed by progressively stronger ones.
