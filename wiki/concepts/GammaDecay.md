## Definition
**Gamma decay** is a [[RadioactiveDecay|radioactive decay]] mode in which an excited nucleus (denoted X*) drops to a lower energy state by emitting one or more high-energy photons ([[GammaRay|gamma rays]]). Unlike alpha or beta decay, it does **not** change the element or isotope — only the nuclear energy state.

## Key Points
- Nuclear excited states are very short-lived (~10⁻¹⁴ s), reflecting the strength of the [[StrongNuclearForce|nuclear force]] returning nucleons to lower states.
- Gamma emission often **follows** alpha or beta decay, when the daughter nucleus is left in an excited state.
- All conservation laws hold; the released energy appears as photon energy rather than particle kinetic energy.
- The emitted [[GammaRay|gamma rays]] are the most penetrating nuclear radiation and are used in cancer therapy and imaging.

## Equations
- General: $^{A}_{Z}X^{*}_{N} \rightarrow\, ^{A}_{Z}X_{N} + \gamma_1 + \gamma_2 + \ldots$
- Example: ⁶⁰Ni* → ⁶⁰Ni + γ₁ + γ₂ (gammas used in cancer treatment)
