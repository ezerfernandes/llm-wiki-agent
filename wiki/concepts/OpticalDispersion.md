## Definition
Optical dispersion is the spreading of white light into its full spectrum of colors because the [[IndexOfRefraction]] of a material varies with wavelength. More generally, any wave disperses when its propagation speed depends on wavelength.

(Distinct from statistical dispersion and from [[LondonDispersionForces]] in chemistry — this is the optics phenomenon.)

## Key Points
- White light contains all visible wavelengths, violet (~400 nm) to red (~700 nm).
- Because `n` increases as wavelength decreases, violet bends more than red.
- A **prism** separates white light into a sequential spectrum (red through violet).
- A **rainbow** forms in water droplets via refraction in, reflection off the back, and refraction out; the back reflection is not wavelength-dependent, but the two refractions are, so colors emerge at different angles.
- Trade-off in communications: lasers emit nearly single wavelengths and disperse little; white light is undesirable in fiber because dispersion spreads signals in time.
- Dispersion of light from space can reveal information about intervening matter.

## Equations
- Wavelength dependence: `n = n(λ)` (no single closed form; drives wavelength-dependent [[SnellsLaw]] bending)
