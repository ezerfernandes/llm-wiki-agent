## Definition
**Radiometric (radioactive) dating** estimates the age of a specimen from the remaining abundance of a naturally occurring unstable [[Isotope|isotope]], using the known [[HalfLife|half-life]] and the exponential decay law N = N₀e^(−λt).

## Key Points
- **Carbon-14 dating:** atmospheric ¹⁴C is taken up by living organisms (living tissue holds ¹⁴C/C ≈ 1.3 × 10⁻¹²). At death uptake stops while existing ¹⁴C keeps decaying; comparing current to expected abundance gives the age. Reliable to roughly **50,000–60,000 years**.
- **Geological dating:** longer-lived isotopes date older material — e.g., uranium-238 (t₁/₂ = 4.5 × 10⁹ yr) dates ancient rocks.
- Worked-example contexts: Shroud of Turin (~92% of expected ¹⁴C remaining), ¹⁴C activity of living tissue, and back-calculating released ¹³⁷Cs mass after Chernobyl from measured activity.

## Equations
- N = N₀e^(−λt) (solve for t)
- λ = 0.693/t₁/₂
