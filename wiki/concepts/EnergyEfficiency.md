## Definition
**Energy efficiency** is the fraction of input energy that emerges as useful output work or energy: Eff = W_out / E_in, often expressed as a percentage. Although total energy is always conserved ([[ConservationOfEnergy]]), real conversions degrade much of it into waste heat, so efficiency is below 100%.

## Key Points
- Quantifies how much input energy does useful work versus how much is lost (mostly as thermal energy).
- Representative values: electric motors ~98%, gas heaters ~90%, diesel engines ~35%, solar cells ~10%, human cycling ~20%.
- Coal-fired plants convert only ~42% of chemical energy to electricity; the rest is waste heat.
- Improving efficiency is a primary lever for energy conservation (see [[EnergyResources]]).

## Equations
- Efficiency = (useful work/energy output) / (total energy input) = W_out / E_in
