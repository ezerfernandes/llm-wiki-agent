## Definition
Geometric optics is the branch of optics that treats light as straight-line **rays**. It is valid when light interacts with objects that are several times larger than its wavelength, so that wave effects (interference, diffraction) are negligible. The complementary regime is wave optics.

## Key Points
- A **ray** is a straight line from a point along which light travels.
- The ray model applies when object size >> wavelength (roughly, features above ~1 micrometer for visible light).
- Light travels in straight lines until it meets matter, where it reflects or refracts, then continues straight.
- Two governing laws: the law of reflection ([[LightReflection]]) and the law of refraction ([[SnellsLaw]]).
- Light reaches us three ways: directly through space, through a medium, or by reflection.
- Underpins lenses, mirrors, fiber optics, and image formation.
