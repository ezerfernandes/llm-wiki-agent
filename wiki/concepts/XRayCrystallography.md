# X-Ray Crystallography and Bragg's Law

**X-ray crystallography** (X-ray diffraction) determines the spacing between planes of atoms in a [[CrystallineSolid|crystal]]. X-rays have wavelengths of a few ångströms — comparable to interatomic distances — so they are diffracted by the regularly spaced atoms of a crystal; the scattered waves interfere, and the pattern reveals the structure.

## Bragg Equation

Constructive interference occurs when the path-length difference between waves scattered from adjacent planes is an integer multiple of the wavelength:

$$n\lambda = 2d\sin\theta$$

- *n* — order of diffraction (an integer)
- *λ* — X-ray wavelength
- *d* — distance between diffracting (lattice) planes
- *θ* — angle of the diffracted beam

A **diffractometer** measures the angles θ at which diffraction occurs; the Bragg equation then yields the interplanar spacings *d* and hence the [[UnitCell|unit-cell]] dimensions and interatomic distances.

**Example:** with λ = 0.1315 nm, n = 1, and θ = 25.25°, d = nλ / (2 sin θ) = 0.154 nm.

## Historical Note

British chemist **Rosalind Franklin** produced the X-ray diffraction images that supplied essential evidence for DNA's double-helix structure and dimensions; she was not included in the 1962 Nobel Prize awarded to Crick, Watson, and Wilkins.
