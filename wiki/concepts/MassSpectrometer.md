## Definition
A mass spectrometer measures the masses of charged particles (ions) by analyzing the curvature of their paths in a [[MagneticField]]. It typically uses a [[VelocitySelector]] to fix the particle speed, then a uniform field to bend the beam into a circular arc.

## Key Points
- A charge moving perpendicular to a uniform field follows a circle of radius r = mv/(qB) (the [[LorentzForce]] supplies the centripetal force).
- With v, q, and B fixed, the radius is proportional to mass, so ions separate by mass-to-charge ratio.
- Used in chemistry and biology to identify substances, analyze proteins and viruses, and measure isotope concentrations.

## Equations
- Path radius: r = mv / (qB)
- Velocity selection (entry stage): v = E / B (see [[VelocitySelector]])
