## Definition
**Mechanical energy** is the sum of a system's [[KineticEnergy]] and [[PotentialEnergy]] (gravitational, elastic, etc.). When only [[ConservativeForce|conservative forces]] act, mechanical energy is conserved — it can shift freely between kinetic and potential forms but its total stays fixed.

## Key Points
- Conserved under conservative forces: KE_i + PE_i = KE_f + PE_f.
- A [[NonconservativeForce]] (e.g., friction) changes mechanical energy by exactly W_nc, usually draining it to thermal energy.
- A roller coaster trading height for speed, or a spring launching a toy, are textbook KE ↔ PE exchanges.
- Mechanical energy is a subset of total energy governed by the broader [[ConservationOfEnergy]] law.

## Equations
- E_mech = KE + PE
- KE_i + PE_i = KE_f + PE_f  (conservative forces only)
- KE_i + PE_i + W_nc = KE_f + PE_f  (with nonconservative work)
