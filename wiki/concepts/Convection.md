## Definition
**Convection** is [[HeatTransfer|heat transfer]] by the macroscopic (bulk) movement of a fluid. Unlike
[[HeatConduction|conduction]], matter physically moves and carries thermal energy from one place to
another.

## Key Points
- **Natural convection** is driven by buoyancy: warmer fluid is less dense and rises while cooler, denser
  fluid sinks, forming circulation loops with no external pump.
- Bulk fluid motion transfers energy much faster than conduction alone — the basis of the **wind-chill
  factor** (the equivalent still-air temperature giving the same cooling rate as moving air).
- Convection is dramatically amplified when coupled with a [[LatentHeat|phase change]]: rising water
  vapor condenses in cold regions, releasing latent heat that drives further ascent — a positive feedback
  powering storms and severe weather.
- **Air infiltration** (outside air leaking through gaps around windows/doors) is a major convective heat
  loss in buildings.
- Applications: gravity furnaces distributing warm air; human thermoregulation (vasodilation moves warm
  blood to the skin; sweating plus air movement gives evaporative cooling); ocean-to-atmosphere energy
  transfer; forced convection in engine/vehicle cooling.

## Equations
- Q = m c ΔT   (sensible heat carried by a moving fluid mass)
- m/t = Q / (t · Lv)   (rate of evaporative mass loss / cooling)
