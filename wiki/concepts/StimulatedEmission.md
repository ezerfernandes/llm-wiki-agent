# Stimulated Emission

**Stimulated emission** is the process in which an incoming [[PhotonQuantum|photon]] of the right energy triggers an already-excited atom to drop to a lower level and emit a second photon that is identical to the first — same frequency, phase, direction, and polarization. Einstein recognized in 1917 that this is the basis for coherent light amplification.

## Key Points
- Contrasts with **spontaneous emission**, where an excited atom de-excites at random with no triggering photon, producing incoherent light.
- The triggered photon is a clone of the incident one, so a single photon can cascade into many identical photons — optical amplification.
- Einstein showed the probabilities of stimulated emission and of absorption are equal. Net amplification therefore requires a **population inversion**: more atoms in the upper (often [[Laser|metastable]]) state than in the lower state.
- It is the core mechanism of the [[Laser|laser]] (Light Amplification by Stimulated Emission of Radiation).

## Equations
- The emitted photon energy equals the level gap: E_photon = hf = E_upper − E_lower (so closely matched levels give longer wavelengths, e.g. the He-Ne 633 nm red line).
