# Lead-Acid Battery

The **lead-acid battery** is a rechargeable (**secondary**) [[ElectrochemicalBattery|battery]] using lead electrodes in **dilute sulfuric acid (H₂SO₄)** electrolyte. It is the standard automobile battery: six cells in series give 12 V.

## Reactions (~2 V per cell)
- **Anode:** Pb(s) + HSO₄⁻(aq) → PbSO₄(s) + H⁺(aq) + 2e⁻
- **Cathode:** PbO₂(s) + HSO₄⁻(aq) + 3H⁺(aq) + 2e⁻ → PbSO₄(s) + 2H₂O(l)
- **Overall:** Pb(s) + PbO₂(s) + 2H₂SO₄(aq) → 2PbSO₄(s) + 2H₂O(l)

Discharge deposits PbSO₄ on both electrodes; charging reverses the reaction.

## Trade-offs
Inexpensive and capable of the **high current** required by starter motors (high current density). Drawbacks: heavy, contains a caustic liquid electrolyte, and the substantial lead content requires proper disposal.
