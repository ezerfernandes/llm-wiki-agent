# Chemical Compound

A **chemical compound** is a pure substance composed of two or more [[ChemicalElement|elements]] chemically combined. Unlike an element, a compound *can* be broken down by chemical changes into its constituent elements or into simpler compounds. Examples:
- Mercury(II) oxide → mercury + oxygen (by heat)
- Sucrose → carbon + water (caramelization, by heat)
- Silver(I) chloride → silver + chlorine (by light; basis of photographic film and photochromic eyeglasses)

A key idea is that **the properties of a compound differ from those of its constituent elements.** Soft, shiny metallic sodium and toxic yellow-green chlorine gas combine into ordinary white crystalline sodium chloride (table salt).

Compounds are one of the two kinds of pure substance in [[MatterClassification]]; their smallest units are typically [[Molecule|molecules]].

## Composition Laws and Types (Ch 2)

[[AtomicTheory|Dalton's atomic theory]] explains why compounds obey the [[LawOfDefiniteProportions|law of definite proportions]] (a pure compound always contains its elements in the same mass ratio) and the [[LawOfMultipleProportions|law of multiple proportions]]. A compound's composition is recorded as a [[ChemicalFormula|chemical formula]]. Compounds fall into two structural classes: [[IonicCompound|ionic compounds]] (a metal + nonmetal, held together by ionic bonds, charge-balanced and electrically neutral) and [[MolecularCompound|molecular/covalent compounds]] (nonmetals sharing electrons). Systematic names follow [[ChemicalNomenclature|chemical nomenclature]] rules.
