# Balancing Chemical Equations

A [[ChemicalEquation|chemical equation]] is **balanced** when it satisfies the [[ConservationOfMatter|law of conservation of matter]]: each element must appear in **equal numbers of atoms** on the reactant and product sides. Balance is achieved only by adjusting **coefficients** — never subscripts.

## Counting Atoms

For each element, multiply that formula's coefficient by the element's subscript, then sum across each side. The equation balances when the per-element totals match.

Verification for CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O:

| Element | Reactants | Products | Balanced? |
|---|---|---|---|
| C | 1×1 = 1 | 1×1 = 1 | yes |
| H | 4×1 = 4 | 2×2 = 4 | yes |
| O | 2×2 = 4 | (1×2)+(2×1) = 4 | yes |

## Balancing by Inspection (step by step)

Decompose water, H₂O → H₂ + O₂ (O unbalanced: 1 vs 2):
1. Change H₂O coefficient to 2: `2H₂O → H₂ + O₂` (now O balances, H does not: 4 vs 2).
2. Change H₂ coefficient to 2: **`2H₂O → 2H₂ + O₂`** (H: 4=4, O: 2=2 — balanced).

## Key Rules

- **Only coefficients may change.** Altering a subscript changes the substance's identity (e.g. H₂O → H₂O₂ turns water into hydrogen peroxide).
- **Fractional coefficients** are allowed as an intermediate step; clear them by multiplying every coefficient by a whole number. Combustion of ethane: `C₂H₆ + (7/2)O₂ → 3H₂O + 2CO₂`, then ×2 → **`2C₂H₆ + 7O₂ → 6H₂O + 4CO₂`**.
- **Smallest whole-number coefficients** are the convention: write `N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃`, not `3N₂ + 9H₂ → 6NH₃` (divide out the GCF).

Balancing redox equations in solution often uses the dedicated half-reaction method — see [[OxidationReduction]].
