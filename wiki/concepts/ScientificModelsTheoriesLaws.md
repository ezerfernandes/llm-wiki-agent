## Definition
A set of related but distinct terms physicists use to describe scientific knowledge with precision. They differ in scope, certainty, and how they are expressed.

## Key Points
- **Model** — a mental or simplified representation of phenomena too small or complex to observe directly. Justified by experimental evidence but limited in scope (e.g., the planetary atomic model of electrons orbiting a nucleus).
- **Theory** — a testable explanation for patterns in nature, supported by scientific evidence and verified by multiple independent researchers. More complex than a law; it describes whole groups of related phenomena and must be experimentally verifiable.
- **Law** — a concise, generalized statement of a natural pattern, supported by evidence and often expressed mathematically. It describes a single action or relationship rather than a complex system (e.g., Newton's Second Law).
- **Principle** — narrower in applicability than a law (e.g., Pascal's principle applies only within fluids).
- These products of inquiry are reached through the [[ScientificMethod]] and are central to how [[Physics]] organizes knowledge.

## Equations
- Example of a law expressed mathematically: F = ma (Newton's Second Law).
