## Definition
Newton's universal law of gravitation states that every particle of mass attracts every other particle with a force proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between their centers of mass. The force acts along the line joining the centers and is always attractive.

## Key Points
- The same force that gives a body its surface weight (w = mg, see [[MassVersusWeight]]) also holds the Moon in orbit and binds all celestial orbits — it supplies the [[CentripetalForce]] for orbital motion.
- The gravitational constant G = 6.674 × 10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg² is universal.
- [[HenryCavendish]] first measured G in 1798 with a torsion balance, which then allowed Earth's mass to be computed from M = gr²/G.
- Gravity depends only on mass, not on material composition (verified to parts-per-billion by Eötvös).
- Extended bodies attract as if their mass were concentrated at their [[GravitationalField|center of mass]].
- Apparent weightlessness in orbit is continuous free fall, not the absence of gravity.

## Equations
- F = G m M / r²
- g = G M / r²  (gravitational field strength / acceleration due to gravity)
- M = g r² / G  (e.g., Earth's mass from surface g and radius)
