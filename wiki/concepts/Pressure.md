## Definition
Pressure is the perpendicular [[Force]] exerted on a surface divided by the area of that surface: P = F/A. It measures how concentrated a force is — the same force produces high pressure over a small area and low pressure over a large area. Pressure is a **scalar**, even though the forces it produces have direction.

## Key Points
- SI unit is the **pascal (Pa)** = 1 N/m². Other units: atmosphere (atm), millibar (mb; 100 mb = 10⁴ Pa), psi, and mm Hg (1.0 mm Hg = 133 Pa).
- In a static fluid, pressure acts **perpendicular** to every contacted surface and is exerted **equally in all directions**, because a fluid cannot sustain a shearing force.
- High pressure across even a small area can generate very large forces (e.g., a pressurized tank's end cap), which is why pressure vessels need robust construction.
- See [[FluidPressure]] for how pressure grows with depth, and [[GaugePressure]] for measuring it relative to the atmosphere.

## Equations
- P = F / A
- F = PA
- 1 Pa = 1 N/m²; 1 atm = 1.01×10⁵ Pa ≈ 101 kPa; 1.0 mm Hg = 133 Pa
