## Definition
The **work-energy theorem** states that the net [[Work]] done on a system equals its change in [[KineticEnergy]]: W_net = ½mv² − ½mv₀². It holds universally, regardless of the number, type, or direction of the forces involved, and is derived by combining the definition of work with kinematics ([[NewtonsSecondLaw]]).

## Key Points
- Net work is the sum of work by all forces, equivalently the net force times displacement.
- Lets you find a final speed, a stopping distance, or a net force without tracking every force separately.
- Energy delivered by work may be stored (lifting against gravity) or dissipated (friction → heat).
- The basis for solving motion problems via energy rather than vector force balance.

## Equations
- W_net = ½ m v² − ½ m v₀²
- W_net = F_net · d · cos θ
- W_net = m a d  (kinematic derivation)
