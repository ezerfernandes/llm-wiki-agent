# Graham's Law (of Effusion)

**Graham's law** states that the rate of [[Effusion|effusion]] of a gas is **inversely proportional to the square root of its molar mass**:

$$\text{rate of effusion} \propto \frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathcal{M}}}$$

For two gases A and B at the same temperature and pressure, the ratio of their effusion rates is:

$$\frac{\text{rate}_A}{\text{rate}_B} = \sqrt{\frac{\mathcal{M}_B}{\mathcal{M}_A}}$$

Lighter gases effuse (and [[GaseousDiffusion|diffuse]]) faster than heavier ones.

## Worked Examples

- **Rate ratio:** rate(H₂)/rate(O₂) = √(32/2) = √16 = **4** — hydrogen effuses four times as fast as oxygen.
- **Effusion time** (equal amounts → time ∝ 1/rate): time(Ne)/time(Xe) = √(ℳ_Ne/ℳ_Xe) = √(20.2/131.3) = 0.392, so if Xe takes 243 s, Ne takes 0.392 × 243 = **95.3 s**.
- **Finding molar mass:** an unknown that effuses 1.66× faster than CO₂ → 1.66 = √(44.0/ℳ) → ℳ = 44.0/1.66² = **16.0 g/mol** (likely CH₄).

## Derivation from Kinetic-Molecular Theory

Since effusion rate ∝ [[RootMeanSquareSpeed|u_rms]] and $u_{rms} = \sqrt{3RT/\mathcal{M}}$, at equal temperature:

$$\frac{\text{rate}_A}{\text{rate}_B} = \frac{u_{rms,A}}{u_{rms,B}} = \sqrt{\frac{\mathcal{M}_B}{\mathcal{M}_A}}$$

confirming the experimentally observed law (see [[KineticMolecularTheory]]). The application to separating ²³⁵UF₆ from ²³⁸UF₆ in uranium enrichment is covered under [[GaseousDiffusion]].
