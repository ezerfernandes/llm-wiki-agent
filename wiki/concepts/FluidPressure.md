## Definition
In a static fluid, the [[Pressure]] at a given depth arises from the weight of the fluid lying above that point. It increases linearly with depth according to **P = hρg**, where h is the depth, ρ the fluid [[Density]], and g the acceleration due to gravity. The pressure depends only on depth and density — not on the shape or total size of the container.

## Key Points
- The relation is exact for nearly incompressible liquids to large depths. For compressible gases it holds only over depths where density barely changes; otherwise an **average density** ρ̄ is used.
- Standard atmospheric pressure (1 atm = 1.01×10⁵ Pa) equals the pressure of only ~10.3 m of water, because water is ~775× denser than air.
- Air density drops sharply with altitude, so atmospheric pressure changes little during a short climb while water pressure rises quickly with depth.
- Explains dam design (thickness must increase with depth) and physiological pressure differences (blood pressure in the feet exceeds that at the heart).
- Builds on [[MassVersusWeight]]: pressure comes from fluid weight, w = mg.

## Equations
- P = hρg  (gauge pressure from a fluid column of depth h)
- P̄ = h̄ρg  (using average depth/density)
- F = P̄A  (force on a submerged surface)
- ρ̄ = P / (hg)  (solving for density)
- ΔP = Δh ρ g  (pressure difference between two depths)
