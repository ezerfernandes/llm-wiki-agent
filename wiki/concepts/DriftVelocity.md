## Definition
**Drift velocity** (v_d) is the average velocity that free charge carriers acquire in a conductor under an applied [[ElectricField]]. It is superimposed on their fast, random thermal motion and is typically tiny — on the order of 10⁻⁴ m/s — far slower than the electrical signal it produces.

## Key Points
- Drift velocity links the microscopic picture to measurable [[ElectricCurrent]]: I = nqAv_d.
- Despite the slow drift, signals propagate at ~10⁸ m/s because the field rearranges all carriers nearly simultaneously — like water already filling a hose moving the instant the tap opens.
- For electrons in a metal, v_d is opposite to the conventional current direction (negative sign).
- A larger carrier density n means a smaller drift velocity is needed for the same current.

## Equations
- I = n q A v_d
- v_d = I / (n q A)
