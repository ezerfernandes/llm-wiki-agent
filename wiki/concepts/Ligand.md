# Ligand

A **ligand** is a molecule or ion that **donates an electron pair to a central metal atom** in a [[ComplexIon|complex ion]] (coordination complex). Ligands are therefore [[LewisBase|Lewis bases]], and the metal–ligand bonds are [[CoordinateCovalentBond|coordinate covalent bonds]].

Ligands may be neutral molecules with a lone pair (H₂O, NH₃) or anions (CN⁻, OH⁻, Cl⁻, S₂O₃²⁻). In the chapter's examples, NH₃ ligands bind Ag⁺ to form [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺, CN⁻ binds Cu⁺ to form Cu(CN)₂⁻, OH⁻ binds Al³⁺ to form Al(OH)₄⁻, and thiosulfate binds Ag⁺ to form Ag(S₂O₃)₂³⁻.

The strength with which ligands bind a metal center is captured by the [[FormationConstant|formation constant Kf]] of the resulting complex.

## Denticity (Ch 19)

A ligand's **denticity** is the number of **donor atoms** it uses to bind the metal — this determines the [[CoordinationCompound|coordination number]] alongside how many ligands are present:
- **Monodentate** — one donor atom (NH₃, Cl⁻, H₂O, CN⁻).
- **Polydentate / chelating** — multiple donor atoms, forming a stable chelate ring (the [[ChelateEffect|chelate effect]]): **bidentate** ethylenediamine (en, H₂NCH₂CH₂NH₂), **tetradentate** porphyrin/heme (see [[Hemoglobin]]), **hexadentate** EDTA.

A few ligands are **ambidentate** — able to bind through either of two atoms (SCN⁻ via S or N; CN⁻ via C or N), causing **linkage isomerism** (see [[CoordinationIsomerism]]). Strong- vs weak-field ligands are ranked by the [[SpectrochemicalSeries|spectrochemical series]].
