## Definition
The normal force (N) is the contact force a surface exerts on an object resting against it, directed perpendicular ("normal") to the surface. For a stationary object on a horizontal support, the normal force pushes up with a magnitude equal to the object's weight.

## Key Points
- "Normal" means perpendicular, not "usual."
- It is a restoring/contact force that prevents objects from passing through surfaces; it is electromagnetic in origin (see [[FundamentalForces]]).
- On an incline, the normal force balances the perpendicular component of weight, w⊥ = mg cos θ.
- A scale reads the normal force it exerts, which equals true weight only when there is no vertical acceleration (apparent weight in an accelerating elevator differs).

## Equations
- Flat horizontal surface (no vertical acceleration): N = mg
- On an incline at angle θ: N = mg cos θ
- Perpendicular weight component: w⊥ = mg cos θ
