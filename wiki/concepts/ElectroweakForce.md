## Definition
The **electroweak force** is the unification of the **electromagnetic** and **weak nuclear** forces into a single interaction at high energy. The gauge theory describing it (**electroweak theory**) was developed in the 1960s by Weinberg, Glashow, and Salam.

## Key Points
- At energies above ~100 GeV (distances ~10⁻¹⁸ m) the electromagnetic and weak forces become identical; at ordinary energies they look distinct because of **symmetry breaking**.
- The theory predicted the masses of the weak [[CarrierParticle|carrier particles]] — W ≈ 81 GeV/c², Z⁰ ≈ 90 GeV/c² — later confirmed at CERN in 1983 (Rubbia and van der Meer, Nobel 1984).
- The **Higgs boson** is the particle associated with electroweak symmetry breaking; it gives the W and Z their large mass while the [[PhotonQuantum|photon]] stays massless. The LHC found tentative Higgs evidence at ~125 GeV.
- Electroweak theory is half of the [[StandardModel]] (the other half being QCD for the strong force); unifying it further with the strong force is the goal of [[GrandUnifiedTheory|GUTs]].

## Equations
- W mass ≈ 81 GeV/c²; Z⁰ mass ≈ 90 GeV/c²
- Unification energy ≈ 100 GeV ↔ ~10⁻¹⁸ m
