## Definition
The **energy stored in a capacitor** is the [[ElectricPotentialEnergy|electric potential energy]] accumulated as [[ElectricCharge|charge]] builds up on the plates of a [[Capacitor]] under an applied [[Voltage]]. It can be expressed equivalently in terms of charge Q, voltage V, and [[Capacitance]] C.

## Key Points
- During charging, the capacitor's voltage rises from 0 to its final value V; the **average** voltage seen by the total charge is V/2 — the origin of the ½ factor.
- The three forms are interchangeable via Q = CV.
- Capacitors can release stored energy very quickly, enabling high-power bursts (defibrillators, camera flashes) that a steady source cannot easily supply.

## Equations
- E_cap = QV/2 = CV²/2 = Q²/(2C)

## Applications
- Defibrillators delivering precise therapeutic energy doses
- Camera flash energy storage
- Calculator memory backup during battery changes
