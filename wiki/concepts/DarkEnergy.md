## Definition
Dark energy is the unknown form of energy invoked to explain the observed acceleration of the universe's expansion. It is often associated with Einstein's **cosmological constant**, a term in [[GeneralRelativity]] that acts as a repulsive, large-scale influence opposing gravity.

## Key Points
- Recent observations indicate that cosmic expansion ([[HubblesLaw]]) is speeding up rather than slowing, the opposite of what ordinary gravity alone would produce.
- Einstein originally introduced the cosmological constant to allow a static universe, then discarded it; it has been revived to account for accelerating expansion.
- Together with [[DarkMatter]], dark energy dominates the universe's total energy budget, leaving ordinary luminous matter as a tiny fraction.
- Its magnitude helps set whether the total density reaches the critical value, and thus the geometry and fate of the universe (open, closed, or flat). Current evidence favors near-flatness with eternal, accelerating expansion.
- The accelerating phase has been compared to a renewed, milder inflationary epoch.

## Equations
- Critical density (reference scale): ρ_c ≈ 10⁻²⁶ kg/m³
- Cosmological constant Λ enters the general-relativistic field equations as a repulsive term.
