## Definition
Hooke's law states that, for small deformations, the force applied to an object is directly proportional to the resulting deformation: F = k ΔL. It describes the linear (elastic) region of a material's response and underlies [[Elasticity]]. When written as a restoring force, F = -kx, the minus sign shows the force opposes the displacement, driving the object back toward equilibrium — the foundation of [[SimpleHarmonicMotion]] and [[Oscillation]].

## Key Points
- The force constant k depends on the material, the object's shape, and the direction of the applied force.
- Valid only for small displacements; beyond the elastic limit the proportionality breaks down.
- Recast in geometry-independent form as a [[Stress]]-[[Strain]] relation: stress = Y × strain, where Y is Young's modulus.
- The force constant relates to Young's modulus and geometry by k = Y A / L₀.
- A Hooke's-law restoring force gives rise to oscillation; a system whose net force is F = -kx (without dissipation) undergoes [[SimpleHarmonicMotion]].
- Deforming an elastic system stores elastic potential energy PE_el = ½ k x², which converts to [[KineticEnergy]] on release (see [[ElasticPotentialEnergy]]).

## Equations
- F = k ΔL  (and ΔL = F / k)
- Restoring-force form: F = -kx
- k = Y A / L₀
- Stress-strain form: F/A = Y (ΔL/L₀)
- Elastic potential energy: PE_el = ½ k x²
