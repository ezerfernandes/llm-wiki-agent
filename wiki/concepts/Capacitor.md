## Definition
A **capacitor** is a device that stores [[ElectricCharge|electric charge]], made of two conductors separated by an insulator. It stays net-neutral overall but holds separated +Q and −Q on its plates, producing an [[ElectricField]] and storing [[EnergyStoredInCapacitors|energy]] between them. Its charge-per-voltage is its [[Capacitance]].

## Key Points
- A **parallel-plate** capacitor is the canonical model: larger plate area and smaller separation store more charge; a [[Dielectric|dielectric]] between the plates raises capacitance and breakdown [[Voltage]].
- Field and voltage between plates: E = V/d; the maximum voltage is set by the dielectric strength (air ≈ 3 × 10⁶ V/m).
- Combinations: **series** divides voltage and lowers total capacitance; **parallel** shares voltage and raises it (like adding plate area).
- Charges build gradually during charging, so the energy stored carries a factor of ½ (see [[EnergyStoredInCapacitors]]).

## Equations
- Q = C V
- Parallel plate: C = κ ε₀ A / d
- E = V / d ; V_max = E_breakdown · d
- Series: 1/C_S = Σ 1/Cᵢ ; Parallel: C_P = Σ Cᵢ
