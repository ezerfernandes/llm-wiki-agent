## Definition
**Osmosis** is the transport of water across a semipermeable membrane from a region of high water concentration to one of low water concentration (i.e., toward higher solute concentration). A **semipermeable membrane** selectively passes some molecules but not others.

## Key Points
- As water crosses, a hydrostatic **back pressure** builds and eventually halts net flow; the pressure needed to stop osmosis is the **(relative) osmotic pressure**.
- Distinguished from **dialysis** (transport of solute molecules down their gradient) and **active transport** (energy-using movement *against* a gradient).
- Driven by the same statistical tendency as [[MolecularDiffusion|diffusion]] — random motion equalizing concentrations.
- Back pressure can be expressed as a fluid column: ρgh (see [[FluidPressure]]).
- Applications: kidney function (osmosis + dialysis + active transport), Epsom-salt soaks drawing fluid from swollen tissue, plant turgor pressure, and reverse-osmosis water desalination (forcing water the "wrong" way through a membrane to leave salt behind).

## Equations
- Back/osmotic pressure (column): ρgh = relative osmotic pressure
