# Heterogeneous Equilibrium

A **heterogeneous equilibrium** involves reactants and products in **two or more different phases**. Examples:

$$\mathrm{PbCl_2(s) \rightleftharpoons Pb^{2+}(aq) + 2Cl^-(aq)}$$
$$\mathrm{CaO(s) + CO_2(g) \rightleftharpoons CaCO_3(s)}$$

## Pure solids and liquids are left out of K

The defining feature: **pure solids and pure liquids do not appear** in the [[ReactionQuotient|reaction quotient]] or [[EquilibriumConstant|equilibrium constant]] expression. Their *relative* concentrations (activities) are equal to 1 because the "concentration" of a pure condensed phase is fixed by its density and does not change as the reaction proceeds. Consequently, Q and K include terms **only for gaseous and solute (aqueous) species**.

For the examples above:

$$K_c = [\mathrm{Pb^{2+}}][\mathrm{Cl^-}]^2 \qquad K_p = \frac{1}{P_{\mathrm{CO_2}}}$$

(the solids contribute nothing).
