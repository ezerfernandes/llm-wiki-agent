## Definition
**Radioactivity** is the spontaneous emission of energy (nuclear radiation) from unstable atomic nuclei. Discovered through the work of [[HenriBecquerel]] and [[MarieCurie]], it involves the conversion of a small amount of mass into energy according to [[MassEnergyEquivalence|E = mc²]].

## Key Points
- A **radioactive** substance is one that emits **nuclear radiation**; the rays come from the [[AtomicNucleus|nucleus]], not the electron cloud.
- Three primary radiations are emitted (see [[AlphaDecay]], [[BetaDecay]], [[GammaDecay]]):
  - **Alpha (α):** a ⁴He nucleus, charge +2|qₑ|, least penetrating (~mm in tissue).
  - **Beta (β):** an electron (or positron), charge ∓|qₑ|, intermediate range.
  - **Gamma (γ):** a high-energy photon, neutral, most penetrating (see [[GammaRay]]).
- Radiation deposits energy in matter mainly through ionization, which makes it [[IonizingRadiation|ionizing]] and both biologically hazardous and useful.
- **Range** (penetration depth) increases with particle energy and decreases with material density/electron density; charged particles ionize more and stop sooner than neutral photons.

## Equations
- E = mc² — mass-to-energy conversion underlying every decay
- KE = ½mv²; Δp = FΔt — classical energy/momentum in ionization collisions
