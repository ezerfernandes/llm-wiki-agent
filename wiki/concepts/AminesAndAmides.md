# Amines and Amides

The two nitrogen-containing organic families. (Named *AminesAndAmides* to be chemistry-explicit.)

## Amines (C–N, basic)

An **amine** is a molecule with **carbon–nitrogen bonds** in which the nitrogen has a **lone pair of electrons** and three bonds to carbon or hydrogen. Suffix **-ine**: methylamine CH₃NH₂, dimethylamine (CH₃)₂NH, trimethylamine (CH₃)₃N.

- **Basicity:** like ammonia, amines are **[[BronstedLowryAcidsAndBases|weak bases]]** because of the nitrogen lone pair; they protonate to ammonium ions —
  NH₃ + H⁺ → NH₄⁺ and CH₃NH₂ + H⁺ → [CH₃NH₃]⁺.
- **Heterocyclic amines** put nitrogen into a ring (a **heterocyclic compound** has two or more different elements in its ring) — e.g., **pyridine**, a six-membered ring with one N, alternating double bonds, and an unshared electron pair on N.
- **Alkaloids** are plant amines (nicotine, morphine, codeine, heroin) that form ammonium salts with dilute acid: R₃N + H₃O⁺ + Cl⁻ → [R₃NH⁺]Cl⁻ + H₂O (reversed by base). Small structural edits change potency — morphine → codeine (methylate one –OH) → heroin (acetylate both –OH).
- Amine groups appear in amino acids, hormones, neurotransmitters, DNA, vitamins, dyes, and drugs (penicillin, codeine).

## Amides (N on a carbonyl)

An **amide** has a **nitrogen atom bonded to the carbon of a [[AldehydesAndKetones|carbonyl]] group (C=O)**. Suffix **-amide**: acetamide CH₃CONH₂, hexanamide CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂CH₂CONH₂.

- **Amidation:** amides form when a **[[CarboxylicAcidsAndEsters|carboxylic acid]] reacts with an amine (or ammonia)**, **eliminating water** (a condensation analogous to [[Esterification|esterification]]):
  CH₃CH₂COOH + CH₃NH₂ → CH₃CH₂CONHCH₃ + H₂O.
- **Peptide bonds and proteins:** this is biologically central — **amino acids** (which carry both an amine and a carboxylic acid group) link via **peptide bonds** (carbonyl-C to amine-N, releasing water) into **polypeptides**; proteins contain at least one long polypeptide chain.
- **Kevlar** is an aromatic-amide polymer (from 1,4-phenylene-diamine + terephthaloyl chloride) strengthened by inter-chain hydrogen bonds and **aromatic stacking**; ~5× stronger than steel by weight — body armor, helmets.
