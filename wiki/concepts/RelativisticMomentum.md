## Definition
Relativistic momentum is the redefinition of [[LinearMomentum|momentum]] that holds at speeds approaching the [[SpeedOfLightPhysics|speed of light]]: p = γmu, the product of an object's rest mass m, its velocity u, and the [[LorentzFactor|Lorentz factor]] γ. This definition keeps [[ConservationOfMomentum|momentum conservation]] valid in every inertial frame.

## Key Points
- **Rest mass m** is the mass measured by an observer at rest relative to the object — the only well-defined ("meaningful") mass.
- At low speed γ ≈ 1, so p → mu, recovering classical [[LinearMomentum|momentum]].
- As u → c, γ → ∞ and p → ∞, so a massive object would need infinite momentum to reach c — a physical speed limit.
- Conserving p = γmu rather than mu satisfies the [[EinsteinPostulates|first postulate]] (laws hold in all inertial frames).
- Essential for analyzing high-energy collisions in particle accelerators.

## Equations
- p = γ m u
- γ = 1 / √(1 − u²/c²)
