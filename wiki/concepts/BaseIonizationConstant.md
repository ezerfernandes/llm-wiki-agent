# Base Ionization Constant (Kb)

The **base ionization constant Kb** is the [[EquilibriumConstant|equilibrium constant]] for the ionization of a [[BronstedLowryAcidsAndBases|Brønsted-Lowry base]] in water. For

$$\mathrm{B(aq) + H_2O(l) \rightleftharpoons HB^+(aq) + OH^-(aq)}$$

$$K_b = \frac{[\mathrm{HB^+}][\mathrm{OH^-}]}{[\mathrm{B}]}$$

The larger the Kb, the more OH⁻ forms and the **stronger the base**. Strong bases ionize completely; weak bases (e.g. ammonia) have small, measurable Kb. For a base, [[PercentIonization|percent ionization]] = ([OH⁻]eq/[B]₀) × 100.

## Relation to Ka and Kw

For a [[ConjugateAcidBasePair|conjugate acid-base pair]], the base's Kb and the conjugate acid's [[AcidIonizationConstant|Ka]] satisfy

$$K_a \times K_b = K_w \quad(= 1.0\times10^{-14}\text{ at }25\,^\circ\mathrm{C}),\qquad K_b = \frac{K_w}{K_a}$$

This is how the [[SaltHydrolysis|hydrolysis]] constant of a weak-acid anion is obtained: the acetate ion's Kb = Kw/Ka(acetic acid) = (1.0×10⁻¹⁴)/(1.8×10⁻⁵) = 5.6×10⁻¹⁰. The stronger the conjugate acid, the weaker the base.

## Calculations

Kb problems use the same [[ICETable|ICE table]] machinery as Ka, solving $K_b = x^2/([\mathrm{B}]_0 - x)$ for [OH⁻] = x (then pOH and [[pHScale|pH]]), with the small-x approximation when valid.
