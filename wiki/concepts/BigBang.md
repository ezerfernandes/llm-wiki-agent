## Definition
The Big Bang is the prevailing cosmological model in which all matter and energy originated about 13–15 billion years ago from an extremely hot, dense state, and the universe has been expanding and cooling ever since. It is not an explosion within space but an expansion of space itself.

## Key Points
- Inferred by extrapolating the observed expansion ([[HubblesLaw]]) backward in time to a single origin.
- The cosmic microwave background radiation (CMBR) — a 2.725 K blackbody spectrum filling all space — is the cooled remnant of the primordial fireball and a key confirmation.
- Tiny ~200 µK temperature fluctuations in the CMBR are "wrinkles" that grew by gravitational instability into galaxies and large-scale structure.
- An **inflationary scenario** (a fraction-of-a-second epoch of exponential expansion) smoothed the early universe and accounts for the CMBR's near-uniformity.
- A slight matter–antimatter asymmetry in particle physics let matter survive annihilation, explaining a matter-dominated universe.
- As the universe expanded and cooled, unified forces separated through spontaneous symmetry breaking (see [[GrandUnifiedTheory]], [[ElectroweakForce]]).

## Equations
- Hubble's law (basis for the age estimate): v = H₀ d
- Age scale: roughly t ~ 1/H₀ (order-of-magnitude)
