# Electrochemistry

**Electrochemistry** is the branch of chemistry concerned with [[OxidationReduction|redox reactions]] in which electron transfer is routed through an **external circuit** rather than occurring by direct contact of reactants. Doing so converts chemical energy into electrical work (in spontaneous **[[GalvanicCell|galvanic cells]]**) or uses electrical work to drive nonspontaneous reactions (in **[[ElectrolysisChemistry|electrolytic cells]]**).

> Disambiguation: this is the *chemistry* topic (cells, electrodes, redox potentials). It builds on but is distinct from the physics treatment of [[ElectricPotential|electric potential]] and [[Voltage|voltage]] in College Physics 2e.

## Two Cell Types
| | Galvanic / voltaic | Electrolytic |
|---|---|---|
| Redox reaction | spontaneous (E°cell > 0) | nonspontaneous (E°cell < 0) |
| Energy flow | system does electrical work on surroundings | surroundings do work on system |
| Example | battery, fuel cell | electroplating, Downs cell, water splitting |

In every cell, oxidation occurs at the **anode** and reduction at the **cathode**; an [[Electrolyte|electrolyte]] (often via a **salt bridge**) carries ionic current internally while electrons flow externally.

## The Quantitative Backbone
- Driving force is measured as a [[CellPotential|cell potential]], referenced to the [[StandardHydrogenElectrode|standard hydrogen electrode]] through [[StandardReductionPotential|standard reduction potentials]].
- Thermodynamic link: ΔG° = −nF·E°cell and E°cell = (RT/nF)ln K, tying electrochemistry to [[GibbsFreeEnergy|free energy]] and [[EquilibriumConstant|equilibrium]].
- Nonstandard conditions: the [[NernstEquation|Nernst equation]].
- Charge accounting: Q = It = nF (with the [[FaradaysLawsOfElectrolysis|Faraday constant]]) connects current and time to amount of substance reacted.
