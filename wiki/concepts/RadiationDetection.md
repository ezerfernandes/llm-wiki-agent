## Definition
**Radiation detection** is the measurement of [[IonizingRadiation|ionizing radiation]] using instruments, because nuclear radiation cannot be sensed directly. Detectors exploit the ionization or excitation the radiation produces in matter.

## Key Points
- **Photographic film:** radiation deposits energy (above a few eV) causing chemical darkening; used in medical/dental radiography. Efficiency is imperfect.
- **Geiger tube (Geiger counter):** a gas-filled cylinder with an axial wire; incoming radiation ionizes the gas, ions drift to the electrodes and produce a current pulse. It reports presence and relative intensity only — not energy, charge, or particle type.
- **Scintillator + photomultiplier:** the scintillator emits light flashes proportional to deposited energy; a photomultiplier tube amplifies that light into an electrical signal via cascading electrons. Allows energy/particle analysis at high count rates.
- **Solid-state (semiconductor) detectors:** convert radiation-induced ionization directly to current with unidirectional conduction; high stopping power gives greater efficiency than gas detectors.
