## Definition
Color vision is the perception of light wavelength (roughly 350–700 nm) by the eye-brain system. It is **trichromatic**: three types of cone photoreceptor, combined and compared neurally, produce all perceived hues.

## Key Points
- **Cones** (~6 million, concentrated in the fovea) give high-resolution color vision; the three types peak near 420 nm (blue), 520 nm (green), and 590 nm (yellow).
- **Rods** (~120 million, ~1000× more sensitive) handle low-light and peripheral vision and are color-insensitive.
- Color matching is non-unique: different wavelength mixtures can look identical (pure yellow vs. red+green); RGB displays exploit this.
- An object's "true" color comes from its absorption/reflection properties, independent of the illuminating source.
- **Color constancy**: the system perceives an object's color despite changing illumination.
- **Retinex theory** (Land): three cone images are jointly processed; retinal connections compare neighboring receptors, heightening edge sensitivity over uniform illumination.
