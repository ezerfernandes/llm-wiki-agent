## Definition
A fictitious (pseudo-) force is an apparent force that appears only in a non-inertial (accelerating or rotating) reference frame. It has no real physical source; it reflects the observer's own acceleration. The centrifugal force (apparent outward push) and the Coriolis force (sideways deflection) are the main rotating-frame examples.

## Key Points
- **Inertial frame:** non-accelerating; [[NewtonsSecondLaw|Newton's laws]] apply directly and every force has a real source. Earth is approximately inertial.
- **Non-inertial frame:** accelerating/rotating; fictitious forces must be added to make Newton's laws appear to hold.
- **Centrifugal force:** the outward "force" a rider feels on a merry-go-round; really inertia carrying them tangentially while the platform curves beneath them. Distinct from the real, inward [[CentripetalForce]].
- **Coriolis force:** deflects objects moving within a rotating frame perpendicular to their velocity. On Earth it bends large-scale winds, giving storms their rotation.
- The deeper explanation for all these effects is inertia ([[NewtonsFirstLaw]]), not an invented force.
