## Definition
X-rays are a high-frequency [[ElectromagneticSpectrum]] band that overlaps the high end of [[Ultraviolet|ultraviolet]] and extends to much higher frequencies. They are highly penetrating electromagnetic radiation produced by electron processes in atoms (distinguishing them from same-frequency [[GammaRay|gamma rays]], which come from the nucleus).

## Key Points
- Two production mechanisms: **characteristic X-rays** — high-voltage electrons eject inner-shell electrons and the cascade emits element-characteristic energies; **bremsstrahlung** ("braking radiation") — fast electrons decelerate in collisions, emitting a broad spectrum.
- Highly penetrating; damage is density-dependent, which is what makes radiography possible.
- Wavelengths below ~0.01 nm allow diffraction studies of molecular and crystal structure.
- Can induce cancer and genetic defects but also treat cancer (rapidly dividing cells are more sensitive).

## Equations
- `c = f λ` (very short λ, very high f).

## Applications
Medical imaging (radiography, CT), security screening, X-ray diffraction (key to determining DNA structure — see [[XRayCrystallography]]), and trace-element analysis via X-ray fluorescence.
