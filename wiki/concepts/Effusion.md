# Effusion

**Effusion** is the escape of gas molecules through a tiny hole — such as a pinhole in a balloon — into a vacuum. It is closely related to [[GaseousDiffusion|diffusion]] (the dispersal of molecules through space): both rates depend on molar mass, and although their absolute rates differ, the **ratios** of their rates are the same.

The rate of effusion depends directly on the average molecular speed (the [[RootMeanSquareSpeed|root-mean-square speed]] from the [[KineticMolecularTheory|kinetic-molecular theory]]):

$$\text{effusion rate} \propto u_{rms} \propto \frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathcal{M}}}$$

so lighter gases effuse faster. This molar-mass dependence is quantified by [[GrahamsLaw|Graham's law]].

A related microscopic quantity is the **mean free path** — the average distance a molecule travels between collisions, which increases as pressure decreases and is typically hundreds of times a molecule's own diameter.
