## Definition
Sound intensity is the power per unit area carried by a [[Sound]] wave — the rate at which acoustic energy passes through a surface, measured in W/m². It is the physical (objective) measure of a sound's strength, in contrast to perceived [[Loudness]] and the [[Decibel]] scale.

## Key Points
- Intensity is a special case of [[WaveIntensity]] applied to sound.
- It relates to the wave's **pressure amplitude** Δp (half the max–min pressure difference): larger pressure swings carry more energy.
- The reference intensity `I₀ = 10⁻¹² W/m²` marks the threshold of human hearing at 1000 Hz; the pain threshold is ~1 W/m², spanning a ~10¹² dynamic range.
- Because perception is logarithmic, intensity is usually reported as a sound level in [[Decibel]]s rather than raw W/m².

## Equations
- `I = P / A` (power per unit area)
- `I = (Δp)² / (2·ρ·v_w)` (ρ = medium density, v_w = [[SpeedOfSound]])
