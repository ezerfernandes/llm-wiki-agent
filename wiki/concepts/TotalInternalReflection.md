## Definition
Total internal reflection occurs when light traveling in a higher-index medium meets a boundary with a lower-index medium at an incidence angle greater than the **critical angle**; all the light reflects back into the first medium and none refracts out.

## Key Points
- Requires `n₁ > n₂` (light going from denser to less-dense medium).
- The **critical angle** `θc` is the incidence angle for which the refracted ray would be at 90°.
- Above `θc`, reflection is 100% efficient with no silvering required.
- **Fiber optics:** light is guided down glass/plastic fibers by repeated total internal reflection; lower-index **cladding** confines the light and prevents crosstalk. Carries telecom, internet, and cable signals.
- **Endoscopy:** fiber bundles transmit images and laser light for minimally invasive medicine.
- **Diamond brilliance:** a low critical angle (~24.4°) traps light until it exits chosen facets, concentrating it into sparkle.
- **Corner reflectors** send light back parallel to its source (bike reflectors, lunar ranging, binoculars, periscopes).

## Equations
- Snell's law: `n₁ sin θ₁ = n₂ sin θ₂`
- At critical angle: `n₁ sin θc = n₂`
- Critical angle: `θc = sin⁻¹(n₂ / n₁)` for `n₁ > n₂`
