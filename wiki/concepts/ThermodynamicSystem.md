# Thermodynamic System and Surroundings

To track energy in a chemical or physical change, thermochemistry partitions the universe into:
- the **system** — the substance (or reaction) undergoing the change of interest, and
- the **surroundings** — all other matter that can provide or absorb [[HeatThermodynamics|heat]].

This division is the basic accounting framework of [[Calorimetry]]: the heat released or absorbed by the system is detected as a temperature change in the surroundings (e.g., the water in a calorimeter).

## Energy Bookkeeping
Energy crosses the system/surroundings boundary as heat (q) and work (w). Per the [[FirstLawOfThermodynamics|first law of thermodynamics]], any energy leaving the system enters the surroundings and vice versa; total energy is conserved. In an idealized isolated calorimeter, no energy escapes to the wider environment, so the heat lost by one part equals the heat gained by another:

**q_system = −q_surroundings**  (e.g., q_reaction = −q_solution).

## Sign Convention (system perspective)
- **[[ExothermicEndothermic|Exothermic]]**: system releases heat to surroundings (q_system < 0).
- **[[ExothermicEndothermic|Endothermic]]**: system absorbs heat from surroundings (q_system > 0).
