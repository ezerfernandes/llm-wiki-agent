## Definition
**Latent heat** is the energy absorbed or released when a substance undergoes a [[PhaseChange|phase change]]
at constant temperature. "Latent" (hidden) reflects that this energy does not change the temperature —
it instead breaks (or forms) the intermolecular bonds that distinguish the phases. It is reported per
unit mass, in J/kg.

## Key Points
- During a phase transition the temperature stays fixed until the change is complete; only afterward does
  further [[HeatThermodynamics|heat]] raise the temperature.
- **Latent heat of fusion (Lf)** — energy per kg to melt a solid (or released on freezing). For water,
  Lf = 334 kJ/kg.
- **Latent heat of vaporization (Lv)** — energy per kg to boil a liquid (or released on condensation).
  For water ≈ 2256 kJ/kg at 100 °C, ≈ 2428 kJ/kg at body temperature.
- **Latent heat of sublimation (Ls)** — for the direct solid→vapor transition (e.g. dry ice).
- Latent heats are large, so small masses move a lot of energy: a little melting ice cools a drink
  sharply, and evaporating sweat is an effective coolant (evaporative cooling).
- Energy balance in an isolated system still holds: heat released by freezing/condensing equals heat
  absorbed elsewhere (Q_ice + Q_soda = 0), enabling final-temperature [[Calorimetry|calorimetry]] problems.
- Real-world uses: orchard frost protection (freezing water releases latent heat), evaporative cooling of
  the body, and climate moderation by evaporation/condensation.

## Equations
- Q = m·Lf  (melting / freezing)
- Q = m·Lv  (vaporization / condensation)
- Q = m·Ls  (sublimation)
