# Hybridization ↔ VSEPR Geometry Relationship

The arrangement of [[OrbitalHybridization|hybrid orbitals]] predicted by [[ValenceBondTheory|valence bond theory]] is **identical** to the electron-pair geometry predicted by [[VSEPRTheory|VSEPR theory]]. This one-to-one correspondence is what lets hybridization be assigned directly from a [[LewisStructure|Lewis structure]] without any quantum-mechanical calculation. (Named explicitly to avoid generic-name collisions.)

## The Bridge: Regions of Electron Density

Both models count the same thing — **regions (domains) of electron density** on the central atom, where each single bond, [[MultipleBonds|multiple bond]], radical, and lone pair counts as **one** region. VSEPR turns that count into a geometry; hybridization turns the same count into a set of equivalent orbitals.

| Regions | VSEPR electron-pair geometry | Hybridization | Bond angle(s) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | linear | sp | 180° |
| 3 | trigonal planar | sp² | 120° |
| 4 | tetrahedral | sp³ | 109.5° |
| 5 | trigonal bipyramidal | sp³d | 90° and 120° |
| 6 | octahedral | sp³d² | 90° |

## Why It Works

VSEPR places electron-density regions as far apart as possible to minimize repulsion; hybridization mathematically mixes atomic orbitals into the same number of equivalent lobes pointing in exactly those directions. So the *number of regions* alone fixes both the predicted shape and the hybridization. [[SigmaAndPiBonds|π bonds]] and [[ResonanceStructure|resonance]] do not change the region count, so they never change the hybridization — only σ bonds, lone pairs, and radicals matter.

## Worked Examples

- **SO₂**: 2 bonds + 1 lone pair on S = 3 regions → trigonal planar → **sp²** (the same in either resonance form).
- **SeF₄**: 4 bonds + 1 lone pair = 5 regions → trigonal bipyramidal → **sp³d**.
- **CH₄ / NH₃ / H₂O**: all 4 regions → tetrahedral → **sp³**, with lone pairs compressing the bond angle.
