## Definition
Magnetic field lines are a visual representation of the [[MagneticField]]: their tangent gives field direction at each point and their areal density (lines per unit perpendicular area) is proportional to field magnitude. Externally they run from a magnet's north pole to its south pole.

## Key Points
Four properties:
1. The field is tangent to the line at every point; a compass needle aligns with it.
2. Field strength is proportional to line density (closer lines = stronger field).
3. Lines never cross, so the field is single-valued everywhere.
4. Lines are continuous closed loops with no endpoints — they pass through the magnet's interior.

The closed-loop property follows from the absence of magnetic monopoles, distinguishing magnetic lines from [[ElectricField]] lines, which begin and end on charges. Field-line patterns are identical for a bar magnet and a current loop (a magnetic dipole); a straight current-carrying wire produces concentric circular lines (right-hand rule).
