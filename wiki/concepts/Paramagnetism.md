# Paramagnetism (and Diamagnetism)

**Paramagnetism** is the attraction of a substance to a magnetic field, arising in molecules that have **unpaired electrons**. **Diamagnetism** is the opposite: substances in which all electrons are paired are weakly *repelled* by a magnetic field. These magnetic properties are a signature success of [[MolecularOrbitalTheory|molecular orbital theory]], which predicts them where [[LewisStructure|Lewis structures]] cannot. (Named *Paramagnetism* to keep the chemistry sense distinct.)

## The O₂ Puzzle

N₂ and O₂ have similar Lewis structures, yet liquid nitrogen passes through a magnetic field undisturbed while **liquid oxygen is attracted to a magnet and floats between the poles**. A [[MODiagram|MO diagram]] resolves this: O₂ has enough electrons to half-fill the **degenerate π₂ₚ\*** [[BondingAndAntibondingOrbitals|antibonding orbitals]], and by Hund's rule the last two electrons occupy them **singly and unpaired**. So O₂ has bond order 2 *and* two unpaired electrons — it is paramagnetic. The text calls this "a strong piece of support for the molecular orbital theory," since the Lewis model predicts no unpaired electrons in O₂.

## Measuring It

A **Gouy balance** weighs a sample with and without an applied magnetic field; **magnetic susceptibility** measures the force the substance experiences. A paramagnetic sample is pulled into the field (appears heavier), and the magnitude indicates the number of unpaired electrons; a diamagnetic sample is slightly pushed out.

## In coordination complexes (Ch 19)

Transition-metal [[CoordinationCompound|coordination compounds]] are paramagnetic or diamagnetic depending on their **number of unpaired d electrons**, which [[CrystalFieldTheory|crystal field theory]] sets through the high-spin/low-spin choice. The magnetic moment grows with the number of unpaired electrons. Same metal, opposite behavior: low-spin **[Fe(CN)₆]⁴⁻ is diamagnetic** (all six d electrons paired) while high-spin **[Fe(H₂O)₆]²⁺ is paramagnetic** (four unpaired). See [[ComplexColorAndMagnetism]].
