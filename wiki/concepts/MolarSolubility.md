# Molar Solubility

**Molar solubility** is the number of moles of a solute that dissolve per liter to produce a saturated solution. For a slightly soluble ionic compound it is directly related to the [[SolubilityProduct|solubility product constant Ksp]]: each can be calculated from the other using an [[ICETable|ICE table]] and the dissolution stoichiometry. (This refines the qualitative [[Solubility|solubility]] of Ch 11 into a calculable quantity.)

## Ksp from molar solubility

Multiply the molar solubility by each ion's stoichiometric coefficient, then substitute into the Ksp expression. For CaF₂ with molar solubility 2.15 × 10⁻⁴ M:

$$[\mathrm{F^-}] = 2 \times (2.15 \times 10^{-4}) = 4.30 \times 10^{-4}\ \mathrm{M}$$
$$K_{sp} = [\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}][\mathrm{F^-}]^2 = (2.15 \times 10^{-4})(4.30 \times 10^{-4})^2 = 3.98 \times 10^{-11}$$

## Molar solubility from Ksp

Let the molar solubility be x and express each ion concentration in terms of x. For Ca(OH)₂(s) ⇌ Ca²⁺ + 2 OH⁻: [Ca²⁺] = x, [OH⁻] = 2x, so

$$K_{sp} = (x)(2x)^2 = 4x^3 = 1.3 \times 10^{-6} \quad\Rightarrow\quad x = \sqrt[3]{\tfrac{1.3 \times 10^{-6}}{4}} = 6.9 \times 10^{-3}\ \mathrm{M}$$

The stoichiometry-dependent factor (here 4x³; for a 1:1 salt simply x²; for Al(OH)₃ it is 27x⁴) is the crucial detail — two salts with similar Ksp can have very different molar solubilities if their formulas differ.
