## Definition
An optical aberration is any deviation of a real lens's (or system's) image from the ideal predicted by the thin-lens model, caused by lens shape, material, thickness, aperture, or object position.

## Key Points
- **Chromatic aberration**: refractive index varies with wavelength, so colors focus at different points with different magnifications; corrected by an **achromatic doublet** (converging crown-glass + diverging flint-glass lens).
- **Spherical aberration**: outer lens zones focus light closer than central zones, blurring the focus.
- **Coma**: an off-axis aberration where lens zones miss a common focus, giving pear-shaped distortion.
- **Astigmatism**: focus differs between two perpendicular planes (in a lens or the eye).
- Mirrors avoid chromatic aberration because reflection is wavelength-independent — a key reason large telescopes use mirrors.
- Smaller apertures reduce aberrations but dim the image; aspheric surfaces, graded-index (GRIN) materials, and digital post-processing also correct aberrations.

## Equations
- None (qualitative); aberrations are departures from the [[ThinLensEquation]] ideal.
