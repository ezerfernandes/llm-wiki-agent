# Solution Concentration

**Concentration** is the relative amount of a component in a solution — a homogeneous mixture of a **solute** dissolved in a **solvent**. Beyond [[Molarity|molarity]] (mol/L), several other units express concentration, chosen to suit the application and the size of the amounts involved. The qualitative terms **dilute** (low concentration) and **concentrated** (high concentration) describe these relatively.

## Mass Percentage

$$\text{mass \%} = \frac{\text{mass of component}}{\text{mass of solution}} \times 100\%$$

Also called percent mass, percent weight, or (w/w)%. Any mass unit works as long as solute and solution use the *same* unit so they cancel.
- 3.75 mg glucose in 5.0 g fluid → (0.00375/5.0) × 100% = 0.075%.
- Concentrated HCl is 37.2% HCl; with density 1.19 g/mL, 0.500 L (= 595 g solution) contains 595 × 0.372 = 221 g HCl.

## Volume Percentage

$$\text{volume \%} = \frac{\text{volume of solute}}{\text{volume of solution}} \times 100\%$$

Written %vol or (v/v)%, used for liquid-in-liquid solutions. e.g., 70% vol rubbing alcohol in a 355 mL bottle = 248.5 mL isopropanol (× 0.785 g/mL = 195 g).

## Mass-Volume Percentage

A mixed unit: solute mass per solution volume, as a percentage (m/v); common in biomedical work. Physiological saline is 0.9% (m/v) = 0.9 g per 100 mL. Blood glucose is reported in mg/dL (mg per 100 mL); a normal fasting range is ≈ 70–100 mg/dL.

## Parts Per Million and Parts Per Billion

For trace amounts (pollutants, contaminants):

$$\text{ppm} = \frac{\text{mass solute}}{\text{mass solution}} \times 10^6 \qquad \text{ppb} = \frac{\text{mass solute}}{\text{mass solution}} \times 10^9$$

**1 ppm = 10³ ppb.** Regulatory examples: the EPA fluoride limit in tap water is 4 ppm; lead triggers action at 15 ppb. e.g., 15 ppb = 0.015 ppm; a 300 mL (≈300 g) glass of water at 15 ppb lead holds (15 × 300)/10⁹ = 4.5 × 10⁻⁶ g = 4.5 µg lead.

## Molality and Mole Fraction (temperature-independent)

Two further units, preferred for [[ColligativeProperties|colligative-property]] calculations because they do not change with temperature (unlike volume-based units):

$$\text{molality } m = \frac{\text{mol solute}}{\text{kg solvent}} \qquad \text{mole fraction } X_A = \frac{\text{mol }A}{\text{total mol of all components}}$$

Note molality uses the mass of the *solvent* (not solution), and the mole fractions of all components sum to 1.
