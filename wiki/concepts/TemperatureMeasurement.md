# Temperature Measurement

Temperature is a measure of hotness or coldness and an **intensive** property (see [[ExtensiveAndIntensiveProperties]]). Three scales are common, all anchored to the freezing and boiling points of water at a specified pressure.

- **Celsius (°C):** 0 °C (freeze) to 100 °C (boil), 100 equal intervals.
- **Fahrenheit (°F):** 32 °F (freeze) to 212 °F (boil), 180 equal intervals.
- **Kelvin (K):** the SI base unit; an *absolute* scale where 0 K is the theoretical lowest temperature. Written without a degree symbol. Water freezes at 273.15 K, boils at 373.15 K.

Celsius and Fahrenheit share no common zero, so their relation is **linear** (y = mx + b), not proportional. The slope is (212 − 32)/(100 − 0) = 9/5 and the intercept is 32:

> T(°F) = (9/5 × T(°C)) + 32 and T(°C) = (5/9)(T(°F) − 32)

Kelvin shares Celsius's interval size (slope 1) and is just offset by 273.15 (an experimentally determined, non-exact value):

> T(K) = T(°C) + 273.15 and T(°C) = T(K) − 273.15

**Examples:** 37.0 °C = 310.2 K = 98.6 °F; 450 °F = 230 °C = 5.1 × 10² K. These conversions are an application of [[DimensionalAnalysis]] (with care for the additive offsets) and follow [[SignificantFigures]] rules.
