# Standard Molar Entropy (S°) and Standard Entropy Change (ΔS°)

The **standard molar entropy (S°)** is the absolute entropy of one mole of a substance under standard conditions (1 bar, conventionally 298.15 K). Units: **J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹**. Because the [[ThirdLawOfThermodynamics|third law]] fixes S = 0 for a perfect crystal at 0 K, standard molar entropies are **absolute** values — and unlike the [[StandardEnthalpyOfFormation|standard enthalpy of formation ΔHf°]] (which is 0 for elements), **S° is nonzero even for elements in their standard states**.

## Standard Entropy Change of Reaction
$$\Delta S^\circ = \sum \nu\,S^\circ(\text{products}) - \sum \nu\,S^\circ(\text{reactants})$$

where ν are the stoichiometric coefficients. This parallels the Hess-style summation used for [[StandardEnthalpyOfFormation|ΔH°_rxn]] (see [[HessLaw]]).

## Worked Examples
- **Condensation of water**, H₂O(g) → H₂O(l): ΔS° = (1 mol)(70.0 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹) − (1 mol)(188.8 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹) = **−118.8 J/K** (negative, as expected for a transition to a denser phase).
- **Methanol combustion**, 2 CH₃OH(l) + 3 O₂(g) → 2 CO₂(g) + 4 H₂O(l): ΔS° = [2(213.8) + 4(70.0)] − [2(126.8) + 3(205.2)] = **−161.6 J/K**.
