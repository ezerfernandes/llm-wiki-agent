## Definition
The moment of inertia (rotational inertia), I, is the rotational analog of mass: it measures an object's resistance to [[AngularAcceleration]]. Unlike mass, it depends not only on how much mass an object has but also on how that mass is distributed relative to the axis of rotation. Units: kg·m².

## Key Points
- For a single point mass at radius r, I = mr²; for a body, sum (or integrate) over all the mass: I = Σ mr².
- Mass farther from the axis contributes much more (r² dependence) — distribution matters more than total mass.
- I is the key term in the rotational form of [[NewtonsSecondLaw]] for rotation (net τ = Iα) and in [[RotationalKineticEnergy]] (½Iω²) and [[AngularMomentum]] (L = Iω).
- The same body has different I about different axes.

## Equations
- Point mass: I = m r²
- General body: I = Σ m r²
- Solid disk / cylinder (central axis): I = ½ M R²
- Hoop / thin ring (central axis): I = M R²
- Uniform sphere: I = (2/5) M R²
- Thin rod about its end: I = (1/3) M ℓ²
