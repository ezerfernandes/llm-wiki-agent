## Definition
Electric field lines are continuous curves that map an [[ElectricField]] in space: the field direction is tangent to a line at each point, and the local field strength is proportional to the density of lines (lines per unit perpendicular area).

## Key Points
Rules for drawing field lines:
1. Lines begin on positive [[ElectricCharge|charges]] and end on negative charges (or run to/from infinity for an isolated charge).
2. The number of lines is proportional to the magnitude of the charge.
3. Field strength is proportional to line density (closer lines = stronger field).
4. The field direction is the tangent to the line.
5. Lines never cross, since the field has a unique direction at each point.

- The net field of several charges is the **vector sum** of their individual fields (superposition principle).
- Between like charges the fields partly cancel (weak region between them); far away they look like one larger charge.
- Between unlike charges the fields reinforce between them (a dipole pattern); far away they look like a single smaller charge.

## Equations
- Per-charge field: E = k |Q| / r²
- Resultant of perpendicular components: E_tot = √(E₁² + E₂²)
- Direction: θ = tan⁻¹(E₁ / E₂)
