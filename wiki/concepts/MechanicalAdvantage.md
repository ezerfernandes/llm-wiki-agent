## Definition
Mechanical advantage (MA) is the ratio of the output force a machine delivers to the input force applied to it. It quantifies how much a [[SimpleMachine]] multiplies (or reduces) an applied force.

## Key Points
- MA greater than 1 means the machine amplifies force (output exceeds input); MA less than 1 means it reduces force in exchange for greater range or speed of motion.
- Because of energy conservation, force gain comes at the cost of distance — a machine never produces more work than the energy supplied.
- For a [[Lever]], MA equals the ratio of the input lever arm to the output lever arm (`l_i / l_o`), derived from [[Torque]] balance at the fulcrum.
- For cranks and wheels, MA equals the ratio of radii (`r_i / r_o`).
- Human skeletal systems usually have MA well below 1: short muscle lever arms force large muscle forces, but yield useful gains in limb range and speed.

## Equations
- General: `MA = F_o / F_i`
- Lever: `MA = l_i / l_o`
- Crank/wheel: `MA = r_i / r_o`
