## Definition
The speed of light *c* is the speed at which all [[ElectromagneticWave|electromagnetic waves]] propagate through vacuum, c = 3.00×10⁸ m/s. [[MaxwellsEquations|Maxwell's equations]] show this speed is fixed entirely by two fundamental constants — the permittivity (ε₀) and permeability (μ₀) of free space — and its agreement with the measured speed of light established that light is an electromagnetic wave.

> Disambiguation: this is the physical constant. The page [[SpeedOfLight]] is a different, ML-systems "light barrier" latency concept (light in optical fiber as a latency floor).

## Key Points
- Derived, not assumed: it falls out of Maxwell's equations rather than being put in by hand.
- In a transparent medium of refractive index n, EM waves travel slower, at c/n (e.g., ~2/3 c in glass).
- Sets the field ratio in an EM wave: `E/B = c`, so the magnetic component is numerically tiny relative to the electric one.
- Links frequency and wavelength across the entire [[ElectromagneticSpectrum]] via `c = f λ`.

## Equations
- `c = 1 / sqrt(μ₀ ε₀)`
- `c = 1/sqrt[(8.85×10⁻¹² C²/N·m²)(4π×10⁻⁷ T·m/A)] = 3.00×10⁸ m/s`
- `c = f λ`; `E / B = c`
