# Atomic Mass

The **atomic mass** of a [[ChemicalElement|element]], as listed on the [[PeriodicTable|periodic table]], is the abundance-weighted average mass of its naturally occurring [[Isotope|isotopes]], expressed in **unified atomic mass units (amu / dalton, Da)** — where 1 amu = 1/12 of the mass of a carbon-12 atom = 1.6605 × 10⁻²⁴ g.

## Weighted-Average Formula

$$\text{average mass} = \sum_i (\text{fractional abundance} \times \text{isotopic mass})_i$$

No individual atom has exactly this average mass; it represents the population of all atoms of the element.

## Worked Example — Boron

Boron is 19.9% ¹⁰B (10.0129 amu) and 80.1% ¹¹B (11.0093 amu):

$$(0.199 \times 10.0129) + (0.801 \times 11.0093) = 1.99 + 8.82 = 10.81\ \text{amu}$$

So natural boron averages 10.81 amu even though every individual atom weighs about 10 or 11 amu.

## Measurement — Mass Spectrometry

Isotopic masses and abundances are measured by **mass spectrometry (MS)**: a sample is vaporized, ionized, and separated by mass-to-charge ratio using electric/magnetic fields. Each peak's height is proportional to the fraction of [[ChemicalIon|cations]] of that mass-to-charge ratio. MS is widely used in chemistry, forensics, medicine, and environmental science.

## Note on Radioactive Elements

For elements whose isotopes are all unstable/radioactive, the periodic table lists the mass number of the most stable isotope in square brackets rather than a weighted average.

## Use in Composition (Ch 3)

Summing the average atomic masses of every atom in a [[ChemicalFormula|formula]] gives the [[FormulaMass|formula mass]] (amu), which is numerically equal to the substance's [[MolarMass|molar mass]] (g/mol) — the basis for [[TheMoleConcept|mole]] conversions.
