# Law of Mass Action

The **law of mass action** states that, at a given temperature, the [[ReactionQuotient|reaction quotient]] of a system **at equilibrium is constant**. That constant value is the [[EquilibriumConstant|equilibrium constant]] K.

The practical consequence is powerful: for a particular reaction at a particular temperature, **all** equilibrium mixtures share the same value of

$$K = \frac{[\text{products}]^{\text{coeffs}}}{[\text{reactants}]^{\text{coeffs}}}$$

no matter what concentrations you start from. Different starting mixtures reach different *amounts* of each species, but always the same ratio K.

## Why it works

Each species' concentration (or partial pressure) is raised to its stoichiometric coefficient — the "mass-action" form. Because the forward and reverse [[ReactionRate|rates]] depend on those concentrations, when the rates balance at [[ChemicalEquilibrium|equilibrium]] the resulting ratio is pinned to a single number set only by temperature.
