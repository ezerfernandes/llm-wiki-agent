## Definition
Length contraction is the relativistic effect in which an object's measured length along its direction of motion is shorter when it moves relative to the observer. The contracted length L is the **proper length** L₀ divided by the [[LorentzFactor|Lorentz factor]] γ. Lengths perpendicular to the motion are unchanged.

## Key Points
- **Proper length (L₀)** is the distance between two points measured by an observer at rest relative to both.
- It is the spatial counterpart of [[TimeDilation|time dilation]]: observers agree on relative velocity but disagree on both elapsed time and distance, so distances must contract to keep v = distance/time consistent.
- Negligible at everyday speeds (γ ≈ 1) and only noticeable as v approaches c.
- Example: an astronaut at γ = 30 sees the 4.3 ly distance to Alpha Centauri contracted to 0.143 ly, so she can cross it without exceeding c in her frame.
- Applications: SLAC's 3 km linac appears ~0.5 m long to high-speed electrons; a moving charge's electric field lines compress along the motion.

## Equations
- L = L₀ / γ = L₀ √(1 − v²/c²)
- γ = 1 / √(1 − v²/c²)
