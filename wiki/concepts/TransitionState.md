# Transition State (Activated Complex)

The **transition state**, or **activated complex**, is the unstable, high-energy species formed at the instant reactants collide with both the correct orientation and enough energy (≥ the [[ActivationEnergy|activation energy]]). It sits at the energy maximum between reactants and products, is extremely short-lived, and is usually undetectable by ordinary analytical instruments.

## Reaction energy diagram

A plot of potential energy vs **reaction coordinate** visualizes the process:
- Reactants begin at one energy level and climb to the peak — the transition state.
- The height of that climb is the [[ActivationEnergy|activation energy]] $E_a$ (a *kinetic* quantity — the barrier).
- Products end at another level; the reactant→product energy difference is ΔH, the [[EnthalpyChemistry|enthalpy]] of reaction (a *thermodynamic* quantity).
  - **Exothermic:** ΔH < 0 (products below reactants).
  - **Endothermic:** ΔH > 0 (products above reactants).

A [[Catalysis|catalyst]] introduces an alternative path with a lower-energy transition state (lower Eₐ) while leaving the reactant and product energies — and thus ΔH — unchanged.
