## Definition
Acoustic impedance (Z) characterizes how strongly a medium resists the propagation of a [[Sound]] wave. It equals the product of the medium's density and its [[SpeedOfSound]], and it determines how much sound reflects versus transmits at a boundary between two media.

## Key Points
- **Impedance matching:** energy transfers efficiently between media of similar impedance; a large impedance mismatch produces strong reflection.
- The fraction of incident intensity reflected at a boundary is given by the **intensity reflection coefficient**.
- Soft tissues have similar impedances, so [[Ultrasound]] reflects only weakly between them and can penetrate to image deeper structures, while large mismatches (e.g., tissue–air, tissue–bone) reflect strongly.

## Equations
- `Z = ρ·v` (ρ = density kg/m³, v = sound speed m/s)
- Intensity reflection coefficient: `a = [(Z₂ − Z₁) / (Z₁ + Z₂)]²`
