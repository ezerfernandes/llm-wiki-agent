# Nuclear Binding Energy (Chemistry)

> Disambiguation: this is the OpenStax *Chemistry 2e* (Ch 21) treatment. For the College Physics 2e treatment see [[NuclearBindingEnergy]]; the underlying relativity relation is [[MassEnergyEquivalence]].

The **mass defect** of a nucleus is the difference between the calculated combined mass of its individual protons, neutrons, and electrons and its experimentally measured mass — the assembled nucleus weighs *less* than its parts. That missing mass is converted to energy when the nucleons bind together.

The **nuclear binding energy** is the energy released when nucleons bind into a nucleus; equivalently, the energy required to break the nucleus back into free protons and neutrons. It is computed from the mass defect via Einstein's **mass-energy equivalence**:

$$E = mc^2 \qquad (c = 2.998 \times 10^8 \text{ m/s})$$

### Worked example — Helium-4
- Calculated mass: 2(1.0073) + 2(1.0087) + 2(0.00055) amu = 4.0331 amu
- Measured mass: 4.0026 amu → **mass defect = 0.0305 amu**
- As 0.0305 g/mol = 3.05 × 10⁻⁵ kg/mol: E = mc² = **2.74 × 10¹² J/mol (2.74 TJ/mol)**
- Per nucleus: ÷ 6.022 × 10²³ = 4.55 × 10⁻¹² J = **4.55 pJ = 28.4 MeV**

Useful conversions: 1 eV = 1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ J; 1 MeV = 1.602 × 10⁻¹³ J; 1 amu = 1.66605 × 10⁻²⁷ kg.

### Binding energy per nucleon
$$\text{Binding energy per nucleon} = \frac{\text{total binding energy}}{\text{number of nucleons}}$$

This is the measure of relative nuclear stability. It peaks near mass number A ≈ 56 — **iron-56** has 493.9 MeV total ÷ 56 = **8.820 MeV/nucleon**, and nickel-58 is similar. Because the curve peaks in the middle, both [[NuclearFusionChemistry|fusion]] of light nuclei *up* toward the peak and [[NuclearFissionChemistry|fission]] of heavy nuclei *down* toward the peak release energy.

The huge magnitude of binding energies (billions of kJ/mol) is why [[NuclearChemistry|nuclear]] energy changes dwarf chemical ones, and feeds directly into the [[BandOfStability|band of stability]].
