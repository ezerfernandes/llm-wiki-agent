# Electron Configuration

An **electron configuration** specifies how an atom's [[Electron|electrons]] are distributed among its [[AtomicOrbital|atomic orbitals]]. Notation gives, for each subshell, the principal quantum number n, the subshell letter (s/p/d/f), and a superscript electron count — e.g., **2p⁴** = four electrons in the n=2 p subshell.

## How configurations are built
Apply three rules together:
- [[AufbauPrinciple|Aufbau]] — fill lowest-energy subshells first (1s 2s 2p 3s 3p 4s 3d 4p …).
- [[PauliExclusionPrinciple|Pauli]] — at most two electrons per orbital, opposite spins.
- [[HundsRule|Hund]] — fill degenerate orbitals singly with parallel spins before pairing.

Examples (H → Ne): H 1s¹, He 1s², Li 1s²2s¹, C 1s²2s²2p², N 1s²2s²2p³, O 1s²2s²2p⁴, Ne 1s²2s²2p⁶.

## Core vs valence; noble-gas notation
**Valence electrons** occupy the outermost shell (highest n) and drive chemistry; **core electrons** are the inner shells. Configurations are abbreviated with the preceding noble gas in brackets: Na = 1s²2s²2p⁶3s¹ = **[Ne]3s¹**; Al = **[Ne]3s²3p¹**. Same group ⇒ same valence-electron count (Group 1 = 1, Group 2 = 2, Group 17 = 7), which is why groups share chemical behavior.

## Blocks of the periodic table
The last electron added classifies the element:
- **Main group (s/p block)** — last electron in outer s or p; valence = highest-n s and p (e.g., Ga = [Ar]4s²3d¹⁰4p¹, valence 4s²4p¹; 3d is core).
- **Transition metals (d block)** — last electron in (n−1)d; valence = ns + (n−1)d.
- **Inner transition (f block)** — last electron in (n−2)f; lanthanides (La–Lu) and actinides (Ac–Lr).

Exceptions (Cr [Ar]4s¹3d⁵, Cu [Ar]4s¹3d¹⁰, Nb [Kr]5s¹4d⁴) reflect the stability of half-/fully-filled d subshells — see [[AufbauPrinciple]].

## Ions
- **Cations**: remove highest-n electrons first. For transition/inner-transition metals, remove ns *before* (n−1)d or (n−2)f. E.g., Fe → Fe²⁺ = [Ar]3d⁶ (loses both 4s); Na⁺ = [Ne].
- **Anions**: add electrons in Aufbau order. E.g., P³⁻ = 1s²2s²2p⁶3s²3p⁶.
