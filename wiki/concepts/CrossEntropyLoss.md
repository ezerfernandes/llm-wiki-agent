# Cross-Entropy Loss

The negative log-likelihood of the true class under a model's predicted distribution — the standard loss for classification and language modeling. Drives [[Backpropagation]] in [[LogisticRegression]], [[CNN]] classifiers, and every autoregressive [[transformer]] LM during [[pretraining]].

## Cross-entropy = NLL under categorical likelihood

For predictions $p(y=c\mid\mathbf{x};\boldsymbol\theta) = \text{softmax}_c(f(\mathbf{x};\boldsymbol\theta))$:

$$\text{NLL}(\boldsymbol\theta) = -\sum_n\log p(y_n\mid\mathbf{x}_n;\boldsymbol\theta) = -\sum_n\log\,\text{softmax}_{y_n}(f(\mathbf{x}_n;\boldsymbol\theta)).$$

This is the cross-entropy between the one-hot label distribution and the model's predicted distribution — i.e., [[MaximumLikelihoodEstimation|MLE]] specialized to categorical observations. The framing is unified across [[mml-book]] Ch 8 (ERM and MLE), Ch 6 ([[ExponentialFamily]]), and Ch 9 (linear regression's Gaussian-NLL analog).

## Why the softmax + cross-entropy pairing

The gradient of softmax-cross-entropy has the simple form $\hat{p}_c - \mathbb{1}[y=c]$ — *prediction minus target*. This is the clean signal backprop propagates through the rest of the network. The pairing isn't arbitrary; it's the conjugacy of the [[ExponentialFamily|exponential-family]] log-partition function with its sufficient-statistic gradient.

## Information-theoretic interpretation

[[d2l-linear-classification]] derives cross-entropy from Shannon information theory: $H(P, Q) = \sum_j -P(j)\log Q(j)$ is the **expected surprisal** of an observer with subjective probabilities $Q$ upon seeing data generated by $P$. Minimized when $Q = P$, in which case it equals the entropy $H(P)$. The classification objective thus has two equivalent readings: (i) maximizing the likelihood of observed labels, (ii) minimizing the number of bits required to transmit the labels under our predicted distribution.

## Numerical stability

Naive computation $-\sum_j y_j \log\hat y_j$ with $\hat y$ from a manual softmax overflows / underflows in FP32. The fix is the [[LogSumExp|LogSumExp trick]] fused into a single operator that takes **logits** (not probabilities) directly: `F.cross_entropy(logits, target)`, `SparseCategoricalCrossentropy(from_logits=True)`, `optax.softmax_cross_entropy_with_integer_labels`, `gluon.loss.SoftmaxCrossEntropyLoss`. Passing pre-softmax logits — not probabilities — is the correct interface.
