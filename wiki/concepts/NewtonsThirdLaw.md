## Definition
Newton's third law of motion: whenever one body exerts a force on a second body, the first experiences a force equal in magnitude and opposite in direction. Forces always occur in matched **action–reaction** pairs, expressing a fundamental symmetry in nature.

## Key Points
- The two forces in a pair act on **different objects**, which is precisely why they do not cancel each other.
- Only forces external to a chosen system affect that system's motion, so defining the **system of interest** is essential (see [[FreeBodyDiagram]]).
- The same physical situation yields different analyses depending on which objects are defined as the system.

## Equations
- For a paired force: F₁₂ = −F₂₁ (equal magnitude, opposite direction)
- Applied via [[NewtonsSecondLaw]]: F_net = ma
