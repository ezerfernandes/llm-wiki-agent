## Definition
Wave optics (physical optics) is the study of light phenomena that require treating light as a [[Wave]] rather than as rays. These effects — interference, diffraction, thin-film color, and polarization — become prominent when light interacts with structures comparable in size to its wavelength.

## Key Points
- Complements [[GeometricOptics]]: ray optics works for objects much larger than a wavelength; wave optics is needed at the wavelength scale.
- Built on [[HuygensPrinciple]], superposition ([[WaveInterference]], [[Superposition]]), and the wave-particle duality of light.
- Frequency is preserved across media while speed and wavelength scale by 1/n; perceived color tracks frequency, not wavelength.
- Explains soap-bubble and oil-slick color, diffraction patterns, instrument resolution limits, and polarized light.

## Equations
- `c = f λ` (vacuum), `v = c / n`, `λₙ = λ / n`
