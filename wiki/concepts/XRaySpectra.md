# X-Ray Spectra

**X-rays** are high-energy [[ElectromagneticSpectrum|electromagnetic radiation]] produced in an X-ray tube when fast [[Electron|electrons]], accelerated through a voltage V, strike a metal anode. Their spectrum has two atomic-origin components: a continuous background and sharp characteristic lines.

## Key Points
- **Bremsstrahlung ("braking radiation")** — a continuous spectrum emitted as the bombarding electrons decelerate in the anode. The shortest wavelength (maximum [[PhotonQuantum|photon]] energy) corresponds to an electron giving up all its kinetic energy in one event.
- **Characteristic X-rays** — discrete, element-specific lines emitted when an inner-shell electron is knocked out and a higher-shell electron drops to fill the vacancy. The Kα line is the n=2 → n=1 transition.
- Inner shells are modeled as [[BohrModel|hydrogen-like]] with an effective nuclear charge Z_eff (≈ Z − 1 for Kα) because of shielding by remaining electrons.
- Heavy elements produce high-energy characteristic X-rays (e.g., tungsten Z=74, Kα ≈ 54.4 keV), which is why an X-ray tube needs a high accelerating voltage.

## Equations
- Maximum photon energy: E_max = h·f_max = qₑ·V
- Hydrogen-like inner-shell energy: E_n = −(Z²/n²)·E₀, with E₀ = 13.6 eV (use Z_eff)
- Characteristic Kα energy: E_Kα = E₂ − E₁
- Photon wavelength: λ = hc/E

## Applications
Medical/dental imaging, mammography, computed tomography (CT), X-ray crystallography (von Laue 1912 proved X-rays are EM waves; later DNA and protein structures), radiotherapy, astronomy, and X-ray fluorescence material analysis.
