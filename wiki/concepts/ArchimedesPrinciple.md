## Definition
**Archimedes' principle** states that the upward buoyant [[Force]] on an object immersed in a fluid equals the weight of the fluid the object displaces: F_B = w_fl. It quantifies [[Buoyancy]], which arises because [[FluidPressure]] is greater on the object's lower surface than on its upper surface.

## Key Points
- An object floats when its average [[Density]] is less than the fluid's, sinks when greater, and is neutrally suspended when equal.
- For a floating object, the **fraction submerged** equals the density ratio ρ̄_obj / ρ_fl.
- **Specific gravity** = ρ̄ / ρ_water (dimensionless): < 1 floats, > 1 sinks.
- The apparent weight (or mass) loss of a submerged object equals the weight (or mass) of fluid displaced — the basis of hydrostatic weighing to find density.
- Flotation depends on **shape**, not just material: solid steel sinks, but the same steel as a hull displaces enough water to float.
- Named for [[Archimedes]]; applications include ships, submarine ballast, hydrometers, body-composition measurement, and isostasy (continents on the mantle).

## Equations
- F_B = w_fl  (buoyant force = weight of displaced fluid)
- F_B = m_fl g = ρ_fl V_fl g
- Fraction submerged = V_sub/V_obj = ρ̄_obj / ρ_fl
- specific gravity = ρ̄ / ρ_water
- Apparent weight loss = weight of fluid displaced
