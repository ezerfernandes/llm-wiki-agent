## Definition
A **carrier particle** (gauge boson) mediates one of the [[FundamentalForces|fundamental forces]] by being exchanged — as a **[[VirtualParticle|virtual particle]]** — between interacting objects. All carrier particles have integer spin, so they are **bosons**.

## Key Points
- **The carriers**: [[PhotonQuantum|photon]] (electromagnetic, massless, infinite range), W⁺/W⁻/Z⁰ (weak, massive ~81–90 GeV/c², very short range), gluons (strong, between [[Quark|quarks]]), and the conjectured graviton (gravity, massless).
- **Carrier mass sets the range**: a massless carrier gives infinite range; a heavy carrier gives short range. The W/Z are ~1000× the pion mass, giving the weak force its <10⁻¹⁸ m range.
- **Yukawa idea**: forces transmitted by exchange particles. The pion was proposed as the [[StrongNuclearForce|strong-force]] carrier between [[Nucleon|nucleons]]; its mass (~100 MeV/c²) was estimated from the force range using the uncertainty principle (the residual force; gluons are the fundamental strong carriers).
- Interactions are visualized with **Feynman diagrams** (time vertical, position horizontal), which also let physicists compute interaction strengths — the basis of quantum electrodynamics (QED).

## Equations
- Range–mass estimate: d ≈ cΔt with ΔE·Δt ≥ h/(4π), and ΔE = mc²
