# Rate Constant

The **rate constant** $k$ is the proportionality constant in a [[RateLaw|rate law]], rate = k[A]^m[B]^n…. It is specific to a particular reaction at a particular temperature. Key properties:

- **Independent of reactant concentrations** — concentration dependence is carried entirely by the [[ReactionOrder|order]] terms.
- **Temperature dependent** — k rises sharply with temperature according to the [[ArrheniusEquation|Arrhenius equation]] $k = A\,e^{-E_a/RT}$, which links k to the [[ActivationEnergy|activation energy]].

## Units depend on overall order

Because rate always has units of concentration/time (mol L⁻¹ s⁻¹), the units of k change with the [[ReactionOrder|overall order]] x so the equation stays dimensionally consistent:

| Overall order | Units of k |
|---|---|
| 0 | mol L⁻¹ s⁻¹ |
| 1 | s⁻¹ |
| 2 | L mol⁻¹ s⁻¹ |
| 3 | L² mol⁻² s⁻¹ |

General form: $\mathrm{L}^{(x-1)}\,\mathrm{mol}^{(1-x)}\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$.

k is obtained experimentally by substituting a measured rate and concentrations into the rate law, or from the slope/intercept of an [[IntegratedRateLaw|integrated rate-law]] plot.
