# SI Units

The **International System of Units (SI)** provides the standard units used throughout chemistry. Every measurement has three parts: a **magnitude** (a number), a **unit** (a standard of comparison), and an implicit **uncertainty**. Units are essential — a dose of "100" is meaningless or dangerous without "mg" vs. "g."

## Seven SI base units

| Property | Unit | Symbol |
|---|---|---|
| Length | meter | m |
| Mass | kilogram | kg |
| Time | second | s |
| Temperature | kelvin | K |
| Electric current | ampere | A |
| Amount of substance | mole | mol |
| Luminous intensity | candela | cd |

## Prefixes (powers of ten)

Fractional or multiple units use a prefix on a base unit: femto (10⁻¹⁵), pico (10⁻¹²), nano (10⁻⁹), micro (10⁻⁶), milli (10⁻³), centi (10⁻²), deci (10⁻¹), kilo (10³), mega (10⁶), giga (10⁹), tera (10¹²).

## Notes on base units
- The **second** is defined (since 1967) by 9,192,631,770 oscillations of a cesium atom.
- The **meter** is defined as the distance light travels in a vacuum in 1/299,792,458 of a second.
- The **kilogram** has, since May 2019, been defined by fundamental physical constants — specifically Planck's constant (formerly a platinum-iridium cylinder).
- The **kelvin** is written without a degree symbol.

**Derived units** combine base units — e.g., volume (m³, L, mL), [[Density|density]] (kg/m³, g/cm³, g/L), and speed (m/s = distance/time). Unit handling is the basis of [[DimensionalAnalysis]] and [[UnitConversion]].

## In Physics (OpenStax College Physics 2e, Ch. 1)
College Physics 2e introduces the SI system through four fundamental units relevant to mechanics and electricity — meter (length), kilogram (mass), second (time), and ampere (electric current) — and emphasizes the metric advantage: conversions are pure powers of 10, and prefixes let one unit span enormous ranges (atomic diameter ~10⁻¹⁰ m to the Sun's ~10⁹ m). By contrast the English system uses non-decimal relationships (12 in/ft, 5280 ft/mile). Modern SI definitions are anchored to physical constants for stability and reproducibility.
