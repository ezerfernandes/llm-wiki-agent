## Definition
**Bernoulli's equation** states that for an incompressible, frictionless (nonviscous) fluid the total mechanical energy per unit volume is constant along a streamline: P + ½ρv² + ρgh = constant. **Bernoulli's principle** is the special case at constant height — faster-moving fluid has lower [[Pressure]].

## Key Points
- It is [[ConservationOfEnergy|conservation of energy]] per unit volume: ½ρv² is kinetic energy per volume and ρgh is gravitational potential energy per volume.
- At constant height (h₁ = h₂): P₁ + ½ρv₁² = P₂ + ½ρv₂² — pressure trades off against speed.
- For a static fluid (v = 0) it reduces to the hydrostatic relation P₁ + ρgh₁ = P₂ + ρgh₂ (see [[FluidPressure]]).
- Explains wing lift, sails, entrainment devices (atomizers, aspirators, Bunsen burners), and the inward-pulled shower curtain.
- Applies only where flow is approximately frictionless and laminar; [[Viscosity]] and turbulence ([[ReynoldsNumber]]) limit its validity.
- Named for the 18th-century physicist Daniel Bernoulli.

## Equations
- General: P + ½ρv² + ρgh = constant
- Two points: P₁ + ½ρv₁² + ρgh₁ = P₂ + ½ρv₂² + ρgh₂
- Principle (constant height): P₁ + ½ρv₁² = P₂ + ½ρv₂²
- Torricelli's theorem (efflux): v² = v₁² + 2g(h₁ − h₂); v² = 2gh from rest
- Power in flow: Power = (P + ½ρv² + ρgh)·Q
