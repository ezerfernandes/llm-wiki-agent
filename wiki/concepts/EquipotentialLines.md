## Definition
**Equipotential lines** (or surfaces in 3D) are loci where the [[ElectricPotential|electric potential]] is constant. They are a geometric way to visualize potential, complementary to [[ElectricFieldLines|electric field lines]].

## Key Points
- Equipotential lines are **always perpendicular** to [[ElectricField|electric field]] lines — moving along an equipotential does no [[Work]] (W = qEd cos 90° = 0).
- Any conductor in electrostatic equilibrium is an equipotential surface; a [[PotentialDifference|potential difference]] across it would drive charge until it equalized.
- **Grounding** connects a conductor to Earth, fixing it at 0 V — the basis of electrical safety (grounded appliance housings).
- Geometry by configuration: concentric spheres around a point charge; closed contours around two opposite charges; uniformly spaced parallel surfaces between parallel plates.

## Equations
- W along an equipotential: W = −ΔPE = −qΔV = 0
- W = F d cos θ = qE d cos θ = 0  ⇒ perpendicularity (cos 90° = 0)
- Point charge: V = kQ/r
