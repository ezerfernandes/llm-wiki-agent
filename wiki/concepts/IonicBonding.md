# Ionic Bonding

**Ionic bonding** is a type of [[ChemicalBond|chemical bond]] consisting of the electrostatic forces of attraction between oppositely charged **cations** and **anions**. It forms when a metal (low ionization energy, loses electrons easily — left/bottom of the [[PeriodicTable|periodic table]]) transfers electrons to a nonmetal (high [[ElectronAffinity|electron affinity]] — upper right). (Named *IonicBonding* to avoid generic-name collisions.)

## Ion Formation and Electronic Structure

- A **cation** forms when an atom loses valence electrons; main-group atoms lose *all* valence electrons to reach the preceding noble-gas configuration (Ca → Ca²⁺ = [Ar]; Al → Al³⁺). [[ElectronConfiguration|Transition metals]] lose outermost *s* electrons first, then *d* electrons (Fe → Fe²⁺ [Ar]3d⁶ → Fe³⁺ [Ar]3d⁵).
- An **anion** forms when an atom gains enough electrons to fill its outer *s* and *p* orbitals, reaching the next noble gas (O + 2e⁻ → O²⁻ = [Ne]; Se²⁻ = [Ar]3d¹⁰4s²4p⁶; I⁻ = [Kr]4d¹⁰5s²5p⁶).
- The **inert pair effect**: heavy group 13–15 elements can lose fewer electrons than expected (Tl⁺, Sn²⁺, Pb²⁺, Bi³⁺).

See [[ChemicalIon]] for cation/anion fundamentals.

## Structure and Properties

Ionic bonds are **isotropic** (equally strong in all directions), so ions pack into tightly bound three-dimensional **lattices** rather than discrete molecules. In NaCl, each ion is surrounded by six oppositely charged neighbors. The chemical formula gives only the simplest charge-balanced ratio of ions, not the physical arrangement (Al₂O₃ = two Al³⁺ + three O²⁻; net charge 0). The resulting [[IonicCompound|ionic compounds]] are crystalline, rigid and brittle, with high melting/boiling points; they conduct electricity only when molten or dissolved (when ions are free to move).

These are the **ionic solids** among the four types of [[CrystallineSolid|crystalline solid]]. Their geometry follows from packing: the larger anions form a close-packed array and the cations occupy the [[UnitCell|holes]] (tetrahedral, octahedral, or cubic) whose size matches the cation:anion radius ratio — giving structures such as CsCl (1:1, cubic holes), NaCl (1:1, octahedral holes), zinc blende (1:1, tetrahedral holes), and fluorite CaF₂ (1:2).

## Strength

The strength of ionic bonding is measured by [[LatticeEnergy|lattice energy]]. Dissociating one mole of solid NaCl into gaseous ions requires 769 kJ.
