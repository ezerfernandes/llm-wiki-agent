# RLHF — Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback

The dominant post-training algorithm for aligning large language models with human preferences. RLHF runs a four-stage **data flywheel**:

1. **Prompt curation.** Collect a diverse, realistic distribution of user-shaped prompts.
2. **SFT on demonstrations.** Train the base model to imitate target responses on a curated set of (prompt, response) pairs.
3. **Reward Model training.** Collect pairwise (or list-wise) human preferences over candidate responses; train a reward model (RM) to predict them.
4. **RL fine-tuning.** Optimize the policy against the RM (typically with PPO or GRPO-family algorithms — see [[GRPO]]) while penalizing KL divergence from the SFT model.

The four stages run **iteratively**: RL pushes the policy past the RM's coverage, the RM is refreshed via new preference data on the new policy's outputs, and both improve in lockstep.

## Canonical reference in the wiki

[[2312.11805-gemini]] (Section 6.3) gives an unusually concrete walkthrough of the flywheel applied to a frontier model:

- Demonstrations are written by humans, generated by the model and revised by humans, or sampled with diversity heuristics.
- Pairwise preferences are collected across creativity, safety, factuality, and other targeted axes.
- A separate reward model is trained on safety-specific preferences and folded into the overall reward signal.
- Capability-specific overlays (instruction following, tool use, multilinguality, multimodal vision, coding) sit on top of the same SFT → RM → RL skeleton.

## Variants and pressure points

- **Constitutional AI** ([[ConstitutionalAI]]) replaces some human preferences with model-generated critiques rooted in an explicit policy "constitution"; Gemini's safety SFT is described as *loosely inspired* by this idea.
- **DPO / IPO** drop the explicit RM and optimize the policy directly against pairwise preferences.
- **Reward hacking** — RL can exploit RM weaknesses; iterative RM refreshes are the working mitigation.
- **Capability/safety trade-off** — over-tuned safety RMs collapse into "I cannot help" responses (the *harmlessness vs helpfulness* failure mode flagged in [[2312.11805-gemini]]).
