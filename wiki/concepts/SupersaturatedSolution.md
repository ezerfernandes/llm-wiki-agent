# Supersaturated Solution

A **supersaturated solution** contains a solute concentration *greater* than its equilibrium [[Solubility|solubility]]. It is a **nonequilibrium state** that is relatively stable but will precipitate (or, for gases, release) the excess solute when disturbed.

## How They Form
Because most solid solubilities rise with temperature, a supersaturated solution can be made by:
1. dissolving solute at an elevated temperature (where it is more soluble),
2. cooling slowly **without disturbance**, so the concentration exceeds the lower-temperature solubility.

The metastable solution then holds until crystallization is triggered by a **seed crystal** or **mechanical agitation**.

## Examples and Applications
- **Hand warmers** — supersaturated sodium acetate; clicking an internal metal disk sends a mechanical shockwave that initiates crystallization, releasing heat.
- **Gases** — a gas solution prepared cold or at high pressure becomes supersaturated when warmed or depressurized (opening a soda; the 1986 Lake Nyos CO₂ disaster that killed over 1,700; see [[HenrysLaw]]).
