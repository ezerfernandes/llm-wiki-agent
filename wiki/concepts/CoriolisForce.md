## Definition
The Coriolis force is a fictitious force observed in a rotating reference frame that deflects a moving object perpendicular to its velocity. Like the centrifugal force, it has no real physical source — it is a consequence of describing inertial straight-line motion from a rotating frame. See [[CentrifugalForce]] for the broader fictitious-force discussion.

## Key Points
- Appears only in non-inertial (rotating) frames; in an inertial frame the object simply moves in a straight line ([[NewtonsFirstLaw]]).
- A ball rolled across a rotating platform follows a straight Earth-frame path but appears to curve relative to the platform.
- On Earth it deflects large-scale winds, producing the rotation of storm systems: counterclockwise hurricanes in the Northern Hemisphere, clockwise cyclones in the Southern.
- Strongest effect for long-range, large-scale motion; negligible for everyday short-distance motion.
