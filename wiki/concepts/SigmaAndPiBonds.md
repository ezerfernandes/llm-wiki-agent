# Sigma (σ) and Pi (π) Bonds

In [[ValenceBondTheory|valence bond theory]], the [[AtomicOrbital|atomic-orbital]] overlap that forms a [[CovalentBonding|covalent bond]] produces one of two bond types distinguished by *where* the electron density lies relative to the internuclear axis. (Named *SigmaAndPiBonds* to keep the chemistry sense distinct from unrelated wiki pages.)

## σ (Sigma) Bonds

A **σ bond** is a covalent bond in which the electron density is concentrated **along the internuclear axis** — a line between the nuclei passes through the center of the overlap region. σ bonds form from:

- overlap of two *s* orbitals (H₂),
- overlap of an *s* orbital and a *p* orbital (HCl), or
- **end-to-end** overlap of two *p* orbitals (Cl₂).

Every single bond in a [[LewisStructure|Lewis structure]] is a σ bond.

## π (Pi) Bonds

A **π bond** results from the **side-by-side** overlap of two *p* orbitals. The overlap regions lie on **opposite sides** of the internuclear axis, and along the axis itself there is a **node** — a plane with zero probability of finding an electron.

## Single, Double, and Triple Bonds

- **Single bond** (H–Cl): one σ, no π.
- **Double bond** (O₂): one σ + one π.
- **Triple bond** (N₂): one σ + two π.

The **first** bond between any two atoms is always the σ bond, and there can be only one σ bond in a given location; remaining bonds in a [[MultipleBonds|multiple bond]] are π bonds.

## Relative Strength

A π bond is generally **weaker** than a σ bond between the same atoms, because the σ bond has greater overlap. Carbon–carbon bonds show this additively (per [[BondEnergy|bond energies]]): C–C 347 kJ/mol (σ); C=C 614 (σ+π); C≡C 839 (σ+2π).

## Rotation

Rotation about a σ bond is easy (end-to-end overlap is orientation-independent), but rotation about a [[MultipleBonds|multiple bond]] is restricted because it would reduce the side-by-side π overlap, effectively breaking the π bond — this is what makes alkenes planar.

This σ/π distinction drives [[OrganicChemistry|organic]] reactivity: the [[Alkene|alkene]] C=C (1 σ + 1 π) and [[Alkyne|alkyne]] C≡C (1 σ + 2 π) have weak, exposed π bonds that break in [[AdditionReaction|addition reactions]], while [[Alkane|alkanes]] (σ only) merely [[SubstitutionReaction|substitute]]. Restricted rotation about the C=C π bond produces alkene **cis/trans** ([[OrganicIsomerism|geometric]]) isomers; the [[AromaticHydrocarbon|benzene]] ring is a delocalized π system over an sp² σ framework.
