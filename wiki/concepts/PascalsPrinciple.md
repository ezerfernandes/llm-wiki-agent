## Definition
**Pascal's principle** states that a change in [[Pressure]] applied to an enclosed (confined) fluid is transmitted undiminished to every part of the fluid and to the walls of its container. Because the added pressure appears everywhere equally, applying it to pistons of different areas lets a small input force produce a large output force.

## Key Points
- Requires an **enclosed fluid** — one that cannot escape — so pressure can build and propagate.
- Forms the basis of **hydraulic systems** (car brakes, lifts, presses): a small force on a small-area piston creates the same pressure that acts over a larger-area piston, multiplying the [[Force]].
- Force multiplication equals the area ratio; it trades distance for force (the larger piston moves less), consistent with energy conservation.
- The simple piston relation assumes both pistons are at the same height and friction is negligible.
- Named for [[BlaisePascal]]; underlies remote pressure measurement (see [[GaugePressure]]).

## Equations
- P = F / A
- F₁/A₁ = F₂/A₂  (hydraulic force relationship, equal-height pistons)
