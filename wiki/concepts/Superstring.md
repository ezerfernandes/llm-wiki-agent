## Definition
Superstring theory is a theoretical framework proposing that fundamental particles — including the graviton that carries gravity — are not point particles but tiny vibrating one-dimensional objects (strings). Different vibration modes correspond to different particles. Because it incorporates gravity, it is a leading candidate for a **Theory of Everything (TOE)**.

## Key Points
- A **Theory of Everything** would unify all four fundamental forces; superstrings can do so because gravity shapes the spacetime in which all other phenomena occur (see [[QuantumGravity]]).
- Operates in **superspace**, a higher-dimensional construct where each independent quantum number is a distinct dimension and string type.
- **Spontaneous symmetry breaking** as the universe expanded after the [[BigBang]] caused unified forces to separate and extra superspace dimensions to **compactify** (curl up) and become imperceptible.
- The relevant **Planck scale** (~10⁻³⁵ m) is the size at which quantum gravity dominates; if superstrings are correct, the string is the ultimate minimum scale with no further substructure.
- Experiments currently probe only to ~10⁻¹⁸ m, leaving ~17 orders of magnitude between data and the string scale — granting large theoretical freedom and limited testability.
- Raises ideas about multiverse scenarios with varying physical constants, and has advanced pure mathematics independent of empirical confirmation.
