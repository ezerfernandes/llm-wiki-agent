## Definition
A nuclear reactor sustains a controlled [[NuclearChainReaction]] to release energy steadily, using [[NuclearFission]] of fuels such as ²³⁵U or ²³⁹Pu. It is engineered to stay critical (x ≈ 1) without becoming supercritical.

## Key Points
- **Moderator** — material (often water) that slows fast neutrons to thermal speeds where ²³⁵U readily absorbs them and fissions.
- **Control rods** — strong neutron absorbers inserted or withdrawn to regulate neutron flux and keep the reaction critical, preventing runaway fission and meltdown.
- **Pressurized-water reactors:** fuel rods sit in water that both moderates neutrons and provides negative feedback — if the core overheats and water boils away, moderation drops and the chain reaction stops.
- **Breeder reactors:** convert abundant ²³⁸U into fissile ²³⁹Pu, potentially using ~99% of natural uranium; economic and safety concerns remain.

## Equations
- Energy per fission from [[NuclearFission]]: E = Δm·c²
- Criticality condition from [[NuclearChainReaction]]: x ≈ 1 for steady operation
