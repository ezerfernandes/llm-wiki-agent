## Definition
The kinematic equations (motion equations for constant acceleration in one dimension) relate [[Displacement]], [[Velocity]], [[Acceleration]], and time for an object undergoing constant acceleration. They let you predict future motion from initial conditions.

## Key Points
- Valid only when [[Acceleration]] is constant; then average and instantaneous acceleration are equal, and average velocity is the arithmetic mean of initial and final velocities.
- Notation uses t₀ = 0 so Δt = t; x₀, v₀ are initial position/velocity; x, v are final.
- Displacement grows with the square of elapsed time when acceleration is nonzero.
- Problems can require solving a quadratic for time (discard the unphysical negative root).

## Equations
- v̄ = (v₀ + v)/2
- x = x₀ + v̄t
- v = v₀ + at
- x = x₀ + v₀t + ½at²
- v² = v₀² + 2a(x − x₀)
