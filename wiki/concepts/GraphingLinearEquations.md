# Graphing Linear Equations

A **linear equation in two variables** has standard form `Ax + By = C` (A, B, C constants), and **its graph is a straight line** in the [[RectangularCoordinateSystem|rectangular coordinate system]]. The line is the complete picture of the equation's solution set: *every point on the line is a solution, and every solution is a point on the line*. Covered in OpenStax [[Prealgebra]] 2e Chapter 11 ([[prealgebra-2e-ch11-graphs]]). This extends the one-variable [[LinearEquation|linear equations]] of Ch 8 to two variables.

## Method 1 — Plotting points
1. Find solutions by choosing values for one variable and using [[SolvingLinearEquations|equation-solving]] to get the other; organize them in a table of `(x, y)` pairs.
2. **Plot** the points and check that they are collinear.
3. Draw the line through them, with arrows showing it continues.

Two points determine a line, but **three are recommended**: if one is wrong, three points won't line up, exposing the error.

## Method 2 — Intercepts
Graph from the points where the line crosses the axes — see [[Intercepts]]. This is the natural choice when the equation is given in standard form `Ax + By = C`.

## Method 3 — Point plus slope
Plot one point, then use the [[Slope|slope]] `m = rise/run` to count off a second point.

## Special lines
- **Vertical line `x = a`** — every point has x-coordinate a; a vertical line through `(a, 0)`. Its slope is **undefined**.
- **Horizontal line `y = b`** — every point has y-coordinate b; a horizontal line through `(0, b)`. Its slope is **0**.

## Choosing a method
| Equation form | Best method |
|---|---|
| `x = a` or `y = b` | Draw the vertical/horizontal line directly |
| `y = mx + b` (y isolated) | Plot points using chosen x-values |
| `Ax + By = C` (standard form) | Use the intercepts |
