# Radioactive Decay (Chemistry)

> Disambiguation: this is the OpenStax *Chemistry 2e* (Ch 21) treatment. For the College Physics 2e treatment see [[RadioactiveDecay]] (and [[AlphaDecay]], [[BetaDecay]], [[Radioactivity]]).

**Radioactive decay** is the spontaneous change of an unstable **parent** nuclide into a **daughter** nuclide (which may be stable or decay further). **Radioactivity** (a term coined by Marie Curie) is the resulting emission of radiation. An unstable [[NuclideNotation|nuclide]] decays in the mode that moves its daughter toward the [[BandOfStability|band of stability]].

### Modes of decay

| Mode | Emits / absorbs | Δ A | Δ Z | Nuclear change | Occurs when |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Alpha (α)** | emits $^{4}_{2}\text{He}$ | −4 | −2 | loses 2p + 2n | heavy nuclei (A>200, Z>83) |
| **Beta (β)** | emits $^{0}_{-1}\text{e}$ | 0 | +1 | n → p + e | high n:p (above band) |
| **Gamma (γ)** | emits $^{0}_{0}\gamma$ | 0 | 0 | excited → ground state | accompanies other decays |
| **Positron (β⁺)** | emits $^{0}_{+1}\text{e}$ | 0 | −1 | p → n + e⁺ | low n:p (below band) |
| **Electron capture** | absorbs $^{0}_{-1}\text{e}$ | 0 | −1 | p + e → n | proton-rich (below band) |

Example equations:
- α: $^{210}_{84}\text{Po} \rightarrow ^{4}_{2}\text{He} + ^{206}_{82}\text{Pb}$
- β: $^{131}_{53}\text{I} \rightarrow ^{0}_{-1}\text{e} + ^{131}_{54}\text{Xe}$
- γ: $^{60}_{27}\text{Co}^{*} \rightarrow ^{0}_{0}\gamma + ^{60}_{27}\text{Co}$
- β⁺: $^{15}_{8}\text{O} \rightarrow ^{0}_{+1}\text{e} + ^{15}_{7}\text{N}$
- Electron capture: $^{40}_{19}\text{K} + ^{0}_{-1}\text{e} \rightarrow ^{40}_{18}\text{Ar}$

Positron emission and electron capture have the *same* effect on the nucleus (Z−1); electron capture also leaves an inner-shell vacancy that emits X-rays. Every equation obeys the [[NuclearEquation|conservation of A and Z]].

### Predicting the mode
- Above the band (high n:p) → **β decay**
- Below the band (low n:p) → **positron emission** or **electron capture**
- Heavy, beyond the band → **α decay**

### Radioactive decay series
The heaviest natural radioisotopes decay through a chain of successive disintegrations — a **radioactive decay series** (radioactive family). The three major series (**uranium, actinide, thorium**) each begin with a long-lived parent and end at a stable lead isotope; a fourth (neptunium) series has faded because its members are short-lived.

The timing of decay is strictly first-order — see [[RadioactiveHalfLife]].
