## Definition
A solenoid is a tightly wound helical coil of wire that produces a strong, nearly uniform [[MagneticField]] in its interior while keeping the external field weak. It is the canonical electromagnet geometry.

## Key Points
- The interior field depends only on the current and the turns per unit length, not on the coil radius: B = μ₀nI.
- A ferromagnetic core (see [[Ferromagnetism]]) greatly increases the field for a given current.
- Reaching MRI-grade fields (≈ 2 T) requires very large currents, motivating superconducting coils.
- The interior field is the limiting, uniform case of the fields described by [[AmperesLaw]].

## Equations
- B = μ₀nI, where n = N/l (turns per unit length) and μ₀ = 4π × 10⁻⁷ T·m/A
