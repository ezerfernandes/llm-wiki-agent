# Complex Ion

A **complex ion** (or **coordination complex**) is an ion in which a **central atom** — typically a transition-metal cation — is bonded to a set of surrounding **[[Ligand|ligands]]**. The ligands are [[LewisBase|Lewis bases]] (neutral molecules such as H₂O or NH₃, or ions such as CN⁻, OH⁻, S₂O₃²⁻) that donate electron pairs to the central metal ([[LewisAcid|Lewis acid]]) through [[CoordinateCovalentBond|coordinate covalent bonds]]. The study of such species is **coordination chemistry**.

Examples: [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺, Cu(CN)₂⁻, [BF₄]⁻ (boron center), Al(OH)₄⁻, Ag(S₂O₃)₂³⁻.

## Formation and dissociation

A complex ion forms by combination of a metal ion with ligands; the equilibrium constant is the [[FormationConstant|formation constant Kf]] (a large Kf means a very stable complex). For Ag(NH₃)₂⁺ (Kf = 1.7 × 10⁷), a 0.10 M solution dissociates only ~1.1% ([Ag⁺] = 1.1 × 10⁻³ M).

Because complex formation removes free metal ion from solution, coupling it to a dissolution equilibrium can dramatically increase a solid's solubility — see [[CoupledEquilibria|coupled equilibria]] (e.g. dissolving AgCl with NH₃ or AgBr with thiosulfate in photographic fixing).

## Structure and coordination chemistry (Ch 19)

The full structural vocabulary is developed under [[CoordinationCompound|coordination compounds]]: the **coordination sphere** (the metal plus its ligands, shown in brackets, with any **counter ions** outside) and the **coordination number** (the count of donor atoms — 2 linear, 4 tetrahedral/square planar, 6 octahedral). Complex ions show [[CoordinationIsomerism|geometric, optical, linkage, and ionization isomerism]], are named by [[CoordinationNomenclature|IUPAC rules]], and their color/magnetism follow from [[CrystalFieldTheory|crystal field d-orbital splitting]]. They appear in [[TransitionMetalMetallurgy|metallurgy]] too (silver as [Ag(CN)₂]⁻).
