## Definition
**Approximation** (estimation) is making a calculated guess about a quantity when precise measurements are unavailable, often because the inputs to a formula are themselves known only to limited accuracy.

## Key Points
- A practical physics skill built from quantitative thinking, willingness to take calculated risks, familiarity with [[UnitConversion]], and experience with reference quantities.
- Its purpose is to exclude unrealistic scenarios, challenge assumptions, and guide scientific reasoning — and to sanity-check exact calculations.
- Closely related to [[OrderOfMagnitude]] reasoning (estimating the nearest power of 10).
- Worked Example 1.3 estimates a 39-story building's height by scaling from a human reference (~2 m/story → ≈156 m).
- Worked Example 1.4 makes an abstract quantity visualizable: one trillion dollars in $100 bills stacked on a football field ≈ 8 ft high.

## Equations
- No fixed formula; estimates are built by scaling known reference quantities and combining them with [[DimensionalAnalysis]].
