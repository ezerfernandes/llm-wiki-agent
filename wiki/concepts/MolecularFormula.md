# Molecular Formula

A **molecular formula** gives the *actual* number of atoms of each element in one molecule of a compound. (For its definition alongside empirical and structural formulas, see [[ChemicalFormula]].) This page covers how a molecular formula is **derived** from an [[EmpiricalFormula|empirical formula]] plus a measured molar mass.

## Derivation Method

The molecular formula is always a whole-number multiple of the empirical formula. The multiplier n is:

$$n = \frac{\text{molecular (or molar) mass}}{\text{empirical formula mass}}$$

Then multiply every subscript in the empirical formula by n: (AₓBᵧ)ₙ = AₙₓBₙᵧ. Determining n requires *both* the empirical formula and an independently measured molecular/molar mass, because composition alone fixes only the ratio.

## Worked Cases

- **Glucose:** empirical formula CH₂O (empirical mass ≈ 30 amu); if molecular mass = 180 amu, n = 180/30 = 6 → (CH₂O)₆ = **C₆H₁₂O₆**.
- **Nicotine:** 74.02% C, 8.710% H, 17.27% N give empirical formula C₅H₇N (empirical mass 81.13 g/mol). A 40.57 g sample = 0.2500 mol, so molar mass = 162.3 g/mol; n = 162.3 / 81.13 = 2 → (C₅H₇N)₂ = **C₁₀H₁₄N₂**.
