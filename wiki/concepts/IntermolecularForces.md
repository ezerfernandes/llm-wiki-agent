# Intermolecular Forces

**Intermolecular forces (IMFs)** are the attractions *between* neutral molecules (or atoms), as opposed to the **intramolecular forces** (covalent bonds) that hold atoms *within* a molecule. IMFs determine the physical properties of condensed phases — melting and boiling points, [[LiquidViscosity|viscosity]], [[LiquidSurfaceTension|surface tension]], [[VaporPressureChemistry|vapor pressure]], and the enthalpies of phase change.

IMFs are typically much weaker than covalent bonds: overcoming the IMFs in one mole of liquid HCl takes ~17 kJ, while breaking the H–Cl covalent bonds takes ~430 kJ.

## Phase Depends on IMFs vs. Kinetic Energy

"The phase in which a substance exists depends on the relative extents of its intermolecular forces (IMFs) and the kinetic energies (KE) of its molecules." IMFs pull particles together; kinetic energy (set by temperature) drives them apart. Cooling lowers KE, allowing attractions to win (condensation, freezing); heating raises KE until attractions are overcome. This is why the [[KineticMolecularTheory|kinetic-molecular]] picture of solids, liquids, and gases ([[StatesOfMatter]]) follows from the IMF/KE balance.

## Van der Waals Forces

"All of the attractive forces between neutral atoms and molecules are known as van der Waals forces." There are three, in order of increasing strength:

1. **[[LondonDispersionForces|London dispersion forces]]** — present in *all* substances; arise from instantaneous/induced dipoles; scale with size and polarizability.
2. **[[DipoleDipoleForces|Dipole-dipole attractions]]** — between permanent dipoles in [[MolecularPolarity|polar]] molecules.
3. **[[HydrogenBonding|Hydrogen bonding]]** — the strongest IMF; a special, strong dipole-dipole force requiring an F–H, O–H, or N–H group.

(Ion-dipole and ion-induced-dipole forces involve ions and so are sometimes grouped separately.)

## IMFs and the Dissolution Process

IMFs also determine whether and how substances dissolve. The [[DissolutionProcess|dissolution process]] is a competition among three IMF sets — solute-solute, solvent-solvent, and solute-solvent ([[Solvation|solvation]]) attractions — whose net gives the [[EnthalpyOfSolution|enthalpy of solution]]. When all three are equal, an [[IdealSolution|ideal solution]] forms with no energy change. Matching polarity (the [[LikeDissolvesLike|"like dissolves like"]] rule) reflects which IMFs can form across the solute-solvent boundary; **ion-dipole** attractions in particular drive the solvation of ions in water that makes ionic compounds strong [[Electrolyte|electrolytes]].
