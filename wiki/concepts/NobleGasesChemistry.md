# Noble Gases Chemistry (Group 18)

The **noble gases** — helium, neon, argon, krypton, xenon, radon — are the group 18 elements with filled valence shells. Once thought completely inert, this was **disproved in 1962** by Neil Bartlett (University of British Columbia), who showed xenon forms real compounds.

## Occurrence and Preparation
Present in small amounts in the atmosphere. Helium is isolated from natural gas (1–2% He by mass; most commercial He from US gas fields) by liquefying the condensable components. Ar, Ne, Kr, Xe come from fractional distillation of liquid air. Radon arises from radioactive decay in soils and minerals and accumulates in well-insulated buildings (a lung-cancer hazard).

## Physical Properties
Very low boiling/melting points (weak London dispersion forces). **Helium** is the only substance that does not solidify on cooling at normal pressure (liquid near 0.001 K; bp 4.2 K).

## Reactivity and Compounds
No stable He or Ne compounds are known; stable Ar compounds form only at low temperatures; **xenon** readily forms stable compounds with fluorine and oxygen:
- **XeF₂** (excess Xe + F₂), **XeF₄** (stoichiometric F₂, excess Xe), **XeF₆** (excess F₂).
- Xenon oxides by replacing F with O: XeF₆(s) + 3H₂O(l) → XeO₃(aq) + 6HF(aq) (dry solid XeO₃ is explosive).
- XeF₆ and XeO₃ disproportionate in base to Xe, O₂, and **perxenate XeO₆⁴⁻** (xenon at the maximum 8+ state).
- Krypton forms KrF₂ (thermally unstable at room T); radon forms RnF₂.

## Applications
He (balloons, He–O₂ diving mixes, cryogenics, MRI superconductivity), Ne (neon signs), Ar (light bulbs, fluorescent tubes; third most abundant gas in dry air), Kr (high-speed flash photography), Xe (compounds above; lighting).
