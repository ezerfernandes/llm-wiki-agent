# Nuclear Equation

A **nuclear reaction** is any change of nuclei that alters their atomic numbers, mass numbers, or energy states. A **nuclear equation** represents such a reaction, using [[NuclideNotation|nuclide notation]] $^{A}_{Z}X$ for every nuclide and particle. Unlike a chemical equation (which rearranges *atoms*), a balanced nuclear equation reflects a rearrangement of **nucleons**.

### Two conservation laws
A nuclear equation is balanced when both hold:
1. **Conservation of mass number (A)** — the sum of the mass numbers of the reactants equals the sum for the products.
2. **Conservation of atomic number / charge (Z)** — the sum of the charges (atomic numbers) of the reactants equals the sum for the products.

If every particle but one has known A and Z, the missing particle is found by difference, then identified by its Z on the periodic table.

### Worked example
The reaction of an α particle with magnesium-25 yields a proton plus an unknown nuclide:
$$^{25}_{12}\text{Mg} + ^{4}_{2}\text{He} \rightarrow ^{1}_{1}\text{H} + ^{A}_{Z}\text{X}$$
- Mass numbers: 25 + 4 = A + 1 → **A = 28**
- Atomic numbers: 12 + 2 = Z + 1 → **Z = 13** (aluminum)
- Product: $^{28}_{13}\text{Al}$

Antimatter annihilation, $^{0}_{-1}\text{e} + ^{0}_{+1}\text{e} \rightarrow \gamma + \gamma$, likewise conserves A (0) and Z (0).

### Historic examples (all balanced)
- $^{212}_{84}\text{Po} \rightarrow ^{208}_{82}\text{Pb} + ^{4}_{2}\text{He}$ (Curies, 1898)
- $^{14}_{7}\text{N} + ^{4}_{2}\text{He} \rightarrow ^{17}_{8}\text{O} + ^{1}_{1}\text{H}$ (Rutherford, 1919 — first transmutation)
- $^{9}_{4}\text{Be} + ^{4}_{2}\text{He} \rightarrow ^{12}_{6}\text{C} + ^{1}_{0}\text{n}$ (Chadwick, 1932 — neutron discovery)

The same conservation rules govern every [[RadioactiveDecayChemistry|radioactive-decay]] mode, [[NuclearTransmutation|transmutation]], [[NuclearFissionChemistry|fission]], and [[NuclearFusionChemistry|fusion]] equation.
