# Conservation of Momentum

## Definition
The total [[LinearMomentum]] of an isolated system — one with zero net external force — remains constant over time. It is one of the fundamental conservation laws of physics, on par with [[ConservationOfEnergy]].

## Key Points
- The law follows from [[NewtonsThirdLaw]]: the internal forces two colliding bodies exert on each other are equal and opposite, so the momentum one gains the other loses, and the totals cancel (Δp₁ + Δp₂ = 0).
- "Isolated" means F_net = 0; external forces such as friction or gravity, if present, change the system's momentum.
- Conservation can apply per-axis: a projectile conserves horizontal momentum (no net horizontal force) but not vertical momentum (gravity acts downward).
- The law makes collisions analyzable even when internal forces are unknown, and is the backbone of scattering experiments in particle physics (revealing properties of unseen targets and new particles like quarks).
- Recoil — the backward momentum a body gains from pushing on another — is a direct consequence (guns, squids, jellyfish, rockets).

## Equations
- F₂ = −F₁ ; Δp₁ + Δp₂ = 0
- p_tot = constant ; p_tot = p'_tot
- p₁ + p₂ = p'₁ + p'₂
- F_net = Δp_tot/Δt
