## Definition
Gamma rays (γ) are the highest-frequency, shortest-wavelength band of the [[ElectromagneticSpectrum]], overlapping the upper end of [[XRay|X-rays]]. They are produced by nuclear decay and nuclear processes in reactors and weapons. Named "gamma" because their nature was unknown at their 1896 discovery.

## Key Points
- The most penetrating form of nuclear radiation.
- Physically identical to X-rays of the same frequency — the two differ only by **source** (nuclear for gamma, atomic/electronic for X-rays).
- Highly ionizing, so both biologically hazardous and therapeutically useful.

## Equations
- `c = f λ` (highest f, shortest λ of all EM radiation).

## Applications
Nuclear medicine (diagnostic imaging from radioisotopes concentrating in tissue), cancer therapy, food preservation, security scanning, and studying stellar/galactic phenomena via space-based gamma-ray observatories.
