## Definition
Collisions of extended bodies are impacts involving objects that can rotate (not just point masses). When external forces exert no torque about a chosen pivot, total [[AngularMomentum]] is conserved through the collision (L = L′), even when linear momentum is not.

## Key Points
- A support/pivot (a nail, a racket handle) can exert an unbalanced external *force* without producing *torque*, provided its moment arm about the pivot is zero — so [[ConservationOfAngularMomentum]] still applies even though linear momentum is not conserved.
- Such collisions are typically inelastic: kinetic energy is not conserved (the post-collision rotational KE is less than the incoming translational KE).
- The percussion point ("sweet spot") is the impact location that produces no net force at the pivot/support — hitting there minimizes the jarring transmitted to a hand (tennis racket, baseball bat).
- Linear momentum can even *increase* after such a collision because of the pivot's forward reaction force.

## Equations
- Conservation about pivot: L = L′
- Incoming point mass: L = m v r
- Disk striking a rod pivoted at one end: I′ = m r² + M r²/3 = (m + M/3) r²
- Post-collision angular velocity: ω′ = m v r / I′
- KE = ½ m v² (before); KE′ = ½ I′ ω′² (after); p′ = (m + M/2) r ω′
