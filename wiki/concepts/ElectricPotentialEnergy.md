## Definition
**Electric potential energy** (PE) is the energy a [[ElectricCharge|charge]] possesses by virtue of its position in an [[ElectricField|electric field]] (measured in joules). Because the electrostatic [[Force]] is a [[ConservativeForce|conservative force]] — its [[Work]] is path-independent — a potential energy can be defined for it, exactly as for gravity (see [[PotentialEnergy]]).

## Key Points
- Work done by the conservative electrostatic force equals the negative of the change in electric PE: W = −ΔPE.
- Energy is conserved: KE_i + PE_i = KE_f + PE_f. A free charge released in a field converts PE into [[KineticEnergy]] (see [[ConservationOfEnergy]]).
- Electric PE relates to [[ElectricPotential]] (voltage) by PE = qV; only differences ΔPE matter, so the zero reference is arbitrary (often infinity).
- The [[ElectronVolt]] is the natural unit at submicroscopic scales.

## Equations
- ΔPE = q ΔV
- W = −ΔPE
- KE_i + PE_i = KE_f + PE_f
- Speed of a charge accelerated through V: v = √(2qV/m)
