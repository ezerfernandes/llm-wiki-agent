# Sulfur Chemistry

**Sulfur** is a group 16 chalcogen, chemically similar to [[OxygenChemistry|oxygen]] but with a far richer allotropy and many positive oxidation states. It is essential in many proteins.

## Occurrence and Preparation
Found as elemental deposits, sulfide minerals (Fe, Zn, Pb, Cu), sulfate minerals (Na, Ca, Ba, Mg), and as H₂S in natural gas and volcanic gases. Mined industrially by the **Frasch process**: three concentric pipes — superheated water (170 °C, 10 atm) melts the deposit, compressed air lifts the molten sulfur to the surface, yielding 99.5–99.9% pure sulfur. H₂S recovered during natural-gas purification is an alternative source.

## Allotropes
The stable room-temperature form contains eight-membered crown rings — true formula **S₈** (equations conventionally write S for simpler coefficients). **Rhombic sulfur** (yellow) is most stable; **monoclinic sulfur** (needles) is stable 96–119 °C. On melting, S₈ rings break into polymeric chains, darkening and thickening the liquid; rapid cooling gives plastic (amorphous) sulfur. Near 1000 °C the vapor is mostly **S₂** (paramagnetic, like O₂).

## Chemical Properties
A nonmetal that forms binary sulfides (2−) with metals and shows multiple positive states. It oxidizes less electronegative nonmetals and is oxidized by O₂, halogens, and strong oxidizers:

> S(s) + 6HNO₃(aq) → 2H₃O⁺(aq) + SO₄²⁻(aq) + 6NO₂(g)

**Hydrogen sulfide (H₂S)** — the rotten-egg gas, as toxic as HCN (it paralyzes the olfactory nerves, so it is deceptively hard to detect). A weak diprotic acid (hydrosulfuric acid) and good reducing agent: 2H₂S(g) + O₂(g) → 2S(s) + 2H₂O(l). Prepared by FeS(s) + 2H₃O⁺(aq) → Fe²⁺(aq) + H₂S(g) + 2H₂O(l). (See also [[HydrogenChemistry]].)
