## Definition
**Electric power** is the rate at which electrical energy is converted or dissipated in a circuit, P = IV. Its SI unit is the watt (W), the same unit as mechanical [[Power]]. Combining with [[OhmsLaw|Ohm's law]] gives the equivalent forms P = I²R and P = V²/R.

## Key Points
- Higher-wattage devices draw more [[ElectricCurrent|current]] at the same [[Voltage|voltage]].
- Because V appears squared in P = V²/R, doubling the voltage across a fixed resistor quadruples the power.
- In a resistor, power becomes heat (Joule heating), the mechanism behind both useful heaters and dangerous wire overheating ([[ElectricalSafety]]).
- Electrical **energy** is E = Pt, billed in kilowatt-hours (1 kW·h = 3.6 × 10⁶ J); cost = power × time × rate.
- Efficient lighting (LED/CFL) delivers the same useful output at 4–10× lower power than incandescent bulbs, cutting energy cost over the device lifetime.

## Equations
- P = I V
- P = I² R
- P = V² / R
- E = P t
- 1 kW·h = 3.6 × 10⁶ J;  cost = P × t × rate
