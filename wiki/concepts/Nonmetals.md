# Nonmetals (General Properties and Structures)

**Nonmetals** occupy the upper-right of the [[PeriodicTable|periodic table]]. More than half are gases, one is a liquid (Br₂), and the rest are solids spanning the softest to the hardest materials. They include the most and least reactive elements and form many ionic and covalent compounds.

## Bonding, Ions, and Oxidation States
Nonmetals have higher [[Electronegativity|electronegativities]] than metals. Metal–nonmetal compounds are generally ionic; nonmetal–nonmetal compounds are covalent and tend to be molecular. Nonmetals do not form monatomic positive ions (ionization energies too high); all monatomic nonmetal ions are anions (Cl⁻, N³⁻, Se²⁻). Common states: H (+1, −1); C (+4, −4); N/P/As (+5, +3, −3); O/S/Se (+6, +4, −2); halogens (+7, +5, +3, +1, −1); Xe (+8, +6, +4, +2).

**First-member effect:** the first member of each group differs (smaller, higher ionization energy, only four valence orbitals — no d orbitals, and stronger π bonding). E.g., N forms only NF₃ while P forms PF₃ and PF₅.

## Redox Chemistry
Five general patterns: (1) nonmetals oxidize metals (4Fe + 3O₂ → 2Fe₂O₃); (2) more electronegative nonmetals oxidize less electronegative ones (Cl₂ + 2I⁻ → I₂ + 2Cl⁻); (3) F and O are the strongest oxidizers in their groups; (4) the stronger the oxidizer, the harder its anion is to oxidize; (5) F is never positive and O is positive only with F. See [[OxidationReduction]].

## Oxides of Nonmetals
Mostly **acidic covalent oxides** (exceptions CO, N₂O, NO). Three traits: **acid anhydrides** (SO₂ + H₂O → H₂SO₃; N₂O₅ + H₂O → 2HNO₃); **disproportionation** (3NO₂ + H₂O → 2HNO₃ + NO); acid strength rises with central-atom electronegativity. Binary hydrogen-compound acid strength rises left→right and down a group (only HCl, HBr, HI are strong).

## Structures and Allotropes
Nonmetal solids have localized covalent bonds, often discrete molecules. Noble gases are monatomic; H₂/N₂/O₂/F₂/Cl₂ are diatomic. The **[[OctetRule|octet rule]]** explains bond counts: group 15 forms three bonds, group 16 two, group 17 one. Many nonmetals have allotropes — oxygen (O₂, ozone O₃), phosphorus (white/red/black), sulfur (S₈ rings and more), and carbon (diamond, graphite, fullerenes, nanotubes, graphene), detailed in [[CarbonGroupChemistry]], [[NitrogenGroupChemistry]], [[OxygenChemistry]], and [[SulfurChemistry]].
