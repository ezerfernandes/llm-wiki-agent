# Hydrolysis of Salts

**Salt hydrolysis** is the acid-base reaction of a salt's dissolved ions with water. Because a salt's cation and anion are each the [[ConjugateAcidBasePair|conjugate]] of some acid or base, an aqueous salt solution may be **acidic, basic, or neutral** depending on the relative acid-base strengths of its constituent ions.

## The four cases

| Salt of… | Cation behavior | Anion behavior | Solution |
|---|---|---|---|
| strong acid + strong base | inert | inert | **neutral** (e.g. NaCl, KBr) |
| weak acid + strong base | inert | weak base (anion hydrolysis) | **basic** (e.g. NaCH₃CO₂) |
| strong acid + weak base | weak acid (cation hydrolysis) | inert | **acidic** (e.g. NH₄Cl) |
| weak acid + weak base | weak acid | weak base | compare Ka vs Kb |

**Basic (anion hydrolysis):** the conjugate base of a weak acid reacts with water,
$$\mathrm{CH_3CO_2^-(aq) + H_2O(l) \rightleftharpoons CH_3CO_2H(aq) + OH^-(aq)},\quad K_b = K_w/K_a.$$

**Acidic (cation hydrolysis):** the conjugate acid of a weak base reacts with water,
$$\mathrm{NH_4^+(aq) + H_2O(l) \rightleftharpoons H_3O^+(aq) + NH_3(aq)},\quad K_a = K_w/K_b.$$
The conjugate base of a *strong* acid (Cl⁻, Br⁻, NO₃⁻) is inert: Kb = Kw/Ka ≈ 0.

**Both ions reactive:** compare the two constants — if Ka > Kb the solution is acidic; if Kb > Ka it is basic. E.g. for [[Amphoteric|amphiprotic]] HCO₃⁻ (in NaHCO₃), Kb (2.3×10⁻⁸) ≫ Ka (4.7×10⁻¹¹), so the solution is basic; for NH₄F, Ka(NH₄⁺) > Kb(F⁻), so it is acidic.

## Hydrated metal ions

Most metal ions (except group 1 and some group 2) act as weak acids by hydrolyzing their coordinated water, because coordination polarizes the O–H bonds:
$$\mathrm{[Al(H_2O)_6]^{3+}(aq) + H_2O(l) \rightleftharpoons H_3O^+(aq) + [Al(H_2O)_5(OH)]^{2+}(aq)},\quad K_a = 1.4\times10^{-5}.$$
These are [[PolyproticAcid|polyprotic]] (successive proton losses) and their acid strength increases with higher metal charge and smaller size (Fe³⁺ pKa 2.74, Cu²⁺ ≈ 6.3, Zn²⁺ 9.6).
