# Acid-Base Reaction

An **acid-base reaction** involves the transfer of a **hydrogen ion (H⁺)** from one chemical species to another. It is one of the three classes in [[ClassifyingChemicalReactions]]. The general proton-transfer view (donor = acid, acceptor = base) and the equilibrium treatment of acid/base strength are developed in [[BronstedLowryAcidsAndBases]] and the rest of Ch 14.

## Acids

An **acid** dissolves in water to yield hydronium ions (H₃O⁺):

HCl(aq) + H₂O(l) → Cl⁻(aq) + H₃O⁺(aq)

- **Strong acids** react with water essentially 100% (complete ionization). The common ones: **HBr, HCl, HI, HNO₃, HClO₄, H₂SO₄**.
- **Weak acids** ionize only partially; most molecules stay intact and only a little H₃O⁺ forms. Acetic acid is typical (~1% ionized):
  CH₃CO₂H(aq) + H₂O(l) ⇌ CH₃CO₂⁻(aq) + H₃O⁺(aq)

## Bases

A **base** dissolves in water to yield hydroxide ions (OH⁻). The most common are ionic hydroxides of alkali / alkaline-earth metals (NaOH, Ca(OH)₂).

- **Strong bases** dissociate completely: NaOH(s) → Na⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq).
- **Weak bases** generate OH⁻ by reacting only partially with water. Ammonia is typical (~1% as NH₄⁺):
  NH₃(aq) + H₂O(l) ⇌ NH₄⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq)

## Neutralization

A **neutralization reaction** is the specific acid-base reaction of an acid with a base, typically giving a **salt** and **water**:

acid + base → salt + water

Antacid example: Mg(OH)₂(s) + 2HCl(aq) → MgCl₂(aq) + 2H₂O(l) (MgCl₂ is the salt).

Neutralization stoichiometry of known concentration is the basis of acid-base [[QuantitativeChemicalAnalysis|titration]].
