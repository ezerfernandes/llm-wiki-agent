# Tyndall Effect

The **Tyndall effect** is the scattering of light by the suspended particles of a [[Colloid|colloid]]. Colloidal particles are "large enough to scatter light," which makes colloidal mixtures appear cloudy or opaque — for example, a searchlight beam made visible in fog, or the whiteness of clouds (colloidal water droplets).

## Why It Distinguishes Colloids
True solutions do not show the Tyndall effect: their dissolved molecules and ions are too small to scatter visible light, so light passes through unscattered. The Tyndall effect is therefore a simple qualitative test that separates a colloid from a true [[SolutionConcentration|solution]].
