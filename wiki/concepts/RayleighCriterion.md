## Definition
The Rayleigh criterion defines the diffraction limit on resolution: two point sources are just barely resolvable when the center of one's [[Diffraction]] pattern falls on the first minimum of the other's. It sets the fundamental limit on how finely any optical system can distinguish detail.

## Key Points
- Diffraction is unavoidable (a consequence of light's [[Wave]] nature), so it limits all optics regardless of build quality.
- A larger aperture diameter D narrows the diffraction spread and improves resolution; the same applies to higher numerical aperture NA = n sin α.
- Limits telescope resolution (e.g., Hubble's 2.40 m mirror), microscope detail, and eye acuity (set by pupil diameter).
- Governs beam divergence: a laser/microwave beam spreads at angle θ = 1.22 λ/D, so a wider beam diverges less over long distances.
- Connects to the Heisenberg uncertainty principle as a deep limit on observation.

## Equations
- Angular resolution (circular aperture): `θ = 1.22 λ / D`
- Linear resolution: `x = 1.22 λ d / D` (d = object-to-lens distance)
- Microscope form: `x = 0.61 λ / (n · NA)`
