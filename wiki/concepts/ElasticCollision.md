# Elastic Collision

## Definition
An elastic collision is a [[Collision]] that conserves internal kinetic energy — the sum of the colliding bodies' kinetic energies is the same before and after. Both [[LinearMomentum|momentum]] and kinetic energy are conserved.

## Key Points
- Solving an elastic collision uses two equations simultaneously: conservation of momentum and conservation of internal [[KineticEnergy]]. This typically yields two algebraic roots, of which one is physically meaningful.
- A light object striking a stationary heavier one bounces backward (reverses direction) while the heavier body moves slowly forward.
- Truly elastic collisions are essentially idealizations at the macroscopic scale — friction and sound always remove some kinetic energy — but are well approximated by steel balls, air-track carts with spring bumpers, and especially subatomic particle interactions.

## Equations
- Momentum: m₁v₁ + m₂v₂ = m₁v'₁ + m₂v'₂
- Internal KE: ½m₁v₁² + ½m₂v₂² = ½m₁v'₁² + ½m₂v'₂²
