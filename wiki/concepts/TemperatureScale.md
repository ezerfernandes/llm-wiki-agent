## Definition
A **temperature scale** assigns numbers to degrees of hotness. The three common scales — Celsius,
Fahrenheit, and Kelvin — are anchored to fixed physical reference points and differ in zero point
and degree size.

## Key Points
- **Celsius (°C)**: water freezes at 0 °C, boils at 100 °C (100 equal intervals).
- **Fahrenheit (°F)**: water freezes at 32 °F, boils at 212 °F (180 intervals).
- **Kelvin (K)**: the SI absolute scale; 0 K is [[ThermodynamicTemperature|absolute zero]]
  (−273.15 °C). Written without a degree symbol. Water freezes at 273.15 K, boils at 373.15 K.
- Celsius and Kelvin share the same degree size (offset by 273.15); one Celsius degree equals
  1.8 Fahrenheit degrees (the 9/5 factor).
- Gas-law calculations ([[IdealGasLaw]]) require **absolute** temperature in kelvin.
- Complements the chemistry-focused [[TemperatureMeasurement]] page.

## Equations
- T(°F) = (9/5)·T(°C) + 32
- T(°C) = (5/9)·(T(°F) − 32)
- T(K) = T(°C) + 273.15
- T(°C) = T(K) − 273.15
- T(K) = (5/9)·(T(°F) − 32) + 273.15
