# Electron Affinity

**Electron affinity (EA)** is the energy change when an electron is added to a gaseous atom — X(g) + e⁻ → X⁻(g). It can be **exothermic** (negative EA, energy released, favorable) or **endothermic** (positive EA, energy required).

## Periodic trends
- **Across a period (left → right):** EA tends to become **more negative** as [[PeriodicTrends|effective nuclear charge]] rises.
- **Down a group:** the second-period element often has the most negative EA (its small n=2 shell still wins despite repulsion).

## Exceptions (less favorable than expected)
- **Group 18 (noble gases):** filled shells; the added electron must enter a higher n.
- **Group 2:** filled ns; the added electron must enter the higher-energy np.
- **Group 15:** half-filled np; the added electron must pair, raising repulsion.

## A key datum
**Chlorine** has the most negative EA of any element, **−348 kJ/mol**, exceeding fluorine's **−322 kJ/mol** — chlorine's added electron enters the larger n=3 shell with less electron-electron repulsion than fluorine's crowded n=2 shell.

## Significance
Fluorine's favorable EA combined with a large [[IonizationEnergy|ionization energy]] makes anion formation far easier than cation formation — central to understanding which elements gain vs lose electrons in reactions.
