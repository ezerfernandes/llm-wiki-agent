# Colligative Properties

**Colligative properties** are solution properties that depend only on the **total number of dissolved solute particles**, not on their chemical identity. The four are **vapor pressure lowering**, **boiling point elevation**, **freezing point depression**, and **osmotic pressure**.

A 1 m sucrose solution and a 1 m ethylene glycol solution have identical colligative effects because each supplies one mole of particles per kilogram of solvent. [[Electrolyte|Electrolytes]] supply more particles per formula unit, amplifying the effect — accounted for by the [[VantHoffFactor|van 't Hoff factor]] i.

## Concentration Units Used
These two **temperature-independent** units are preferred:
- **Mole fraction** X_A = (mol A)/(total mol of all components); the mole fractions sum to 1.
- **Molality** m = (mol solute)/(kg solvent).

## The Four Properties
- **Vapor pressure lowering** — a nonvolatile solute lowers solvent vapor pressure; quantified by [[RaoultsLaw|Raoult's law]] P_A = X_A·P°_A.
- **[[BoilingPointElevation|Boiling point elevation]]** — ΔT_b = i·K_b·m.
- **[[FreezingPointDepression|Freezing point depression]]** — ΔT_f = i·K_f·m.
- **[[OsmoticPressureChemistry|Osmotic pressure]]** — Π = i·M·R·T.

Because each is directly proportional to solute particle number, measuring any of them for a known solute mass yields the solute's **molar mass**.

## Phase Diagram Effect
On a solution's phase diagram the liquid-vapor curve lies below the pure solvent's (vapor pressure lowering), so the boiling point shifts higher and the freezing point lower; the solid-gas curve is unchanged since solute is excluded from the solid solvent.
