## Definition
Newton's second law of motion states that the acceleration of a system is directly proportional to the net external force acting on it and inversely proportional to its mass. It provides the quantitative cause-and-effect link between [[Force]] and [[Acceleration]].

## Key Points
- A larger net force produces proportionally larger acceleration; a larger mass produces smaller acceleration for the same force.
- Only **external** forces on the chosen **system of interest** count; internal forces cancel (see [[FreeBodyDiagram]]).
- The law defines the newton: the force that accelerates 1 kg at 1 m/s².
- Applied per axis: F_net = ma where there is acceleration, F_net = 0 where there is none (equilibrium).
- Used to define weight as the gravitational force on a mass (see [[MassVersusWeight]]).

## Equations
- a ∝ F_net and a ∝ 1/m
- a = F_net / m
- F_net = ma
- 1 N = 1 kg·m/s² (≈ 0.225 lb)
- Weight: w = mg, g ≈ 9.80 m/s² near Earth
