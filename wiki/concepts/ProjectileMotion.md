## Definition
Projectile motion is the motion of an object thrown or launched into the air and subject only to gravitational [[Acceleration]] (air resistance neglected). The object is the projectile and its curved path is the trajectory, which is a parabola. It is a prime application of [[TwoDimensionalKinematics]].

## Key Points
- The motion separates into independent horizontal and vertical parts (independence of perpendicular motions); time is the common variable linking them.
- Horizontal: zero acceleration (ax = 0) → constant horizontal velocity.
- Vertical: constant downward acceleration ay = −g = −9.80 m/s² (taking up as positive) — identical to [[FreeFall]].
- The apex (highest point) is where the vertical velocity is zero; range R is the horizontal distance on level ground.
- Four-step method: resolve into components → treat as two 1D motions → solve each with the [[KinematicEquations]] → recombine for total displacement and velocity.
- Maximum range on level ground occurs at a 45° launch angle (~38° once air resistance is included); complementary angles (summing to 90°) give equal ranges but different maximum heights.

## Equations
- Resolve initial velocity: v0x = v0 cos θ0 ; v0y = v0 sin θ0
- Horizontal: x = x0 + vx t ; vx = v0x
- Vertical: vy = v0y − g t ; y = y0 + v0y t − ½ g t² ; vy² = v0y² − 2 g (y − y0)
- Recombine: v = √(vx² + vy²) ; θv = tan⁻¹(vy / vx)
- Maximum height: h = v0y² / (2g)
- Range (level ground): R = v0² sin(2θ0) / g
