# Alkene

An **alkene** is an **unsaturated** [[Hydrocarbon|hydrocarbon]] containing one or more **carbon–carbon double bonds (C=C)**. The double bond is **one [[SigmaAndPiBonds|σ bond plus one π bond]]**; the doubly bonded carbons are **sp² hybridized** ([[OrbitalHybridization]]), **trigonal planar**, with **120°** bond angles. The simplest alkene is **ethene** (ethylene, C₂H₄).

## Nomenclature

Replace the alkane "-ane" with **"-ene"**; identify the double-bond position by the smaller of the two participating carbon numbers: ethene (C₂H₄), propene (C₃H₆), 1-butene (CH₂=CHCH₂CH₃), 2-butene (CH₃CH=CHCH₃).

## Isomerism (see [[OrganicIsomerism]])

- **Structural isomers:** 1-butene vs 2-butene differ in where the double bond sits.
- **Geometric isomers (cis/trans):** because the C=C bond is **rigid** (rotation would break the π overlap), 2-butene exists as a **cis** isomer (both methyl groups on the same side) and a **trans** isomer (opposite sides). These have different physical properties and can be separated.

## Reactivity — addition

Alkenes are far more reactive than [[Alkane|alkanes]] because the C=C is a reactive [[OrganicFunctionalGroup|functional group]] and the **π bond is weaker** and more easily disrupted than a σ bond. The characteristic reaction is the **[[AdditionReaction|addition reaction]]**: the π bond breaks and is replaced by two new σ bonds, and carbon hybridization changes **sp² → sp³**. Halogens add (rather than substitute as in alkanes):

C₂H₄ + Cl₂ → ClCH₂CH₂Cl  (ethene + chlorine → 1,2-dichloroethane)

## Polymerization

Ethylene polymerizes (transition-metal catalysts) into **polyethylene**, long –CH₂– chains and a major packaging plastic — an example of [[OrganicPolymer|polymer]] formation from monomers.
