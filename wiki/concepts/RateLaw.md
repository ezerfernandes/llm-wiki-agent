# Rate Law

A **rate law** (rate equation, or differential rate law) expresses the [[ReactionRate|reaction rate]] as a function of reactant [[Molarity|concentrations]]:

$$\text{rate} = k[A]^m[B]^n\ldots$$

- $k$ is the [[RateConstant|rate constant]].
- $m$, $n$ are the [[ReactionOrder|reaction orders]] — exponents that must be **determined experimentally**.

The headline rule of the chapter: *rate laws are determined by experiment only and are not reliably predicted by reaction stoichiometry.* Orders often differ from the coefficients in the [[ChemicalEquation|balanced equation]] and can be fractional, negative, or zero. (A concentration term raised to the zero power equals 1 and is dropped.) The exception is an [[ElementaryReaction|elementary reaction]], whose rate law *can* be written directly from its equation.

Example forms:
- `rate = k[H₂O₂]` — first order overall
- `rate = k[C₄H₆]²` — second order overall
- `rate = k[H⁺][OH⁻]` — second order overall (first order in each)

The orders are found by the [[ReactionOrder|method of initial rates]]; integrating the rate law gives the [[IntegratedRateLaw|integrated rate laws]] that describe concentration over time.
