# Molecular Geometry

**Molecular geometry** (molecular structure) is the three-dimensional arrangement of the *atoms* in a molecule. It is predicted from [[VSEPRTheory|VSEPR theory]] and must be distinguished from electron-pair geometry. (Named *MolecularGeometry* to avoid a generic `Geometry` page.)

## Bond Angle and Bond Length

- **Bond angle**: the angle between two bonds that share a common atom (degrees).
- **Bond distance / bond length**: the distance between the nuclei of two bonded atoms (Å; 1 Å = 10⁻¹⁰ m; 1 pm = 10⁻¹² m; 100 pm = 1 Å).

## Electron-Pair Geometry vs. Molecular Structure

- **Electron-pair geometry** counts *all* regions of electron density (bonds + lone pairs).
- **Molecular structure** describes only the positions of the atoms.

They are **identical when the central atom has no lone pairs** and **differ when lone pairs are present** (lone pairs occupy geometry positions but hold no atom):

- CH₄: 4 bonds, 0 lone pairs → tetrahedral electron-pair geometry **and** tetrahedral molecular structure.
- NH₃: 3 bonds, 1 lone pair → tetrahedral electron-pair geometry, but **trigonal pyramidal** molecular structure.
- H₂O: 2 bonds, 2 lone pairs → tetrahedral electron-pair geometry, but **bent** molecular structure.
- Octahedral with 2 lone pairs (180° apart) → **square planar** molecular structure.

Lone-pair repulsion compresses bond angles below ideal values (NH₃ < 109.5°). The five parent electron-pair geometries — linear (180°), trigonal planar (120°), tetrahedral (109.5°), trigonal bipyramidal (90°/120°), octahedral (90°) — give rise to derived molecular shapes (bent, trigonal pyramidal, seesaw, T-shaped, square planar, etc.) as lone pairs replace bonds.

## Orbital Basis of the Geometry

The five parent geometries are reproduced by [[OrbitalHybridization|orbital hybridization]] in [[ValenceBondTheory|valence bond theory]]: linear↔sp, trigonal planar↔sp², tetrahedral↔sp³, trigonal bipyramidal↔sp³d, octahedral↔sp³d² (see [[HybridizationAndVSEPRGeometry]]). The bond distance/bond length above is the internuclear separation of minimum energy in orbital-overlap terms.
