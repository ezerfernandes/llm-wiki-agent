# AI Winter

Periods when AI funding and optimism collapsed. Reddi's *Machine Learning Systems* ([[mlsysbook-ch01-introduction|Vol 1, Ch 1]]) reframes both winters as **systems failures, not algorithmic dead-ends**: the algorithms were mathematically sound but required hardware that did not yet exist.

- **First winter (1974–1980)** — precipitated by the 1973 Lighthill Report; underlying cause was a mismatch between algorithm ambition and available compute.
- **Second winter (1987–1993)** — triggered by the collapse of the Lisp Machine market when cheaper general-purpose workstations undercut specialized AI hardware.

The same compute-constraint pattern drove neural-network research underground from 1969 (Minsky's perceptron critique) to 1986 (the Rumelhart-Hinton-Williams [[Backpropagation|backpropagation]] paper). Each winter arrived when the dominant paradigm hit its *systems ceiling*; each resurgence followed an infrastructure breakthrough.
