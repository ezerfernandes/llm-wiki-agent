## Definition
A banked curve is a turn tilted inward so that a component of the road's normal force helps supply the [[CentripetalForce]], reducing reliance on tire friction. An "ideally banked" curve needs no friction at all for one specific speed.

## Key Points
- On a flat curve, friction alone provides F_c; the required friction coefficient is μ_s = v²/(rg).
- For an ideally banked (frictionless) curve, the banking angle depends only on speed and radius — not on vehicle mass.
- Race tracks use steep banking to allow high cornering speeds without depending solely on friction.
- At speeds above the ideal value a car tends to slide outward; below it, inward — friction handles the difference in practice.

## Equations
- Flat curve: μ_s = v² / (r g)
- Ideal banking angle: θ = tan⁻¹(v² / (r g))
