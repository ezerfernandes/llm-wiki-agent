## Definition
A **nucleon** is a constituent particle of the [[AtomicNucleus|nucleus]] — that is, a [[Proton]] or a [[Neutron]]. The two have nearly equal mass (m_p ≈ 1836 m_e, m_n ≈ 1839 m_e) and are bound together by the [[StrongNuclearForce|strong nuclear force]].

## Key Points
- **Mass number A** = total nucleon count = N + Z, where Z = proton number ([[AtomicNumberAndMassNumber|atomic number]]) and N = neutron number.
- A specific nucleus with given Z and N is a **nuclide**, written $^{A}_{Z}X_{N}$.
- Nucleons pack tightly: nuclear volume ∝ A, so nuclear density is nearly constant and enormous (~10¹⁴× water), as seen in neutron stars.
- Nuclear radius scales as r = r₀A^(1/3) with r₀ ≈ 1.2 fm.
- The mass of a nucleus is slightly less than the sum of its free nucleons; that mass defect is the [[NuclearBindingEnergy|binding energy]].

## Equations
- A = N + Z
- r = r₀A^(1/3), r₀ = 1.2 fm
