# Solvation

**Solvation** is the establishment of attractive **solute-solvent** [[IntermolecularForces|intermolecular forces]] when a solute dissolves — solvent molecules surround and stabilize each solute particle. When the solvent is water, solvation is specifically called **hydration**. Solvation is the **exothermic** step of the [[DissolutionProcess|dissolution process]]: it releases energy that offsets the energy spent separating solute particles and expanding the solvent (see [[EnthalpyOfSolution]]).

## Ion-Dipole Attractions
For ions, solvation operates through **ion-dipole attractions** — "the electrostatic attraction between an ion and a molecule with a dipole." Polar water molecules orient their positive (hydrogen) ends toward anions and their negative (oxygen) ends toward cations. In dissolving KCl, water surrounds individual K⁺ and Cl⁻ ions, reducing the strong interionic forces and letting the now-solvated (hydrated) ions move freely into solution. This depends on the solvent's [[MolecularPolarity|polarity]].

## Why Solvation Strength Matters
The magnitude of solvation energy relative to the solute-solute attraction decides solubility:
- Solvation energy ≫ solute-solute attraction → exothermic, highly soluble (e.g., NaOH).
- Solute-solute attraction ≫ solvation energy → endothermic, poorly soluble (e.g., CaCO₃).

This balance also explains why gaseous solutes (no solute-solute interactions) dissolve according only to their solute-solvent attractions: O₂ is ~3× more soluble in water than He, but ~100× less soluble than chloroform.
