# Self-Delusion

The **first of two hypotheses for why language models hallucinate**, originally expressed by **Ortega et al. at [[googledeepmind|DeepMind]] in 2021**. Per [[ai-engineering-ch02-foundation-models|*AI Engineering* Ch 2]]:

> "A language model hallucinates because it can't differentiate between the data it's given and the data it generates."

## Mechanism

A model is given the prompt: *"Who's Chip Huyen?"* The model generates: *"Chip Huyen is an architect."* On the next token, **the model's input is now**: *"Who's Chip Huyen? Chip Huyen is an architect."* — and the model treats its own generated assertion *the same way it treats a given fact*.

Starting with even a slightly-out-of-the-ordinary generated sequence, the model can expand on it and generate outrageously wrong "facts" — *self-delusion*.

## Worked example (Ch 2 Fig 2-24)

The model LLaVA-v1.5-7B is asked to identify ingredients on a shampoo bottle's label. The model first **convinces itself that the product is milk**, then **adds milk to the extracted ingredient list** — even though the actual image is shampoo.

## [[SnowballingHallucination|Snowballing hallucination]]

Zhang et al. (2023) call this phenomenon **snowballing hallucination**. After an incorrect initial assumption, the model continues hallucinating to **justify the initial wrong assumption**. Strikingly, initial wrong assumptions can cause the model to **make mistakes on questions it would otherwise answer correctly** — e.g. asserting "9677 is divisible by 13" because the model already committed to that line of reasoning.

## Mitigations (per the DeepMind paper)

1. **From [[reinforcementlearning|reinforcement learning]]**: train the model to differentiate between **user-provided prompts** ("observations about the world" in RL terminology) and **tokens generated by the model** ("the model's actions").
2. **From supervised learning**: include **factual and counterfactual signals** in the training data.

## Complement: [[InternalKnowledgeMismatch|internal-knowledge mismatch]]

The wiki's complementary hypothesis ([[InternalKnowledgeMismatch]], from Leo Gao / John Schulman) attributes hallucination to mismatches between *labelers'* knowledge and the *model's* knowledge during SFT. Ch 2 says the two are **complementary, not contradictory**:

> "The self-delusion hypothesis focuses on how self-supervision causes hallucinations, whereas the mismatched internal knowledge hypothesis focuses on how supervision causes hallucinations."
