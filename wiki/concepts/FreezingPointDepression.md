# Freezing Point Depression

**Freezing point depression** is the [[ColligativeProperties|colligative property]] by which a solution freezes at a lower temperature than the pure solvent.

$$\Delta T_f = i \, K_f \, m$$

- ΔT_f — freezing point depression
- K_f — **cryoscopic constant** (freezing point depression constant), characteristic of the solvent, in °C·m⁻¹
- m — molality of all solute species
- i — [[VantHoffFactor|van 't Hoff factor]] (= 1 for nonelectrolytes; counts ions for electrolytes)

## Solvent Constants (K_f)
- Water: 1.86 °C·m⁻¹
- Benzene: 5.12 °C·m⁻¹
- Chloroform: 4.68 °C·m⁻¹

## Applications
- **Road de-icing** with salts (electrolytes; large i amplifies the effect).
- **Automobile antifreeze** (ethylene glycol) in radiators.
- **Seawater** freezes below 0 °C, keeping polar oceans liquid.

A 1.0 m NaCl solution gives an *expected* ΔT_f of 2.0 mol ions/kg × 1.86 = 3.7 °C, but the measured value is 3.4 °C — reflecting ion pairing captured by i (see [[VantHoffFactor]]). Measuring ΔT_f for a known mass yields the solute's molar mass.
