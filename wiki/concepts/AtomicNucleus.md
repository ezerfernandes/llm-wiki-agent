# Atomic Nucleus

The **atomic nucleus** is the small, relatively heavy, positively charged body at the center of an [[Atom]], in which essentially all of the atom's mass is concentrated. It contains [[Proton|protons]] (positive) and [[Neutron|neutrons]] (uncharged), collectively called nucleons, surrounded at a great relative distance by [[Electron|electrons]].

Its existence was deduced by [[ErnestRutherford]] from the gold-foil α-scattering experiment (see [[EvolutionOfAtomicTheory]]): because most α particles passed straight through the foil while a few bounced sharply back, the atom must be mostly empty space with its positive charge and mass packed into a minuscule core. The nucleus diameter (~10⁻¹⁵ m) is about 100,000 times smaller than the whole atom (~10⁻¹⁰ m).

The number of protons in the nucleus is the [[AtomicNumberAndMassNumber|atomic number Z]], which defines the element; the total of protons and neutrons is the mass number A.

## Nuclear Physics View (College Physics 2e Ch.31)
Protons and neutrons are collectively [[Nucleon|nucleons]]; a specific nucleus is a **nuclide**, written $^{A}_{Z}X_{N}$. The nuclear radius follows **r = r₀A^(1/3)** with r₀ ≈ 1.2 fm, so nuclear volume ∝ A and the density is nearly constant and enormous (~10¹⁴× water, as in neutron stars). Nucleons are held together by the short-range [[StrongNuclearForce|strong nuclear force]], which competes with proton [[CoulombsLaw|Coulomb]] repulsion; the resulting mass defect is the [[NuclearBindingEnergy|nuclear binding energy]]. Unstable nuclides undergo [[RadioactiveDecay|radioactive decay]].

## Nuclear Chemistry View (Chemistry 2e Ch.21)
In [[NuclearChemistry|nuclear chemistry]] the nucleus is described by [[NuclideNotation|nuclide notation]] $^{A}_{Z}X$; its [[NuclearBindingEnergyChemistry|mass defect and binding energy]] and neutron-to-proton ratio place it on the [[BandOfStability|band of stability]]. Unstable nuclei undergo [[RadioactiveDecayChemistry|radioactive decay]] with first-order [[RadioactiveHalfLife|half-life kinetics]], and the [[NuclearBindingEnergyChemistry|per-nucleon binding-energy]] curve makes both [[NuclearFissionChemistry|fission]] and [[NuclearFusionChemistry|fusion]] release energy.
