## Definition
Centripetal force (F_c) is the net force directed toward the center of a circular path that keeps an object in [[UniformCircularMotion]]. It is not a distinct type of force: it is whatever real net force happens to point inward — tension, friction, gravity, or a normal force.

## Key Points
- Applying [[NewtonsSecondLaw]] to circular motion: the net inward force equals mass times [[CentripetalAcceleration]].
- For a given mass and speed, a larger F_c produces a smaller radius of curvature (a tighter curve).
- On a flat curve, static friction supplies F_c; the minimum friction coefficient needed is μ_s = v²/(rg).
- On a [[BankedCurves|banked curve]], a component of the normal force can supply F_c.
- Distinguish from the fictitious [[CentrifugalForce]], which is not a real force.

## Equations
- F_c = m a_c
- F_c = m v² / r
- F_c = m r ω²
- r = m v² / F_c
