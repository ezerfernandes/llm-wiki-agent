## Definition
Simple harmonic motion (SHM) is periodic oscillation in which the net restoring force obeys [[HookesLaw]] (F = -kx) and there is no energy dissipation. The result is sinusoidal motion symmetric about equilibrium.

## Key Points
- Both [[Period]] and [[Frequency]] are independent of amplitude — a defining feature that makes oscillators reliable clocks.
- Only mass m and force constant k set the period: stiffer (larger k) → faster; heavier (larger m) → slower.
- Position, velocity, and acceleration are all sinusoidal; acceleration is largest at the extremes and zero at equilibrium, while velocity peaks at equilibrium.
- SHM is the projection of [[UniformCircularMotion]] onto a fixed axis.
- A [[Pendulum]] approximates SHM for small angles; energy cycles between kinetic and elastic potential (see [[ConservationOfEnergy]]).

## Equations
- T = 2π √(m/k)
- f = (1/2π) √(k/m)
- x(t) = X cos(2π t / T)
- v(t) = -v_max sin(2π t / T), with v_max = 2π X / T = X √(k/m)
- a(t) = -(kX/m) cos(2π t / T)
