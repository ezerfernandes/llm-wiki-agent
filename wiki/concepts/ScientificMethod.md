# Scientific Method

The **scientific method** is the path by which scientific knowledge is built: from observation and curiosity, to forming a [[ScientificHypothesis|hypothesis]] and prediction, to performing experiments and further observations, to evaluating results against predictions, and to contributing to (and refining) the body of knowledge. Its common foundation is *knowledge based on experiments that can be reproduced to verify the results.*

[[Chemistry]] is a science based on observation and experimentation, and it relies on this method to answer questions and explain observations. The process distinguishes a tentative [[ScientificHypothesis|hypothesis]] from a [[ScientificLawAndTheory|scientific law and theory]]. The textbook stresses that scientific progress "is seldom neat and clean: it requires open inquiry and the reworking of questions and ideas in response to findings."

## In Physics (OpenStax College Physics 2e, Ch. 1)
[[Physics]] describes the method as a systematic sequence: observation → research → hypothesis → experimentation → analysis → conclusion, adaptable well beyond formal science. Physics frames the products of this method using a precise vocabulary — see [[ScientificModelsTheoriesLaws]] for the distinction among model, theory, law, and principle. Reproducibility and experimental verification remain the foundation across both chemistry and physics treatments.
