## Definition
The **equation of continuity** expresses conservation of volume flow for an incompressible fluid: the same volume must pass every point in a closed system per unit time. For a single tube, A₁·v̄₁ = A₂·v̄₂.

> Note: a *continuity equation* also appears in mathematics/physics as a differential conservation statement. This page is the fluid-mechanics form from College Physics 2e Ch. 12.

## Key Points
- Direct consequence of [[FlowRate|flow-rate]] conservation: Q₁ = Q₂.
- Where a conduit narrows, velocity rises; where it widens, velocity falls — velocity is inversely proportional to area (and to radius squared for circular tubes).
- Explains why a hose nozzle produces a fast jet.
- For branching systems (e.g. arteries to capillaries) the conserved quantity is total cross-section times velocity: n₁A₁v̄₁ = n₂A₂v̄₂. The huge combined capillary area makes blood flow slowly there.

## Equations
- Q₁ = Q₂
- A₁·v̄₁ = A₂·v̄₂
- Branching: n₁·A₁·v̄₁ = n₂·A₂·v̄₂
