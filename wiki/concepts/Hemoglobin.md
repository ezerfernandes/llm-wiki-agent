# Hemoglobin (and Biological Metal Chelates)

**Hemoglobin** is the oxygen-transport protein of blood built around **heme** — the **iron-containing [[CoordinationCompound|coordination complex]]** in which a **tetradentate porphyrin [[Ligand|ligand]]** (four nitrogen donor atoms) coordinates an iron center. This is a classic biological **chelate** (see [[ChelateEffect]]).

- O₂ is carried bound to the iron center; releasing it shifts the color toward bluish red.
- **Hemoglobin transports oxygen only if the iron is Fe²⁺** — oxidation to **Fe³⁺ destroys oxygen transport**.

## Related biological metal complexes

- **Chlorophyll** — the green plant pigment, a **magnesium** coordination complex. Plants look green because chlorophyll **absorbs red and purple light** and reflects green (the [[ComplexColorAndMagnetism|complementary-color]] effect); the absorbed energy drives photosynthesis.
- **Vitamin B-12** — a **cobalt** coordination complex.

## Applied coordination chemistry (same chapter)

- **Cisplatin**, cis-[Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂], discovered 1965, approved 1978 — an anticancer drug effective only as the **cis** isomer (see [[CoordinationIsomerism]]). Relatives: carboplatin, oxaliplatin, satraplatin.
- **Chelation therapy** — EDTA, BAL, DMSA, enterobactin sequester toxic/excess metals (see [[ChelateEffect]]).
- **Electroplating** — [Ag(CN)₂]⁻ and [Au(CN)₂]⁻ give smooth uniform metal coatings.
- **Pigments** — square-planar copper(II) phthalocyanine blue (ink, blue jeans, paint).
