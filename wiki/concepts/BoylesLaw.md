# Boyle's Law

**Boyle's law** states that the volume of a confined amount of gas held at constant temperature is **inversely proportional to the pressure**:

$$P \propto \frac{1}{V} \quad\Rightarrow\quad PV = k \quad\Rightarrow\quad P_1 V_1 = P_2 V_2$$

(at constant T and n). Doubling the pressure halves the volume. A plot of P vs. V is a hyperbola, while a plot of P vs. 1/V (or 1/P vs. V) is linear — the preferred form for fitting data.

**Example:** a gas at 15.0 mL and 13.0 psi compressed to 7.5 mL reaches 13.0 × 15.0 / 7.5 = 26 psi.

**Everyday application — breathing:** when the diaphragm contracts, lung volume increases, so lung pressure drops (Boyle's law) and air flows in; relaxing the diaphragm reverses this. The lung-to-surroundings pressure difference is only about 1–3 torr.

One of the four simple [[GasLaws|gas laws]]; combined with the others it gives the [[IdealGasLawChemistry|ideal gas law]]. The [[KineticMolecularTheory|kinetic-molecular theory]] explains it: a smaller volume crowds molecules so they strike the walls more frequently, raising pressure.
