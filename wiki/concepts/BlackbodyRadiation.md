# Blackbody Radiation

**Blackbody radiation** is the electromagnetic emission of an ideal radiator — a perfect absorber and emitter (emissivity = 1) — whose spectrum depends only on its absolute temperature, not on its material. Its experimental spectrum is the puzzle that launched quantum mechanics.

## Key Points
- Classical wave theory predicted intensity should rise without bound at short wavelengths — the **ultraviolet catastrophe** — which is physically absurd.
- Real blackbody curves peak and fall; the peak shifts to shorter wavelength as temperature rises.
- Total radiated intensity grows with the fourth power of absolute temperature (Stefan-Boltzmann behavior).
- [[MaxPlanck|Planck]] (c. 1900) matched the observed curves by assuming oscillating atoms exchange energy only in discrete steps — the first statement of [[EnergyQuantization|energy quantization]].

## Equations
- Oscillator energy: E = (n + ½)hf, n = 0, 1, 2, 3, …
- Energy-level spacing: ΔE = hf
- Total intensity ∝ T⁴ (Stefan-Boltzmann)
