# Galvanic (Voltaic) Cell

A **galvanic cell** (or **voltaic cell**) is an [[ElectrochemicalCell|electrochemical cell]] in which a **spontaneous** [[OxidationReduction|redox]] reaction takes place, so the cell delivers electrical work to its surroundings. It is the operating principle of every [[ElectrochemicalBattery|battery]] and [[FuelCell|fuel cell]].

## How It Works
Consider the spontaneous reaction Cu(s) + 2Ag⁺(aq) → Cu²⁺(aq) + 2Ag(s). Splitting it across two half-cells:
- **Anode (oxidation):** Cu(s) → Cu²⁺(aq) + 2e⁻
- **Cathode (reduction):** 2Ag⁺(aq) + 2e⁻ → 2Ag(s)

Electrons flow through the external wire **from anode to cathode**; a **salt bridge** (inert [[Electrolyte|electrolyte]], e.g., NaNO₃) lets spectator ions migrate to keep each half-cell electrically neutral (NO₃⁻ into the anode side as Cu²⁺ builds; Na⁺ into the cathode side as Ag⁺ depletes).

Mnemonic: **AN OX** (ANode = OXidation), **RED CAT** (REDuction = CAThode).

## Key Terms
- **Anode** — electrode where oxidation occurs (the negative terminal in a galvanic cell).
- **Cathode** — electrode where reduction occurs (the positive terminal in a galvanic cell).
- **Salt bridge** — completes the internal circuit and balances charge.
- **Active electrode** — made of a couple member (e.g., Cu, Mg); **inert electrode** — Pt, when both couple members are solutes.

The cell's driving force is its [[CellPotential|cell potential]] E°cell = E°cathode − E°anode (positive for a galvanic cell). Its composition is written in [[ElectrochemicalCellNotation|cell notation]]: anode║cathode, e.g., Cu(s)│1 M Cu(NO₃)₂(aq)║1 M AgNO₃(aq)│Ag(s).
