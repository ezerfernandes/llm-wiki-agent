# Avogadro's Law

**Avogadro's law** states that equal volumes of all gases, measured under identical conditions of temperature and pressure, contain equal numbers of molecules. Equivalently, for a confined gas the volume is **directly proportional to the number of moles** at constant temperature and pressure:

$$V \propto n \quad\Rightarrow\quad V = kn \quad\Rightarrow\quad \frac{V_1}{n_1} = \frac{V_2}{n_2}$$

This is distinct from [[AvogadrosNumber|Avogadro's number]] (6.022 × 10²³ mol⁻¹), though both originate in Amedeo Avogadro's hypothesis that equal gas volumes hold equal particle counts.

Avogadro's law is the key that makes [[GasStoichiometry|gas stoichiometry]] simple: because equal volumes contain equal numbers of molecules, the volume ratios of gaseous reactants and products (at the same T and P) equal the coefficients of the balanced equation. For N₂ + 3 H₂ → 2 NH₃, one volume of N₂ reacts with three volumes of H₂ to give two volumes of NH₃.

One of the four simple [[GasLaws|gas laws]]; supplying the n-dependence completes the [[IdealGasLawChemistry|ideal gas law]]. It also gives the [[MolarVolume|standard molar volume]] (≈ 22.4 L at STP), which is the same for every ideal gas.
