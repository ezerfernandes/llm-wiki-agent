# Hydrogen Chemistry

**Hydrogen** is the most abundant element in the universe (~90% of atoms). It is a colorless, odorless, nonpoisonous diatomic gas (H₂). With a single 1s electron it shows traits of both group 1 (forming H⁺) and group 17 (forming the hydride H⁻ and single covalent bonds) — "a unique element that almost deserves its own location in the [[PeriodicTable|periodic table]]." (This is the chemistry of the element; the related [[HydrogenBonding]] page covers the intermolecular force.)

## Isotopes
- **Protium (¹H)** — ordinary hydrogen.
- **Deuterium (²H or D)** — ~1 per 7,000 H atoms.
- **Tritium (³H or T)** — radioactive, ~1 per 10¹⁸ H atoms.

Same chemistry (identical electron structure), differing physical properties because of mass.

## Preparation
- Steam over coke at 1000 °C (**water gas**): C(s) + H₂O(g) → CO(g) + H₂(g); a water-gas shift then converts CO to CO₂ for more H₂.
- Steam reforming of hydrocarbons over Ni at 900 °C: C₃H₈(g) + 3H₂O(g) → 3CO(g) + 7H₂(g).
- [[ElectrolysisChemistry|Electrolysis]] of water: 2H₂O(l) + energy → 2H₂(g) + O₂(g).
- Metal + dilute acid (best lab method): Fe(s) + 2H₃O⁺(aq) + 2Cl⁻(aq) → Fe²⁺(aq) + 2Cl⁻(aq) + H₂(g) + 2H₂O(l).
- Ionic hydride + water: CaH₂(s) + 2H₂O(l) → Ca²⁺(aq) + 2OH⁻(aq) + 2H₂(g).

## Reactions
Unreactive normally; reacts when heated. Combustion with O₂ releases 286 kJ per mole of H₂O (oxyhydrogen torch ~2800 °C; liquid H₂ rocket fuel). Forms ionic hydrides (H⁻) with group 1 and Ca/Sr/Ba; 3H₂ + N₂ → 2NH₃; H₂ + X₂ → 2HX; reduces hot metal oxides.

## Key Hydrogen Compounds
- **Ammonia (NH₃):** lab from NH₄⁺ + OH⁻; commercially via the **Haber process** N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃ (ΔH° = −92 kJ). H-bonded liquid (bp −33 °C), very soluble, Brønsted base; basis for [[NitrogenGroupChemistry|nitrogen chemistry]].
- **Hydrazine (N₂H₄):** rocket fuel; N₂H₄(l) + O₂(g) → N₂(g) + 2H₂O(l), ΔH° = −621.5 kJ/mol.
- **Phosphine (PH₃):** poisonous; AlP + 3H₃O⁺ → PH₃ + Al³⁺ + 3H₂O.
- **Hydrogen sulfide (H₂S):** rotten-egg odor, as toxic as HCN; weak diprotic acid; good reducing agent — see [[SulfurChemistry]].
- **Hydrogen halides (HF, HCl, HBr, HI):** soluble hydrohalic acids, all strong except HF; HF attacks glass (SiO₂ + 4HF → SiF₄ + 2H₂O) — see [[HalogensChemistry]].

Two-thirds of world H₂ output goes to ammonia (fertilizer, nitric acid); much also to hydrogenation.
