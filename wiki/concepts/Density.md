# Density

**Density** (symbol ρ) is the ratio of the mass of a sample of a substance to its volume:

> density = mass / volume   →   ρ = m / V   (so m = ρV)

It is a **derived** unit and an **intensive** property (see [[ExtensiveAndIntensiveProperties]]) — independent of sample size — which makes it useful for identifying substances. The SI unit is kg/m³; in practice g/cm³ is used for solids and liquids and g/L for gases. Most liquids and solids fall in 0.7–19 g/cm³; air is ~1.2 g/L.

In **fluid statics** (OpenStax College Physics 2e, Ch. 11) density is the key to flotation and pressure: liquids and solids are far denser than gases because their atoms stay in contact rather than being widely spaced. The metric system was originally defined so water has density exactly 1 g/cm³ = 10³ kg/m³. Density drives hydrostatic [[Pressure]] (P = hρg, see [[FluidPressure]]) and decides whether an object floats — an object floats when its average density is below the fluid's (see [[ArchimedesPrinciple]] and [[Buoyancy]]).

Representative values: water 1.0, ice 0.92, ethanol 0.79, iron 7.9, copper 9.0, silver 10.5, lead 11.3, mercury 13.6, gold 19.3 g/cm³.

**Worked examples (Ch 1):**
- A lead cube of edge 2.00 cm (V = 8.00 cm³) and mass 90.7 g → 90.7/8.00 = 11.3 g/cm³.
- *Water displacement:* an iron piece raising water from 25.5 to 29.5 mL (V = 4.0 mL) with mass 31.48 g → 7.9 g/mL. A rebar piece (69.658 g, ΔV = 8.9 mL) → 7.8 g/cm³, close to iron's 7.9, indicating it is mostly iron.

Computing density from raw measurements often requires [[DimensionalAnalysis]] and respecting [[SignificantFigures]].
