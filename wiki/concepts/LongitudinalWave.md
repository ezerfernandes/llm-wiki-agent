## Definition
A longitudinal wave is a [[Wave]] in which the disturbance (particle displacement) is parallel to the direction of propagation, producing alternating compressions and rarefactions.

## Key Points
- Examples: sound waves in air and water, and compression pulses traveling along a spring.
- Contrast with a [[TransverseWave]], where displacement is perpendicular to travel.
- Seismic P-waves are longitudinal and travel faster than transverse S-waves, a difference used to locate earthquakes.
- Like all waves, it obeys v_w = f λ and combines by [[Superposition]].

## Equations
- Wave speed: v_w = f λ
