# Chelate Effect

A **chelate** forms when a **polydentate ligand** wraps around a central metal "rather like a crab's claw" (Greek *chele*, claw), bonding through **two or more donor atoms** at once. The resulting ring-containing [[CoordinationCompound|coordination compound]] is unusually stable — the **chelate effect**. (Chemistry sense.)

## Denticity of ligands

A [[Ligand|ligand]]'s **denticity** is the number of donor atoms it uses:
- **Monodentate** — one donor atom (NH₃, Cl⁻, H₂O, CN⁻).
- **Bidentate** — two (ethylenediamine, *en*, H₂NCH₂CH₂NH₂).
- **Tridentate / tetradentate / pentadentate / hexadentate** — three through six donor atoms.

Key polydentate examples from the chapter:
- **Ethylenediamine (en)** — bidentate; abbreviates in names like [Co(en)₃]³⁺.
- **Heme / porphyrin** — **tetradentate**, four nitrogen donors coordinating iron (see [[Hemoglobin]]).
- **EDTA** — **hexadentate**, (HO₂CCH₂)₂NCH₂CH₂N(CH₂CO₂H)₂; six donor atoms grip a metal completely.

A bidentate ligand contributes **2** to the [[CoordinationCompound|coordination number]] (e.g. [Fe(C₂O₄)₃]³⁻ has CN 6 from three bidentate oxalates).

## Biological and applied chelation

- **Hemoglobin / chlorophyll / vitamin B-12** — biological chelates (Fe, Mg, Co centers).
- **Chelation therapy** — chelating agents tie up undesirable or toxic metal ions for excretion: **EDTA** sequesters Ca²⁺/Mg²⁺/Fe²⁺; **BAL** (British Anti-Lewisite, HSCH₂CH(SH)CH₂OH) was developed in WWI against arsenical Lewisite; **DMSA** (dimercaptosuccinic acid) treats heavy-metal poisoning, usually binding via one S and one O in a five-membered ring; **enterobactin** controls iron overload (Cooley's anemia). The chelate is water-soluble and eliminated by the kidneys.
