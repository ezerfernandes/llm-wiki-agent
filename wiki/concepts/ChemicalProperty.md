# Chemical Property

A **chemical property** is the ability (or inability) of [[Matter]] to change into a different type of matter. Examples include flammability, toxicity, acidity, and reactivity (iron rusts in the presence of oxygen and water while chromium does not; nitroglycerin explodes easily while neon is unreactive).

A **chemical change** always produces one or more types of matter that differ from the matter present before the change. Examples: rust formation, the explosion of nitroglycerin, copper reacting with nitric acid, combustion, food cooking/digesting/rotting, the browning of meat (oxidation of myoglobin's iron), and banana ripening.

Chemical changes contrast with [[PhysicalProperty|physical changes]], which leave chemical identity intact. In both kinds of change, total matter is preserved per the [[ConservationOfMatter|law of conservation of matter]]. Decomposing a [[ChemicalCompound|compound]] into its elements is a chemical change.
