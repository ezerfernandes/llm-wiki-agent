## Definition
**Vapor pressure** is the [[Pressure|pressure]] exerted by a substance's gas phase when it is in
equilibrium with its liquid (or solid) phase. It depends only on the substance and its
temperature, not on the surrounding air.

## Key Points
- Molecules escape a liquid/solid surface when their kinetic energy exceeds the binding forces. In
  a sealed container, evaporation continues until condensation balances it — equilibrium at the
  **saturation vapor pressure**.
- Higher temperature → faster molecules → higher vapor pressure (and higher saturation vapor
  density, see [[Humidity]]).
- **Boiling** occurs when a liquid's vapor pressure rises to equal the ambient pressure. At 1 atm,
  water's vapor pressure reaches 1.01×10⁵ Pa at 100 °C. Lower the ambient pressure and the boiling
  point drops; raise it (pressure cooker) and the boiling point rises.
- The partial pressure framing connects vapor pressure to [[PhaseChange|phase equilibria]] and
  Dalton's law.
- Vapor density follows from vapor pressure via the [[IdealGasLaw]].

## Equations
- n/V = P/(RT)  (vapor density from vapor pressure, ideal gas)
- Boiling condition: P_vapor(T) = P_ambient
