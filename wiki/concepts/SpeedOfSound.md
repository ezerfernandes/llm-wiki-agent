## Definition
The speed of sound is the rate at which a [[Sound]] wave propagates through a medium. It is set by the medium's stiffness (resistance to compression) and density, not by the source — and it links frequency and wavelength through the universal wave relation.

## Key Points
- Sound speed increases with medium rigidity (incompressibility) and decreases with density: fastest in stiff solids, slowest in compressible gases like air (~340 m/s near room temperature).
- In a given medium, sound speed is essentially **independent of frequency** across the audible range (20–20,000 Hz), so pitch does not change propagation speed.
- In gases, speed rises with absolute temperature; warmer air carries sound faster.
- Differing speeds underlie applications: P-/S-wave timing locates earthquake epicenters, and bats/sonar use echo return time to gauge distance.

## Equations
- Wave relation: `v_w = f·λ`
- Air, temperature dependence: `v_w = 331 m/s · √(T / 273 K)` (T in kelvin)
- Microscopic gas speed: `v_rms = √(3kT / m)`, k = 1.38×10⁻²³ J/K
