# Kinetic-Molecular Theory (of Gases)

The **kinetic-molecular theory (KMT)** is a microscopic model that explains the macroscopic [[GasLaws|gas laws]] in terms of the motion of individual molecules. It accurately describes gas behavior at pressures below ~1–2 atm. This is the OpenStax *Chemistry 2e* (Ch 9) treatment; for the physics-domain version see the separate [[KineticTheoryOfGases]] page (College Physics 2e).

## The Five Postulates

1. Gases consist of molecules in **continuous motion**, traveling in straight lines and changing direction only on collision (with each other or the walls).
2. The molecules are **negligibly small** compared with the distances between them.
3. Gas **pressure** arises from molecular **collisions with the container walls** (see [[GasPressure]]).
4. Molecules exert **no attractive or repulsive forces** on one another or the walls; collisions are **elastic** (no energy loss).
5. The **average kinetic energy** of the molecules is **proportional to the kelvin temperature**.

## Explaining the Gas Laws

- **Amontons's law** (P ∝ T at constant V): higher T → faster molecules → more frequent, forceful wall collisions → higher P.
- **[[CharlesLaw|Charles's law]]** (V ∝ T at constant P): higher T raises collision force, but the gas expands so that collision frequency per unit area drops to compensate.
- **[[BoylesLaw|Boyle's law]]** (PV = k at constant T): smaller V crowds molecules → more frequent wall collisions → higher P.
- **[[AvogadrosLaw|Avogadro's law]]** (V ∝ n at constant T, P): more molecules need proportionally more volume to keep collisions per unit area constant.
- **[[DaltonsLawOfPartialPressures|Dalton's law]]**: large intermolecular distances let each gas bombard the walls independently, so pressures add.

## Molecular Speed and Energy

The kinetic energy of one particle is KE = ½mu². The average kinetic energy per mole links to temperature:

$$KE_{avg} = \frac{1}{2}\mathcal{M}\,u_{rms}^2 = \frac{3}{2}RT$$

This yields the [[RootMeanSquareSpeed|root-mean-square speed]] $u_{rms} = \sqrt{3RT/\mathcal{M}}$ and explains [[GrahamsLaw|Graham's law]] of [[Effusion|effusion]] and [[GaseousDiffusion|diffusion]], since effusion rate ∝ u_rms.

## Across the Phases (Ch 10)

OpenStax Ch 10 extends the kinetic-molecular picture to all three [[StatesOfMatter|states of matter]]: in **solids** particles vibrate about fixed positions in a regular pattern; in **liquids** they stay in contact but move past one another; in **gases** they move independently between collisions. Which phase exists depends on the balance between [[IntermolecularForces|intermolecular forces]] (which pull particles together) and molecular kinetic energy (which drives them apart) — the framing for the whole chapter on liquids and solids.
