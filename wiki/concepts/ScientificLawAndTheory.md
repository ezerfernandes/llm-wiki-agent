# Scientific Law and Theory

In the [[ScientificMethod]], "law" and "theory" are distinct, well-defined terms — neither is "just a guess" (that is a [[ScientificHypothesis|hypothesis]]):

- A **scientific law** is a summary of a vast number of experimental observations that *describes or predicts* some aspect of the natural world. It says *what* happens. Example: the [[ConservationOfMatter|law of conservation of matter]].
- A **scientific theory** is a well-substantiated, comprehensive, testable *explanation* of a particular aspect of nature. It says *why* and *how*. Theories are accepted while they provide satisfactory explanations but can be modified or replaced when new data demand it.

Laws and theories are built and revised through repeated, reproducible experimentation; both are open to refinement as evidence accumulates.
