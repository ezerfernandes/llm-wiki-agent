# Empirical Formula

An **empirical formula** gives the *simplest whole-number ratio* of the atoms (or ions) in a compound — the relative numbers, not the actual count per molecule. (For the definition alongside molecular and structural formulas, see [[ChemicalFormula]].) This page covers how an empirical formula is **derived from composition data**.

## Derivation Method

1. **Mass → moles:** divide each element's mass by its [[MolarMass|molar mass]] to get moles of each element.
2. **Normalize:** divide every molar amount by the *smallest* one to get tentative subscripts.
3. **Scale to whole numbers:** if subscripts are not whole numbers, multiply all of them by the smallest integer that makes them whole.

## Worked Cases

- **Simple ratio (CH₂):** 1.71 g C → 0.142 mol; 0.287 g H → 0.284 mol. Divide by 0.142 → C₁H₂ = **CH₂**.
- **Decimal subscript (Cl₂O₇):** 5.31 g Cl → 0.150 mol; 8.40 g O → 0.525 mol. Divide by smallest → Cl₁O₃.₅; multiply by 2 → **Cl₂O₇**.
- **Hematite (Fe₂O₃):** 34.97 g Fe → 0.6261 mol; 15.03 g O → 0.9394 mol. Divide → Fe₁O₁.₅; ×2 → **Fe₂O₃**.

## From Percent Composition

If only [[PercentComposition|percent composition]] is known, assume a convenient sample mass (typically **100 g**) so each percent becomes a mass in grams, then apply the method above.
- CO₂: 27.29% C, 72.71% O → in 100 g, 2.272 mol C and 4.544 mol O → divide by smallest → C₁O₂ = **CO₂**.

## From Combustion Analysis (Ch 4)

For hydrocarbons and related compounds, the element masses can come from [[QuantitativeChemicalAnalysis|combustion analysis]]: burn a weighed sample completely in O₂ and capture all CO₂ and H₂O. All C ends up in CO₂ and all H in H₂O, so moles of each element follow directly, then the standard ratio method gives the empirical formula.
- **Polyethylene:** 0.00394 g CO₂ → 8.95 × 10⁻⁵ mol C; 0.00161 g H₂O → 1.79 × 10⁻⁴ mol H. Ratio H:C = 2:1 → **CH₂**.

## Relationship to the Molecular Formula

The empirical formula fixes only the ratio; the actual [[MolecularFormula|molecular formula]] is a whole-number multiple of it, found from the molar mass.
