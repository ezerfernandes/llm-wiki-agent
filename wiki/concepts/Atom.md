# Atom

An **atom** is the smallest particle of a [[ChemicalElement|element]] that retains the properties of that element and can enter into a chemical combination.

The idea originated with the Greek philosophers Leucippus and Democritus (5th century BCE) and was put on a quantitative, experimental footing by [[JohnDalton]] in the early nineteenth century (see [[AtomicTheory]]).

Atoms are extraordinarily small and light. A single carbon atom is about 0.000000015 cm in diameter — roughly 7,000 carbon atoms would span the diameter of one strand of spider web. A billion lead atoms weigh only about 3 × 10⁻¹³ g.

When two or more atoms bond together they form a [[Molecule]]. Atoms are the microscopic-domain building blocks studied in the [[DomainsOfChemistry]].

## Internal Structure (Ch 2)

Dalton's "indivisible" atom was later shown to be divisible. Through the [[EvolutionOfAtomicTheory|experimental work]] of Thomson, Millikan, Rutherford, and Chadwick, the [[AtomicStructure|structure of the atom]] was established: a tiny, dense, positively charged [[AtomicNucleus|nucleus]] of [[Proton|protons]] and [[Neutron|neutrons]] surrounded by [[Electron|electrons]]. The atom's diameter (~10⁻¹⁰ m) is roughly 100,000 times that of its nucleus (~10⁻¹⁵ m). An atom that gains or loses electrons becomes a charged [[ChemicalIon|ion]]. The proton count ([[AtomicNumberAndMassNumber|atomic number Z]]) defines the element; atoms with the same Z but different neutron counts are [[Isotope|isotopes]].

## Electronic Structure (Ch 6)

The surrounding electrons are not on fixed orbits but occupy quantized energy levels described by the quantum-mechanical model: each electron has a set of [[QuantumNumbers|quantum numbers]] and occupies an [[AtomicOrbital|atomic orbital]] (a probability cloud). The resulting [[ElectronConfiguration|electron configuration]] determines the atom's chemical behavior and its place in the [[PeriodicTable|periodic table]]. The [[BohrModel|Bohr model]] was the first quantized picture of the atom.
