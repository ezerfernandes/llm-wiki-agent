## Definition
The Hall effect is the appearance of a voltage (Hall emf, ε) across a current-carrying conductor in a direction perpendicular to both the current and an applied [[MagneticField]]. Discovered by Edwin Hall in 1879.

## Key Points
- The [[LorentzForce]] deflects mobile charge carriers toward one edge of the conductor; the resulting charge buildup creates an [[ElectricField]] that opposes further deflection.
- At equilibrium the electric and magnetic forces balance: qE = qvB, so E = vB.
- The polarity of the Hall voltage reveals the sign of the charge carriers — confirming that electrons carry current in metals while positive carriers dominate some semiconductors.
- The signal is typically small (microvolts) and often requires AC amplification.

## Equations
- Force balance: qE = qvB → E = vB
- Hall voltage (B, v, conductor width l mutually perpendicular): ε = Blv
