## Definition
**Nuclear binding energy (BE)** is the total energy required to disassemble a [[AtomicNucleus|nucleus]] into its separate [[Nucleon|nucleons]]. A bound nucleus has less mass than its separated parts; this **mass defect (Δm)** equals BE/c² via [[MassEnergyEquivalence|E = mc²]].

## Key Points
- **Binding energy per nucleon (BE/A)** measures how tightly each nucleon is bound on average and allows comparison across nuclides.
- The **BE/A curve** (BE/A vs A) rises for light nuclei, peaks near **iron-56** (most stable), then declines for heavy nuclei as [[CoulombsLaw|Coulomb]] proton repulsion increasingly competes with the short-range [[StrongNuclearForce|strong force]].
- Spikes on the curve mark "magic numbers" (closed shells) and Z = N nuclei; ⁴He has an unusually high BE/A (~7.07 MeV/nucleon), explaining [[AlphaDecay|alpha]] emission.
- The curve's shape explains why **fusion** (light nuclei combining) and **fission** (heavy nuclei splitting) both release energy by moving toward iron-56.
- Cosmic abundance of H, ⁴He, ¹²C tracks BE/A peaks; decay of ²³⁸U and ⁴⁰K keeps Earth's interior molten.

## Equations
- Mass defect: Δm = [Z·m(¹H) + N·m_n] − m(ᴬX)
- BE = {[Z·m(¹H) + N·m_n] − m(ᴬX)}c²
- E = (Δm)c²; 1 u = 931.5 MeV/c²
