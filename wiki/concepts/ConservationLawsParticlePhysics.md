## Definition
In [[ParticlePhysics|particle physics]], **conservation laws** on a set of additive **quantum numbers** determine which particle reactions and decays are allowed. Each particle is tagged with quantum numbers, and a process is permitted only if the totals balance on both sides.

## Key Points
- **Exact / always conserved**: energy–mass, momentum, and electric charge (see [[ConservationOfCharge]]).
- **Baryon number (B)**: +1 for matter baryons, −1 for antibaryons, 0 for [[Meson|mesons]] and [[Lepton|leptons]]; total B is conserved (each [[Quark|quark]] contributes +1/3).
- **Lepton family numbers (Lₑ, Lμ, Lτ)**: conserved separately for each [[Lepton|lepton]] family (neutrino oscillations are the known exception).
- **Strangeness (S)**: conserved by the strong force but **not** the weak force — strange particles are produced quickly (strong) yet decay slowly (weak), giving them long lifetimes. The weak force can change quark flavor.
- **Decay lifetimes signal the force**: strong-force decays ~10⁻²³–10⁻¹⁶ s; weak-force decays ~10⁻¹²–10⁻¹⁶ s (longer ⇒ weak). These patterns in the "particle zoo" pointed toward [[Quark|quark]] substructure.

## Equations / examples
- B: each quark +1/3, antiquark −1/3 ⇒ baryon +1, meson 0
- Allowed weak decay: Ξ⁻ → Λ⁰ + π⁻ (ΔS = +1); K⁺ → μ⁺ + νμ
- Neutron beta decay: n → p + e⁻ + ν̄ₑ (B conserved)
