## Definition
Newton's first law of motion: a body at rest stays at rest, and a body in motion continues at constant velocity, unless acted on by a net external force. It is also called the **law of inertia** and identifies that a change in velocity (in magnitude or direction) requires a net external force.

## Key Points
- The law preserves the "status quo" of motion; it does not contradict everyday experience but explains it — friction is usually the hidden net force that decelerates objects.
- On nearly frictionless surfaces (lubricated surfaces, air-hockey tables) motion persists far longer, illustrating the law.
- Mass is the quantitative measure of [[Inertia]] — an object's resistance to changes in motion.
- Forces are vectors, so "net external force" means the vector sum of all external forces.

## Equations
- Condition for constant velocity (equilibrium): F_net = 0
