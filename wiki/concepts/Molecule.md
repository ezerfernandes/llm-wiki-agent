# Molecule

A **molecule** consists of two or more [[Atom|atoms]] joined by strong forces called **chemical bonds**; the bonded atoms move around as a single unit. A molecule's identity is determined by the numbers and types of atoms it contains and how they are bonded together.

Molecules may be made of like or unlike atoms:
- **Elemental molecules:** a few elements exist as single atoms (helium, neon, argon), while others form molecular units — H₂, N₂, O₂, Cl₂ (pairs), P₄ (four atoms), S₈ (eight atoms).
- **Compound molecules:** different elements combined — water (H₂O), carbon dioxide (CO₂), glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆).

Like atoms, molecules are extremely small: if a glass of water were enlarged to the size of the Earth, its molecules would be about golf-ball sized.

Molecules are part of the microscopic [[DomainsOfChemistry|domain of chemistry]] and are the smallest units of many [[ChemicalCompound|compounds]].

## Composition and Bonding (Ch 2)

A molecule's composition is written as a [[ChemicalFormula|chemical formula]] (molecular, empirical, or structural). Molecules of [[MolecularCompound|molecular (covalent) compounds]] form when nonmetal atoms **share** electrons via covalent bonds, in contrast to the electron transfer that produces [[IonicCompound|ionic compounds]]. Molecules with the same formula but different atomic arrangements are **isomers** (structural or spatial). Diatomic elemental molecules include H₂, N₂, O₂, F₂, Cl₂, Br₂, and I₂.
