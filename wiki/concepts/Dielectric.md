## Definition
A **dielectric** is an insulating material placed between the plates of a [[Capacitor]] that increases its [[Capacitance]] and lets it withstand a higher [[Voltage]] than air. Its effect is quantified by the **dielectric constant** κ (dimensionless, κ = 1 for vacuum).

## Key Points
- Mechanism is **polarization**: the applied [[ElectricField]] separates molecular charge, creating an internal field that opposes the applied one, lowering the net field and allowing more charge per volt — no new charge is added.
- **Dielectric strength** is the maximum field before the material breaks down and conducts; it exceeds air's ~3 × 10⁶ V/m for many dielectrics.
- Multiplies parallel-plate capacitance by κ: C = κε₀A/d.
- Example: a Teflon-filled capacitor can tolerate ~60,000 V vs. ~3,000 V for air at the same gap, storing far more charge.

## Equations
- C = κ ε₀ A / d
- ε₀ = 8.85 × 10⁻¹² F/m
- Net field in dielectric reduced by factor κ for fixed charge
