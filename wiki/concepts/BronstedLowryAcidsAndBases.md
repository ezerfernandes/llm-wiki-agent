# Brønsted-Lowry Acids and Bases

The **Brønsted-Lowry definition** (Johannes Brønsted and Thomas Lowry, 1923) describes acids and bases in terms of **proton (H⁺) transfer**:

- A **Brønsted-Lowry acid** is a species that **donates** a proton.
- A **Brønsted-Lowry base** is a species that **accepts** a proton.

Every acid-base reaction is the transfer of a proton from a donor (acid) to an acceptor (base). This is more general than the earlier **Arrhenius** picture (acid yields H₃O⁺ in water; base yields OH⁻), because it does not require water as solvent and applies to any proton transfer. Because the only component of a cation derived from ¹H is a proton, "hydrogen ion" and "proton" are used interchangeably.

## Acid and base ionization

**Acid ionization** is the reaction of a Brønsted-Lowry acid with water, e.g. hydrogen fluoride:

$$\mathrm{HF(aq) + H_2O(l) \rightleftharpoons H_3O^+(aq) + F^-(aq)}$$

**Base ionization** is a species accepting protons from water, e.g. pyridine:

$$\mathrm{H_2O(l) + C_5NH_5(aq) \rightleftharpoons C_5NH_6^+(aq) + OH^-(aq)}$$

In each reaction a [[ConjugateAcidBasePair|conjugate acid-base pair]] is produced. Water itself can act as acid or base — it is [[Amphoteric|amphoteric]] — and reacts with itself in [[WaterAutoionization|autoionization]].

## Strong vs weak

A strong acid/base ionizes essentially completely; a weak one only partially. The extent of ionization is quantified by the [[AcidIonizationConstant|Ka]] / [[BaseIonizationConstant|Kb]]. The strongest acid that can exist in water is H₃O⁺ (the [[LevelingEffect|leveling effect]]).

## Organic weak acids and bases (Ch 20)

The two main weak acid/base families in [[OrganicChemistry|organic chemistry]] fit this proton-transfer picture: **[[CarboxylicAcidsAndEsters|carboxylic acids]]** (–COOH) are **weak acids** (only ~1% ionized in water; they donate H⁺ to a base to form a carboxylate salt), and **[[AminesAndAmides|amines]]** (C–N with a nitrogen lone pair) are **weak bases** like ammonia, accepting a proton to give an ammonium ion (CH₃NH₂ + H⁺ → [CH₃NH₃]⁺). Alkaloids are amine bases that form ammonium salts with dilute acid.

## A subcategory of the Lewis model (Ch 15)

The broader [[LewisAcidsAndBases|Lewis model]] (electron-pair acceptor/donor) subsumes the Brønsted-Lowry picture: a Brønsted-Lowry reaction is exactly a Lewis reaction in which the [[LewisAcid|Lewis acid]] is the proton H⁺. Donating a proton corresponds to the base donating its lone pair (acting as a [[LewisBase|Lewis base]]) to form a [[CoordinateCovalentBond|coordinate covalent bond]] — e.g. H₂O + H⁺ → H₃O⁺.
