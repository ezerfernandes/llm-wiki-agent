# Electrolysis (Electrolytic Cells)

> Disambiguation: this is the *chemistry* concept (driving nonspontaneous redox with applied voltage). Named `ElectrolysisChemistry` to avoid collision with any physics electricity usage.

**Electrolysis** is a process in which an **external circuit does work on a redox system**, imposing a voltage large enough to drive an **otherwise nonspontaneous** reaction. The device is an **electrolytic cell** — the mirror image of a [[GalvanicCell|galvanic cell]], where the reaction is spontaneous and does work *on* the surroundings.

| | Galvanic cell | Electrolytic cell |
|---|---|---|
| E°cell | > 0 (spontaneous) | < 0 (nonspontaneous) |
| Energy | supplies electrical work | consumes electrical work |

To run electrolysis, an applied voltage must **exceed the magnitude of the negative E°cell**.

## Examples
- **Molten NaCl (Downs cell):** industrial Na and Cl₂.
  - Anode: 2Cl⁻(l) → Cl₂(g) + 2e⁻; Cathode: Na⁺(l) + e⁻ → Na(l)
  - Cell: 2Na⁺(l) + 2Cl⁻(l) → 2Na(l) + Cl₂(g)
- **Water:** 2H₂O(l) → 2H₂(g) + O₂(g); E°cell = −1.229 V (needs applied V > +1.229 V).
- **Aqueous NaCl:** water and Cl⁻ compete at the anode (both oxidize → O₂ + Cl₂ mix); water is reduced at the cathode (since [H⁺] ≈ 10⁻⁷ M ≪ 1 M), giving 2H₂O + 2Cl⁻ → H₂ + Cl₂ + 2OH⁻ (the **chlor-alkali process** for Cl₂ and NaOH).
- **Electroplating:** deposits a thin metal coat (e.g., Ag(s) → Ag⁺ at anode, Ag⁺ + e⁻ → Ag at cathode).

## Quantitative Side
The amount deposited follows [[FaradaysLawsOfElectrolysis|Faraday's laws]]: Q = It = nF connects current and time to moles of electrons (and thus mass) via the half-reaction's electron count.
