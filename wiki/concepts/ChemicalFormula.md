# Chemical Formula

A **chemical formula** uses element symbols and subscripts to express the composition of a [[Molecule|molecule]] or [[ChemicalCompound|compound]]. There are three main kinds:

## Molecular Formula

A **molecular formula** gives the actual type and number of atoms in a molecule, with subscripts shown only when more than one atom of a type is present (e.g., CH₄, H₂O, C₆H₁₂O₆).

**Subscripts vs coefficients** differ in meaning:
- **H₂** = one diatomic hydrogen molecule (two bonded atoms).
- **2H** = two separate, unbonded H atoms.
- **2H₂** = two diatomic hydrogen molecules.

## Empirical Formula

An **empirical formula** gives the types of atoms and the **simplest whole-number ratio** of atoms (or ions). A molecular formula is always a whole-number multiple of the empirical formula:

| Compound | Molecular | Empirical |
|----------|-----------|-----------|
| Benzene | C₆H₆ | CH |
| Glucose | C₆H₁₂O₆ | CH₂O |
| Acetic acid | C₂H₄O₂ | CH₂O |
| Titanium dioxide | TiO₂ | TiO₂ |

## Structural Formula

A **structural formula** shows the same atom counts plus **how the atoms are bonded**, with lines representing chemical bonds (an attraction holding atoms/ions together in a molecule or crystal). Three-dimensional shape is conveyed by **ball-and-stick models** (geometry, sizes not to scale) and **space-filling models** (relative atomic sizes).

## Isomers

Compounds sharing a formula but differing in structure are **isomers**:
- **Structural isomers** differ in connectivity — e.g., acetic acid vs methyl formate, both C₂H₄O₂.
- **Spatial isomers** differ in 3-D orientation — e.g., the mirror-image carvones (S-(+) smells of caraway; R-(−) smells of spearmint).

## Determining Formulas (Ch 3)

Chapter 3 develops the quantitative procedures for *finding* these formulas from composition data: deriving an [[EmpiricalFormula|empirical formula]] from [[PercentComposition|percent composition]] (mass → moles → simplest ratio), then scaling it to the [[MolecularFormula|molecular formula]] via n = molar mass ÷ empirical-formula mass. The summed-up mass of a formula is its [[FormulaMass|formula mass]].
