## Definition
A simple pendulum is a small-mass bob suspended from a light string or wire that swings under gravity, with negligible air resistance and string stretch. For small swing angles it behaves as a [[SimpleHarmonicMotion]] oscillator.

## Key Points
- The restoring force is the tangential component of gravity; string tension cancels the component along the string.
- The small-angle approximation (sin θ ≈ θ, valid below ~15°) makes the restoring force proportional to displacement, so the motion is SHM.
- The effective force constant is k = mg/L, giving a period independent of the bob's mass and of amplitude.
- Because T depends on L and g, a pendulum can measure local gravitational acceleration precisely.

## Equations
- Restoring force (small angle): F ≈ -(mg/L) s, where s = L θ
- Effective spring constant: k = mg/L
- Period: T = 2π √(L/g)
- Measuring gravity: g = 4π² L / T²
