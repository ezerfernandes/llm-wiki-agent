## Definition
Electrostatics is the branch of physics dealing with the [[Force|forces]] and [[ElectricField|fields]] produced by stationary (or slowly moving) [[ElectricCharge|electric charge]]. It encompasses [[CoulombsLaw]], the field model, conductor behavior, and a wide range of practical technologies.

## Key Points
- Governing relations: F = qE (force on a charge) and [[CoulombsLaw]] F = k|q₁q₂|/r².
- Excess charge moves to outer surfaces; strong fields can ionize the surrounding medium and bleed charge off.
- Many devices exploit a **photoconductor** (selenium) — an insulator in the dark that conducts in light — and electrostatically charged **toner**.

## Applications
- **Van de Graaff generator:** a belt carries sprayed charge to a large sphere; excess spreads to the outer surface, reaching ~15 MV before air ionization limits it. Used in nuclear-physics research.
- **Xerography / laser printers:** a uniformly charged selenium-coated drum loses charge where light strikes it (a latent image); negative toner clings to charged regions and is transferred to more-positive paper, then heat-fused. A laser writes the charge image for high resolution.
- **Ink-jet printers:** droplets are charged and steered by charged plates; CMYK jets give full color additively.
- **Electrostatic painting:** charged paint repels itself and is attracted to an oppositely charged surface for even coating.
- **Electrostatic precipitator:** charges airborne particles, then collects them on an oppositely charged grid — removes >99% of combustion particulates.
