## Definition
A force is a push or a pull on an object. It has both magnitude and direction, so it is a [[VectorQuantity]]. Forces are the agents that cause acceleration or deformation of matter, and they are the central subject of dynamics.

## Key Points
- Multiple forces acting on a body combine vectorially (head-to-tail), not algebraically, producing a **net (resultant) force**.
- The SI unit is the newton (N), defined as 1 kg·m/s² (≈ 0.225 lb); see [[NewtonsSecondLaw]].
- Force magnitudes can be measured against a reproducible standard such as a stretched spring's restoring force.
- Only **external** forces on a chosen system change its motion; internal forces are ignored (see [[FreeBodyDiagram]]).
- Nearly all everyday forces ([[NormalForce]], [[Tension]], [[Friction]], [[DragForce]]) are electromagnetic in origin; gravity is the exception (see [[FundamentalForces]]).

## Equations
- Net force: F_net = ΣF (vector sum)
- F_net = ma (Newton's second law)
- Resultant of perpendicular components: F = √(Fx² + Fy²); direction θ = tan⁻¹(Fy/Fx)
- 1 N = 1 kg·m/s²
