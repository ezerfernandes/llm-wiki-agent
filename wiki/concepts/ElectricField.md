## Definition
The electric field is a vector field surrounding any [[ElectricCharge|charge]], defined as the electrostatic [[Force]] per unit positive test charge at a point. It is a property of the **source** charge alone, identical at a given distance regardless of the test charge used to probe it — a "force field" enabling action at a distance.

## Key Points
- A **test charge** is small enough not to disturb the source distribution it measures.
- Direction: along the field for a positive charge, opposite for a negative charge; field points away from + sources and toward − sources.
- Field strength of a point charge follows the same inverse-square form as [[CoulombsLaw]] and as the [[GravitationalField]].
- Fields from multiple charges add by the **superposition principle** (vector sum) — see [[ElectricFieldLines]].
- The same field concept generalizes the electromagnetic [[FundamentalForces|fundamental force]].

## Equations
- Field definition: E = F / q
- Point-charge field: E = k |Q| / r²  (k = 8.99 × 10⁹ N·m²/C²)
- Force on a charge in a field: F = q E
- Units: N/C
