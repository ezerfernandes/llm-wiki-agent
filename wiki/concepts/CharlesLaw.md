# Charles's Law

**Charles's law** states that the volume of a confined amount of gas held at constant pressure is **directly proportional to its absolute (kelvin) temperature**:

$$V \propto T \quad\Rightarrow\quad V = kT \quad\Rightarrow\quad \frac{V_1}{T_1} = \frac{V_2}{T_2}$$

(at constant P and n). The temperature **must** be in kelvin. It is named for Jacques Alexandre César Charles, the French scientist and balloon-flight pioneer.

**Example:** CO₂ occupying 0.300 L at 283 K expands to 0.300 × (303/283) = 0.321 L at 303 K and the same pressure.

**Gas thermometer application:** hydrogen at 273.15 K occupying 150.0 cm³ shrinks to 131.7 cm³ at an unknown temperature, giving T = 273.15 × (131.7/150.0) = 239.8 K (−33.4 °C, the boiling point of liquid ammonia).

One of the four simple [[GasLaws|gas laws]]. The [[KineticMolecularTheory|kinetic-molecular theory]] explains it: at higher T molecules move faster, but at constant pressure the gas expands so that collision frequency per unit area stays balanced.
