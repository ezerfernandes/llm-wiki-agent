# Wave-Particle Duality

**Wave-particle duality** is the principle that both light and matter exhibit wave-like *and* particle-like behavior. Light shows interference (wave) yet ejects electrons as discrete packets (particle, see [[PhotonQuantum]]); matter, conversely, has an associated wavelength.

## de Broglie Matter Waves
Louis de Broglie (1924) proposed that any particle of momentum p = mv has a wavelength:

> **λ = h/(mv) = h/p**

This is large enough to matter for tiny particles and negligible for macroscopic ones:
- Electron at v = 1.000 × 10⁷ m/s: λ = 7.274 × 10⁻¹¹ m — comparable to atomic dimensions, so wave behavior is observable in atoms.
- Softball (0.1 kg, 35 m/s): λ = 1.9 × 10⁻³⁴ m — undetectable, so it behaves purely as a particle.

For an electron in a circular orbit, the standing-wave condition **2πr = nλ** (n = 1, 2, 3, …) reproduces the [[BohrModel|Bohr model]]'s quantization: only an integer number of wavelengths fits the orbit.

## Experimental Confirmation
Davisson and Germer fired electrons at a crystalline nickel target and recorded interference patterns identical to light through a double slit. Individual electrons arrive as localized particles, but their accumulated distribution is wavelike — their motion is governed by a wave equation (the [[QuantumNumbers|Schrödinger]] picture).

## Universality and Observability (College Physics 2e Ch.29)
The physics text stresses that duality is **universal**: every particle — massless ([[PhotonQuantum|photons]]) or massive (electrons, atoms, molecules) — has both characters via [[DeBroglieWavelength|λ = h/p]]. A single observation reveals one set of properties, never both at once. Which dominates is mass- and scale-dependent: massive macroscopic bodies look like particles (negligible wavelength), while large numbers of quanta look like waves — the correspondence principle. The matter half is confirmed by the [[ComptonEffect|Compton effect]] (photon momentum) on one side and Davisson-Germer electron diffraction (matter waves) on the other. A photon recoiling off a 1 µg dust grain illustrates [[LinearMomentum|momentum]] conservation across the massless/massive divide.

## Significance
Wave-particle duality underlies the [[HeisenbergUncertaintyPrinciple|uncertainty principle]] and the quantum-mechanical [[AtomicOrbital|orbital]] description of electrons, distinguishing quantum theory from classical mechanics.
