# Photon (Quantum of Light)

A **photon** is a discrete particle (quantum) of [[ElectromagneticRadiation|electromagnetic radiation]]. Its energy depends only on frequency:

> **E = hν = hc/λ**

where h = 6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s and c = 2.998 × 10⁸ m/s. Higher frequency (shorter wavelength) ⇒ higher-energy photon. Light intensity (brightness) corresponds to the *number* of photons per unit time, not the energy of each photon.

## The Photoelectric Effect
Electrons are ejected from a clean metal surface only when the incident light exceeds a **threshold frequency**. Two surprising observations broke classical wave theory:
- The kinetic energy of ejected electrons depends on light **frequency**, not on brightness (amplitude).
- Below the threshold, no electrons are ejected no matter how bright the light; above it, brighter light ejects *more* electrons but does not raise their kinetic energy.

**Einstein's resolution (1905):** treating light as photons (E = hν) explains everything. A single photon must carry enough energy to overcome the electron's binding energy; excess photon energy becomes the electron's kinetic energy. More photons (brighter light) ⇒ more ejected electrons.

### Worked values
- Neon-sign photon (640 nm): E = hc/λ = 3.10 × 10⁻¹⁹ J.
- Aluminum threshold (ν = 9.87 × 10¹⁴ Hz): E = hν = 6.53 × 10⁻¹⁹ J/photon = 394 kJ/mol.

## Photon Energies Across the EM Spectrum (College Physics 2e Ch.29)
Photon energy rises with frequency, so the [[ElectromagneticSpectrum|EM spectrum]] is also an energy ladder. Handy constants: **h = 4.14 × 10⁻¹⁵ eV·s** and **hc = 1240 eV·nm** (see [[PlancksConstant]]). Energy determines the effect on matter: gamma/X-rays (keV–MeV) penetrate and ionize; UV (4–300 eV) ionizes and breaks bonds; [[VisibleLight|visible]] (1.63–3.26 eV) matches atomic transitions and triggers single retinal receptors; IR/microwaves are non-ionizing and heat by exciting water. Even ordinary sources emit vast photon counts — a 100 W bulb radiates ~10¹⁹ visible photons per second. X-ray production gives hf = qV (max photon energy = accelerated electron's kinetic energy).

## Photon Momentum
Although massless, a photon carries momentum p = h/λ = E/c, from the relativistic relation E² = (pc)² + (mc²)² with m = 0. This was confirmed by the [[ComptonEffect|Compton effect]] and drives comet tails and solar sails. See [[ComptonEffect]] for the full treatment.

## The Photon as Force Carrier (College Physics 2e Ch.33)
In particle physics the photon is the **[[CarrierParticle|carrier particle]]** of the electromagnetic force: two charged particles interact by exchanging **[[VirtualParticle|virtual photons]]**, depicted in Feynman diagrams. Because the photon is massless, the electromagnetic force has infinite range. The photon is a spin-1 **boson** with no charge, baryon, or lepton number, and is stable. Quantum electrodynamics (QED) — the quantum mechanics of electromagnetism — is one component of the [[StandardModel|Standard Model]]; at ~100 GeV the photon's interaction merges with the W/Z-mediated weak force as the [[ElectroweakForce|electroweak]] force.

## Significance
The photoelectric effect, alongside [[AtomicSpectra|line spectra]] and [[BlackbodyRadiation|blackbody radiation]], established that light carries energy in quantized packets — half of [[WaveParticleDuality|wave-particle duality]].
