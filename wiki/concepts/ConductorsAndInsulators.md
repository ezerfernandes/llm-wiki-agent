## Definition
A **conductor** is a material whose charge carriers (free electrons in metals, or ions in solution) move readily through it; an **insulator** is a material in which charges are bound and flow is suppressed — roughly 10²³ times slower than in a conductor. Examples: metals and salt water conduct; glass, dry salt, and pure water insulate.

## Key Points
- **Free electrons** are electrons not bound to particular atoms; in a charged metal only electrons move while positive ions stay fixed.
- An **ion** is an atom or molecule with net charge.
- **Polarization** is charge separation within an object that stays overall neutral; **polar molecules** like water have built-in dipoles (see [[ElectricField]]).
- A conductor in static equilibrium has zero internal field and excess charge on its surface — see [[ElectrostaticInduction]].
- Because the electrostatic [[Force]] weakens with distance, induced charge nearer a charged inducer attracts more strongly than the farther like charge repels, so neutral objects are drawn to any charged body.

## Charging mechanisms
1. **By contact (conduction):** a charged object touches a neutral conductor and transfers charge.
2. **By induction:** a nearby charge rearranges charge in a neutral conductor without contact; splitting the conductor leaves opposite net charges (see [[ElectrostaticInduction]]).
3. **By induction with grounding:** a ground wire to Earth lets charge flow; disconnecting before removing the inducer leaves an opposite net charge.
