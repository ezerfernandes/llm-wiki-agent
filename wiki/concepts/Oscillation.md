## Definition
An oscillation is a motion that repeats cyclically between two extremes about an equilibrium position. Examples span ocean buoys, swings, speaker cones, vibrating strings, crystal lattices, and biological rhythms.

## Key Points
- Oscillation requires a restoring force that pushes the system back toward equilibrium (often a [[HookesLaw]] force, F = -kx).
- A net force is essential; by [[NewtonsFirstLaw]] motion would otherwise be uniform and straight.
- Characterized by [[Period]], [[Frequency]], and amplitude (maximum displacement).
- The special, idealized case with a Hooke's-law restoring force and no dissipation is [[SimpleHarmonicMotion]].
- Real oscillators lose energy ([[DampedOscillation]]) and can be driven externally, producing [[Resonance]].

## Equations
- f = 1/T
- Restoring force (linear): F = -kx
