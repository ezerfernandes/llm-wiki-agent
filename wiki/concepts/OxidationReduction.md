# Oxidation-Reduction (Redox) Reaction

An **oxidation-reduction (redox) reaction** is one in which one or more elements undergo a change in [[OxidationNumber|oxidation number]]. It is one of the three classes in [[ClassifyingChemicalReactions]]. (Note: this is the chemistry concept; the algorithmic "reduction" lives on unrelated pages.)

## Electron-Transfer View

In many redox reactions electrons pass between species. For 2Na(s) + Cl₂(g) → 2NaCl(s), the **half-reactions** are:

- 2Na(s) → 2Na⁺(s) + 2e⁻
- Cl₂(g) + 2e⁻ → 2Cl⁻(s)

Definitions:
- **Oxidation** = **loss** of electrons
- **Reduction** = **gain** of electrons

Sodium is oxidized; chlorine is reduced. The species that is oxidized acts as the **reducing agent (reductant)**; the species that is reduced acts as the **oxidizing agent (oxidant)**. Here Na is the reducing agent and Cl₂ is the oxidizing agent.

## Oxidation-Number View

Using [[OxidationNumber|oxidation numbers]], the definitions generalize even when no full electron transfer occurs:
- **Oxidation** = **increase** in oxidation number
- **Reduction** = **decrease** in oxidation number

For H₂(g) + Cl₂(g) → 2HCl(g): H goes 0 → +1 (oxidized); Cl goes 0 → −1 (reduced).

## Subclasses

- **Combustion** — a fuel (reductant) reacts vigorously with an oxidant (often O₂), releasing heat and usually light. Solid rocket fuel: 10Al(s) + 6NH₄ClO₄(s) → 4Al₂O₃(s) + 2AlCl₃(s) + 12H₂O(g) + 3N₂(g).
- **Single-displacement (replacement)** — a metallic element is oxidized, displacing an ion from solution: Zn(s) + 2HCl(aq) → ZnCl₂(aq) + H₂(g); Cu(s) + 2AgNO₃(aq) → Cu(NO₃)₂(aq) + 2Ag(s).
- **Disproportionation** — the *same* element is both oxidized and reduced: 2H₂O₂(aq) → 2H₂O(l) + O₂(g) (O goes −1 → 0 and −1 → −2).

## Half-Reaction Method (acidic solution)

1. Write the two half-reactions. 2. Balance all atoms except O and H. 3. Balance O with H₂O. 4. Balance H with H⁺. 5. Balance charge with electrons. 6. Scale half-reactions so electrons cancel. 7. Add and simplify. 8. For basic media, add OH⁻ equal to H⁺, combine into H₂O, simplify. 9. Check atoms and charge.

Example (dichromate + iron(II), acidic): 6Fe²⁺ + Cr₂O₇²⁻ + 14H⁺ → 6Fe³⁺ + 2Cr³⁺ + 7H₂O.

## Oxidizing and Reducing Strength of the Elements
Across the descriptive chemistry of the [[RepresentativeElements|representative elements]] (Ch 18), redox strength follows the periodic table: the [[AlkaliMetals|alkali]] and heavier [[AlkalineEarthMetals|alkaline earth metals]] are strong reducing agents (more easily oxidized than hydrogen, so they release H₂ from water/acids), while the [[Nonmetals|nonmetals]] are oxidizers — **fluorine and oxygen are the strongest elemental oxidizing agents**, each oxidizing every element below it in its group. The ease of oxidizing a [[HalogensChemistry|halide]] ion runs F⁻ (hardest) → I⁻ (easiest), the inverse of the parent halogen's oxidizing power; chemical reduction by carbon or silicon underlies much of [[Metallurgy|metal isolation]].

## In Electrochemistry
When the two half-reactions are physically separated, the electron flow can be routed through an external circuit — the basis of [[Electrochemistry|electrochemistry]]. A spontaneous redox reaction powers a [[GalvanicCell|galvanic cell]] (battery); an applied voltage drives a nonspontaneous one via [[ElectrolysisChemistry|electrolysis]]. The redox driving force is quantified as a [[CellPotential|cell potential]] built from [[StandardReductionPotential|standard reduction potentials]].
