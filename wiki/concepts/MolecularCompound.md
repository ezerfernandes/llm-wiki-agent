# Molecular Compound

A **molecular compound** (or **covalent compound**) is a [[ChemicalCompound|compound]] made of discrete, electrically neutral [[Molecule|molecules]] formed when atoms **share** [[Electron|electrons]] rather than transferring them.

## Covalent Bonding

The atoms are held together by **covalent bonds** — attractive forces between the positively charged nuclei of the bonded atoms and one or more shared electron pairs located between them. This electron *sharing* distinguishes molecular compounds from [[IonicCompound|ionic compounds]], where electrons are *transferred*.

## Recognition Rule

*Covalent compounds are usually formed by a combination of nonmetals.* Using [[PeriodicTable|periodic-table]] position:
- **Metal + nonmetal(s)** → typically ionic.
- **Nonmetal + nonmetal(s)** → typically molecular.

There are "shades of gray" between ionic and molecular character, and the simple rule has exceptions.

## Physical Properties

Under ordinary conditions, molecular compounds are commonly gases, low-boiling liquids, or low-melting solids — generally lower melting/boiling points than ionic compounds.

## Naming

Because covalent bonding allows variable combining ratios, molecular-compound names use Greek numerical prefixes (mono-, di-, tri-, …) to specify atom counts — see [[ChemicalNomenclature]] (e.g., CO carbon monoxide, CO₂ carbon dioxide, N₂O₄ dinitrogen tetroxide).
