# Electrochemical Cell

An **electrochemical cell** is a device that contains all the reactants and products of a [[OxidationReduction|redox]] system while **preventing their direct physical contact**, so that electron transfer is forced to occur through an **external circuit**. This separation is what allows the redox reaction's electron flow to be harnessed (or driven) as electrical current.

## Anatomy
- **Two half-cells**, each holding one redox **couple** (a conjugate oxidant/reductant pair).
- **Electrodes** — solid conductors where the half-reactions occur: the **anode** (oxidation) and the **cathode** (reduction). Electrodes may be **active** (a member of the couple, e.g., a Mg strip) or **inert** (Pt, used when neither couple member is a usable solid).
- **External circuit** — carries electrons between electrodes.
- **Salt bridge / porous barrier** — an inert [[Electrolyte|electrolyte]] connection that maintains charge balance by letting spectator ions migrate between half-cells.

## Two Subtypes
- **[[GalvanicCell|Galvanic (voltaic) cell]]** — the redox reaction is **spontaneous** (E°cell > 0); the cell sources electrical power.
- **Electrolytic cell** — the reaction is **nonspontaneous**; an external supply drives it (see [[ElectrolysisChemistry]]).

In both, electrons leave the anode and arrive at the cathode; the difference is whether the reaction supplies or consumes electrical work. The composition is summarized with [[ElectrochemicalCellNotation|cell notation]].
