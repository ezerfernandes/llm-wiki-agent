## Definition
A current-carrying [[Inductor|inductor]] stores energy in its [[MagneticField|magnetic field]]. The stored energy is `E = ½LI²`, set by the device's [[Inductance|inductance]] L and the current I. This is the magnetic counterpart of the energy `½CV²` stored in a [[Capacitor|capacitor]]'s electric field.

## Key Points
- Building up the current does work against the inductor's self-induced [[BackEMF|back emf]] ([[LenzsLaw|Lenz's law]]); that work is recoverable as stored magnetic energy.
- The energy is released when the current is reduced — so abruptly interrupting current in a large inductor produces a dangerous high-voltage spike.
- Energy scales with the square of the current, so doubling the current quadruples the stored energy.

## Equations
- `E = ½ L I²`  (energy stored in an inductor)
