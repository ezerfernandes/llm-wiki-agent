## Definition
A radiopharmaceutical is a radioactive compound — a radioisotope built into a drug — engineered to localize in a specific organ or tissue so its radiation can be detected (diagnosis) or used to deliver dose (therapy). A **tagged (labeled) compound** is a molecule in which a radioactive nuclide replaces a stable atom, preserving the chemistry while making the molecule traceable.

## Key Points
- **Technetium-99m (⁹⁹ᵐTc)** is the workhorse: half-life 6.0 h, emits a single 0.142-MeV gamma, produced by neutron activation of molybdenum followed by beta decay; used in ~80% of nuclear-medicine procedures.
- Tracer selection balances target affinity, emission type/energy, and a [[HalfLife]] short enough to limit dose yet long enough to image.
- Common diagnostic tracers: ¹³¹I / ¹²³I (thyroid uptake), ²⁰¹Tl (cardiac perfusion), ⁶⁷Ga (tumor/infection), ⁹⁹ᵐTc (bone scan), and PET agents ¹⁸F, ¹¹C, ¹³N, ¹⁵O.
- Therapeutic versions deliver localized dose (e.g. radioactive iodine concentrating in the thyroid).

## Equations
- Activity vs decays: N = R · t (R = activity in decays/s).
- Activity decays exponentially per [[HalfLife]].
