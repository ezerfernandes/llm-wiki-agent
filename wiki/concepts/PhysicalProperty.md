# Physical Property

A **physical property** is a characteristic of [[Matter]] that is *not* associated with a change in its chemical composition. Examples include density, color, hardness, melting and boiling points, and electrical conductivity. Some physical properties can be observed without changing the substance's state (color, density); others can only be observed during a change of state (the melting temperature of iron).

A **physical change** is a change in the state or properties of matter without any change in the chemical identities of the substances. Examples: wax melting, sugar dissolving in coffee, steam condensing, magnetizing/demagnetizing metals, and grinding solids into powder. Phase transitions among the [[StatesOfMatter]] are physical changes.

Physical changes contrast with [[ChemicalProperty|chemical changes]] (which produce new substances). Physical properties are also classed as extensive or intensive — see [[ExtensiveAndIntensiveProperties]].
