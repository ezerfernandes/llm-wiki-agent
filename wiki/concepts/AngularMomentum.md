## Definition
Angular momentum, L = Iω, is the rotational analog of [[LinearMomentum]]: a measure of how much rotational motion a body has, set by its [[MomentOfInertia]] and [[AngularVelocity]]. Units: kg·m²/s. It is a vector directed along the rotation axis (see [[GyroscopicPrecession]]).

## Key Points
- The rotational form of [[NewtonsSecondLaw]] in momentum terms is net τ = ΔL/Δt: a net [[Torque]] changes angular momentum, just as a net force changes linear momentum.
- A torque must act *over time* to change L (analogous to impulse changing linear momentum).
- When net external torque is zero, L is conserved — see [[ConservationOfAngularMomentum]].
- Massive bodies carry enormous L even at small ω (e.g., the spinning Earth).
- Angular momentum is a vector; the related [[RotationalKineticEnergy]] is a scalar.

## Equations
- L = I ω   (units kg·m²/s)
- net τ = ΔL / Δt
- For a point mass at radius r: L = m r² (v/r) = m v r
- I (uniform sphere) = (2/5) M R²;  I (disk) = ½ M R²
