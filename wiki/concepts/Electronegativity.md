# Electronegativity

**Electronegativity** is a measure of the tendency of an atom to attract electrons (electron density) toward itself within a chemical bond. It determines how the shared electrons in a [[CovalentBonding|covalent bond]] are distributed: electrons shift toward the more electronegative atom, which thereby acquires the partial negative charge (δ−). The greater the electronegativity difference, the more polarized the distribution and the larger the partial charges.

## The Pauling Scale and Periodic Trends

The most common scale was proposed by [[LinusPauling|Linus Pauling]]. Electronegativity **increases** left-to-right across a period and **decreases** down a group. Consequently the nonmetals in the upper right are most electronegative — **fluorine is the most electronegative element (EN = 4.0)** — while the group 1 metals are least electronegative. Noble gases are excluded, as they normally do not share electrons. These trends parallel other [[PeriodicTrends|periodic trends]].

## Electronegativity vs. Electron Affinity

These are distinct. [[ElectronAffinity|Electron affinity]] is a *measurable* physical quantity — the energy released/absorbed when a gas-phase atom gains an electron (kJ/mol). Electronegativity is a *dimensionless, calculated* quantity describing how tightly an atom holds electrons within a bond.

## Predicting Bond Type from ΔEN

The absolute electronegativity difference |ΔEN| roughly predicts [[BondPolarity|bond polarity]] and bond type:

| Bond Type | |ΔEN| |
|---|---|
| Pure (nonpolar) covalent | < 0.4 |
| Polar covalent | 0.4 – 1.8 |
| Ionic | > 1.8 |

Examples: H–H (0, nonpolar); H–Cl (0.9, polar covalent); Na–Cl (2.1, ionic).
