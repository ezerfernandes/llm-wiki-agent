# Knowledge Distillation

Training a smaller / cheaper *student* model to mimic a larger / stronger *teacher* model. The student inherits much of the teacher's capability at a fraction of the deployment cost — the recipe behind the BERT → DistilBERT, GPT-3 → instruction-tuned-smaller, and (most recently) frontier-LM → small-validator distillation lines.

## MedVAL as a concrete instance

[[2507.03152-medval|Aali et al. (MedVAL, 2026)]] is the wiki's most thorough single-paper account of distillation applied to **medical text validation**. The pipeline:

1. **Generator $g_\theta$ (teacher curator)** — typically [[gpt54|GPT-4o]] — produces synthetic training data with controlled perturbations.
2. **[[GeneratorValidatorConsistency|$\mathcal{M}_\mathrm{MedVAL}$ filter]]** — retains examples where teacher and student agree on the perturbation level; discards 43%.
3. **[[BootstrapFinetune|`dspy.BootstrapFinetune`]] + [[QLoRA]]** — student LM ([[qwen|Qwen3-4B]] / Llama-3.1-8B / Llama-3.2-3B for open; GPT-4o / GPT-4o Mini via OpenAI's API for proprietary) is fine-tuned on the filtered subset.

Headline results that reframe what "distillation" can mean here:
- **Filtering > teacher strength**: self-distilled+filtered ([[gpt54|GPT-4o]] not involved) on **3% of data** beats unfiltered ([[gpt54|GPT-4o]] teacher) on **100% of data** for Llama-3.2-3B (12.8% → 22.1% vs 12.8% → 13.6%).
- **Frontier-teacher distillation lifts even the frontier student**: GPT-4o MedVAL beats GPT-4o baseline (+8%) — distillation isn't just for smaller students.
- **4B student exceeds 70B baseline**: [[MedVAL4B|MedVAL-Qwen3-4B]] (F1 = 0.527) beats Llama-3.3-70B (0.480) zero-shot.

## From [[ai-engineering-ch07-finetuning|AI Engineering Ch 7]]

Ch 7 frames distillation as **the canonical use case for finetuning a small model to imitate a large one**, with two practical recipes:

> "A common approach is to finetune a small model to imitate the behavior of a larger model using data generated by this large model. Because this approach distills the larger model's knowledge into the smaller model, it's called distillation."

[[ChipHuyen|Huyen]] also names distillation as one of the **two [[openai|OpenAI]] base-model selection workflows** (vs. the [[OpenAIProgressionPath|progression path]]):

### [[OpenAIDistillationPath|The distillation path]] (Ch 7)

1. **Start with a small dataset and the strongest model you can afford.** Finetune this teacher on the small dataset.
2. **Use this finetuned teacher to generate more training data.**
3. **Train a cheaper student model** on the synthetic dataset.

The key insight: the strong base model needs **less data** to reach good quality, but its teacher-quality outputs are then large enough to train a cheaper student.

### The [[Grammarly]] case (referenced in Ch 7)

Not strictly distillation, but adjacent: [[Grammarly]] finetuned [[FlanT5|Flan-T5]] models on 82,000 (instruction, output) pairs and beat a GPT-3 variant specialized in text editing across writing-assistant tasks — at **1/60 the size**. The lesson: small models + good task data can outperform large models out-of-the-box. Ch 8 (next chapter) covers the synthetic-data side of distillation more fully.

### Canonical Ch 8 distillation examples

| Student | Teacher | Result |
|---|---|---|
| [[DistilBERT]] | [[bert\|BERT]] | 40% smaller, 60% faster, 97% of language-comprehension capability (Sanh et al. 2019) |

### The Ch 8 caveat — "not all synthetic-data training is distillation"

Distillation implies the teacher's performance is the student's **gold standard**. But:

- **[[Nemotron4|Nemotron-4-340B]]** used [[Mixtral8x7B|Mixtral-8x7B-Instruct-v0.1]] as teacher (56B params, MoE) and student is 340B dense — **student exceeds teacher**. This is reverse-direction synthetic training, not distillation.
- **Model bootstrapping with [[ReverseInstruction|reverse instruction]]** (Li et al. 2023): a weak model generates instructions used to finetune itself iteratively — student and teacher are *the same* model.

The chapter's reframe: **"distillation" is the subset of synthetic-data training where teacher ≥ student in capability**; the broader category is just *training with AI-generated data*.

### The license caveat

> "Many model licenses prohibit using their outputs to train other models, particularly to train competing models."

A legal constraint orthogonal to feasibility — distillation may be technically easy and contractually forbidden.

### Why distillation is structurally different from other synthesis use cases

Per Ch 8: distillation is **the one use case where AI-generated data isn't supplementary — it's required**. You cannot distill a model using human-generated data (you'd be doing something else). All other Ch 8 synthesis use cases (quantity, coverage, quality, privacy) allow mixing or substitution with human data.
