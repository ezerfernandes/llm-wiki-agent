# Lewis Structure

**Lewis symbols** and **Lewis structures** are notations (introduced by [[GilbertLewis|Gilbert N. Lewis]]) for depicting valence electrons and [[CovalentBonding|covalent bonding]]. (Named *LewisStructure*; the bare name avoids unrelated wiki pages.)

## Lewis Symbols

A **Lewis symbol** (electron-dot symbol) places one dot per valence electron around an element's symbol: Na (1 dot), Mg (2), P (5), Cl (7), Ar (8). For ions, dots are removed for cations (Na → Na⁺ + e⁻) or added for anions (Cl + e⁻ → Cl⁻, 8 dots). They illustrate electron transfer in ionic-compound formation (e.g., Na• + •Cl → Na⁺ + [Cl]⁻).

## Lewis Structures

A **Lewis structure** depicts a molecule or polyatomic ion, showing **bonding pairs** (shared electrons, drawn as a line) and **lone pairs** (nonbonding). Atoms may form **single** (one pair), **double** (two pairs, e.g., C=O in CH₂O), or **triple** bonds (three pairs, e.g., C≡O in CO, C≡N in CN⁻). They are governed by the [[OctetRule|octet rule]].

## Five-Step Procedure

1. **Count total valence electrons.** Add atom valences; for anions add one e⁻ per negative charge (CHO₂⁻ = 18); for cations subtract one per positive charge (NO⁺ = 10).
2. **Draw the skeleton**: least electronegative atom central (never H; never F); single-bond each terminal atom to it. Bracket polyatomic ions with the charge.
3. **Add lone pairs to terminal atoms** to complete their octets (except H).
4. **Place leftover electrons on the central atom**.
5. **Form multiple bonds** if the central atom lacks an octet, by shifting terminal lone pairs into double/triple bonds.

Worked examples: HCN → H–C≡N; HCCH → H–C≡C–H; NH₃ (one lone pair on N); CO₂ → O=C=O.

## Exceptions to the Octet Rule

See [[OctetRule]]: odd-electron free radicals (NO, 11 e⁻), electron-deficient molecules (BeH₂, BF₃), and hypervalent/expanded-octet molecules (PCl₅, SF₆, XeF₄). [[FormalCharge|Formal charge]] and [[ResonanceStructure|resonance]] refine the choice of structure.

## Limits Addressed by Deeper Theories

Lewis structures show shared and lone pairs but not the quantum origin of bonds, and they sometimes mispredict: most famously they show O₂ with all electrons paired, yet O₂ is [[Paramagnetism|paramagnetic]] (two unpaired electrons), which only [[MolecularOrbitalTheory|molecular orbital theory]] predicts. A Lewis structure is nonetheless the starting point for assigning [[OrbitalHybridization|hybridization]] (count regions of electron density) and for [[ValenceBondTheory|valence bond]] σ/π analysis.
