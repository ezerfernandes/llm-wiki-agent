## Definition
Reflection is the bouncing of light off a surface. The **law of reflection** states that the angle of reflection equals the angle of incidence, both measured from the normal (perpendicular) to the surface at the point of incidence.

(Distinct from the agentic-design pattern [[Reflection]] and from programming reflection — this page is the optics phenomenon.)

## Key Points
- **Specular reflection:** organized, directional reflection from a smooth surface (mirror, polished metal).
- **Diffuse reflection:** scattering in many directions from a rough surface; light hits different parts at different angles, so it reflects every which way. This is why we see most ordinary objects from many angles.
- The law applies to every reflecting surface and is the basis of mirror image formation ([[OpticalMirror]]).
- Reflecting telescopes and moonlight on water (mixing both kinds) are everyday examples.

## Equations
- Law of reflection: `θ_r = θ_i` (angles measured from the normal)
