# Fuel Cell

A **fuel cell** is a [[GalvanicCell|galvanic cell]] that uses traditional combustive fuels — most often **hydrogen or methane** — that are **continuously fed** into the cell along with an oxidant (usually O₂). Unlike a [[ElectrochemicalBattery|battery]], its reactants are not sealed inside; it generates power as long as fuel is supplied.

## Hydrogen Fuel Cell (~1.2 V)
Graphite electrodes embedded with platinum-based catalysts:
- **Anode:** 2H₂(g) → 4H⁺(aq) + 4e⁻
- **Cathode:** O₂(g) + 4H⁺(aq) + 4e⁻ → 2H₂O(g)
- **Overall:** 2H₂(g) + O₂(g) → 2H₂O(g)

## Efficiency and Use
Energy efficiency is typically **50–75%**, versus ~20–25% for an internal combustion engine running the same reaction. Fuel cells are commonly used on extended space missions; personal-vehicle prototypes exist, but the technology remains relatively immature.
