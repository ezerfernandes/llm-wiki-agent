## Definition
**Faraday's law of induction** states that a changing [[MagneticFlux|magnetic flux]] through a circuit induces an [[ElectromotiveForce|emf]] proportional to the rate of change of that flux and to the number of turns N in the coil. Discovered by [[MichaelFaraday]], it is the quantitative core of electromagnetic induction and the operating principle behind [[ElectricGenerator|generators]], [[ElectricalTransformer|transformers]], and [[Inductance|inductors]].

## Key Points
- Induced emf grows with the rate of flux change (ΔΦ/Δt), with more coil turns N, and with stronger/faster field changes.
- The negative sign expresses [[LenzsLaw]]: the induced effect opposes the change that caused it.
- Practical voltages typically require strong magnets, many turns, or rapid changes — a single slow loop yields only millivolts.
- Applies to any flux change: moving magnet/coil, changing field strength, or changing loop area/orientation.

## Equations
- `emf = −N (ΔΦ/Δt)`  (with direction)
- `emf = N (ΔΦ/Δt)`  (magnitude only)
- `ΔΦ = Δ(B A cos θ) = A Δ(B cos θ)`
