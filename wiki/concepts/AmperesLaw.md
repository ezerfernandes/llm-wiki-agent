## Definition
Ampère's law is the general relationship between an electric current and the [[MagneticField]] it produces. It is one of Maxwell's equations. The Biot–Savart law is a complementary statement: each current segment contributes a field like a long straight wire, and the total field is the vector sum of all contributions.

## Key Points
- The fundamental constant is the permeability of free space, μ₀ = 4π × 10⁻⁷ T·m/A.
- A current's field direction follows the right-hand rule RHR-2: thumb along the current, fingers curl in the direction of the field loops.
- A straight wire produces concentric circular field lines; a circular loop produces a dipole field; a [[Solenoid]] produces a near-uniform interior field.
- Ordinary currents (~25 A) produce fields comparable to Earth's at a measurable distance; MRI-strength fields require very large currents (or superconducting coils).

## Equations
- Long straight wire: B = μ₀I / (2πr)
- Center of a circular loop: B = μ₀I / (2R); for N loops, B = Nμ₀I / (2R)
- Solenoid interior: B = μ₀nI (n = N/l turns per unit length)
