# Thermochemical Equation

A **thermochemical equation** represents the changes in both matter and energy for a reaction: it is a balanced [[ChemicalEquation|chemical equation]] followed by its [[EnthalpyChemistry|enthalpy change]] ΔH. The ΔH value applies to the molar amounts of reactants and products *exactly as written*.

Example:

> H₂(g) + ½O₂(g) → H₂O(l)   ΔH = −286 kJ

means that when 1 mol H₂ and ½ mol O₂ form 1 mol liquid water, 286 kJ of heat are released (exothermic).

## Rules for Manipulation
1. **Extensive scaling** — multiplying the coefficients by a factor multiplies ΔH by the same factor:
   - 2H₂(g) + O₂(g) → 2H₂O(l)   ΔH = 2 × (−286) = −572 kJ
   - ½H₂(g) + ¼O₂(g) → ½H₂O(l)   ΔH = ½ × (−286) = −143 kJ
2. **Reversal** — reversing the equation reverses the sign of ΔH.
3. **Physical-state dependence** — ΔH depends on the states of reactants and products, so (s), (l), (g), (aq) must be shown. For example, forming H₂O(l) releases more heat (−286 kJ) than forming H₂O(g) (−242 kJ).

These rules are exactly the properties of ΔH exploited by [[HessLaw|Hess's law]].
