## Definition
The components of a vector are its perpendicular projections onto the coordinate axes (typically x and y). A vector of magnitude A pointing at angle θ from the x-axis has a horizontal component Ax and a vertical component Ay; these independent parts can be recombined to reconstruct the original vector.

## Key Points
- Resolving into components is the foundation of analytical [[VectorAddition]] and of analyzing [[TwoDimensionalKinematics]].
- Components along perpendicular axes are independent (independence of perpendicular directions); components on the same axis add algebraically like scalars.
- Given magnitude and angle, use cosine for the x-component and sine for the y-component; given components, use the Pythagorean theorem and arctangent to recover magnitude and direction.
- Underpins [[ProjectileMotion]] (resolving initial velocity into v0x, v0y) and [[RelativeVelocity]].

## Equations
- Resolve: Ax = A cos θ ; Ay = A sin θ
- Recombine: A = √(Ax² + Ay²) ; θ = tan⁻¹(Ay / Ax)
