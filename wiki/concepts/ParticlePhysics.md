## Definition
**Particle physics** is the study of the subatomic constituents of matter and the forces between them, seeking truly **[[FundamentalParticle|fundamental particles]]** that have no internal structure. It extends [[college-physics-2e-ch30|atomic]] and [[college-physics-2e-ch31|nuclear]] physics to the deepest known level of matter.

## Key Points
- Matter is organized in a nested hierarchy: solids → molecules → atoms → nuclei → [[Nucleon|nucleons]] → [[Quark|quarks]]. The open question is whether quarks and [[Lepton|leptons]] are the ultimate constituents.
- Like the periodic table organized chemistry, particles show systematic patterns (quantum numbers, decay modes) that enable classification and prediction — the route that led to quarks.
- The two classes of fundamental fermions are [[Lepton|leptons]] (no strong force) and [[Quark|quarks]] (build [[Hadron|hadrons]]); forces act through [[CarrierParticle|carrier particles]]. All of this is codified in the [[StandardModel]].
- Progress is driven by [[ParticleAccelerator|particle accelerators and colliders]] (e.g. the LHC at CERN), which create new matter from energy via [[MassEnergyEquivalence|m = ΔE/c²]] and reveal substructure through high-energy scattering.
- Allowed reactions are governed by [[ConservationLawsParticlePhysics|conservation laws]] (charge, baryon number, lepton family number, strangeness).

## Equations
- m = ΔE/c² (matter created from collision energy)
- ΔE·Δt ≥ h/(4π) (uncertainty principle behind [[VirtualParticle|virtual]] carrier particles)
