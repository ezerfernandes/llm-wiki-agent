# Coordination Compound

A **coordination compound** (the molecular extension of a [[ComplexIon|complex ion]]) consists of a **central metal ion or atom** — usually a [[TransitionMetals|transition metal]] — bonded to a set of **[[Ligand|ligands]]** through [[CoordinateCovalentBond|coordinate covalent bonds]]. This is a [[LewisAcidsAndBases|Lewis acid–base]] interaction: the metal is the [[LewisAcid|Lewis acid]] (electron-pair acceptor) and each ligand is a [[LewisBase|Lewis base]] (electron-pair donor). (This is the *chemistry* sense of "coordination" — unrelated to multi-agent or system "coordination.")

Metals with **partially filled d subshells** typically form **colored** complexes; **d⁰ or d¹⁰** metals usually give colorless ones (see [[ComplexColorAndMagnetism]]).

## Core structural terms

- **Coordination sphere** — the central metal plus its attached ligands. In a formula, **brackets enclose the coordination sphere**; species outside the brackets are **counter ions**, not part of the sphere. Example: in [Co(NH₃)₅Cl]Cl₂, the sphere is [Co(NH₃)₅Cl]²⁺ and the two outer Cl⁻ are counter ions.
- **Coordination number** — the number of **donor atoms** bonded to the metal. Examples: [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺ = 2; [CuCl₄]²⁻ = 4; [Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺ = 6. (Note: coordination number counts donor *atoms*, not ligands — a bidentate ligand contributes 2.)

## Geometries by coordination number

| CN | Geometry | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | Linear | [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺ |
| 3 | Trigonal planar | [Cu(CN)₃]²⁻ |
| 4 | Tetrahedral (often d⁰/d¹⁰) | [Ni(CO)₄], [Zn(CN)₄]²⁻ |
| 4 | Square planar (often d⁸) | [Ni(CN)₄]²⁻, [Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂] |
| 5 | Trigonal bipyramidal / square pyramidal | [CoCl₅]²⁻ / [VO(CN)₄]²⁻ |
| 6 | Octahedral | [CoCl₆]³⁻, [Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺ |
| 7 | Pentagonal bipyramid | [ZrF₇]³⁻ |
| 8 | Square antiprism / dodecahedron | [ReF₈]²⁻ / [Mo(CN)₈]⁴⁻ |

In a **square planar** complex each ligand has two others at **90° (cis)** and one at **180° (trans)** — the basis of [[CoordinationIsomerism|geometric isomerism]].
