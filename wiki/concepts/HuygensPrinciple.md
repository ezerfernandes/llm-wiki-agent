## Definition
Huygens's principle states that every point on a [[Wave]]front acts as a source of secondary spherical wavelets spreading forward at the wave speed; the new wavefront is the surface tangent to all those wavelets.

## Key Points
- A wavefront is the leading edge of a wave (a crest or trough); rays are lines perpendicular to it.
- Reconstructing wavefronts from wavelets reproduces the law of reflection (angle in = angle out).
- In refraction, wavelets travel shorter distances in the slower (higher-n) medium, bending the wavefront toward the normal — the geometric basis of Snell's law.
- Predicts [[Diffraction]]: bending around edges/through openings is significant when the aperture is comparable to the wavelength (why sound spreads through doorways but light casts sharp shadows).
- Attributed to Christiaan Huygens ([[ChristiaanHuygens]]).

## Equations
- `s = v t` — distance a wavelet travels in time t at speed v
