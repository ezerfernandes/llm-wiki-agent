## Definition
The Lorentz force is the magnetic force on a single electrically charged particle moving through a [[MagneticField]]. Its magnitude is F = qvB sin θ, where θ is the angle between the velocity and the field. It is always perpendicular to both **v** and **B**.

## Key Points
- A stationary charge feels no magnetic force; the force exists only for moving charge.
- Because the force is perpendicular to velocity, it does no work and cannot change the particle's speed — only its direction.
- A charge moving perpendicular to a uniform field undergoes uniform circular motion; the magnetic force supplies the centripetal force, giving radius r = mv/(qB).
- If velocity has a component along **B**, the path is a helix that spirals along field lines (field-line trapping, as in the Van Allen belts).
- Direction is found with the right-hand rule RHR-1 (reverse for negative charges).

## Equations
- F = qvB sin θ
- Circular motion: qvB = mv²/r → r = mv / (qB)
