# Electromagnetic Radiation

**Electromagnetic radiation** is energy that travels as a wave of oscillating electric and magnetic fields, perpendicular to each other and to the direction of travel. In a vacuum all such radiation moves at the speed of light, **c = 2.998 × 10⁸ m/s**. Physically this radiation is an [[ElectromagneticWave]] predicted by [[MaxwellsEquations]]; the speed follows from `c = 1/sqrt(μ₀ε₀)` (see [[SpeedOfLightPhysics]]).

## Wave Properties
- **Wavelength (λ)** — distance between consecutive peaks (or troughs), in meters.
- **Frequency (ν)** — cycles per second, in hertz (Hz); 1 MHz = 10⁶ Hz, 1 GHz = 10⁹ Hz.
- **Amplitude** — magnitude of displacement; relates to intensity (brightness/loudness).

The fundamental relationship for radiation in vacuum:

> **c = λν**

So wavelength and frequency are **inversely proportional**: longer wavelength ⇒ lower frequency. Each photon's energy follows directly (see [[PhotonQuantum]]): E = hν = hc/λ.

## The Electromagnetic Spectrum
From shortest to longest wavelength: [[GammaRay|gamma rays]] (~10⁻¹² m) → [[XRay|X-rays]] → [[Ultraviolet|ultraviolet]] → **[[VisibleLight|visible light]] (~400–700 nm)** → [[InfraredRadiation|infrared]] → [[Microwaves|microwave]] → [[RadioWaves|radio]] (~10³ m). Visible light is a tiny slice of the whole [[ElectromagneticSpectrum|spectrum]]. The radio region (3 kHz–300 GHz) carries AM/FM broadcast, cellular, GPS, and Wi-Fi signals.

## Wave Behaviors
- **Interference** — overlapping waves add (constructive, bright fringes) or cancel (destructive, dark fringes); the double-slit pattern is wavelike behavior that classical particles cannot produce.
- **Standing waves** — confined waves (e.g., a string fixed at both ends) admit only an integer number of half-wavelengths, an early example of [[EnergyQuantization|quantization]] with **nodes** (points of no motion).

## Significance
Because each element emits/absorbs characteristic wavelengths (see [[AtomicSpectra]]), electromagnetic radiation is the primary tool for identifying the composition of distant objects and the energy levels of atoms.
