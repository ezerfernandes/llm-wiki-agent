## Definition
A sonic boom is the shock wave produced when an object travels faster than the [[SpeedOfSound]] in a medium. Sound waves emitted by the supersonic source pile up and interfere constructively along a cone-shaped surface (a **bow wake** in two dimensions), producing a sharp pressure pulse heard as a boom.

## Key Points
- As source speed approaches sound speed, the [[DopplerEffect]] formula's denominator approaches zero and observed frequency diverges — compressions superimpose at one instant.
- Beyond sound speed, no sound reaches an observer until the source has passed; the aircraft is often already gone when the boom arrives.
- A bow wake is the analogous V-shaped pattern when a source outruns the wave speed (e.g., a boat on water).
- Aircraft typically generate **two booms** — one from the nose and one from the tail — separated by the transit time past the observer.
- The optical analogue is Cerenkov radiation: a glow from charged particles exceeding the speed of light in a medium (e.g., ~0.75c in water).

## Equations
- Limiting behavior of `f_obs = f_s · v_w / (v_w ± v_s)` as `v_s → v_w` (see [[DopplerEffect]])
