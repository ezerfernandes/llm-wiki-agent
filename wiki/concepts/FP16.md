# FP16 — Half-Precision Float

The IEEE 754 binary16 format: **half-precision floating point**, 16 bits total. Per [[ai-engineering-ch07-finetuning|*AI Engineering* Ch 7]], one of the most common AI numerical formats alongside [[FP32]] and [[BF16]].

## Bit allocation

- **Sign**: 1 bit.
- **Range** (exponent): 5 bits.
- **Precision** (mantissa): 10 bits.

Total: 16 bits.

## Range and precision

- **Range**: ~6.1 × 10⁻⁵ to ~65,504 — *much narrower than [[BF16]] / [[FP32]]*.
- **Precision**: ~3.5 decimal digits — *more precise than [[BF16]]* per representable value.

## The Ch 7 comparison with [[BF16]]

| Property | FP16 | [[BF16]] |
|---|---|---|
| Bits | 16 | 16 |
| Range | ±65,504 | ±3.4 × 10³⁸ |
| Out-of-range values | → INF (catastrophic) | → still representable |
| Precision | More | Less |
| `1234.56789` becomes | 1235.0 | 1232.0 |

The decisive trade: **FP16 has less range, more precision; BF16 has more range, less precision**. They have the same bit count but are **not interchangeable**.

## Where FP16 is the right choice

- **Inference** where activation magnitudes are bounded.
- **Models originally trained in FP16** (older lineage; some pre-2022 models).
- **Older NVIDIA hardware** (V100 lineage) where FP16 has wider support than BF16.

## Where FP16 is risky

- **Training foundation-scale models** — gradients and activations can exceed FP16's narrow range, blowing up to INF. The historical mitigation: **loss scaling** (scale loss up by a constant, then unscale gradients before applying) — built into [[AutomaticMixedPrecision|AMP]].
- **Loading a BF16-trained model** — see the [[BF16]] page for the Llama 2/3.1 cautionary tale.
