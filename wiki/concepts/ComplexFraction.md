# Complex Fraction

A **complex fraction** is a [[Fraction|fraction]] in which the **numerator or the denominator (or both) itself contains a fraction** — for example a stacked `(3/4)/(5/8)` or `(x/2)/6`. OpenStax [[Prealgebra]] 2e (Chapter 4, [[prealgebra-2e-ch04-fractions]], section 4.3) presents it as just another way of writing a division: the main fraction bar means "the numerator divided by the denominator."

**To simplify a complex fraction, rewrite it as a division and apply the [[FractionArithmetic|fraction division]] rule** — multiply the top fraction by the [[Reciprocal|reciprocal]] of the bottom fraction — then simplify. For instance `(3/4)/(5/8) = (3/4) ÷ (5/8) = (3/4)·(8/5) = 24/20 = 6/5`. The same approach handles complex fractions containing variables or [[MixedNumber|mixed numbers]] (convert mixed numbers to improper fractions first).

The complex fraction also illustrates the **fraction bar as a grouping symbol**. When the numerator or denominator is an *expression* rather than a single fraction, treat the bar like parentheses: simplify the numerator fully and the denominator fully — respecting the [[OrderOfOperations|order of operations]] and any [[Exponent|exponents]] — and only then divide the simplified numerator by the simplified denominator. This makes complex fractions a natural place where order-of-operations and fraction arithmetic meet.
