# Reciprocal

The **reciprocal** of the [[Fraction|fraction]] `a/b` is `b/a`, where `a ≠ 0` and `b ≠ 0` — you simply **invert** the fraction, swapping numerator and denominator, and keep the same sign. The defining property is that **a number and its reciprocal multiply to 1**: `(a/b)·(b/a) = 1`. OpenStax [[Prealgebra]] 2e (Chapter 4, [[prealgebra-2e-ch04-fractions]], section 4.2) introduces the reciprocal as the key to dividing fractions, and it returns in section 4.7 as the key to solving equations with a fractional coefficient.

To take the reciprocal of any number, first write it as a fraction: an integer `n` is `n/1`, so its reciprocal is `1/n`; a [[MixedNumber|mixed number]] is converted to an improper fraction first. The sign is preserved, so the reciprocal of `−3/4` is `−4/3`. **Zero has no reciprocal** — there is no number you can multiply by 0 to get 1 (equivalently, `1/0` is undefined because the denominator may not be 0).

The reciprocal does two jobs in this chapter. In **division**, the [[FractionArithmetic|Fraction Division]] rule says `(a/b) ÷ (c/d) = (a/b)·(d/c)` — "multiply the first fraction by the reciprocal of the second" — which turns every division into a multiplication. In **solving equations** ([[Equation]]), multiplying both sides of `(a/b)x = k` by the reciprocal `b/a` produces a coefficient of 1 on `x` and isolates the variable; this is the Multiplication Property of Equality in action (e.g. `(3/4)x = 24` ⇒ multiply by `4/3` ⇒ `x = 32`). In both roles the reciprocal works precisely because its product with the original is 1, the multiplicative identity.
