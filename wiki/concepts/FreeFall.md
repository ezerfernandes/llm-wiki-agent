## Definition
Free fall is motion under the influence of gravity alone, with air resistance and friction negligible. In a given location, all objects in free fall accelerate toward Earth's center at the same constant rate, independent of their mass.

## Key Points
- The acceleration due to gravity g averages about 9.80 m/s² on Earth (varying 9.78–9.83 m/s² by location).
- Mass-independence was demonstrated by astronaut David Scott on the Moon (g ≈ 1.67 m/s²), where a hammer and feather fell together.
- Sign convention: with up positive, a = −g; with down positive, a = +g.
- An object thrown up and one thrown down from the same height reach the same speed at a given height — speed depends only on initial speed and vertical displacement.
- These are the [[KinematicEquations]] specialized to a = ±g.

## Equations
(taking up as positive, a = −g)
- v = v₀ − gt
- y = y₀ + v₀t − ½gt²
- v² = v₀² − 2g(y − y₀)
