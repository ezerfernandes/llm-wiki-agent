# Variable (Algebra)

A **variable** is a letter — most often `x`, `y`, `n`, or `a` — that stands for a number whose value **may change** or is not yet known. It is the single idea that turns arithmetic into algebra: instead of writing only specific numbers, you can write a statement that holds for *any* number a letter might represent. Its counterpart is a **constant**, a number (like `3`, `0`, or `42`) whose value always stays the same.

In OpenStax [[Prealgebra]] 2e (Chapter 2, [[prealgebra-2e-ch02-language-of-algebra]]), variables first appear inside [[AlgebraicExpression|expressions]] and [[Equation|equations]]. For instance, "5 more than a number" becomes the expression `n + 5`, where `n` is the unknown number; and "a number plus 5 equals 12" becomes the equation `n + 5 = 12`. Because the letter `x` and the multiplication cross `×` look alike, algebra avoids `×` and instead writes multiplication with a dot (`a · b`), with parentheses (`a(b)`), or simply by juxtaposition (`ab` means "a times b").

A variable carries a **coefficient** — the constant that multiplies it — and may be raised to an **exponent**. In the term `5x²`, the coefficient is 5 and the variable `x` is raised to the power 2 (see [[Exponent]] for exponential notation, though that page focuses on the distinct floating-point exponent field). A variable with no written coefficient has an understood coefficient of 1: `x` means `1x`. Two terms are **like terms** when they have the same variable(s) raised to the same exponents, which is exactly the condition under which they can be combined.

Variables are used in two complementary ways in this chapter. To **evaluate** an expression you *substitute* a specific number for the variable and simplify — so `2x + 7` with `x = 3` becomes `2(3) + 7 = 13`. To **solve** an [[Equation|equation]] you instead *find* the value of the variable that makes the statement true, isolating it with the Subtraction and Addition Properties of Equality. The same letter thus serves as a placeholder you fill in (evaluation) or an unknown you uncover (solving).

This pre-algebra notion of a variable as "a number that can change" is the everyday-math sense; in programming and other formal settings the word is overloaded with related but distinct meanings (see, e.g., [[SymbolicVariable]], [[LocalVariable]], [[RandomVariable]]).
