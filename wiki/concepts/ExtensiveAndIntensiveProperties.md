# Extensive and Intensive Properties

Properties of [[Matter]] are classified by whether they depend on the *amount* of matter present:

- **Extensive property** — depends on the amount of matter; its value is directly proportional to the quantity present. Examples: mass, volume, and heat content. A gallon of milk has more mass than a cup of milk.
- **Intensive property** — does *not* depend on the amount of matter present. Examples: temperature, [[Density|density]], and color. A gallon and a cup of milk at 20 °C are both at 20 °C when combined.

A single illustration ties them together: a drop and a pot of hot cooking oil are at the same **temperature** (intensive), but the pot contains more **heat** (extensive). This extensive/intensive distinction is orthogonal to the [[PhysicalProperty|physical]]-vs-[[ChemicalProperty|chemical]] property distinction. Density is a particularly useful intensive property for identifying substances.
