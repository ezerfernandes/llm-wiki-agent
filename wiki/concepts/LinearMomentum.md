# Linear Momentum

> Disambiguation: this is the physics quantity p = mv. For the gradient-descent optimization technique, see [[Momentum]].

## Definition
Linear momentum is the product of an object's mass and velocity. It is a vector pointing in the direction of motion, with SI unit kg·m/s. Momentum increases with both mass and speed, so a heavy slow body and a light fast body can carry comparable momentum.

## Key Points
- Momentum captures an object's "tendency to keep moving"; greater mass or speed means more momentum.
- [[NewtonsSecondLaw]] is most generally stated in terms of momentum: net external force equals the time rate of change of momentum. This is more general than F = ma and handles variable-mass systems like rockets.
- For constant mass, F_net = Δp/Δt reduces to F_net = ma.
- Momentum is the conserved quantity behind [[ConservationOfMomentum]] and the analysis of every [[Collision]].

## Equations
- p = mv
- F_net = Δp/Δt
- Δp = mΔv = m(v_f − v_i)
- F_net = m(Δv/Δt) = ma  (constant mass)
