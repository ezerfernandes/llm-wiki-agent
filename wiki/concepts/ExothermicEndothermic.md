# Exothermic and Endothermic Processes

A chemical or physical change is classified by the direction of [[HeatThermodynamics|heat]] flow between the [[ThermodynamicSystem|system]] and its surroundings:

- **Exothermic process** — releases heat (the system gives heat to the surroundings). The heat of reaction is negative: q < 0 and, at constant pressure, [[EnthalpyChemistry|ΔH]] < 0. The surroundings' temperature rises. Example: combustion.
- **Endothermic process** — absorbs heat (the system takes heat from the surroundings). q > 0 and ΔH > 0. The surroundings' temperature falls. Example: dissolving ammonium nitrate (NH₄NO₃) in water in an instant cold pack.

## Reversal Rule
Reversing the direction of a reaction reverses the arithmetic sign of its enthalpy change: a process that is endothermic in one direction is exothermic in the reverse direction, with equal magnitude of ΔH. This is one of the key facts used in [[HessLaw|Hess's law]] manipulations.
