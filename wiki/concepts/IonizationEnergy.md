# Ionization Energy

**Ionization energy (IE)** is the energy required to remove an electron from a gaseous atom or ion. All ionization processes are endothermic, so IE values are always positive.

- **First ionization energy (IE₁):** removes the most loosely bound electron — X(g) → X⁺(g) + e⁻.
- **Second (IE₂):** X⁺(g) → X²⁺(g) + e⁻, and so on.

## Periodic trends
- **Down a group:** IE₁ **decreases** — valence electrons are farther from the nucleus and easier to remove.
- **Across a period:** IE₁ **generally increases** — stronger [[PeriodicTrends|effective nuclear charge]] holds electrons more tightly.

## Exceptions
- **B < Be**: boron removes a 2p electron, beryllium a lower-energy 2s electron (harder to remove).
- **O < N**: removing an electron from oxygen leaves a stable half-filled 2p³; the same dip appears for S after P.

## Successive ionization energies
Each successive IE is larger than the last. A **large jump** appears once core electrons begin to be removed, marking the valence/core boundary (e.g., Sc and Ga, both with three valence electrons, jump sharply after the third ionization).

Example ordering: IE₁(In) < IE₁(Al) < IE₃(Al) < IE₂(Na) — the last removes a core electron, hence the highest.
