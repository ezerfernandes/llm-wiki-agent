# Dalton's Law of Partial Pressures

**Dalton's law of partial pressures** states that the total pressure of a mixture of ideal gases equals the sum of the **partial pressures** of the component gases:

$$P_{\text{Total}} = P_A + P_B + P_C + \ldots = \sum_i P_i$$

The **partial pressure** of a gas is the pressure it would exert if it alone occupied the container. Because the molecules of an ideal gas are far apart and exert no forces on each other (see [[KineticMolecularTheory]]), each gas in a non-reacting mixture behaves independently and contributes its own pressure. Formulated by [[JohnDalton]].

## Mole Fraction

The **mole fraction** of component A is its share of the total moles:

$$X_A = \frac{n_A}{n_{\text{Total}}}$$

and its partial pressure is that fraction of the total pressure:

$$P_A = X_A \times P_{\text{Total}}$$

Mole fractions are dimensionless and sum to 1.

## Collecting Gases over Water

When a gas is collected by displacing water, it becomes saturated with water vapor, so the measured total pressure includes water's vapor pressure. The "dry" gas pressure is:

$$P_{\text{gas}} = P_{\text{Total}} - P_{\text{H}_2\text{O}}$$

The **vapor pressure of water** depends only on temperature (read from a table) and equals the pressure of water vapor in equilibrium with liquid water in a closed container.
