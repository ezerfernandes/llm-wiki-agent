# Mixed Number

A **mixed number** consists of a whole number `a` together with a proper [[Fraction|fraction]] `b/c` (with `c ≠ 0`), written `a b/c` — for example `3 1/4` means `3 + 1/4`. It names the same value as an **improper fraction** (one whose numerator is at least its denominator), just in a form that makes the whole-number part visible. OpenStax [[Prealgebra]] 2e (Chapter 4, [[prealgebra-2e-ch04-fractions]], sections 4.1, 4.3, 4.6) introduces mixed numbers when visualizing fractions and then extends every fraction operation to them.

**Interconversion.** To convert an **improper fraction to a mixed number**, divide the denominator into the numerator; the quotient is the whole part, the remainder is the new numerator, and the divisor stays as the denominator: `11/6 = 1 5/6`. To convert a **mixed number to an improper fraction**, multiply the whole number by the denominator, add the numerator, and write the sum over the original denominator: `4 2/3 = (4·3 + 2)/3 = 14/3`.

**Multiplying and dividing mixed numbers** is done by first converting each mixed number to an improper fraction, applying the [[FractionArithmetic|fraction multiplication or division]] rule (division uses the [[Reciprocal|reciprocal]]), and simplifying — often converting back to a mixed number for the answer.

**Adding mixed numbers**: add the whole parts, add the fraction parts, and simplify; if the fraction parts sum to an improper fraction, convert it and **carry** the extra whole into the whole-number part (e.g. `2 1/2 + 5 2/3` over the LCD 6 gives `7 7/6 = 8 1/6`). **Subtracting mixed numbers** is usually done in vertical form: if the top fraction is smaller than the bottom one, **regroup (borrow)** one whole as a fraction over the common denominator before subtracting the fractions and then the whole numbers (e.g. `4 3/4 − 2 7/8` over LCD 8 borrows to `3 14/8 − 2 7/8 = 1 7/8`). When denominators differ, rewrite both fraction parts over the [[LeastCommonMultiple|LCD]] first; when the result would be negative, converting everything to improper fractions first is often cleaner.
