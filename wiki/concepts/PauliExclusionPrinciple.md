# Pauli Exclusion Principle

The **Pauli exclusion principle** (Wolfgang Pauli) states:

> "No two electrons in the same atom can have exactly the same set of all four quantum numbers."

Because two electrons can share the same orbital (same n, l, m_l) only if their spin quantum numbers m_s differ, and m_s has just two values (+½, −½), **any [[AtomicOrbital|atomic orbital]] holds at most two electrons, and those two must have opposite spins.**

This is why each box in an orbital diagram contains at most ↑↓, and it sets the capacity of every subshell (s = 2, p = 6, d = 10, f = 14) and shell (2n²).

## Physics Framing (College Physics 2e, Ch.30)
Pauli applies to electrons because they are identical particles with half-integer [[ElectronSpin|spin]] (s = 1/2). The chapter derives the capacities directly from the quantum numbers:
- Maximum electrons per subshell = **2(2l + 1)** → s:2, p:6, d:10
- Maximum electrons per shell = **2n²** → n=1:2, n=2:8, n=3:18

It also shows the principle, with orbital-penetration energetics (s-orbitals penetrate closest to the nucleus, so 4s fills before 3d — e.g. K = [Ar]4s¹), generates the structure of the [[PeriodicTable|periodic table]]: noble gases have filled outer subshells, halogens are p⁵, and alkali metals are s¹.
