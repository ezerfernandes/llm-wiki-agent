## Definition
The magnetic field **B** is a vector field that mediates magnetic forces, allowing magnets and moving charges to exert force across distance without contact. Its SI unit is the tesla (T); the smaller gauss (G) satisfies 1 G = 10⁻⁴ T (Earth's surface field ≈ 0.5 G; strong permanent magnets ≈ 2 T).

## Key Points
- Only moving [[ElectricCharge]] feels a magnetic force; stationary charges do not (contrast with [[ElectricField]]).
- Field strength can be defined operationally from the force on a moving charge: B = F/(qv sin θ).
- 1 T is the field exerting 1 N on a 1 C charge moving at 1 m/s perpendicular to the field: 1 T = 1 N/(A·m) = 1 N/(C·m/s).
- The field is visualized with [[MagneticFieldLines]] and produced by currents per [[AmperesLaw]].

## Equations
- B = F / (qv sin θ)
- 1 T = 1 N / (A·m) = 1 N / (C·m/s); 1 G = 10⁻⁴ T
