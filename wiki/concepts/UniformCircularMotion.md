## Definition
Uniform circular motion is movement along a circular path at constant speed. Although the speed (magnitude of velocity) is constant, the velocity direction changes continuously, so the motion is accelerated.

## Key Points
- By [[NewtonsFirstLaw]], a straight-line path is the default; sustaining a circular path requires a continuously applied net force toward the center.
- The acceleration is centripetal (center-directed): see [[CentripetalAcceleration]].
- The net inward force producing it is the centripetal force: see [[CentripetalForce]].
- Motion can be pure translation, pure rotation (all points trace arcs about one fixed point), or a mix (e.g., a spinning puck sliding across ice).
- Linear and angular descriptions connect via v = rω (see [[AngularVelocity]]).

## Equations
- v = r ω
- a_c = v² / r = r ω²
- F_c = m v² / r
