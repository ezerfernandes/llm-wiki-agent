## Definition
The electromagnetic spectrum is the full, continuous range of [[ElectromagneticWave|electromagnetic radiation]] ordered by frequency or wavelength — from radio waves (lowest frequency, longest wavelength) through microwaves, infrared, visible light, ultraviolet, and X-rays to gamma rays (highest frequency, shortest wavelength). All bands are the same physical phenomenon, differing only in frequency, related by `c = f λ`.

## Key Points
- **Inverse relation**: higher frequency ⇒ shorter wavelength.
- **Three rules of thumb**: (1) higher frequency carries more energy and penetrates matter better; (2) higher frequency can encode more information; (3) shorter wavelength resolves finer detail (resolution limit ≈ wavelength).
- **Boundary behavior**: radiation is transmitted, reflected, and/or absorbed at matter; transparency is frequency-dependent (glass passes visible light but blocks UV).
- Adjacent bands overlap; X-rays and gamma rays of the same frequency are identical, distinguished only by source.

## Bands (low → high frequency)
- [[RadioWaves]] — ~50 Hz to ~1000 MHz; comms, broadcasting, MRI.
- [[Microwaves]] — ~10⁹–10¹² Hz; radar, satellite comms, ovens.
- [[InfraredRadiation]] — below visible; thermal imaging, greenhouse effect.
- [[VisibleLight]] — 400–750 nm; vision, photosynthesis.
- [[Ultraviolet]] — ~400–10 nm; sterilization, UV-B skin damage, vitamin D.
- [[XRay]] — high-frequency, penetrating; imaging, diffraction.
- [[GammaRay]] — highest frequency; nuclear medicine, therapy.

## Equations
- `c = f λ` (c = 3.00×10⁸ m/s)
- Examples: AM at 1530 kHz → λ ≈ 196 m; cell phone at 1.90 GHz → λ ≈ 0.158 m.
