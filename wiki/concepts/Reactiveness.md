# Reactiveness

**Reactiveness** is the agent characteristic of **responding effectively to changes in its environment**. One of the defining features of an [[AgenticAI|agentic system]] in [[AgenticDesignPatterns|*Agentic Design Patterns*]] (Gulli). Whereas a classic rules-based automaton follows a fixed path and breaks when it hits an unexpected obstacle, a reactive agent — powered by a reasoning model — can "observe, adapt, and find another way," navigating the edge cases of reality with a form of "digital common sense."

## How it works
Reactiveness pairs with [[Proactiveness|proactiveness]]: the agent both initiates goal-directed action and continuously senses and adjusts to environmental change (new information, failures, shifting conditions). The book's GPS-vs-subway-map metaphor captures it — a static subway map is a fixed plan; a dynamic GPS reroutes in real time when conditions change.

## Why it matters
Reactiveness is what makes agents resilient in messy, non-deterministic environments. It underpins patterns like [[ExceptionHandlingAndRecovery|exception handling & recovery]] and [[Reflection|reflection]], where the agent observes outcomes and self-corrects. It is also the trait that lets agents handle ambiguous tasks that would break rigid scripts.
