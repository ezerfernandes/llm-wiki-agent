## Definition
Torque is the rotational analog of force — a measure of how effectively a force changes or accelerates rotation about a pivot. It depends not only on the force but on where and at what angle the force is applied relative to the pivot point.

## Key Points
- Torque depends on three things: the magnitude of the force, the distance from the pivot to the point of application, and the angle between the force and the lever.
- The **perpendicular lever arm** (r⊥) is the shortest distance from the pivot to the line of action of the force; using it simplifies torque to `τ = r⊥ F`.
- A force produces zero torque if it acts at the pivot or along a line passing through the pivot — this fact lets you eliminate unknown forces by choosing the pivot wisely.
- SI unit is the newton-meter (N·m).
- Net zero torque is the [[StaticEquilibrium]] second condition; it holds for any chosen pivot as long as it is applied consistently.

## Equations
- `τ = r F sin θ`
- `τ = r⊥ F`, where `r⊥ = r sin θ`
- Equilibrium: `net τ = 0`
