## Definition
A compound microscope is a multi-element optical instrument that magnifies small nearby objects using at least two lenses in sequence: an **objective** near the specimen and an **eyepiece (ocular)** near the eye.

## Key Points
- The objective (≈5×–100×) forms an enlarged, inverted, real intermediate image beyond its focal length; the eyepiece then magnifies that image as a magnifying glass, producing a larger virtual image.
- Total magnification is the **product** of the stages: m = m_o · m_e (not the sum).
- **Numerical aperture** NA = n sin α sets light gathering and resolution; immersion media (oil, glycerin, water) raise the medium index n and push NA above 1.0.
- **Working distance** (objective-to-specimen, mm) shrinks as NA and magnification rise; the **f-number** f/# ≈ 1/(2·NA) describes image-plane brightness.
- Optical microscopes reach ~1500× with resolution near 0.2 μm; electron microscopes reach ~50 million× and can locate single atoms (in vacuum).
- Disambiguation: this is the optical instrument, distinct from software/ML tools named "microscope".

## Equations
- m = m_o · m_e
- m_o = − d_i/d_o
- 1/d_i = 1/f_o − 1/d_o
- m_e = − d_i'/d_o'
- NA = n sin α
- f/# = f/D ≈ 1/(2·NA)
