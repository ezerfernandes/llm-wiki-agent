# Substitution Reaction (Organic)

A **substitution reaction** replaces **one or more hydrogen atoms** of an organic molecule with a **different atom or group**. Crucially, **no carbon–carbon bonds break** and the carbon's **hybridization is unchanged** ([[OrbitalHybridization]]). (Named *SubstitutionReaction* to keep the organic-chemistry sense explicit.)

It is characteristic of **saturated [[Alkane|alkanes]]** and of **[[AromaticHydrocarbon|aromatic]] rings**:

- Alkane + halogen (substitution, not addition):
  CH₃CH₃ + Cl₂ → CH₃CH₂Cl + HCl (chloroethane + hydrogen chloride).
- Aromatic compounds **more readily substitute than add**, because replacing a ring hydrogen **leaves the delocalized π double bonds intact**, preserving the stable aromatic structure.

This contrasts with [[Alkene|alkenes]]/[[Alkyne|alkynes]], whose weaker [[SigmaAndPiBonds|π bonds]] make them undergo **[[AdditionReaction|addition]]** instead.
