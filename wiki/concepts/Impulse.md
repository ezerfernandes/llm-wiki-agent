# Impulse

## Definition
Impulse is the product of the average net external force on an object and the time interval over which that force acts. By the impulse-momentum theorem, the impulse delivered to an object equals its change in [[LinearMomentum]].

## Key Points
- A given change in momentum can be produced by a large force acting for a short time or a small force acting for a long time.
- Extending the contact time lowers the peak force for the same momentum change — the principle behind airbags, padded dashboards, crumple zones, collapsible motorsport structures, and rolling when landing a jump.
- Direction matters: for a ball bouncing off a wall, the velocity component along the wall can be unchanged while the perpendicular component reverses, so impact angle changes the impulse magnitude.

## Equations
- Δp = F_net·Δt
- F_net = Δp/Δt
