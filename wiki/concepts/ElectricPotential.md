## Definition
**Electric potential** (V) is the [[ElectricPotentialEnergy|electric potential energy]] per unit [[ElectricCharge|charge]] at a point: V = PE/q. It is a **scalar** property of the source field alone, independent of the test charge used to probe it, and is measured in volts (1 V = 1 J/C). It is commonly called **[[Voltage]]** when referring to a [[PotentialDifference|difference]] between two points.

## Key Points
- Distinct from [[ElectricField|electric field]]: potential is a scalar (energy view); field is a vector (force view). The field points toward decreasing potential.
- Potential is the work per unit charge to bring a test charge from infinity (the usual zero reference) to the point.
- Potentials from multiple charges add **algebraically** (scalar sum), unlike fields, which add vectorially.
- For a point charge, V = kQ/r — falling off as 1/r, more slowly than the field's 1/r².

## Equations
- V = PE / q
- V = kQ / r  (point charge), k = 8.99 × 10⁹ N·m²/C²
- E = −ΔV / Δs  (field is the negative potential gradient)
- 1 V = 1 J/C
