# Molecular Orbital (MO) Diagram

A **molecular orbital diagram** is the energy-level diagram of [[MolecularOrbitalTheory|MO theory]]: it shows the [[AtomicOrbital|atomic orbitals]] of one atom on the left, those of the second atom on the right, and the [[BondingAndAntibondingOrbitals|molecular orbitals]] (formed by LCAO) in the center. Each horizontal line is one orbital holding at most two electrons. (Named *MODiagram* to keep the chemistry sense distinct.)

For each pair of combining atomic orbitals, one lower-energy **bonding** orbital and one higher-energy **antibonding** orbital appear.

## Filling Rules

- **Aufbau principle** — fill the lowest-energy orbitals first.
- **Hund's rule** — degenerate orbitals (e.g., the pair of π₂ₚ or π₂ₚ*) each receive one electron before any pairs up.
- **Pauli** — at most two electrons of opposite spin per orbital.

From the filled diagram one reads the electron configuration, the [[BondOrder|bond order]], and whether unpaired electrons (hence [[Paramagnetism|paramagnetism]]) exist.

## Two Orderings for Period-2 Diatomics

The vertical order of the 2*p*-derived orbitals depends on **s–p mixing**:

- **Li₂ → N₂** (≤3 p electrons): mixing raises σ₂ₚ **above** the degenerate π₂ₚ orbitals. Order: σ₂ₛ < σ₂ₛ* < (π₂ₚ_y, π₂ₚ_z) < σ₂ₚ < (π₂ₚ_y\*, π₂ₚ_z\*) < σ₂ₚ*.
- **O₂, F₂, Ne₂**: negligible mixing, so σ₂ₚ sits **below** the π₂ₚ orbitals (the "normal" order).

This switch is why N₂ (bond order 3) and O₂ (bond order 2, two unpaired electrons) read out differently from their diagrams.
