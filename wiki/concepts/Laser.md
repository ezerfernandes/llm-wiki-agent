# Laser

A **laser** (Light Amplification by Stimulated Emission of Radiation) produces an intense, coherent, monochromatic, directional beam of light by exploiting [[StimulatedEmission|stimulated emission]] in a medium held in population inversion.

## Key Points
- **Three-stage mechanism:**
  1. **Pumping** — external energy (electric discharge, flash lamp) excites many atoms to upper levels.
  2. **Population inversion** — atoms accumulate in a long-lived **metastable state** (lifetime ms–hours vs. the usual ~10⁻⁸ s), so more atoms sit in the upper state than the lower.
  3. **Stimulated-emission cascade** — one spontaneously emitted [[PhotonQuantum|photon]] triggers identical photons from other excited atoms; mirrors recirculate light through the medium, and a partially transmitting mirror lets the beam out.
- **Coherence** (photons in phase) and a single wavelength distinguish laser light from ordinary light.
- The He-Ne laser transfers ~1.96 eV between closely matched helium and neon metastable levels to emit 633 nm red light.

## Equations
- Output wavelength is set by the lasing transition: E_photon = hf = ΔE (lower-energy transitions → longer wavelengths).

## Applications
Laser surgery and retinal repair (wavelength-selective tissue absorption), CD/DVD reading, fiber-optic communications, inertial-confinement fusion, holography, and metrology. Powers range from ~10⁻³ W (pointers) to >10¹² W (fusion); pulses can be as short as ~10⁻¹⁴ s. Milestones: Maiman's first ruby laser (1960); Nobel Prizes to Townes/Basov/Prokhorov (1964) and Strickland/Mourou (2018, chirped-pulse amplification).
