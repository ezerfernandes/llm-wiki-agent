# Halogens Chemistry (Group 17)

The **halogens** — fluorine, chlorine, bromine, iodine, astatine — are the group 17 [[Nonmetals|nonmetals]]. Too reactive to occur free, their compounds are widespread (chlorides most abundant). Physical states grade with London dispersion forces: F₂ pale-yellow gas, Cl₂ greenish-yellow gas, Br₂ reddish-brown liquid, I₂ grayish-black solid that sublimes to violet vapor.

## Occurrence and Preparation
Halogens are prepared by **oxidizing halide ions** to the diatomic element; difficulty F > Cl > Br > I.
- **Fluorine:** electrolysis of molten KHF₂ in anhydrous HF.
- **Chlorine:** electrolysis of aqueous NaCl (chlor-alkali process); chemically MnO₂(s) + 2Cl⁻(aq) + 4H₃O⁺(aq) → Mn²⁺(aq) + Cl₂(g) + 6H₂O(l).
- **Bromine:** 2Br⁻(aq) + Cl₂(g) → Br₂(l) + 2Cl⁻(aq).
- **Iodine:** 2IO₃⁻(aq) + 5HSO₃⁻(aq) → 3HSO₄⁻(aq) + 2SO₄²⁻(aq) + H₂O(l) + I₂(s).

## Oxidizing Strength
The easier a halide is to oxidize, the weaker the halogen as an oxidizer. **Fluorine is the most powerful elemental oxidizing agent** — it oxidizes nearly all elements (water inflames), is handled in passivated Cu/Fe/Ni, and is the only element reacting directly with xenon. Chlorine is strong but less active (disproportionates in water: Cl₂ + 2H₂O → HOCl + H₃O⁺ + Cl⁻). Iodine is the weakest oxidizer; the starch–iodide test (deep blue) is a sensitive I₂ test. I₂ + I⁻ ⇌ I₃⁻ (triiodide) explains iodine's solubility in iodide solutions.

## Halides and Interhalogens
Most binary metal halides are ionic; Hg, group-13 (3+), Sn(IV), and Pb(IV) form covalent halides. Stoichiometry sets the state: Sn + Cl₂ → SnCl₂; Sn + 2Cl₂ → SnCl₄. **Interhalogens** join two halogens — a heavier halogen bonded to an odd number of lighter ones (IF₃ T-shaped, IF₅ square pyramidal, IF₇ pentagonal bipyramidal); F oxidizes I to the 7+ limit while Br/Cl reach only 5+. **Polyhalide anions** (KI₃, KICl₄, CsIBr₂) are related.

## Hydrogen Halides and Applications
Hydrogen halides (HF, HCl, HBr, HI) are soluble hydrohalic acids, all strong except HF; HF uniquely attacks glass (SiO₂ + 4HF → SiF₄ + 2H₂O) — detailed under [[HydrogenChemistry]]. Applications: fluorine (Teflon, fluoride in toothpaste/water converting tooth enamel to Ca₅(PO₄)₃F); chlorine (PVC, CCl₄/CHCl₃, water disinfection, bleaching via HOCl); bromine (AgBr photography, dyes); iodine (tincture antiseptic, iodized salt for the thyroid, AgI cloud seeding).
