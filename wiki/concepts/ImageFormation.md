## Definition
Image formation is the process by which lenses ([[OpticalLens]]) and mirrors ([[OpticalMirror]]) redirect light to create an image of an object. **Magnification** quantifies the image's size and orientation relative to the object.

## Key Points
- **Real image:** light rays physically converge at the image location; it can be projected on a screen or sensor.
- **Virtual image:** rays only appear to come from the image; it cannot be projected (e.g., behind a plane mirror or from a magnifying glass).
- **Ray tracing:** a few principal rays (parallel ray through F, ray through the center/vertex, ray through F emerging parallel) locate the image graphically.
- **Magnification** `m = h_i/h_o = −d_i/d_o`: sign gives orientation (negative = inverted), magnitude gives relative size.
- Light paths are reversible, so a point source at a focal point yields a parallel outgoing beam.
- Quantified by the shared [[ThinLensEquation]] form for both lenses and mirrors.

## Equations
- Lens / mirror equation: `1/d_o + 1/d_i = 1/f`
- Magnification: `m = h_i / h_o = − d_i / d_o`
