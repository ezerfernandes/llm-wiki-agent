## Definition
Thin film interference is the [[WaveInterference]] between light rays reflected from the top and bottom surfaces of a film whose thickness t is less than a few wavelengths. It produces the colors of soap bubbles and oil slicks, and Newton's rings.

## Key Points
- The ray reflecting off the film's bottom travels ~2t farther than the ray off the top; this path difference sets the interference condition.
- Phase change on reflection: reflecting off a higher-index medium flips the phase by 180° (λ/2 shift); off a lower-index medium there is no shift. The asymmetry between the two surfaces decides constructive vs. destructive interference.
- Because the relevant wavelength is the in-medium value `λₙ = λ/n`, film color depends on thickness, viewing angle, and refractive index.
- Newton's rings: concentric fringes where two lens surfaces touch, used to test optical surface precision (one ring per wavelength of gap).
- Anti-reflective (non-reflective) coatings are engineered for destructive interference of reflected light.

## Equations
- `λₙ = λ / n`
- Constructive: `2t = λₙ, 2λₙ, 3λₙ, …`
- Destructive: `2t = λₙ/2, 3λₙ/2, 5λₙ/2, …`
- (Which set is constructive vs. destructive depends on the phase-shift situation at the two surfaces; e.g., a quarter-wave MgF₂ coating, t = (λ/n)/4, minimizes reflection.)
