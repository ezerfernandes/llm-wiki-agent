## Definition
Nuclear fusion is a reaction in which two light nuclei combine into a heavier nucleus, releasing energy. Energy is released because the binding energy per nucleon (see [[NuclearBindingEnergy]]) rises toward iron-56 for light nuclei, so the mass deficit converts to kinetic energy via [[MassEnergyEquivalence]]. Harnessing it for power is called thermonuclear power.

## Key Points
- **Coulomb barrier:** positive nuclei repel, so fusion requires very high temperature (high kinetic energy) plus **quantum tunneling**; fusion rate rises steeply with temperature.
- **Stellar fusion:** the Sun runs the proton-proton cycle in its core; escaping neutrinos confirm it. Stars self-regulate by negative feedback (more fusion → hotter → expansion → cooling).
- **Break-even** (output = heating input) and **ignition** (self-sustaining) are key milestones.
- Practical fusion trades off temperature, density, and confinement time.
- Confinement approaches: **magnetic** (tokamak with toroidal fields, e.g. ITER) and **inertial** (laser-compressed D-T pellets, e.g. the National Ignition Facility's 192 UV beams).
- Fusion's energy per kilogram exceeds that of [[NuclearFission]].

## Equations
- Energy released: E = Δm·c²
- Proton-proton cycle (net): 2e⁻ + 4¹H → ⁴He + 2νₑ + 6γ (26.7 MeV)
- ¹H + ¹H → ²H + e⁺ + νₑ (0.42 MeV); ¹H + ²H → ³He + γ (5.49 MeV); ³He + ³He → ⁴He + 2¹H (12.86 MeV)
- D-T: ²H + ³H → ⁴He + n (17.59 MeV); ²H + ²H → ³He + n (3.27 MeV); ²H + ²H → ³H + ¹H (4.03 MeV)
