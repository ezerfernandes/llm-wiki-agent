# Accuracy and Precision

In measurement science, **accuracy** and **precision** are distinct ideas:

- **Accuracy** — how closely a measurement agrees with the true or accepted value.
- **Precision** — how closely repeated measurements agree with one another.

A measurement can be precise without being accurate, and vice versa. The classic archery analogy: arrows clustered on the bull's-eye are *accurate and precise*; arrows clustered tightly but off-center are *precise but not accurate*; arrows scattered everywhere are *neither*.

**Worked example (Ch 1):** three dispensers meant to deliver 296 mL of cough syrup — Dispenser #1 gives 283.3–284.1 mL (precise but inaccurate); #2 gives 293.9–298.3 mL (accurate but imprecise); #3 gives 295.9–296.1 mL (both accurate and precise).

Uncertainty in the result of a measurement is reported using [[SignificantFigures]].

## In Physics (OpenStax College Physics 2e, Ch. 1)
Physics stresses that the two properties are **independent**: a GPS reading can be accurate but imprecise (scattered near the true spot) or precise but inaccurate (tightly clustered but off-target). All measurements carry [[Uncertainty]] from equipment limits, user skill, object irregularities, and situational factors, expressed as A ± δA or as percent uncertainty, % unc = (δA / A) × 100%. An example: a medical thermometer with ±3.0°C uncertainty would be clinically useless because the error band swamps the meaningful range.

> Note: this is the metrology (chemistry/physics measurement) sense. The ML-classification metrics named "accuracy" and "precision" are separate concepts with their own pages ([[Accuracy]], [[Precision]]).
