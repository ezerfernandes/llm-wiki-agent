## Definition
Ferromagnetism is the strong magnetic response of certain materials — iron, cobalt, nickel, gadolinium, and rare-earth alloys (e.g., neodymium) — that arises from the alignment of magnetic domains and allows permanent magnetization.

## Key Points
- **Magnetic domains** are microscopic regions where atomic magnetic moments align uniformly, each acting like a tiny bar magnet.
- An external [[MagneticField]] grows and reorients favorably aligned domains, magnetizing the material; this is also why magnets attract unmagnetized iron.
- Heating or mechanical shock randomizes domains and demagnetizes a magnet. Above the **Curie temperature** a material cannot stay magnetized (iron: 1043 K / 770 °C).
- **Electromagnets** create fields from [[ElectricCurrent]] in a coil; a ferromagnetic core greatly amplifies the field.
- Like all magnetism, ferromagnetism ultimately originates in moving charge; it always produces dipole (north-south) fields, never monopoles.
