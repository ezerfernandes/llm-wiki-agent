# Electrolyte

An **electrolyte** is a substance that produces ions when dissolved in water, giving the solution the ability to conduct electricity. A **nonelectrolyte** (e.g., ethanol, sucrose) yields no ions and does not conduct. Conduction requires "freely mobile, charged species"; in solutions these are the dissolved ions, and conductivity rises with ion concentration.

## Strong vs. Weak
- **Strong electrolyte** — the ion-producing process is essentially 100% efficient (all dissolved compound yields ions).
- **Weak electrolyte** — only a relatively small fraction undergoes the ion-producing process.

Conductance (measured by current flow or qualitatively by a light-bulb's brightness) classifies a solute as strong, weak, or non-electrolyte.

## Two Mechanisms for Producing Ions

### Dissociation (ionic electrolytes)
[[IonicCompound|Ionic compounds]] already contain ions; dissolving is a **physical change** called **dissociation** in which [[Solvation|ion-dipole solvation]] by water separates and disperses the ions. Under most conditions ionic compounds dissociate nearly completely, so they are **strong electrolytes** — even sparingly soluble ionic compounds are strong electrolytes, because the small portion that dissolves dissociates completely. Example:

KCl(s) → K⁺(aq) + Cl⁻(aq)

### Ionization (covalent electrolytes)
Some covalent compounds produce ions by a **chemical reaction** with the solvent, called **ionization**. Pure HCl is a covalent gas with no ions, yet aqueous HCl conducts well because it reacts with water as an acid:

H₂O + HCl → H₃O⁺ + Cl⁻

This is ~100% complete, making HCl a strong acid and a strong electrolyte. Pure water is itself extremely weakly ionized (about 2 molecules per billion at 25 °C): H₂O + H₂O ⇌ H₃O⁺ + OH⁻. Weak acids and bases ionize only partially and are weak electrolytes.

## Role in Electrochemistry
The mobile ions of an electrolyte carry the **internal ionic current** of every [[ElectrochemicalCell|electrochemical cell]], completing the circuit while electrons travel the external wire. In a [[GalvanicCell|galvanic cell]] a **salt bridge** of inert electrolyte keeps each half-cell neutral; in [[ElectrolysisChemistry|electrolysis]] the electrolyte's ions (or water) are the species oxidized/reduced at the electrodes.
