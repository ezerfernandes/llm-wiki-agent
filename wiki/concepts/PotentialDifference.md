## Definition
**Potential difference** (ΔV), also called **[[Voltage]]**, is the difference in [[ElectricPotential|electric potential]] between two points — the change in [[ElectricPotentialEnergy|electric potential energy]] per unit [[ElectricCharge|charge]] moved between them: ΔV = V_B − V_A = ΔPE/q.

## Key Points
- It is a relation between **two points**; the energy delivered by moving charge q is ΔPE = qΔV.
- The work–potential relation is W = −qΔV.
- Relates the [[ElectricField]] to potential: in a uniform field V_AB = Ed; in general E = −ΔV/Δs (field points toward lower potential).
- No potential difference exists across a conductor in electrostatic equilibrium — it is an [[EquipotentialLines|equipotential]] surface.

## Equations
- ΔV = V_B − V_A = ΔPE / q
- ΔPE = q ΔV
- W = −q ΔV
- V_AB = E d ; E = −ΔV / Δs
