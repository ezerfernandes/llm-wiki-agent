# Viscosity (Liquids)

> Chemistry-domain page (OpenStax *Chemistry 2e* Ch 10). For the fluid-dynamics treatment with the shear definition F = ηvA/L, see the physics page [[Viscosity]].

"The viscosity of a liquid is a measure of its resistance to flow." It can be measured by how slowly a metal ball falls through the liquid, or how slowly the liquid flows through a narrow tube.

## Controlling Factors

Viscosity rises with:
- **[[IntermolecularForces|Intermolecular force]] strength** between molecules
- **Molecular size and shape** (complexity)

and **decreases with temperature**, because faster molecules' kinetic energy overcomes the IMFs.

| Substance | Formula | Viscosity (mPa·s) |
|-----------|---------|-------------------|
| Octane | C₈H₁₈ | 0.508 |
| Water | H₂O | 0.890 |
| Ethanol | C₂H₅OH | 1.074 |
| Mercury | Hg | 1.526 |
| Ethylene glycol | CH₂(OH)CH₂(OH) | 16.1 |

Ethylene glycol's two –OH groups give extensive [[HydrogenBonding|hydrogen bonding]] and a much higher viscosity. (The chapter's opening anecdote — chaulmoogra oil's painful thickness — is a viscosity story.)
