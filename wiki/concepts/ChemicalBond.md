# Chemical Bond

A **chemical bond** is the attractive interaction that holds atoms together in molecules, polyatomic ions, and ionic solids. Bonding theory explains how individual atoms connect to form more complex structures — for example, the C₆₀ buckminsterfullerene molecule. (Named *ChemicalBond* to avoid collision with unrelated `Bond` pages in the wiki.)

## The Two Main Bond Types

- **[[IonicBonding|Ionic bond]]** — electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions, formed by transfer of electrons from a metal to a nonmetal.
- **[[CovalentBonding|Covalent bond]]** — mutual attraction of two atoms for a shared pair of electrons, formed between atoms of similar electron-attracting tendency (typically nonmetal–nonmetal).

These are limiting cases of a continuum. The degree of electron sharing ranges from completely equal (pure covalent) to not at all (ionic), with **[[BondPolarity|polar covalent]]** bonds in between. The best first guide to a bond's character is the types of atoms and their positions in the [[PeriodicTable|periodic table]]: nonmetal–nonmetal bonds are generally covalent; metal–nonmetal bonds are often ionic.

## Energetics

Breaking a chemical bond always requires energy (endothermic); forming a bond always releases energy (exothermic). Bond strength is quantified by [[BondEnergy|bond energy]] (for covalent bonds) and [[LatticeEnergy|lattice energy]] (for ionic solids).
