# Fraction

A **fraction** is written `a/b`, where `a` and `b` are [[Integer|integers]] and the **denominator** `b ≠ 0`. The top number `a` is the **numerator** — how many equal parts are counted — and the bottom number `b` is the **denominator** — how many equal parts the whole has been divided into. So `3/4` names three of the four equal parts of one whole. A fraction is also just a recorded division (`a/b` means `a ÷ b`), which is the bridge from the [[WholeNumbers|whole numbers]] and [[Integer|integers]] to this new kind of number. OpenStax [[Prealgebra]] 2e (Chapter 4, [[prealgebra-2e-ch04-fractions]], section 4.1) introduces fractions visually with circle and tile models and by locating them on the [[NumberLine|number line]].

A fraction is **proper** when `a < b` (its value is less than 1, e.g. `2/5`) and **improper** when `a ≥ b` (its value is at least 1, e.g. `7/4` or `5/5 = 1`). An improper fraction can be rewritten as a [[MixedNumber|mixed number]] — a whole number plus a proper fraction — by dividing the denominator into the numerator and writing the result as `quotient remainder/divisor`; conversely a mixed number becomes an improper fraction by computing `(whole · denominator) + numerator` over the original denominator. The two forms name the same value.

A key foundational fact is the **Property of One**: any nonzero number divided by itself equals 1, `a/a = 1` for `a ≠ 0`. Together with the [[EquivalentFractions|Equivalent Fractions Property]] this is what lets a single value be written in many equivalent forms (`1/2 = 2/4 = 3/6`) and lets fractions be simplified or rewritten over a common denominator. Fractions can also be negative: by the sign-placement rule `−a/b = a/(−b) = −(a/b)`, so the rules of [[SignedNumberArithmetic|signed-number arithmetic]] from Chapter 3 carry over to signed fractions.

Everything else in the chapter is built on this definition: simplifying ([[EquivalentFractions]]), the four operations ([[FractionArithmetic]] via the [[Reciprocal|reciprocal]] and the [[LeastCommonMultiple|LCD]]), [[MixedNumber|mixed numbers]], [[ComplexFraction|complex fractions]], and solving [[Equation|equations]] that contain fractions.
