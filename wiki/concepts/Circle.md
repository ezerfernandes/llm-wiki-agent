# Circle (Circumference and Area)

A **circle** is the set of points a fixed distance from a center. OpenStax [[Prealgebra]] 2e (Chapter 9, [[prealgebra-2e-ch09-math-models-and-geometry]], §9.5) introduces its measures alongside irregular-figure decomposition.

## Vocabulary
- **Radius (r)** — distance from the center to the circle.
- **Diameter (d)** — distance across through the center; twice the radius: **d = 2r**.
- **Circumference** — the **perimeter** of the circle (a linear measure; see [[PerimeterAndArea]]).
- **π (pi)** — the constant ratio of circumference to diameter, **≈ 3.14** (use **22/7** when working with [[Fraction|fractions]]). π is irrational, so circle answers are rounded [[Decimal|decimal]] approximations reported with `≈`.

## Formulas
- **Circumference**: C = 2πr = πd
- **Area**: A = πr²  (a **square** measure)

If only the diameter is known, either use C = πd directly or first convert with r = d/2.

## Irregular figures
Circles and **semicircles** frequently appear inside [[PerimeterAndArea|irregular figures]] — e.g. a rectangle capped by a semicircle on each end. Decompose the figure into known shapes, compute each piece's area (a semicircle contributes ½·πr²), and add.

## Worked-example types
Circumference/area from a radius or diameter; finding the radius/diameter from a given circumference or area (a [[Formula|solve-for-a-variable]] step); real-world circles (wheels, pools, pizzas); composite shapes with semicircular ends.

## Connections

- [[VolumeAndSurfaceArea]] — the sphere, cylinder, and cone formulas all contain πr² / πr³ circle terms.
