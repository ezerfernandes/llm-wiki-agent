## Definition
**Resistivity** (ρ) is an intrinsic property of a material that quantifies how strongly it opposes [[ElectricCurrent|current]], independent of the object's size or shape. It connects to [[ElectricalResistance|resistance]] through geometry: R = ρL/A. Its SI unit is the ohm-meter (Ω·m).

## Key Points
- Materials classify by resistivity: **conductors** (very low ρ, abundant free carriers), **semiconductors** (intermediate ρ, carrier density set by doping), and **insulators** (very high ρ, bound charges) — see [[ConductorsAndInsulators]].
- Resistivity is temperature-dependent: ρ = ρ₀(1 + αΔT), with α the temperature coefficient of resistivity.
- In **conductors**, heating increases atomic vibration and carrier collisions, so ρ rises (positive α).
- In **semiconductors**, heating frees more carriers, so ρ falls (negative α) — the basis of thermistors.
- Some materials lose all resistivity (ρ = 0) below a critical temperature, becoming **superconductors**; alloys like manganin have α ≈ 0 for stable resistance standards.

## Equations
- R = ρ L / A
- ρ = ρ₀ (1 + α ΔT)
- R = R₀ (1 + α ΔT)
