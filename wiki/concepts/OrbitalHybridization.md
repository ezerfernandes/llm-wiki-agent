# Orbital Hybridization

**Orbital hybridization** is the combination of an atom's [[AtomicOrbital|atomic orbitals]] into a new set of equivalent **hybrid orbitals** when the atom is covalently bonded. It is the device [[ValenceBondTheory|valence bond theory]] uses to reconcile the shapes of *s*, *p*, and *d* orbitals with observed [[MolecularGeometry|molecular geometries]] — e.g., why H₂O's bond angle is 104.5° rather than the 90° between pure 2*p* orbitals. The combination is a **linear combination of atomic orbitals (LCAO)**. (Named *OrbitalHybridization* to avoid generic-name collisions.)

## Key Principles

1. Hybrid orbitals exist **only in bonded atoms**, not in isolated atoms.
2. Their shapes and orientations differ markedly from the parent atomic orbitals.
3. The **number of hybrids equals the number of atomic orbitals combined**.
4. All hybrids in a set are equivalent in shape and energy.
5. The hybridization type matches the [[VSEPRTheory|VSEPR]] electron-pair geometry — see [[HybridizationAndVSEPRGeometry]].
6. Hybrid orbitals form [[SigmaAndPiBonds|σ bonds]] and hold lone pairs; unhybridized orbitals form π bonds.

## The Five Hybridization Types

| Hybridization | Orbitals combined | e⁻-density regions | Geometry | Angle | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **sp** | s + 1 p | 2 | linear | 180° | BeCl₂, CO₂, HCCH |
| **sp²** | s + 2 p | 3 | trigonal planar | 120° | BH₃, CH₂O, ethene |
| **sp³** | s + 3 p | 4 | tetrahedral | 109.5° | CH₄, NH₃, H₂O |
| **sp³d** | s + 3 p + 1 d | 5 | trigonal bipyramidal | 90°/120° | PCl₅, SF₄, ClF₃ |
| **sp³d²** | s + 3 p + 2 d | 6 | octahedral | 90° | SF₆, XeF₄, IF₅ |

**sp³** hybrids also hold lone pairs: NH₃ (1 lone pair, 107.3°) and H₂O (2 lone pairs, 104.5°) are both sp³, with lone pairs occupying more space and compressing the angle below 109.5°. The **sp³d** and **sp³d²** sets are only possible for atoms with valence *d* orbitals — i.e., period 3 and beyond.

## Assigning Hybridization

Count **regions of electron density** on the central atom (each single bond, [[MultipleBonds|multiple bond]], radical, and lone pair counts as one — π bonds and resonance do **not** affect it): 2→sp, 3→sp², 4→sp³, 5→sp³d, 6→sp³d². Worked cases: SO₄²⁻ → sp³; SeF₄ → sp³d; urea's carbonyl C → sp²; acetic acid's methyl C → sp³, carboxyl C → sp².

## Limitations

Hybridization is invoked only where needed. Large central atoms farther from the nucleus often bond with near-pure *p* orbitals: H₂S (92.1°) and H₂Te (~90°) show little or no hybridization.

## In organic chemistry (Ch 20)

Carbon hybridization is the backbone of [[OrganicChemistry|organic chemistry]] and sets each [[Hydrocarbon|hydrocarbon]] family's geometry: **sp³** in [[Alkane|alkanes]] (tetrahedral, 109.5°), **sp²** in [[Alkene|alkenes]] and the [[AromaticHydrocarbon|aromatic]] benzene ring and the [[AldehydesAndKetones|carbonyl]] carbon (trigonal planar, 120°), and **sp** in [[Alkyne|alkynes]] (linear, 180°). [[AdditionReaction|Addition reactions]] convert sp²/sp carbons toward sp³ as π bonds are consumed.
