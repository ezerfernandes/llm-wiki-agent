# Enthalpy of Solution

The **enthalpy (heat) of solution**, ΔH_soln, is the net energy absorbed or released when a solute dissolves in a solvent. It is the sum of three hypothetical steps in the [[DissolutionProcess|dissolution process]], two endothermic and one exothermic.

## The Three-Step Model
1. **Separate the solute** (endothermic) — energy is consumed to overcome solute-solute attractions, pulling solute particles apart.
2. **Expand the solvent** (endothermic) — energy is consumed to overcome solvent-solvent attractions, opening space for the solute.
3. **[[Solvation|Solvate]]** (exothermic) — solute-solvent attractions form, releasing energy.

"The relative magnitudes of the energy changes associated with these stepwise processes determine whether the dissolution process overall will release or absorb energy."

## Exothermic vs. Endothermic
- **Exothermic dissolution** (steps 1+2 < step 3): heat released; e.g., NaOH in water.
- **Endothermic dissolution** (steps 1+2 > step 3): heat absorbed; spontaneous nonetheless because the entropy increase from matter dispersal drives it. Example: ammonium nitrate (NH₄NO₃) in instant cold packs absorbs heat from the surroundings.

When the net would be far too endothermic — the energy to separate solute and solvent vastly exceeding solvation energy — no appreciable solution forms.

## Ideal Limit
When solute-solvent forces equal those in the separated components, all three steps cancel and ΔH_soln ≈ 0: an [[IdealSolution|ideal solution]].
