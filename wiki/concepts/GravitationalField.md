## Definition
The gravitational field describes the gravitational acceleration g that a mass M produces at a distance r: g = GM/r². Because the field's effect on a test object is independent of that object's mass, all objects accelerate equally under gravity at a given location.

## Key Points
- Derived from [[NewtonsLawOfUniversalGravitation]] by dividing the force F = GmM/r² by the object's mass m, which cancels.
- At Earth's surface, g ≈ 9.80 m/s²; it decreases with the square of distance from Earth's center.
- The field concept underlies weight: w = mg (see [[MassVersusWeight]]).
- For gravity acting on extended bodies, mass is treated as concentrated at the center of mass (CM).
- Rearranging gives a way to weigh a planet: M = gr²/G.

## Equations
- g = G M / r²
- w = m g
- M = g r² / G
