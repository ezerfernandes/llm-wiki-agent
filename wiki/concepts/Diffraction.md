## Definition
Diffraction is the bending and spreading of [[Wave]]s as they pass through openings or around edges/obstacles. It is a direct consequence of [[HuygensPrinciple]] and is a defining signature of wave behavior, most pronounced when the aperture or obstacle is comparable in size to the wavelength.

## Key Points
- Single-slit diffraction: each point across the slit emits wavelets that interfere; the result is a bright, wide central maximum flanked by dimmer, narrower secondary maxima.
- The central maximum is roughly twice as wide as the secondary maxima; narrower slits (near a wavelength) diffract more strongly.
- Diffraction limits the resolution of all optical instruments — see [[RayleighCriterion]].
- Used to infer slit/aperture dimensions from an observed pattern, and underlies [[DiffractionGrating]] spectroscopy and X-ray crystallography.

## Equations
- Single-slit minima (dark fringes): `D sin θ = m λ`, m = ±1, ±2, ±3, … (D = slit width)
- The central maximum lies between the m = ±1 minima.
