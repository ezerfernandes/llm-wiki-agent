## Definition
Food irradiation exposes food to [[IonizingRadiation]] to kill pests and microbes and slow spoilage. The radiation ionizes molecules, creating **free radicals** and a cascade of **radiolytic products** that damage microbial cell membranes and DNA — the same mechanism that makes radiation lethal to cells.

## Key Points
- **Low dose** (up to ~1000 Gy): reduces microbial load on fresh produce with minimal chemical change.
- **High dose** (above ~10,000 Gy): significant nutrient loss (~15%) and vitamin degradation (~25%).
- **No residual radioactivity** — the photon/electron energies used induce no nuclear reactions, so the food is not made radioactive (key safety principle).
- Sources: ⁶⁰Co gamma (~1.25 MeV), ¹³⁷Cs gamma (~0.67 MeV), X-rays up to 5 MeV, electron beams up to 10 MeV.
- Approved in 40+ countries for spices, produce, red meat, poultry; also sterilizes medical supplies. High-water foods (lettuce, peaches) may spoil faster afterward; milk can develop off-flavors.

- Dose delivered uses [[RadiationDose]]: Dose (Gy) = energy absorbed / mass.
