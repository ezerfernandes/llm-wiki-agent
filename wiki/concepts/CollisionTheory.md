# Collision Theory

**Collision theory** explains chemical [[ReactionRate|reaction rates]] and the [[FactorsAffectingReactionRates|factors that affect them]] at the molecular level: atoms, molecules, or ions must collide before they can react. It rests on three postulates:

1. **Frequency** — the reaction rate is proportional to the rate of reactant collisions (more collisions per unit time → faster reaction). This is why higher [[Molarity|concentration]] and (for gases/solids) greater contact area speed reactions.
2. **Orientation** — the colliding species must be oriented so that the atoms destined to bond are brought into contact; improperly oriented collisions do not react.
3. **Energy** — the collision must carry enough energy to penetrate the reactants' valence shells and let electrons rearrange into new bonds. The minimum required is the [[ActivationEnergy|activation energy]] Eₐ.

A collision that meets all three conditions passes through a high-energy [[TransitionState|activated complex]] on the way to products. Because raising [[Temperature|temperature]] increases both collision frequency and the fraction of molecules exceeding Eₐ (see [[KineticMolecularTheory]]), it strongly accelerates reactions.

The theory is captured quantitatively by the [[ArrheniusEquation|Arrhenius equation]], whose frequency factor A encodes postulates 1 and 2 and whose exponential term encodes postulate 3.
