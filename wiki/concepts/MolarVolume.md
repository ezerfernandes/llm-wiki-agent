# Molar Volume (of a Gas)

The **molar volume** of a gas is the volume occupied by one [[TheMoleConcept|mole]] of it. A direct consequence of [[AvogadrosLaw|Avogadro's law]] is that, under the same temperature and pressure, **one mole of any ideal gas occupies the same volume regardless of chemical identity** (because equal volumes contain equal numbers of molecules).

## Standard Molar Volume

At **STP** (273.15 K, 0 °C) the standard molar volume is:

- **≈ 22.4 L** at 1 atm (the long-standing STP convention)
- **≈ 22.71 L** at 1 bar (the IUPAC standard adopted in 1982)

This is illustrated by samples of He, NH₃, and O₂: although their masses differ, one mole of each occupies ~22.4 L at STP and contains [[AvogadrosNumber|Avogadro's number]] of molecules.

The value follows directly from the [[IdealGasLawChemistry|ideal gas law]]: V = nRT/P = (1 mol)(0.08206 L·atm/mol·K)(273.15 K)/(1 atm) = 22.4 L.
