## Definition
The four fundamental (basic) forces are the elementary interactions from which all observed forces in nature derive: gravitational, electromagnetic, weak nuclear, and strong nuclear. Each is transmitted through a **force field** and mediated by **[[CarrierParticle|carrier particles]]** (gauge bosons exchanged as [[VirtualParticle|virtual particles]]) rather than by direct contact ("action at a distance"). See [[ParticlePhysics]] for the particle-physics treatment.

## Key Points

| Force | Relative strength | Range | Nature | Carrier particle |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gravitational | ~10⁻³⁸ | Infinite | Attractive only | Graviton (proposed) |
| Electromagnetic | ~10⁻² | Infinite | Attractive & repulsive | Photon |
| Weak nuclear | ~10⁻¹³ | < 10⁻¹⁸ m | Attractive & repulsive | W⁺, W⁻, Z⁰ (observed) |
| Strong nuclear | 1 | ~10⁻¹⁵ m | Attractive & repulsive | Gluons |

- Gravity is by far the weakest but always attractive and dominates at astronomical scales; it curves spacetime near massive bodies.
- Nearly all everyday forces ([[Force]], [[NormalForce]], [[Tension]], [[Friction]], [[DragForce]]) are electromagnetic in origin at the atomic/molecular level; gravity is the everyday exception.
- **Carrier mass sets the range**: massless carriers ([[PhotonQuantum|photon]], graviton, gluon) give infinite/long range; the massive W and Z bosons (~1000× the pion mass) give the weak force its sub-10⁻¹⁸ m range. All carriers have integer spin, so they are bosons.
- The strong force at nuclear scale (the [[StrongNuclearForce]] binding nucleons via pion exchange) is the residual effect of gluon exchange between [[Quark|quarks]]; the more fundamental carriers are gluons.
- The electromagnetic and weak forces unify as the **[[ElectroweakForce|electroweak]]** force above ~100 GeV; **Grand Unified Theories** ([[GrandUnifiedTheory|GUTs]]) aim to connect all four, with gravity hardest to include. See the [[StandardModel]].
- Detection milestones: gravitational waves predicted by general relativity and first detected by LIGO (2015) from merging black holes; future LISA space detector; the [[ParticleAccelerator|Large Hadron Collider]] probes carrier particles and early-universe conditions.
