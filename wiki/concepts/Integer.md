# Integer

An **integer** is one of the **counting numbers, their opposites, and zero**: `… −3, −2, −1, 0, 1, 2, 3 …`. The integers extend the [[WholeNumbers|whole numbers]] (`0, 1, 2, 3 …`) by adding the **negative numbers** — values less than zero, written with a leading minus sign. OpenStax [[Prealgebra]] 2e introduces them in Chapter 3 ([[prealgebra-2e-ch03-integers]]).

A **negative number** indicates a quantity below a reference point: `−1°F` is one degree below zero, an elevation of `−282` ft is below sea level, a balance of `−45` dollars is an overdraft. **Zero is neither positive nor negative** — it is the boundary between the two.

## Opposites and the "−" sign
Every integer has an **opposite**: the number the same distance from 0 on the [[NumberLine|number line]] but on the other side. The opposite of `a` is written `−a` and read "the opposite of `a`." Taking the opposite twice returns the original number: `−(−n) = n`.

The "−" symbol carries **three distinct meanings**, distinguished by position:
1. **Subtraction** — between two numbers, as in `10 − 4` ("10 minus 4").
2. **Negative sign** — before a number, as in `−8` ("negative eight").
3. **Opposite of** — before a number or [[Variable|variable]], as in `−x` ("the opposite of `x`"). Note `−x` is not necessarily negative: if `x = −3`, then `−x = 3`.

## Ordering
Integers are ordered by position on the number line: values **increase left to right**. For two integers, `a < b` when `a` lies to the left of `b`, and `a > b` when `a` lies to the right. So `−5 < −1` (a smaller-looking magnitude can be the larger number), `−3 < 0 < 2`.

## Relation to other ideas
- **[[AbsoluteValue|Absolute value]]** measures an integer's distance from 0, discarding its sign.
- **[[SignedNumberArithmetic]]** gives the rules for adding, subtracting, multiplying, and dividing integers.
- In computing, the [[SignedInteger|signed integer]] is the fixed-width binary encoding of this same idea; the prealgebra integer is the unbounded mathematical object.
