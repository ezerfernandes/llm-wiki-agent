## Definition
**Eddy currents** are circulating loops of current induced in a bulk conductor when it moves relative to a [[MagneticField|magnetic field]] (or the field changes near it). By [[LenzsLaw|Lenz's law]] they oppose the flux change that creates them, producing **magnetic damping** — a drag force that resists the conductor's motion and is proportional to its speed.

## Key Points
- A solid plate entering a field develops one sense of eddy loop and exiting develops the opposite, but in both cases the resulting force opposes the motion.
- The damping force is proportional to velocity and vanishes when the conductor stops — useful for smoothly settling oscillations.
- **Slotting** the conductor breaks the large loops into small adjacent ones whose forces cancel, drastically reducing damping (the basis of laminated transformer cores too).
- Insulators develop negligible eddy currents and so feel essentially no damping.

## Equations
- No closed-form equation in this section; governed qualitatively by [[FaradaysLaw]] and [[LenzsLaw]].

## Related
- [[FaradaysLaw]] — changing flux induces the eddy currents
- [[LenzsLaw]] — explains why the drag opposes motion

## Applications
- Magnetically damped laboratory balances; eddy-current braking on trains and roller coasters (needs a friction brake to fully stop); metal detectors; metal separation in recycling; induction cooktops heating ferromagnetic cookware.
