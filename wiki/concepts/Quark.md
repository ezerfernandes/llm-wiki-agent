## Definition
**Quarks** are [[FundamentalParticle|fundamental]] fermions that combine to form [[Hadron|hadrons]]. Proposed in 1963 by [[MurrayGellMann|Murray Gell-Mann]] and George Zweig, they carry **fractional electric charge** and cannot exist in isolation (quark confinement).

## Key Points
- **Six flavors** in three families: up (u, +2/3), down (d, −1/3), strange (s, −1/3), charm (c, +2/3), bottom (b, −1/3), top (t, +2/3) — charges in units of qₑ. The top quark is the heaviest (~173 GeV/c²).
- **Color charge**: each quark carries one of three color values (red, green, blue); antiquarks carry anticolors. This is the "charge" of the strong force in **quantum chromodynamics (QCD)**.
- **Confinement**: only colorless ("white") combinations are observable — three RGB quarks (a [[Hadron|baryon]]) or a quark + antiquark of color/anticolor (a [[Meson|meson]]). Separating quarks costs enormous energy, so free quarks are never seen; collisions produce particle "jets" instead.
- **Composition examples**: proton = uud (charge +1), neutron = udd (charge 0), π⁺ = ud̄. Quark charges sum to the integer charges of observed hadrons.
- **Flavor change**: the [[StrongNuclearForce|strong force]] conserves flavor and color; the weak force can change flavor (e.g. d → u in beta decay: d → u + β⁻ + ν̄ₑ), which explains weak-force strangeness violation.
- Evidence: SLAC electron scattering (1967) found three pointlike charges in the proton; the J/ψ (cc̄, 1974), Upsilon (bb̄, 1976), and top quark (1995) confirmed all six flavors.

## Equations
- Baryon number: each quark +1/3, each antiquark −1/3 (baryon = +1, meson = 0)
- Strangeness: each strange quark contributes S = −1
- Proton charge: (+2/3 + 2/3 − 1/3)qₑ = +qₑ (uud)
