# Solubility Product Constant (Ksp)

The **solubility product constant, Ksp**, is the [[EquilibriumConstant|equilibrium constant]] for the dissolution of a slightly soluble ionic solid. For a general compound MₚXq:

$$\mathrm{M_pX_q(s) \rightleftharpoons}\ p\,\mathrm{M}^{m+}(aq) + q\,\mathrm{X}^{n-}(aq) \qquad K_{sp} = [\mathrm{M}^{m+}]^p[\mathrm{X}^{n-}]^q$$

As with any [[HeterogeneousEquilibrium|heterogeneous equilibrium]], the **pure solid does not appear** in the expression — "only gases and solutes are represented." For silver chloride, AgCl(s) ⇌ Ag⁺(aq) + Cl⁻(aq), so Ksp = [Ag⁺][Cl⁻].

A small Ksp means the solid is only sparingly soluble. Representative values at 25 °C: CaF₂ 3.98 × 10⁻¹¹, AgCl 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁰, AgBr 5.0 × 10⁻¹³, AgI 1.5 × 10⁻¹⁶, Mg(OH)₂ 8.9 × 10⁻¹², CdS 1.0 × 10⁻²⁸, Al(OH)₃ 2 × 10⁻³².

## Relationship to other tools

- Ksp is the value the [[ReactionQuotient|reaction quotient]] Qsp takes at equilibrium; comparing Q to Ksp predicts [[PrecipitationReaction|precipitation]] (Q > Ksp) versus further dissolution (Q < Ksp).
- It is interconvertible with [[MolarSolubility|molar solubility]] using an [[ICETable|ICE table]].
- It explains the [[CommonIonEffect|common-ion effect]] and [[SelectivePrecipitation|selective precipitation]], and it couples with acid–base and complex-ion equilibria in [[CoupledEquilibria|coupled equilibria]] (Ksp/Ka or Ksp × Kf).
