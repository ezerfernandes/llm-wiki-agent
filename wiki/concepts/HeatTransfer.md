## Definition
**Heat transfer** is the movement of thermal energy from a hotter region or body to a cooler one,
driven by a temperature difference. It occurs by three distinct mechanisms — conduction, convection,
and radiation — which often act together.

## Key Points
- **[[HeatConduction|Conduction]]** — transfer through stationary matter by physical contact and
  molecular collisions; no bulk motion of the medium.
- **[[Convection]]** — transfer by the macroscopic (bulk) movement of a fluid, often driven by buoyancy
  of warmer, less-dense fluid (natural convection).
- **[[ThermalRadiation|Radiation]]** — transfer by electromagnetic waves; the only mechanism that needs
  no intervening medium and can cross empty space.
- All three are driven by temperature differences; the rate and dominant mode depend on material
  properties, geometry, and environment.
- Transfer rates are controlled by material choice ([[ThermalConductivity|thermal resistance]] / R-value),
  managing air movement, and surface color/emissivity.
- A single situation usually involves all three. Example — a fireplace: radiation warms the room directly,
  conduction carries heat into the floor, and convection brings cold air in through windows and sends hot
  air up the chimney.

## Equations
- Conduction: Q/t = kA(T2 − T1)/d
- Radiation (net): Q_net/t = σeA(T2⁴ − T1⁴)
- (Convection is typically handled via Q = mcΔT plus mass-flow rate rather than a single closed-form law.)
