## Definition
A telescope is an optical instrument that gathers light from distant objects and increases their **angular** size for the observer, using an objective (lens or mirror) to form a real image and an eyepiece to magnify it.

## Key Points
- Because objects are effectively at infinity, the objective forms its real, inverted image at its focal point: d_i = f_o.
- The eyepiece magnifies this intermediate image into a virtual image; angular magnification is M = − f_o/f_e (negative = inverted).
- Greatest magnification comes from a long-focal-length objective and a short-focal-length eyepiece.
- Designs: **Galilean** (convex objective + concave eyepiece, upright; opera glasses); **Keplerian/refracting** (two convex lenses, inverted; astronomy); **erecting** (extra lens re-inverts for terrestrial use); **reflecting** (concave-mirror objective avoids chromatic aberration and scales to large apertures).
- Light-gathering aperture, more than magnification, limits the faintest observable objects, motivating large mirrors.
- Disambiguation: this is the optical instrument, distinct from any software tool named "telescope".

## Equations
- 1/d_i = 1/f_o − 1/d_o → d_i = f_o when d_o ≈ ∞
- M = θ'/θ = − f_o/f_e
