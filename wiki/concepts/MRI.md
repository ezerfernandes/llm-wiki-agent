## Definition
Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) is a non-invasive medical imaging technique based on nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR): atomic nuclei placed in a strong [[MagneticField]] absorb and re-emit specific radio frequencies determined by nucleus type, chemical environment, and field strength.

## Key Points
- Nuclei in an external field experience a torque that drives them into quantized energy (spin) states, acting as resonators.
- A spatially varying field makes the resonant frequency position-dependent, allowing tissue to be mapped into cross-sectional images.
- The strong, uniform fields (≈ 2 T) are produced by superconducting [[Solenoid|solenoids]] (see [[AmperesLaw]], B = μ₀nI).
- Functional MRI (fMRI) maps brain activity via blood-oxygenation changes; MRI detects tumors, strokes, and joint injuries.

## Equations
- Solenoid field: B = μ₀nI (from [[AmperesLaw]])
