# Avogadro's Number

**Avogadro's number (Nₐ)** is the number of discrete entities in one [[TheMoleConcept|mole]] of a substance:

$$N_A = 6.02214076 \times 10^{23}\ \text{mol}^{-1}$$

It is conventionally rounded to **6.022 × 10²³ /mol** (or mol⁻¹) in calculations. As of the 2019 SI redefinition this value is exact, and it serves as the *definition* of the mole: one mole is exactly 6.02214076 × 10²³ entities.

Avogadro's number is what makes the mole useful as a counting unit. Multiplying a quantity in moles by Nₐ gives the number of atoms, molecules, ions, or formula units; dividing a particle count by Nₐ gives moles.

## Role in Conversions

- **Moles → particles:** number of particles = moles × Nₐ
- **Particles → moles:** moles = number of particles ÷ Nₐ
- Combined with [[MolarMass|molar mass]], it links mass directly to particle count: e.g., 0.0400 g saccharin (183.18 g/mol) → (0.0400 / 183.18) × 6.022 × 10²³ = 1.31 × 10²⁰ molecules.

The constant is named for Amedeo Avogadro, though he never determined its value; it honors his hypothesis that equal volumes of gases contain equal numbers of particles.
