## Definition
Cosmology is the scientific study of the universe's structure, composition, origin, and evolution as a whole. It links the physics of the very large (galaxies, spacetime, expansion) with the very small (particle physics and the early hot universe).

## Key Points
- Observations show all galaxies beyond the local cluster receding from us, with recession speed proportional to distance ([[HubblesLaw]]) — the signature of an expanding universe.
- Running the expansion backward implies a hot, dense origin, the [[BigBang]], roughly 13–15 billion years ago.
- The strongest evidence is the cosmic microwave background radiation (CMBR): a near-perfect 2.725 K blackbody glow filling all space, with ~200 µK fluctuations that seeded galaxies via gravitational instability.
- A brief **inflationary** epoch of extremely rapid early expansion explains the CMBR's smoothness.
- The mass-energy budget is dominated by [[DarkMatter]] (~90% of mass) and [[DarkEnergy]] (driving accelerating expansion); luminous matter is only ~0.5%–2% of the critical density.
- The total density relative to critical density sets cosmic geometry — open (negative curvature), closed (positive), or flat; evidence favors near-flatness with eternal expansion.

## Equations
- Hubble's law: v = H₀ d, with H₀ ≈ 20 (km/s)/Mly
- Critical density: ρ_c ≈ 10⁻²⁶ kg/m³
