# Hydrocarbon

A **hydrocarbon** is the simplest kind of organic compound — one containing **only carbon and hydrogen**. Their enormous variety arises from differing chain lengths, branching, ring formation, and the type of carbon–carbon bond present. Hydrocarbons occur naturally in plants, animals, and fossils, and are also made synthetically; they are used chiefly as **fuels** (natural gas, acetylene, propane, butane, gasoline, diesel, heating oil) and as feedstock for **plastics** ([[OrganicPolymer|polyethylene, polypropylene, polystyrene]]).

## The four families

| Family | Bonding | Hybridization | Geometry / angle | Characteristic reaction |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [[Alkane]] (saturated) | C–C single only | sp³ | tetrahedral, 109.5° | substitution, combustion |
| [[Alkene]] | one or more C=C | sp² | trigonal planar, 120° | addition |
| [[Alkyne]] | one or more C≡C | sp | linear, 180° | addition (×2 reagent) |
| [[AromaticHydrocarbon]] | delocalized π ring | sp² | planar ring | substitution |

- **Saturated** hydrocarbons contain only single C–C bonds; **unsaturated** hydrocarbons contain one or more C=C double or C≡C triple bonds.
- A C=C is one [[SigmaAndPiBonds|σ bond plus one π bond]]; a C≡C is one σ plus two π. The weaker π bonds make unsaturated hydrocarbons more reactive.

## Cross-cutting themes
- **[[OrganicIsomerism]]** — the same molecular formula can give multiple hydrocarbons (structural isomers; geometric cis/trans isomers about a double bond).
- **Nomenclature** — IUPAC names use the longest-chain root (meth-, eth-, prop-, but-, pent-, hex-, hept-, oct-, non-, dec-) plus a suffix for the family (-ane / -ene / -yne).
- **[[OrganicFunctionalGroup|Functional groups]]** — replacing hydrogens with other groups produces all the other organic families.
