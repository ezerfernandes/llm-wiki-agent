## Definition
In physics, **work** is the product of the component of a [[Force]] along the direction of motion and the distance moved. It is the mechanism by which energy is transferred into or out of a system, or converted between forms. This is narrower than everyday usage: holding a heavy object still, or carrying it horizontally, does no work because the motion-aligned force component or the displacement is zero.

## Key Points
- Three things matter: a force acts, a displacement occurs, and the angle θ between force and displacement.
- Work is a scalar; its sign tells direction of energy transfer — positive adds energy, negative removes it (e.g., lowering an object, θ = 180°).
- Carrying horizontally does zero work because the supporting force is perpendicular to motion (cos 90° = 0).
- Net work over all forces drives the [[WorkEnergyTheorem]].
- SI unit: the joule (J) = 1 N·m.

## Equations
- W = F d cos θ  (F = force magnitude, d = displacement, θ = angle between them)
- W = F d  (force parallel to motion)
