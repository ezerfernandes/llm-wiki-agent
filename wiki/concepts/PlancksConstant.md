# Planck's Constant

**Planck's constant** *h* is the fundamental proportionality constant of quantum mechanics, linking a quantum's energy to its frequency and a particle's momentum to its wavelength. Its extreme smallness is why quantization is invisible at everyday scales (the [[WaveParticleDuality|correspondence principle]]).

## Key Points
- Introduced by [[MaxPlanck|Planck]] to fit the [[BlackbodyRadiation|blackbody]] spectrum.
- Appears in every quantum relation in the chapter: photon energy, photon momentum, the de Broglie wavelength, and the uncertainty limits.
- The reduced form ℏ = h/(2π) appears in the uncertainty principle.

## Equations
- h = 6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s
- In convenient units: h = 4.14 × 10⁻¹⁵ eV·s; hc = 1240 eV·nm
- Reduced constant: ℏ = h/(2π) = 1.055 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s
- Photon energy E = hf; photon/matter momentum p = h/λ; uncertainty Δx·Δp ≥ h/(4π)
