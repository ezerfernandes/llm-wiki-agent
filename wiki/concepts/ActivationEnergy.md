# Activation Energy

The **activation energy** $E_a$ is the minimum energy a collision must supply for reactants to form product — the energy barrier between reactants and products. (This is a chemical-reaction concept, unrelated to the neural-network [[ActivationFunction]].)

In [[CollisionTheory|collision theory]], only collisions with energy ≥ Eₐ (and correct orientation) react. When Eₐ greatly exceeds the average molecular kinetic energy, few molecules qualify and the reaction is slow; a small Eₐ allows a fast reaction. Because raising [[Temperature|temperature]] increases the fraction of molecules with energy above Eₐ (see [[KineticMolecularTheory]]), reaction rates rise sharply with temperature.

On a reaction energy diagram (potential energy vs reaction coordinate), Eₐ is the height from the reactants up to the peak — the [[TransitionState|transition state]]. The diagram separately shows ΔH, the [[EnthalpyChemistry|enthalpy]] difference between reactants and products (the *thermodynamics*, distinct from the *kinetic* barrier Eₐ).

Eₐ is the parameter in the [[ArrheniusEquation|Arrhenius equation]] $k = A\,e^{-E_a/RT}$ and is obtained from the slope (−Eₐ/R) of a ln k vs 1/T plot. A [[Catalysis|catalyst]] speeds a reaction precisely by providing a path with a lower Eₐ, without changing ΔH.
