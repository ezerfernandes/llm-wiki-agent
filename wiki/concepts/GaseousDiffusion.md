# Gaseous Diffusion

**Diffusion** (of gases) is the process by which molecules disperse through space in response to differences in concentration. This page is the **chemistry-domain gas phenomenon** from OpenStax *Chemistry 2e* Ch 9; it is distinct from the physics transport page [[MolecularDiffusion]] (College Physics 2e) and entirely unrelated to the machine-learning [[DiffusionModel]] / [[StableDiffusion]] generative process.

## Behavior

Gas molecules move randomly throughout a container. Net movement is from regions of **higher concentration to lower concentration**; in a closed system diffusion continues until concentrations are equal everywhere, after which molecules keep moving but there is no net transfer (dynamic equilibrium).

$$\text{rate of diffusion} = \frac{\text{amount of gas passing through an area}}{\text{unit of time}}$$

The rate depends on temperature, the mass of the particles, the concentration gradient, the available surface area, and the distance traveled. The **mean free path** — the average distance between collisions — is hundreds of times the molecular diameter and grows as pressure falls.

## Relation to Effusion and Graham's Law

Diffusion and [[Effusion|effusion]] both depend on molar mass through molecular speed (see [[RootMeanSquareSpeed]]); lighter gases diffuse faster, following [[GrahamsLaw|Graham's law]] (rate ∝ 1/√ℳ).

## Application: Uranium Enrichment

Gaseous diffusion enriched uranium for the Manhattan Project. Natural uranium is only 0.72% fissile ²³⁵U (reactors need 2–5%). Volatile uranium hexafluoride (**UF₆**) is pumped through porous-barrier diffusers; the lighter ²³⁵UF₆ diffuses slightly faster, enriching the through-gas by ~0.4% per stage, so many diffusers are chained into a **cascade**. First done at Oak Ridge, Tennessee; this energy-intensive method is now being replaced by gas centrifuges.
