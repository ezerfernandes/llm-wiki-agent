# Nuclear Chemistry

**Nuclear chemistry** is the study of changes in the **nucleus** of an atom, as opposed to the changes in *electronic* structure that drive ordinary chemical reactions. A [[NuclearEquation|nuclear reaction]] changes a nuclide's atomic number, mass number, or energy state — rearranging **nucleons** rather than atoms or electrons. The field began with the discovery of [[RadioactiveDecayChemistry|radioactivity]] in 1896 (Henri Becquerel) and now spans energy, medicine, geology, and analysis.

The defining feature is scale of energy: nuclear binding energies are ~**billions of kJ/mol**, whereas the most energetic chemical reactions are ~thousands of kJ/mol. Because mass changes in chemical reactions are correspondingly tiny (nanogram range), they are undetectable, but in nuclear reactions the [[NuclearBindingEnergyChemistry|mass defect]] is large enough (milligram range) to measure and harness via E = mc².
