# Intercepts

The **intercepts** of a line are the points where it crosses the axes of the [[RectangularCoordinateSystem|coordinate plane]]. They give a fast, reliable way to graph a [[LinearEquation|linear equation]] in standard form. From OpenStax [[Prealgebra]] 2e Chapter 11 ([[prealgebra-2e-ch11-graphs]]).

## Definitions
- **x-intercept** — the point `(a, 0)` where the graph crosses the x-axis. It **occurs when `y = 0`**.
- **y-intercept** — the point `(0, b)` where the graph crosses the y-axis. It **occurs when `x = 0`**.

## Finding intercepts from an equation
Set the *other* variable to zero and solve (this is just one-variable [[SolvingLinearEquations|equation solving]]):
- **x-intercept:** substitute `y = 0`, solve for x.
- **y-intercept:** substitute `x = 0`, solve for y.

Example — `2x + y = 6`: with `y = 0`, `x = 3` ⇒ x-intercept `(3, 0)`; with `x = 0`, `y = 6` ⇒ y-intercept `(0, 6)`.

## Graphing using intercepts
1. Find the x- and y-intercepts.
2. Find a **third** solution point as a check.
3. Plot the three points and confirm they are collinear.
4. Draw the line through them.

Intercepts are the preferred method when the equation is in standard form `Ax + By = C`.
