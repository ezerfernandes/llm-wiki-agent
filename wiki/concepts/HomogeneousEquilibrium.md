# Homogeneous Equilibrium

A **homogeneous equilibrium** is one in which all reactants and products (and any catalyst, if present) are in the **same phase** — typically all gases or all species dissolved in one solution. Examples:

$$\mathrm{C_2H_2(aq) + 2Br_2(aq) \rightleftharpoons C_2H_2Br_4(aq)}$$
$$\mathrm{N_2(g) + 3H_2(g) \rightleftharpoons 2NH_3(g)}$$

Because every species is in the same phase, **all** of them appear in the [[ReactionQuotient|reaction quotient]] and [[EquilibriumConstant|equilibrium constant]] expressions.

## Solvent water is omitted

One subtlety: when **water is the solvent** in an aqueous reaction and also appears as a reactant or product, its concentration is **not** included in Q or K. Its relative concentration (activity) is effectively 1 because it is present in vast excess and barely changes. This is the same reasoning that excludes pure solids and liquids in a [[HeterogeneousEquilibrium|heterogeneous equilibrium]] — only gaseous and solute species carry terms.
