## Definition
A lever is a rigid bar that rotates about a fixed pivot (the fulcrum). By applying an input force at one distance from the fulcrum and delivering an output force at another, a lever uses [[Torque]] balance to multiply force — the prototypical [[SimpleMachine]].

> Note: this is the physics/mechanics lever (force multiplication), distinct from financial "leverage" or trading senses.

## Key Points
- In static use, the net torque about the fulcrum is zero, so input torque equals output torque.
- The input force times its lever arm equals the output force times its lever arm: `l_i F_i = l_o F_o`.
- A longer input arm relative to the output arm gives [[MechanicalAdvantage]] greater than 1 (more output force, less distance).
- A shorter input arm (e.g., a shovel, where the input hand is closer to the pivot than the load) gives MA < 1, trading force for range/speed of motion.
- Real biological levers (muscle-bone-joint systems) typically have MA much less than 1: short muscle lever arms mean muscles exert forces many times the load (e.g., biceps ~7.38× the supported weight).

## Equations
- Torque balance: `l_i F_i = l_o F_o`
- Mechanical advantage: `MA = F_o / F_i = l_i / l_o`
