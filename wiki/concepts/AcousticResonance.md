## Definition
Acoustic resonance is the buildup of large-amplitude [[StandingWave]]s in an air column at its natural frequencies, produced when forward and reflected [[Sound]] waves interfere constructively. It is the wave-[[Resonance]] mechanism behind wind instruments and the human voice. Interference effects like these are experimental proof that sound is a wave.

## Key Points
- **Nodes** are points of zero air displacement (at closed tube ends); **antinodes** are points of maximum displacement (at open ends). Node-to-antinode spacing is ¼ wavelength.
- The **fundamental frequency** is the lowest resonance (first harmonic); higher resonances are **overtones**. All resonances together are the **harmonics**.
- A tube closed at one end supports only **odd harmonics**; a tube open at both ends supports **all** harmonics.
- Because resonant frequencies depend on [[SpeedOfSound]] (and thus air temperature), instruments must be warmed and tuned before ensemble play.
- Destructive interference is exploited in noise-canceling headphones (≥30 dB reduction via anti-phase waves).

## Equations
- Closed tube (one end): `f_n = n·v_w / (4L)`, n = 1, 3, 5, … (fundamental `f₁ = v_w/4L`)
- Open tube (both ends): `f_n = n·v_w / (2L)`, n = 1, 2, 3, …
- `v_w = 331 m/s · √(T/273 K)`
