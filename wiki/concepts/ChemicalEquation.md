# Chemical Equation

A **chemical equation** uses element symbols and [[ChemicalFormula|formulas]] to represent both the *identities* and the *relative quantities* of substances undergoing a chemical (or physical) change. Example:

CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O

## Anatomy

- **Reactants** — substances undergoing reaction, written on the **left**.
- **Products** — substances generated, written on the **right**.
- **Separators** — `+` separates individual formulas; `→` separates reactants from products.
- **Coefficients** — numbers immediately left of a formula giving the *relative numbers* of species. A coefficient of 1 is omitted.

Coefficients are read as ratios and apply at any scale: "1 molecule CH₄ + 2 molecules O₂ …" or "1 mol CH₄ + 2 mol O₂ …". They are the basis of [[ReactionStoichiometry|reaction stoichiometry]].

## Physical-State Symbols

Parenthetical labels follow a formula to give its physical state:

- **(s)** — solid
- **(l)** — liquid
- **(g)** — gas
- **(aq)** — aqueous (dissolved in water)

Example: 2Na(s) + 2H₂O(l) → 2NaOH(aq) + H₂(g)

Special conditions (e.g. heating, shown by Δ) are written above or below the arrow: CaCO₃(s) →^Δ CaO(s) + CO₂(g).

## Three Forms for Aqueous Ionic Reactions

For reactions of dissolved ionic compounds, the same chemistry can be written at three levels of detail:

1. **Molecular equation** — every species as a complete formula.
   `CaCl₂(aq) + 2AgNO₃(aq) → Ca(NO₃)₂(aq) + 2AgCl(s)`
2. **Complete ionic equation** — soluble ionic compounds shown as their dissociated ions; insoluble products stay as formulas with (s).
   `Ca²⁺(aq) + 2Cl⁻(aq) + 2Ag⁺(aq) + 2NO₃⁻(aq) → Ca²⁺(aq) + 2NO₃⁻(aq) + 2AgCl(s)`
3. **Net ionic equation** — remove **spectator ions** (ions unchanged by the reaction, present only for charge neutrality), leaving only the essential chemistry.
   `Ag⁺(aq) + Cl⁻(aq) → AgCl(s)`

Here Ca²⁺ and NO₃⁻ are the spectator ions. The net ionic equation says solid AgCl forms from dissolved Ag⁺ and Cl⁻ regardless of their source.
