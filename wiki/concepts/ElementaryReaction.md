# Elementary Reaction

An **elementary reaction** is a single step in a [[ReactionMechanism|reaction mechanism]] that occurs exactly as its equation is written — one discrete molecular event. The elementary steps of a mechanism must sum to the overall balanced [[ChemicalEquation|equation]].

## Molecularity

The **molecularity** of an elementary reaction is the number of reactant entities (atoms, molecules, or ions) that come together in that step:

- **Unimolecular** — one reactant: A → products; rate = k[A] (first order).
- **Bimolecular** — two reactants: A + B → products gives rate = k[A][B]; 2A → products gives rate = k[A]² (second order overall).
- **Termolecular** — three reactants colliding simultaneously: e.g. 2NO + O₂ → 2NO₂, rate = k[NO]²[O₂]. Termolecular steps are rare because a simultaneous three-body collision is improbable.

## Rate law directly from the equation

The defining feature: **the rate law of an elementary reaction can be written directly from its balanced equation**, with each order equal to that species' molecularity. This is the one situation where stoichiometry gives the rate law — for an *overall* reaction the [[RateLaw|rate law]] and its [[ReactionOrder|orders]] must instead be determined experimentally.
