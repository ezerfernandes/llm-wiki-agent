# Electron Spin

**Electron spin** is an intrinsic angular momentum carried by every [[Electron|electron]], independent of its orbital motion and with no classical analogue. It is quantized in both magnitude and direction and makes the electron behave like a tiny magnet.

## Key Points
- The spin quantum number is fixed at **s = 1/2** for electrons; the spin projection along any axis has only two allowed values, **m_s = ±1/2** ("spin up" / "spin down").
- Spin was proposed by Goudsmit and Uhlenbeck (1925) to explain **fine structure** — closely spaced spectral doublets seen even without an applied magnetic field, arising from spin-orbit coupling (the electron's intrinsic magnetic field interacting with its orbital magnetic field).
- In an external magnetic field the spin can only orient at certain angles (~54.7°), a form of space quantization that contributes to the [[AtomicSpectra|Zeeman effect]] and line splitting.
- Spin is one of the four [[QuantumNumbers|quantum numbers]] and, via the [[PauliExclusionPrinciple|Pauli exclusion principle]], is what lets two electrons share an orbital (opposite spins).

## Equations
- Spin magnitude: S = √[s(s+1)]·(h/2π), with s = 1/2
- Spin z-component: S_z = m_s·(h/2π), with m_s = ±1/2

## Applications
Fine-structure spectroscopy, the Zeeman effect, and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), which exploits how intrinsic magnetic moments respond to external fields.
