## Definition
Hubble's law states that the recession velocity of a distant galaxy is directly proportional to its distance from us: more distant galaxies recede faster. It is the primary observational evidence that the universe is expanding.

## Key Points
- Galaxy velocities are measured from the **cosmological red shift** — the stretching of light wavelengths by the expansion of space itself, distinct from a classical Doppler shift.
- The proportionality constant is the **Hubble constant** H₀ ≈ 20 (km/s)/Mly, a measure of the expansion rate of space.
- The relation lets astronomers convert a measured recession velocity into a distance (d = v/H₀), e.g., a galaxy receding at 100,000 km/s lies ~5,000 Mly away.
- Because light takes time to reach us, distant galaxies are seen as they were in the past (Andromeda at 2 Mly is seen as it was 2 million years ago).
- Extrapolating the expansion backward gives the [[BigBang]] origin time (~13–15 billion years); the inverse of H₀ roughly estimates the age of the universe.
- Recent data show the expansion is accelerating, attributed to [[DarkEnergy]].

## Equations
- v = H₀ d  (recession velocity vs. distance)
- d = v / H₀  (distance from velocity)
