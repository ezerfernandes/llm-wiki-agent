## Definition
**Ionizing radiation** is any radiation energetic enough to remove electrons from atoms (ionize them), whether the radiation originates in the nucleus or elsewhere. It affects living tissue without triggering nerve sensation, so it must be detected with instruments rather than felt.

## Key Points
- [[Radioactivity|Nuclear radiation]] — alpha, beta, and gamma — is ionizing; so are X-rays.
- Energy is lost to matter chiefly through successive ionization events; each event removes energy from the radiation until its **range** is exhausted.
- Ionization is the basis of both **damage** (DNA injury, semiconductor faults, cancer) and **benefit** (cancer therapy, sterilization, detection).
- Detection relies on the ionization or excitation it produces — see [[RadiationDetection]].

## Equations
- Threshold: a few eV (see [[ElectronVolt]]) is enough to ionize many atoms or break bonds.
