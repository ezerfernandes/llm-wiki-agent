## Definition
The decibel (dB) is a logarithmic unit expressing the **sound intensity level** β — the ratio of a sound's [[SoundIntensity]] to a reference intensity, multiplied by 10. It is derived from the bel (named after Alexander Graham Bell) and reflects that human loudness perception is logarithmic rather than linear.

## Key Points
- Reference intensity `I₀ = 10⁻¹² W/m²` corresponds to 0 dB (threshold of hearing).
- Each tenfold increase in intensity adds 10 dB; doubling intensity adds ~3 dB (3.01 dB).
- The scale compresses the enormous ~10¹² intensity range of hearing into a manageable ~0–120 dB span.
- Safety thresholds: ~85 dB for 8-hour daily exposure; 120+ dB risks immediate damage.
- The decibel measures physical intensity; perceived [[Loudness]] (in phons) additionally depends on frequency.

## Equations
- Sound intensity level: `β(dB) = 10·log₁₀(I / I₀)`
- Difference between two levels: `β₂ − β₁ = 10·log₁₀(I₂ / I₁)`
