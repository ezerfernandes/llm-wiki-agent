## Definition
**Magnetic flux (Φ)** measures how much [[MagneticField|magnetic field]] passes through a surface. It depends on the field strength B, the area A, and the orientation angle θ between the field and the surface normal. Flux is the central quantity in electromagnetic induction: a *change* in Φ — not the field itself — is what induces an [[ElectromotiveForce|emf]].

## Key Points
- Flux is maximal when the field is perpendicular to the surface (θ = 0) and zero when the field lies in the plane of the surface (θ = 90°).
- Φ can change three ways: by changing B, by changing the area A, or by changing the orientation θ. Any of these drives induction.
- Units are tesla·square meters (T·m²), also called webers (Wb).
- A static field through a fixed loop produces no emf no matter how strong it is; only the rate of change matters ([[FaradaysLaw]]).

## Equations
- `Φ = B A cos θ`
- `Φ = B⊥ A`  (B⊥ = field component perpendicular to the surface)

## Related
- [[MagneticField]] — the field whose passage through a surface is being measured
- [[FaradaysLaw]] — induced emf is proportional to the rate of change of flux
- [[LenzsLaw]] — fixes the direction of the response to a flux change
- [[ElectromotiveForce]] — the quantity induced by changing flux
