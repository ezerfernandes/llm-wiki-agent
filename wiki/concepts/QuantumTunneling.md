## Definition
**Quantum tunneling** (barrier penetration) is the quantum-mechanical process by which a particle crosses a **potential barrier** it classically lacks the energy to enter — appearing on the far side without ever occupying the barrier region.

## Key Points
- Classically a particle without enough kinetic energy is trapped (a marble cannot escape a bowl); quantum-mechanically there is a nonzero probability of crossing.
- The **wave function** varies smoothly and decays exponentially inside the barrier rather than dropping abruptly to zero; since probability ∝ |ψ|², it stays nonzero beyond the barrier, permitting detection there.
- **Alpha decay:** an [[AlphaDecay|alpha particle]] inside a nucleus is ~8 MeV short of the energy needed to escape, yet tunnels through the repulsive Coulomb barrier (George Gamow, 1928; J. R. Oppenheimer contributed theory). More energetic decays correspond to a thinner effective barrier and shorter [[HalfLife|half-lives]] — the theory spans ~17 orders of magnitude in half-life.
- **Scanning tunneling microscope (STM):** electrons tunnel across a thin gap between probe tip and surface; the tunneling current images individual atoms.
