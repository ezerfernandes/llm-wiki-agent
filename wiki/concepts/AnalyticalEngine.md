# Analytical Engine

[[CharlesBabbage|Charles Babbage]]'s 1837 design for a **general-purpose mechanical computer** — never built in his lifetime, but the **conceptual antecedent** of the [[VonNeumannArchitecture|stored-program computer]] by over a century.

## Design ideas (1830s–1840s)

- A **store** (memory) and a separate **mill** (arithmetic unit) — anticipating the [[CPU|CPU]] / [[RAM|memory]] split
- **Punched-card programming** — borrowed from the Jacquard loom; the first proposed external program-input medium
- **Conditional branching** based on intermediate results
- **Loops** via punched-card chains

## Connection to Lovelace

[[AdaLovelace]]'s 1843 notes on the Analytical Engine contained what is widely regarded as the **first published algorithm intended for execution by a machine** — a procedure for computing Bernoulli numbers — and her *"can do whatever we know how to order it to perform"* observation about the machine's nature.

Per [[dis-5-1-history|*Dive into Systems* Ch 5.1]], the Analytical Engine is the 19th-century **conceptual seed** of the 1930s–1940s electronic / electromechanical convergence: it established that *general-purpose, programmable computation* was an achievable engineering goal long before the technology to realize it existed.
