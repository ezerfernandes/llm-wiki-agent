# Atomic Number and Mass Number

Two integers characterize the [[AtomicNucleus|nucleus]] of an [[Atom]]:

- **Atomic number (Z)** = the number of [[Proton|protons]]. Z is the defining characteristic of a [[ChemicalElement|element]] — every atom of a given element has the same Z. In a *neutral* atom, the number of [[Electron|electrons]] also equals Z.
- **Mass number (A)** = the total number of nucleons, i.e. protons + [[Neutron|neutrons]].

From these:

$$\text{number of neutrons} = A - Z$$

## Isotope Symbol

An atom's composition is written with A as a left superscript and Z as a left subscript on the element symbol:

$$^{A}_{Z}X$$

For example, the three magnesium isotopes ²⁴Mg, ²⁵Mg, and ²⁶Mg all have Z = 12 but 12, 13, and 14 neutrons respectively. Atoms with the same Z but different A are [[Isotope|isotopes]]. In [[NuclearChemistry|nuclear chemistry]] this same $^{A}_{Z}X$ form is the [[NuclideNotation|nuclide notation]], and conservation of A and Z is what balances every [[NuclearEquation|nuclear equation]].

## Charge

When the electron count does not equal Z, the atom is a charged [[ChemicalIon|ion]]: charge = (number of protons) − (number of electrons).
