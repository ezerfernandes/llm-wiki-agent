# Law of Definite Proportions

The **law of definite proportions** (also the **law of constant composition**) states that *all samples of a pure [[ChemicalCompound|compound]] contain the same elements in the same proportion by mass*. It was articulated by the French chemist Joseph Proust and is a direct consequence of [[AtomicTheory|Dalton's atomic theory]] (atoms of each element combine in fixed ratios).

## Worked Example — Isooctane

Three samples of isooctane give the same carbon-to-hydrogen mass ratio:

| Sample | C (g) | H (g) | C:H mass ratio |
|--------|-------|-------|----------------|
| A | 14.82 | 2.78 | 5.33 : 1 |
| B | 22.33 | 4.19 | 5.33 : 1 |
| C | 19.40 | 3.64 | 5.33 : 1 |

The constant 5.33:1 ratio illustrates the law. (Caveat: two samples having the same mass ratio are not *necessarily* the same substance.)

## Relationship to Other Laws

Together with the [[LawOfMultipleProportions|law of multiple proportions]], this law is one of the key empirical regularities that [[AtomicTheory|Dalton's atomic theory]] was crafted to explain.
