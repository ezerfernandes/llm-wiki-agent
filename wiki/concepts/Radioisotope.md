# Radioisotope

A **radioactive isotope (radioisotope)** is an isotope with the same chemical properties as the stable isotopes of its element, but which emits detectable radiation as it undergoes [[RadioactiveDecayChemistry|radioactive decay]]. Because chemistry is unchanged but the emissions can be tracked, radioisotopes are powerful tools.

A **radioactive tracer (radioactive label)** is a compound in which one or more atoms are replaced by a radioisotope, so its path or distribution can be followed by monitoring the emissions — used to trace biochemical pathways or locate a substance in an organism.

### Medical applications
- **Technetium-99m** (t₁/₂ = 6.01 h): damaged heart, liver, and lung tissue preferentially absorb Tc compounds, imaged by their γ rays. Generated on-site from **molybdenum-99** (t₁/₂ = 66 h) by β decay; a few µg of Mo-99 yields enough Tc-99 for ~10,000 tests (separated as pertechnetate TcO₄⁻ from molybdate MoO₄²⁻ by column chromatography).
- **Thallium-201**: concentrates in healthy heart tissue; paired with Tc-99 to study cardiac tissue.
- **Iodine-131**: concentrates in the thyroid (also liver, brain); diagnoses goiter and treats Grave's disease and thyroid/liver/brain tumors.
- **Sodium-24**: injected as a salt solution to find blood-flow obstructions.
- **Radioimmunoassay (RIA)** (Yalow & Berson, 1950s): ultra-sensitive antigen detection for hormones, narcotics, blood-bank and cancer screening. The US runs >10 million nuclear-medicine procedures and >100 million tests yearly.

**Radiation therapy** damages cancer-cell DNA with high-energy radiation, by external beam or internal **brachytherapy**. **Cobalt-60** (made by neutron activation of Co-59) supplies focused γ beams: $^{59}_{27}\text{Co} + ^{1}_{0}\text{n} \rightarrow ^{60}_{27}\text{Co} \rightarrow ^{60}_{28}\text{Ni} + ^{0}_{-1}\beta + 2\,^{0}_{0}\gamma$. (See also [[RadiationTherapy]].)

### Research, industry, and home
- **Carbon-14** traced the photosynthesis pathway by showing which organic compounds carried ¹⁴C over time.
- Industry: thickness gauging of films/sheets, metal flaw detection with cobalt-60 γ rays, γ-sterilization of pest insects, and food preservation.
- **Smoke detectors**: **americium-241** (α emitter, t₁/₂ = 458 y) ionizes air between plates; smoke disrupts the ion current, triggering the alarm.

Long-lived radioisotopes (C-14, U-238) also serve as clocks — see [[RadioactiveHalfLife]].
