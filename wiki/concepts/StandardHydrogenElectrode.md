# Standard Hydrogen Electrode (SHE)

The **standard hydrogen electrode (SHE)** is the reference half-cell assigned a potential of **exactly 0 V**, against which all [[StandardReductionPotential|standard electrode potentials]] are defined. Because no half-cell potential can be measured in isolation, the chemistry community fixes one arbitrary zero — the SHE — so every other half-cell's value becomes definite.

## The Half-Reaction
$$2H^+(aq) + 2e^- \rightarrow H_2(g) \qquad E^\circ \equiv 0\text{ V}$$

## Construction
A typical SHE consists of an **inert platinum electrode** immersed in a solution of exactly **1 M aqueous H⁺**, with **H₂ gas bubbled at 1 bar**, maintained at **298 K** — i.e., all standard-state conditions.

## Role
Pairing any half-cell X (as cathode) with the SHE (as anode) gives E_cell = E_X − E_SHE = E_X, so the measured cell voltage *is* the electrode potential of X. This makes the SHE the anchor of the entire [[StandardReductionPotential|standard reduction potential]] table and the basis for computing any [[CellPotential|cell potential]].
