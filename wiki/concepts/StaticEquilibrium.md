## Definition
A system is in equilibrium when it has zero net external force and zero net external torque, so it neither accelerates translationally nor rotationally. Static equilibrium specifically means the system is at rest; dynamic equilibrium means it moves at constant velocity. Both are the zero-acceleration case of [[NewtonsSecondLaw]].

## Key Points
- **First condition**: the net external force on the system must be zero (`net F = 0`), enforced component-wise (`net F_x = 0`, `net F_y = 0`).
- **Second condition**: the net external [[Torque]] must be zero (`net τ = 0`).
- The first condition is necessary but not sufficient — equal and opposite forces applied at different points still cause rotation, so the torque condition is required too.
- The torque condition holds for any pivot point; choosing the pivot along the line of an unknown force removes its torque and simplifies the problem.
- Standard solution method: draw a [[FreeBodyDiagram]], apply both conditions, and solve for unknown forces.
- Statics is a special application of Newton's laws to balanced, non-accelerating systems.

## Equations
- `net F = 0` → `net F_x = 0`, `net F_y = 0`
- `net τ = 0`
