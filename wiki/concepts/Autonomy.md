# Autonomy

**Autonomy** is the capacity of an agent to act and pursue its goals **without constant human oversight**. It is the first of the defining characteristics of an [[AgenticAI|agentic system]] named in [[AgenticDesignPatterns|*Agentic Design Patterns*]] (Gulli), distinguishing agents from traditional software that follows rigid, step-by-step instructions: agents instead "exhibit a degree of flexibility and initiative."

## How it works
An autonomous agent perceives its environment, makes informed decisions from those perceptions and a set of predefined or learned goals, and executes actions to achieve them — closing the loop largely on its own. This is what enables the proactive, multi-step behavior of higher-complexity agents in the [[AgentComplexitySpectrum|agent-complexity spectrum]] (especially Level 2 strategic problem-solvers and Level 3 collaborative systems).

## Why it matters and its tension
Autonomy is the source of agents' power and of their risk. Greater autonomy means fewer points where a [[HumanInTheLoop|human in the loop]] can catch a mistake, so it must be paired with [[Guardrails|guardrails]], [[EvaluationAndMonitoring|monitoring]], and verification. The book's Thought-Leader perspective (Marco Argenti) stresses that "you cannot afford to trust blindly" — autonomy demands resilience-by-design and accountability.
