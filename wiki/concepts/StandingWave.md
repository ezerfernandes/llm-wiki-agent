## Definition
A standing wave is a stationary-looking pattern formed by the [[Superposition]] of two identical [[Wave]]s traveling in opposite directions. It alternates between constructive and destructive interference, producing fixed points of zero and maximum motion.

## Key Points
- **Nodes** are points of permanent zero displacement; **antinodes** are points of maximum amplitude.
- For a string fixed at both ends, resonance occurs when the length holds an integer number of half-wavelengths.
- The lowest resonant frequency is the **fundamental** f₁; higher resonances are **harmonics/overtones** at integer multiples.
- Higher string tension raises [[WaveSpeed]] and thus all resonant frequencies — the basis of tuning string instruments.
- Structures can form standing-wave resonances (e.g., buildings in earthquakes), tying this to [[Resonance]].

## Equations
- Fundamental (string fixed both ends): f₁ = v_w / 2L
- Harmonics: f_n = n f₁ (n = 1, 2, 3, …)
- Wavelengths: λ₁ = 2L, λ₂ = L
