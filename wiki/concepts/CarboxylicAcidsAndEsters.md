# Carboxylic Acids and Esters

Both families have a **[[AldehydesAndKetones|carbonyl]] group (C=O)** with a **second oxygen single-bonded** to the carbonyl carbon. The two differ in what that second oxygen carries:

- **Carboxylic acid** — the second oxygen also bonds to a **hydrogen** (group **–COOH** / **–CO₂H**; suffix **-oic acid**).
- **Ester** — the second oxygen bonds to **another carbon** (group **–CO₂R**; suffix **-oate**).

## Carboxylic acids are weak acids

The acidic hydrogen of –COOH reacts with a base to form an ionic salt:

CH₃CH₂COOH + NaOH → CH₃CH₂COO⁻Na⁺ + H₂O

Carboxylic acids are **[[BronstedLowryAcidsAndBases|weak acids]]** — *not 100% ionized*; generally **only ~1% of the molecules in water are ionized** at any time, the rest undissociated.

**Preparation:** oxidize an aldehyde, or oxidize a terminal (primary) [[AlcoholsAndEthers|alcohol]] in two steps — alcohol → aldehyde → carboxylic acid (see [[AldehydesAndKetones]]).

**Examples:** **formic acid (HCO₂H)** — simplest acid, from Latin *formicus* "ant," responsible for ant/wasp-sting irritation; **acetic acid (CH₃CO₂H)** — 3–6% of vinegar (yeast ferments sugar → ethanol → acetic acid), an excellent solvent and the basis of cellulose acetate (rayon).

## Esterification

**Esters form by reaction of a carboxylic acid with an alcohol**, losing water (see [[Esterification]]). Example — ethyl acetate from acetic acid + ethanol:

CH₃COOH + HOCH₂CH₃ → CH₃CO₂CH₂CH₃ + H₂O

Because esters have **no intermolecular hydrogen bonds**, they have **lower vapor pressures** than the parent alcohols/acids and carry the **pleasant odors of fruits, flowers, and perfumes** — e.g., apple (butyl acetate), pineapple (ethyl butyrate), wintergreen (methyl salicylate).

## Fats and oils

The most important natural esters are **fats** (lard, tallow, butter) and **oils** (linseed, cottonseed, olive) — esters of the trihydroxyl alcohol **glycerine, C₃H₅(OH)₃**, with long-chain acids: **palmitic CH₃(CH₂)₁₄CO₂H** and **stearic CH₃(CH₂)₁₆CO₂H** (saturated), and **oleic CH₃(CH₂)₇CH=CH(CH₂)₇CO₂H** (unsaturated, one C=C).
