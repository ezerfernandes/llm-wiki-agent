# Aromatic Hydrocarbon

An **aromatic hydrocarbon** is a ring-containing [[Hydrocarbon|hydrocarbon]] with **delocalized π bonding**. The simplest and defining member is **benzene, C₆H₆**. (Named *AromaticHydrocarbon* to keep the chemistry sense distinct from any unrelated "aromatic" page.)

## Benzene structure

Benzene is a **hexagonal ring of sp²-hybridized carbon atoms** ([[OrbitalHybridization]]), each with an **unhybridized p orbital perpendicular to the ring plane**. Each carbon uses three sp² electrons (and each H its electron) to form **σ bonds**; the fourth electron on each carbon is shared with its neighbors through the unhybridized p orbitals, producing a **delocalized π system**.

This bonding is described either as a **[[Resonance|resonance]] hybrid** (valence bond theory) or as **delocalization** (molecular orbital theory). As a result, benzene does **not** behave like a typical [[Alkene|alkene]]: all six C–C bonds are **equivalent**, with character **intermediate between a single and a double bond**. Benzene is drawn as a hexagon with a circle inside to represent the delocalized electrons.

## Derivatives and isomers

Replacing ring hydrogens gives derivatives: **toluene** (one –CH₃), **xylene** (two –CH₃), **styrene** (one –CH=CH₂; makes polystyrene). With two substituents, three positional [[OrganicIsomerism|isomers]] are possible — **ortho**, **meta**, and **para** (e.g., the three chlorotoluenes, the three dibromobenzenes).

## Reactivity — substitution, not addition

Unlike alkenes, **aromatic compounds more readily undergo [[SubstitutionReaction|substitution]] than [[AdditionReaction|addition]]**: replacing a hydrogen with another substituent **leaves the delocalized double bonds intact**, preserving the stable aromatic ring.
