# Rectangular Coordinate System

The **rectangular coordinate system** — also called the **Cartesian coordinate system**, the **x-y plane**, or the **coordinate plane** — is formed by a horizontal number line (the **x-axis**) and a vertical number line (the **y-axis**) intersecting at right angles. Their crossing point is the **origin**, `(0, 0)`. It is the setting in which a [[LinearEquation|linear equation]] in two variables is drawn (see [[GraphingLinearEquations]]), introduced in OpenStax [[Prealgebra]] 2e Chapter 11 ([[prealgebra-2e-ch11-graphs]]).

## Locating points: the ordered pair
Every point is named by an **ordered pair** `(x, y)`:
- the first number, the **x-coordinate**, gives horizontal position (right is positive, left negative);
- the second number, the **y-coordinate**, gives vertical position (up is positive, down negative).

Order matters: `(3, 1)` and `(1, 3)` are different points. To **plot** `(x, y)`, move along the x-axis to x, then vertically to y, and mark the intersection. To **read** a point off a graph, find the value directly below/above it on the x-axis and the value directly left/right on the y-axis.

## Quadrants
The axes divide the plane into four **quadrants**, numbered with Roman numerals **counterclockwise starting from the upper right**:

| Quadrant | Sign pattern `(x, y)` |
|---|---|
| I  | `(+, +)` |
| II | `(−, +)` |
| III| `(−, −)` |
| IV | `(+, −)` |

Points **on an axis lie in no quadrant**: a point on the x-axis has form `(a, 0)`, a point on the y-axis has form `(0, b)`, and the origin is `(0, 0)`.

## Ordered pairs as solutions
An ordered pair is a **solution** of a linear equation in two variables when substituting its x- and y-values makes the equation a true statement. This links the geometry of the plane to the algebra of [[Equation|equations]]: the set of all solutions of `Ax + By = C` forms the line graphed in this system.
