## Definition
**Beta decay** is a [[RadioactiveDecay|radioactive decay]] mode mediated by the weak nuclear force in which a nucleon changes type and a beta particle (an electron or positron) is emitted along with a neutrino. The mass number A is unchanged; the atomic number Z shifts by ±1.

## Key Points
- **Beta-minus (β⁻):** a neutron → proton + electron + electron antineutrino; Z increases by 1. Typical of neutron-rich nuclei.
- **Beta-plus (β⁺):** a proton → neutron + positron + electron neutrino; Z decreases by 1. The positron later annihilates an electron: e⁺ + e⁻ → γ + γ.
- **Electron capture (EC):** an inner-shell electron is absorbed (p + e⁻ → n + νₑ), giving the same result as β⁺.
- The **neutrino** was postulated by Pauli (1930) to conserve energy, momentum, and angular momentum (the beta energy spectrum is continuous); it interacts only via the weak force and was detected in 1953. Chien-Shiung Wu confirmed Fermi's weak-force theory.
- Conservation of **electron family number** is required: e.g., in β⁻ the electron (+1) is balanced by the antineutrino (−1).

## Equations
- β⁻: $^{A}_{Z}X_{N} \rightarrow\, ^{A}_{Z+1}Y_{N-1} + \beta^{-} + \bar{\nu}_e$ (example: ⁶⁰Co → ⁶⁰Ni + β⁻ + ν̄ₑ)
- β⁺: $^{A}_{Z}X_{N} \rightarrow\, ^{A}_{Z-1}Y_{N+1} + \beta^{+} + \nu_e$ (example: ²²Na → ²²Ne + β⁺ + νₑ)
- EC: $^{A}_{Z}X_{N} + e^{-} \rightarrow\, ^{A}_{Z-1}Y_{N+1} + \nu_e$
- Using neutral-atom masses: β⁻ ⇒ Δm = m(parent) − m(daughter); β⁺ ⇒ Δm = m(parent) − [m(daughter) + 2mₑ]
