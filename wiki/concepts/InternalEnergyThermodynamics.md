# Internal Energy (U)

The **internal energy (U)**, sometimes symbolized **E**, is the total of all possible kinds of energy present in a substance — including the kinetic energy of translation, vibration, and rotation of its atoms and molecules, and the potential energy of their interactions. Energy is stored as these particle motions increase and released as they decrease.

## State Function
Internal energy is a **state function**: its value depends only on the current state of the system, not on the path taken to reach that state. [[HeatThermodynamics|Heat]] (q) and work (w), by contrast, are *not* state functions — their values depend on the path. (Analogy: altitude at a mountain summit is fixed regardless of route, but distance walked is not.)

## First Law Relationship
Changes in internal energy obey the [[FirstLawOfThermodynamics|first law of thermodynamics]]:

**ΔU = q + w**

where q is heat absorbed by the system (positive in, negative out) and w is work done on the system (positive on, negative by). Internal energy is also the basis of [[EnthalpyChemistry|enthalpy]]: **H = U + PV**.
