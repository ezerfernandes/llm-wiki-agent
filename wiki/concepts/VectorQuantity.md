## Definition
In physics, a vector quantity is a physical measurement that has both magnitude and direction. Examples include [[Displacement]], [[Velocity]] (e.g., 90 km/h east), [[Acceleration]], and force (e.g., 500 N downward). Vectors are drawn as arrows whose length is proportional to magnitude.

## Key Points
- Contrast with a [[ScalarQuantity]] (magnitude only).
- In one-dimensional motion, a vector's direction is captured by a positive or negative sign within a chosen [[ReferenceFrame]].
- Coordinate conventions: right/up = positive, left/down = negative (flexible, but must stay consistent within a problem).
- Two- and three-dimensional vector addition is treated in later chapters.
