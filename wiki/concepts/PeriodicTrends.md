# Periodic Trends

**Periodic trends** are the regular variations in element properties across periods and down groups of the [[PeriodicTable|periodic table]], driven by [[ElectronConfiguration|electron configuration]] and **effective nuclear charge**.

## Effective Nuclear Charge (Z_eff)
The net nuclear pull felt by a given electron, reduced by shielding from other electrons:

> **Z_eff = Z − shielding**

For hydrogen Z_eff = Z; for all other atoms, inner (core) electrons shield outer ones, and core electrons shield more effectively than valence electrons in the same shell. Across a period Z_eff rises (Z grows faster than shielding); down a group the valence shell's n increases.

## Atomic (Covalent) Radius
Covalent radius = half the distance between nuclei of two bonded identical atoms.
- **Down a group:** increases (higher n, outer electrons farther out).
- **Across a period:** decreases (rising Z_eff pulls electrons in).
Halogens: F 64 < Cl 99 < Br 114 < I 133 < At 148 pm.

## Ionic Radius
- **Cations** are smaller than the parent atom (fewer electrons, higher Z_eff per electron); higher charge ⇒ smaller (V²⁺ 79 pm > V³⁺ 64 pm).
- **Anions** are larger (added electrons increase repulsion); S 104 pm → S²⁻ 170 pm.
- **Isoelectronic series** (same configuration, e.g. N³⁻, O²⁻, F⁻, Ne, Na⁺, Mg²⁺, Al³⁺): more protons ⇒ smaller radius.

## Ionization Energy and Electron Affinity
These two trends (covered in detail at [[IonizationEnergy]] and [[ElectronAffinity]]) follow Z_eff: both ionization energy and the magnitude of electron affinity generally **increase across a period** and **decrease down a group**, opposite to atomic radius — with exceptions at filled/half-filled subshells.

## Metallic Character
Metallic character (conductivity, malleability) tracks the ease of losing electrons: it **increases down a group** and **decreases across a period**, following atomic size. This trend organizes the descriptive chemistry of the [[RepresentativeElements|representative elements]]: the most metallic, most easily oxidized elements are the [[AlkaliMetals|alkali metals]] (bottom-left), grading through the [[Metalloids|metalloids]] to the strongly oxidizing [[Nonmetals|nonmetals]] (top-right, where fluorine and oxygen are the strongest oxidizers).

## Transition-series trends (Ch 19)

Down a [[TransitionMetals|transition metal]] group the larger atomic radius makes electron removal easier, so **second- and third-series metals are stable in higher oxidation states than the first series** (Mo, W favor 6+ vs Cr's 3+). Across the first series the highest oxidation state for Sc→Mn equals the loss of all valence s + d electrons. Higher metal charge also gives a smaller ionic radius (V²⁺ 79 pm > V³⁺ 64 pm above), which in turn raises the crystal field splitting (see [[CrystalFieldTheory]]).
