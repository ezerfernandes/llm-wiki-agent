## Definition
Resonance is the large-amplitude response of an oscillator driven by a periodic external force at or near its natural frequency f₀ — the frequency the system would oscillate at with no driving and no damping.

## Key Points
- Energy transfers into the system most efficiently when the driving frequency matches the natural frequency.
- Damping shapes the resonance curve: less damping gives a taller, narrower, more frequency-selective peak; more damping gives a lower, broader response. All curves peak at the same f₀.
- A driving frequency is the rate of the external periodic force and need not match f₀.
- Constructive build-up over many cycles is how small periodic pushes (e.g., a swing) produce large amplitude.

## Equations
- No simple closed-form amplitude formula; amplitude is a peaked function of driving frequency, maximized at f₀.

## Applications
- Radio/TV tuning, MRI (radio waves resonating hydrogen nuclei), pushing a swing, suspension design, and destructive structural resonance (e.g., the 1940 Tacoma Narrows Bridge).
