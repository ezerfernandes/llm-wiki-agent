## Definition
Wave intensity (I) is the rate at which a [[Wave]] transports energy through a unit area oriented perpendicular to propagation. It is power per unit area, measured in watts per square meter (W/m²).

## Key Points
- Intensity is proportional to amplitude squared (energy ∝ X²), so doubling amplitude quadruples intensity.
- Constructive [[WaveInterference]] of two equal waves doubles amplitude and quadruples intensity; energy is conserved by redistribution, with destructive zones carrying none.
- Spreading the same power over a larger area lowers intensity (and concentrating it raises intensity), explaining why distant earthquakes do less damage and why lenses can intensify sunlight.

## Equations
- I = P / A
- Energy: E = I A t
- Concentration ratio (constant power): I'/I = A/A'

## Applications
- Solar flux (~1300 W/m² above atmosphere, ~700 W/m² at surface), sound levels (90 dB ≈ 10⁻³ W/m²), concentrated sunlight (~140,000 W/m²), therapeutic ultrasound heating.
