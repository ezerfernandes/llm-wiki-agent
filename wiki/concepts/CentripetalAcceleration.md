## Definition
Centripetal acceleration (a_c) is the acceleration of an object in [[UniformCircularMotion]], always directed toward the center of the circular path. "Centripetal" means center-seeking.

## Key Points
- Even at constant speed, the continuous change in velocity *direction* is an [[Acceleration]] — magnitude, direction, or both can change velocity.
- The magnitude is derived from the similar triangles formed by the velocity vectors and the radius vectors (Δv/v = Δs/r).
- a_c grows with the square of speed and inversely with radius: tighter, faster turns mean larger acceleration.
- Centrifuges exploit large a_c (an ultracentrifuge can reach hundreds of thousands of g) to separate materials by density.

## Equations
- a_c = v² / r
- a_c = r ω²
- (with v = r ω linking the two forms)
- Derivation relation: Δv / v = Δs / r
