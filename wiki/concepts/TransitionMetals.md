# Transition Metals

**Transition metals** are "those elements that have (or readily form) **partially filled d orbitals**." They occupy the **d-block** (groups 3–11) of the [[PeriodicTable|periodic table]]. The **f-block** inner transition metals — the **lanthanides** (Ce–Lu) and **actinides** (Th–Lr) — also qualify, because the d orbital is partially occupied before the f orbitals fill. Group 12 (Zn, Cd, Hg) is *technically not* transition metal but is conventionally discussed alongside them due to shared chemistry. (Chemistry sense — distinct from any ML/systems use of "transition.")

## Series

- **First transition series:** Sc → Cu (3d)
- **Second transition series:** Y → Ag (4d)
- **Third transition series:** La, Hf → Au (5d)
- **Fourth transition series:** Ac, Rf → Rg (6d)
- **Lanthanide (lanthanoid) series:** Ce → Lu; **Actinide (actinoid) series:** Th → Lr

## Electron configurations

When forming **ions**, transition and inner-transition metals **remove ns electrons before (n−1)d or (n−2)f electrons**:
- Ti²⁺ = [Ar]3d² · Pd²⁺ = [Kr]4d⁸ · Ce³⁺ = [Xe]4f¹ · Am³⁺ = [Rn]5f⁶
- Ions with **no d electrons**: V⁵⁺, Sc³⁺, Ti⁴⁺, Cr⁶⁺, Mn⁷⁺.

## General properties

Almost all are hard, high-melting solids that conduct heat and electricity well, readily form alloys, and lose electrons to form stable cations. They form a wide variety of stable [[CoordinationCompound|coordination compounds]] in which the central metal ion acts as a [[LewisAcid|Lewis acid]]. Reactivity spans a huge range: lanthanides all form stable 3+ aqueous cations, lighter d-block ions (Cr³⁺, Fe³⁺, Co²⁺) give stable colorful hydrated ions, while Pt and Au strongly resist oxidation. The **platinum metals** (Ru, Os, Rh, Ir, Pd, Pt) form simple cations only with difficulty and form no stable oxyanions.

## Variable oxidation states

A defining trait: most transition metals show **multiple oxidation states** (see [[PeriodicTrends]] for the radius trend driving this). For Sc→Mn (first half of the first series) the **highest state = loss of all valence s + d electrons** (e.g. Ti⁴⁺ loses 4s² 3d²). Iron ranges 2+ to 6+, with Fe²⁺/Fe³⁺ most common. Second- and third-series metals are more stable in **higher** oxidation states than the first series (Mo, W favor 6+; Cr favors 3+) because larger atomic radii ease electron removal. Their colored compounds arise from d–d transitions (see [[ComplexColorAndMagnetism]]).

## Compound chemistry (brief)

Bonding ranges ionic → covalent with oxidation state: low states give ionic, high-melting halides/oxides (basic oxides); high states give covalent, volatile, often acidic species (TiCl₄ volatile; CrO₃, Mn₂O₇ acidic, forming H₂CrO₄, HMnO₄). Intermediate oxides are amphoteric.
