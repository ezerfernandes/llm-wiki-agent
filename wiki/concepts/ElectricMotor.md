## Definition
An electric motor converts electrical energy into mechanical energy using the [[Torque]] exerted by a [[MagneticField]] on a current-carrying loop (coil). The same torque principle, with a calibrated restoring spring, underlies analog meters (ammeters, fuel gauges).

## Key Points
- Magnetic [[MagneticForce|forces]] on the loop's segments parallel to the rotation axis produce no torque; the segments perpendicular to the axis feel equal-and-opposite forces that create rotation.
- Torque is maximum when the loop's normal is perpendicular to the field (θ = 90°) and zero when aligned (θ = 0°).
- Past θ = 0° the torque would reverse, so **brushes** periodically reverse the current to sustain continuous rotation in one direction.
- Multiple turns multiply the torque (factor N).
- Analog meters use shaped magnets to keep the field perpendicular over a wide angle, making needle deflection proportional to current.

## Equations
- τ = NIAB sin θ (N turns, current I, loop area A, field B, angle θ between loop normal and B)
- Maximum torque: τ_max = NIAB
