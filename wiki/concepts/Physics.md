## Definition
**Physics** is the natural science that studies the fundamental interactions of energy, matter, space, and time. It seeks the underlying mechanisms behind phenomena across every scale — from subatomic particles to the cosmos — and expresses them through mathematical principles.

## Key Points
- Physical laws are **universal**: the same forces that govern stars, nebulae, and supernovae also govern everyday objects on Earth, and stars are made of the same chemical elements (hydrogen, oxygen, sulfur) as terrestrial matter.
- Nature exhibits an **underlying simplicity** — a small number of unified laws explain an enormous range of observations.
- Physics is the foundation of all technology, from Stone Age tools to Information Age electronics (e.g., the MOSFET transistor, invented 1959).
- It grew out of natural philosophy; **classical physics** (Renaissance–19th century) is distinguished from **modern physics** (20th century onward), which adds relativity and quantum mechanics for extreme speeds and very small scales.
- Knowledge is built via the [[ScientificMethod]] and organized using a precise vocabulary — see [[ScientificModelsTheoriesLaws]].
