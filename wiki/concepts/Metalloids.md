# Metalloids (Boron, Silicon, and the Semimetals)

The **metalloids** — boron, silicon, germanium, arsenic, antimony, and tellurium — lie along the metal/nonmetal boundary of the [[PeriodicTable|periodic table]]. They are **semiconductors**: their electrons are more tightly bound than in metallic conductors. They form covalent crystals like [[Nonmetals|nonmetals]] but generally do not form monatomic anions. (This is the chemistry concept of a semiconductor element; the wiki's hardware [[Transistor]] page is a separate computer-architecture topic.)

## Boron
Configuration 2s²2p¹ leaves one fewer valence electron than valence orbital (electron deficiency); usual state +3, but boron hydrides show fractional states. Pure crystalline boron is built from B₁₂ icosahedra. It is inert at room temperature (reacts only with F₂, O₂ → BF₃, B₂O₃). Production: B₂O₃(s) + 3Mg(s) → 2B(s) + 3MgO(s). **Boric acid** B(OH)₃ is a [[LewisAcid|Lewis acid]] (not a proton donor): B(OH)₃ + 2H₂O ⇌ B(OH)₄⁻ + H₃O⁺ (Ka = 5.8 × 10⁻¹⁰). **Borax** Na₂[B₄O₅(OH)₄]·8H₂O is the most important borate (laundry detergent); **boric oxide** B₂O₃ is used in borosilicate glass.

## Silicon
About one-fourth of Earth's crust by mass (second to oxygen); forms Si–O–Si chains, sheets, and frameworks dominating the mineral world. Usually sp³ tetrahedral, +4; crystallizes (with Ge) in the diamond structure. Prepared by SiO₂(s) + 2C(s) → Si(s) + 2CO(g). For electronics, ultrapure silicon (≤1 impurity part per million) is made via a silicon tetrahalide or **silane (SiH₄)** then **zone refining** (a molten zone sweeps impurities to one end) — the key element of the computer industry (transistors, chips, solar cells).

**Silica (SiO₂)** is a **covalent network solid** — each Si bonded to four O by single bonds in corner-sharing SiO₄ tetrahedra, so a quartz crystal is one macromolecule. This contrasts with molecular CO₂ (discrete O=C=O), because period-3 silicon forms only weak π bonds; SiO₂ has only σ bonds (a key idea also in [[CarbonGroupChemistry]]). HF is the only common acid that attacks SiO₂/silicates (SiO₂ + 4HF → SiF₄ + 2H₂O), so other acids can be stored in glass. **Silicates** are salts of Si–O anions; many ceramics are silicates.

## Germanium, Arsenic, Antimony, Tellurium
- Germanium: chemically very similar to silicon.
- Arsenic and antimony: usually +3 or +5 (As can be −3 in arsenides); puckered-layer (graphite-like) structures. Sb forms trihalides/pentahalides (SbF₅, SbCl₅) and Sb₂S₃/Sb₂S₅.
- Tellurium: tellurides (Te²⁻) most stable; usually +2 or +4 (TeF₆ exists but Te resists the maximum group state); infinite spiral chains.
