## Definition
A **particle detector** records the products of high-energy collisions in a [[ParticleAccelerator|particle accelerator]], measuring the mass, charge, momentum, and tracks of the particle "spray" so that newly created particles can be identified.

## Key Points
- After a collision creates matter from energy, the short-lived particles produced are inferred from their tracks, energy deposits, and decay signatures rather than observed directly.
- Charge and momentum follow from how tracks curve in a magnetic field; mass and lifetime follow from decay patterns and which [[ConservationLawsParticlePhysics|conservation laws]] are satisfied.
- Characteristic three-**jet** patterns in collider events are evidence of three confined [[Quark|quarks]] fragmenting into observable hadrons.
- Large dedicated detectors also search for rare events such as the GUT-predicted [[GrandUnifiedTheory|proton decay]] p → π⁰ + e⁺.
- This is the particle-physics analog of the radiation detectors used in nuclear physics (see [[college-physics-2e-ch31]]).
