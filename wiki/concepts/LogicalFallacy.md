# Logical Fallacy

A **fallacy is a mistake in reasoning.** [[logic-text-v2|Van Cleave]] Ch 4 splits fallacies into two kinds by *how you detect them*.

## Formal fallacies — invalid in virtue of form
A **formal fallacy** is an argument whose **form** is invalid, so *any* argument of that form is invalid **regardless of content** — you can spot it without understanding the words (the "Jabberwocky" test). The two that mimic the valid conditional [[RulesOfInference|rules of inference]]:

| Fallacy | Form | Mimics (but is NOT) |
|---|---|---|
| **Denying the antecedent** | `p ⊃ q`, `~p` ∴ `~q` | modus tollens |
| **Affirming the consequent** | `p ⊃ q`, `q` ∴ `p` | modus ponens |

The probabilistic fallacies of Ch 3 — the [[ConjunctionFallacy|conjunction fallacy]] and [[BaseRateFallacy|base-rate fallacy]] — are also formal in the sense that they violate the rules of [[Probability|probability]].

## Informal fallacies — content-dependent
An **informal fallacy** *cannot* be identified without understanding the concepts in the argument; two arguments can share a form while only one is fallacious (every part of the car is plastic ∴ the car is plastic — fine; every gymnast weighs < 110 lb ∴ the team weighs < 110 lb — fallacious). Van Cleave's catalog:

- **Composition** — what's true of each part is inferred true of the whole.
- **Division** — what's true of the whole is inferred true of each part.
- **Begging the question** — the conclusion is assumed in the premises (circular).
- **False dichotomy** — only two options presented when more exist.
- **Equivocation** — a key term shifts meaning between premises.
- **Slippery slope** — *conceptual* (no sharp line ⇒ no difference) and *causal* (one step inevitably triggers a chain).
- **Fallacies of relevance** — premises that don't bear on the conclusion: **ad hominem**, **straw man**, **tu quoque**, **genetic**, **appeal to consequences**, **appeal to (illegitimate) authority**.

A central lesson: the book deliberately shows **why each fallacy is seductive** (a debt to [[DanielKahneman|Kahneman]]) instead of making them sound too obvious to commit.
