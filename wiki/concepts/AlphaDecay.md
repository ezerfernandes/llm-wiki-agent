## Definition
**Alpha decay** is a [[RadioactiveDecay|radioactive decay]] mode in which a heavy nucleus ejects an **alpha particle** — a ⁴He nucleus (2 protons + 2 neutrons). The proton number drops by 2 and the mass number by 4.

## Key Points
- Most common in heavy nuclei, where Coulomb repulsion strains nuclear stability.
- Charge and nucleon number are conserved; the light alpha particle carries away most of the released kinetic energy.
- Alpha particles are highly ionizing but least penetrating (~mm in tissue), so they are dangerous mainly when ingested/inhaled.
- The alpha particle's exceptional stability (high [[NuclearBindingEnergy|BE/A]], ~7.07 MeV/nucleon, closed shell with Z = N) makes its emission energetically favorable.
- Mechanism: the alpha particle is ~8 MeV short of the classical escape energy yet escapes by [[QuantumTunneling|quantum tunneling]] through the Coulomb barrier (Gamow, 1928). More energetic decays imply a thinner barrier and shorter [[HalfLife|half-life]].

## Equations
- General: $^{A}_{Z}X_{N} \rightarrow\, ^{A-4}_{Z-2}Y_{N-2} +\, ^{4}_{2}He_{2}$
- Examples: ²³⁸U → ²³⁴Th + ⁴He; ²³⁹Pu → ²³⁵U + ⁴He
- Decay energy: Q = (Δm)c²
