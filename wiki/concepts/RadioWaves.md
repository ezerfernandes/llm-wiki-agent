## Definition
Radio (and TV) waves are the lowest-frequency, longest-wavelength band of the [[ElectromagneticSpectrum]], roughly 50 Hz to ~1000 MHz. They are produced by accelerating charges in circuits and conductors (antennas).

## Key Points
- Longest wavelengths of the spectrum — kilometers for power-line frequencies.
- Subdivided into AM (540–1600 kHz), FM (88–108 MHz), and VHF/UHF television bands.
- Very long wavelengths penetrate salt water, enabling submarine communication; long wavelengths also diffract around obstacles, so antennas must be large.
- **AM** (amplitude modulation): the audio signal varies the wave's amplitude at constant frequency.
- **FM** (frequency modulation): the audio signal varies the frequency at constant amplitude — inherently less susceptible to noise.

## Equations
- `c = f λ`: e.g., AM at 1530 kHz → λ ≈ 196 m.

## Applications
Broadcasting and communications, remote controls, MRI (~100 MHz), and long-distance power transmission.
