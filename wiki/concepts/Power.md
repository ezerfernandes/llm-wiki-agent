## Definition
**Power** is the rate at which [[Work]] is done or energy is transferred or transformed: P = W/t. Its SI unit is the watt (W), equal to one joule per second. Greater power means more energy delivered in less time — a powerful engine burns fuel quickly; a 60-watt bulb dissipates 60 joules each second.

## Key Points
- Because work is energy transfer, power equally describes the rate of energy use.
- Mechanical power output can be found from the kinetic and gravitational potential energy gained over time (e.g., climbing stairs, where gravitational work usually dominates).
- Commercial electrical energy is billed in kilowatt-hours (kW·h): power in kilowatts × time in hours.
- Real machines lose power to waste heat; see [[EnergyEfficiency]].

## Equations
- P = W / t
- E = P t  (energy consumed)
- P = (½ m v_f² + m g h) / t  (from mechanical energy change)
- 1 W = 1 J/s; energy (kW·h) = power (kW) × time (h)
