## Definition
Kepler's three laws are empirical rules describing the orbits of planets (and satellites generally) around a much more massive parent body. Newton later showed they follow from his [[NewtonsLawOfUniversalGravitation|law of universal gravitation]].

## Key Points
- **First law (ellipses):** each planet moves in an ellipse with the Sun at one focus. A circle is the special case where the two foci coincide.
- **Second law (equal areas):** the line from the Sun to a planet sweeps equal areas in equal times, so a body moves fastest at closest approach and slowest when farthest.
- **Third law (harmonic law):** for bodies orbiting the same parent, the square of the period is proportional to the cube of the average orbital radius; T²/r³ is the same constant for all of them.
- For circular orbits the third law follows from setting gravity equal to the required [[CentripetalForce]] (see [[OrbitalMechanics]]).
- Kepler's laws are descriptive; Newton's gravitation supplies the cause — an "argument for the simplicity of nature."

## Equations
- Comparative third law: T₁² / T₂² = r₁³ / r₂³
- Derived form: T² = (4π² / (G M)) r³
- Mass from orbit: r³ / T² = G M / (4π²)
