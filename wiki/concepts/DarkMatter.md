## Definition
Dark matter is non-luminous matter that emits and reflects no detectable light but reveals itself through its gravitational effects. It is estimated to make up roughly 90% of the universe's total mass, yet its composition remains unidentified.

## Key Points
- **Galaxy rotation problem**: stars in the outer regions of galaxies orbit far faster than the visible mass can explain. The flatness of rotation curves implies massive invisible halos surrounding galaxies.
- Historically inferred from Fritz Zwicky's 1930s cluster measurements and Vera Rubin's later galaxy-rotation studies, both showing mass-to-luminosity discrepancies.
- Detected indirectly via **gravitational lensing** ([[GeneralRelativity]]) and microlensing brightness fluctuations, and via X-ray emission from intergalactic ionized gas.
- Candidate forms: **MACHOs** (Massive Compact Halo Objects — planet/star-scale bodies) and **WIMPs** (Weakly Interacting Massive Particles such as neutralinos and axions). Neutrinos, now known to have mass, contribute a small amount.
- The amount of dark matter helps determine cosmic geometry and whether the universe is open, closed, or flat (cosmic closure).

## Equations
- Critical density: ρ_c ≈ 10⁻²⁶ kg/m³
- Luminous matter ≈ 0.5%–2% of ρ_c; dark + normal matter ≈ 10%–40% of ρ_c
