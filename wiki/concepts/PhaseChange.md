## Definition
A **phase change** is a transition between the solid, liquid, and gas states of matter. Which phase
exists at given conditions is read from a **phase diagram** (a pressure–temperature plot), whose
boundary lines mark the conditions where two phases coexist in equilibrium.

## Key Points
- **PV diagrams**: for an ideal gas, constant-temperature curves (**isotherms**) are hyperbolas
  (PV = constant). At low temperature, attractions and finite molecular volume distort them and a
  liquid region appears.
- **Triple point**: the unique pressure and temperature where solid, liquid, and gas coexist (water:
  273.16 K, 6.10×10² Pa ≈ 0.006 atm).
- **Critical point**: the end of the liquid–gas boundary. Above the **critical temperature** no
  liquid exists at any pressure (water: 647.4 K / 374.3 °C, 22.12×10⁶ Pa).
- **Sublimation**: direct solid→gas transition (no liquid), as for dry ice (solid CO₂) at 1 atm.
- Phase-boundary behavior: higher pressure lowers ice's melting point (why snowballs pack); the
  liquid–gas line ending at the critical point is why pressure cookers raise water's boiling point.
- **Dalton's law of partial pressures**: total [[Pressure|pressure]] of a gas mixture = sum of the
  partial pressures of its components, following from [[KineticTheoryOfGases|kinetic theory]].
- **Vapor pressure** (see [[VaporPressure]]) sets liquid–gas equilibrium; a liquid boils when its
  vapor pressure equals the ambient pressure.

## Equations
- Ideal gas (fixed N, T): PV = NkT; PV = constant (hyperbolic isotherm)
- Dalton's law: P_total = P₁ + P₂ + P₃ + …
