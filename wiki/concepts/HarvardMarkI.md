# Harvard Mark I

Large **electromechanical** computer designed by [[HowardAiken|Howard Aiken]] at [[HarvardUniversity|Harvard]] in collaboration with [[IBM]]; completed in 1944. Originally named the *IBM Automatic Sequence Controlled Calculator (ASCC)*.

## Physical characteristics

- ~51 feet long, ~5 tons
- Built from electromechanical relays, mechanical counters, and rotating shafts
- Programmed via **paper tape**
- **Decimal** representation
- Performed an addition in ~0.3 seconds; multiplication in ~6 seconds — orders of magnitude slower than fully electronic [[ENIAC]]

## Significance

Per [[dis-5-1-history|*Dive into Systems* Ch 5.1]], the Mark I represents the **electromechanical** branch of the 1930s–1940s computing convergence — slower than the fully electronic [[ENIAC]] and [[Colossus]], but practically operational before either and used for [[ManhattanProject|atomic-bomb]] calculations.

[[GraceHopper|Grace Hopper]] worked on the Mark I as a Navy lieutenant; the famous *"first computer bug"* anecdote — a moth taped into the operations log — actually involved the **Harvard Mark II** (1947), not the Mark I.

The Mark I lineage continued with the **Mark II, III, and IV**. It also gives its name to the **[[HarvardArchitecture|Harvard architecture]]** — the alternative to the [[VonNeumannArchitecture|von Neumann architecture]] in which instruction and data memories are physically separated.
