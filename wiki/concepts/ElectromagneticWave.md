## Definition
An electromagnetic wave is a self-propagating, transverse disturbance of oscillating [[ElectricField|electric]] and [[MagneticField|magnetic]] fields. Unlike a [[Wave|mechanical wave]] (e.g., sound), it requires no medium and travels through vacuum at the [[SpeedOfLightPhysics|speed of light]]. Its existence is predicted by [[MaxwellsEquations]].

## Key Points
- **Production**: an AC source drives charge to accelerate along an antenna; the accelerating charge radiates a propagating E field, and the antenna current (via [[AmperesLaw]]) produces circling B fields. Both move outward at c.
- **In phase**: E and B reach their maxima and minima together.
- **Orthogonal & transverse**: E ⊥ B, and both are perpendicular to the propagation direction.
- **Reception**: an incoming wave accelerates electrons in a receiving antenna into a standing-wave pattern; resonance selects the tuned frequency.
- The magnetic field magnitude is numerically tiny relative to the electric field because of the large factor c (e.g., E = 1000 V/m gives B ≈ 3.3×10⁻⁶ T).
- All bands of the [[ElectromagneticSpectrum]] are the same phenomenon at different frequencies.

## Equations
- Field ratio (vacuum): `E / B = c`, so `B = E / c`
- In a medium of refractive index n: `E / B = c / n`
- Wave relationship: `c = f λ`
- Intensity (see [[ElectromagneticSpectrum]] / energy): `I_ave = c ε₀ E₀² / 2 = c B₀² / (2μ₀) = E₀ B₀ / (2μ₀)`
