# Nuclear Fission (Chemistry)

> Disambiguation: this is the OpenStax *Chemistry 2e* (Ch 21) treatment. For the College Physics 2e treatments see [[NuclearFission]] and [[NuclearReactor]].

**Fission** is the breaking of a large nucleus into smaller pieces. A heavy nucleus with low [[NuclearBindingEnergyChemistry|binding energy per nucleon]] splits into intermediate-mass nuclei of higher binding energy per nucleon, releasing neutrons and enormous energy. First observed in 1939 (Meitner, Hahn, Strassmann) by bombarding uranium-235 with slow neutrons:

$$^{235}_{92}\text{U} + ^{1}_{0}\text{n} \rightarrow ^{236}_{92}\text{U} \rightarrow ^{141}_{56}\text{Ba} + ^{92}_{36}\text{Kr} + 3\,^{1}_{0}\text{n}$$

Many product channels exist (e.g. → Sr-90 + Xe-144 + 2n; → Br-87 + La-146 + 3n); fission products cluster around mass numbers 85–105 and 130–150.

### Energy
Fissioning one mole of U-235 loses ~0.2 g of mass, releasing ~**1.8 × 10¹⁰ kJ** (via E = mc²). Burning 1 kg of U-235 gives ~2.5 million times the energy of burning 1 kg of coal.

### Chain reaction and critical mass
Each fission emits 2–3 neutrons that can trigger further fissions — a **chain reaction**, sustained when the neutrons produced equal or exceed those absorbed plus those that escape. The **critical mass** is the minimum amount of **fissile (fissionable)** material that sustains the chain; below it is subcritical, above it supercritical. Critical mass depends on the material, purity, temperature, shape, and neutron control. **Atomic bombs** rapidly assemble a supercritical mass of U-235 (gun-type: fire two subcritical masses together) or Pu-239 (implosion: compress a hollow sphere).

### Nuclear reactors
A reactor sustains fission *without* explosion; the heat boils water to drive steam turbines and generators. Five required components:
1. **Nuclear fuel** — fissile material (natural U is 99.3% U-238, 0.7% U-235; reactors enrich to ~5% U-235, too little to explode). Enrichment by gaseous diffusion (lighter ²³⁵UF₆ diffuses faster than ²³⁸UF₆), gas centrifuge, or laser; fuel is ceramic UO₂ pellets.
2. **Moderator** — slows neutrons to fission-inducing speeds (graphite, light water H₂O, or heavy water ²H₂O).
3. **Coolant** — carries heat to the boiler/turbine (water in US reactors; also molten sodium, lead, molten salts).
4. **Control rods** — neutron absorbers (boron, cadmium, hafnium), e.g. $^{10}_{5}\text{B} + ^{1}_{0}\text{n} \rightarrow ^{7}_{3}\text{Li} + ^{4}_{2}\text{He}$; inserting them slows or stops the reaction.
5. **Shield / containment** — steel reactor vessel (3–20 cm), 1–3 m high-density concrete shield, personnel shield, often a steel/concrete dome.

Only ~0.1% of fuel mass converts to energy; spent fuel (fission products, transuranium elements, unreacted U) is intensely radioactive and needs millennia to decay to safety.

Fission contrasts with [[NuclearFusionChemistry|fusion]], which joins light nuclei.
