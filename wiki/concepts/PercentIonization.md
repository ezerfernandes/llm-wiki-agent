# Percent Ionization

**Percent ionization** measures how completely a weak acid (or base) ionizes in a given solution — the fraction of the dissolved acid that has actually transferred its proton, expressed as a percentage. For a weak acid HA:

$$\%\ \text{ionization} = \frac{[\mathrm{H_3O^+}]_{eq}}{[\mathrm{HA}]_0} \times 100$$

where [H₃O⁺]eq (= [A⁻]) is the equilibrium hydronium concentration and [HA]₀ is the initial acid concentration. For a weak base, $\%\ \text{ionization} = ([\mathrm{OH^-}]_{eq}/[\mathrm{B}]_0)\times100$.

## Concentration dependence (vs Ka)

Unlike the [[AcidIonizationConstant|acid ionization constant Ka]], which is fixed at a given temperature, **percent ionization varies with the initial concentration** — it typically *decreases* as the acid becomes more concentrated (more dilute weak acids are proportionally more ionized). This is why percent ionization and Ka give complementary, not interchangeable, descriptions of strength: Ka ranks intrinsic strength, while percent ionization reports the situation in a specific solution.
