## Definition
**Cohesive forces** are the attractions between molecules of the *same* substance; they hold a liquid together and let it form drops. **Adhesive forces** are the attractions between molecules of *different* substances; they make a liquid cling to other surfaces (water on glass). The relative strength of these two forces governs surface and interface behavior in liquids.

## Key Points
- Cohesion produces [[SurfaceTension]] — surface molecules are pulled inward because they are attracted only by like molecules below and beside them.
- The balance of cohesion vs. adhesion sets the **contact angle** θ at a solid–liquid boundary and whether a liquid wets a surface (adhesion wins) or beads up (cohesion wins).
- Together they drive [[CapillaryAction]]: water rises in glass (adhesion dominant), mercury is depressed (cohesion dominant).
- Everyday effects: water beading on a waxed car (weak adhesion), liquids spreading on clean glass (strong adhesion).
