# Number Line

A **number line** is a straight line with a chosen point marked **0** (the origin) and the numbers placed at equal intervals along it. It is the organizing picture for the [[Integer|integers]] and for signed numbers generally, introduced in OpenStax [[Prealgebra]] 2e Chapter 3 ([[prealgebra-2e-ch03-integers]]).

## Layout
- **Positive numbers** extend to the **right** of zero.
- **Negative numbers** extend to the **left** of zero.
- **Zero** sits at the center and is neither positive nor negative.
- Values **increase** as you move left-to-right and **decrease** as you move right-to-left.

## What the number line defines
- **Ordering** — for two numbers, `a < b` exactly when `a` lies to the *left* of `b`, and `a > b` when `a` lies to the *right*. This is why `−5 < −1`: the more-negative number is farther left.
- **Opposites** — the opposite of a number is the point the **same distance from 0 but on the opposite side**; e.g. `3` and `−3` are mirror images across 0.
- **[[AbsoluteValue|Absolute value]]** — `|n|` is the **distance** of `n` from 0 on the number line, which is why it is never negative.
- **Plotting** — locating a given integer at its position is the most basic number-line skill in Ch 3.1.

The number line also models the arithmetic of [[SignedNumberArithmetic|signed numbers]]: addition can be visualized as moving right (for positives) or left (for negatives) from a starting point.
