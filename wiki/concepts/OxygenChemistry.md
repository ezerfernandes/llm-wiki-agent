# Oxygen Chemistry (Oxides, Peroxides, Superoxides, Hydroxides)

**Oxygen** is the most abundant element in Earth's crust (~50% of crust mass) and ~20% of air. It is a strong oxidizing agent and the lead element of the group 16 chalcogens (see [[SulfurChemistry]]).

## Allotropes and Preparation
- **Dioxygen (O₂)** — from fractional distillation of liquid air (bp 90 K) and electrolysis of water.
- **Ozone (O₃)** — 3O₂(g) → 2O₃(g) (electric discharge, ΔH° = 287 kJ); stratospheric O₃ absorbs UV; a bleaching agent and disinfectant.

Oxygen reacts with most elements (not noble gases, halogens, a few noble transition metals): 2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO; P₄ + 5O₂ → P₄O₁₀. Only fluorine can oxidize oxides back to O₂.

## Three Classes of Binary Oxygen Compounds
- **Oxides (O²⁻):** made by heating hydroxides/carbonates (CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂); O²⁻ is a powerful H⁺ acceptor. Most react with acids; groups 1–2 oxides react with water to **hydroxides** (Na₂O + H₂O → NaOH). Important oxides: CaO ("limelight"), Al₂O₃ (corundum, ruby, sapphire), ZnO (pigment, sunscreen), PbO₂ (lead battery).
- **Peroxides (O₂²⁻, O–O single bond):** groups 1–2 in pure O₂ — 2Na + O₂ → Na₂O₂. **Hydrogen peroxide H₂O₂** is a bleach/disinfectant.
- **Superoxides (O₂⁻, bond order 1½):** excess O₂ — K + O₂ → KO₂. Peroxide/superoxide stability increases with cation size.

## Hydroxides
Soluble (groups 1–2 except Be, Mg) from metal + water or oxide + water; most metal oxides are base anhydrides. Insoluble hydroxides by adding NaOH to salt solutions (Mg²⁺ + 2OH⁻ → Mg(OH)₂); avoid excess OH⁻ with amphoteric Al/Ga/Zn/Sn(II) hydroxides (they redissolve to Al(OH)₄⁻ etc.). Industrial: **NaOH** (chlor-alkali process, top-10 US chemical), **Ca(OH)₂**, **Mg(OH)₂** (milk of magnesia).
