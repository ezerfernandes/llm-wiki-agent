# Reversible Reaction

A **reversible reaction** is one that proceeds in **both** the forward and reverse directions, so reactants form products *and* products re-form reactants. By convention a [[ChemicalEquation|chemical equation]] is written with reactants on the left and products on the right, representing the forward direction; the reverse reaction runs the same equation right-to-left.

Reversible reactions are denoted with a **double arrow (⇌)** rather than a single arrow, to emphasize that both directions occur:

$$\mathrm{N_2O_4(g) \rightleftharpoons 2NO_2(g)}$$

## Why reversibility leads to equilibrium

Because both directions run simultaneously, their [[ReactionRate|rates]] eventually become equal — at which point the reaction has reached [[ChemicalEquilibrium|chemical equilibrium]]. A strictly one-way ("irreversible") reaction goes essentially to completion and is written with a single arrow; reversible reactions instead settle at an intermediate composition described by the [[EquilibriumConstant|equilibrium constant]].
