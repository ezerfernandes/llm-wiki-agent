# Dipole-Dipole Forces

A **dipole-dipole attraction** is "the electrostatic force between the partially positive end of one polar molecule and the partially negative end of another." It is one of the three [[IntermolecularForces|van der Waals forces]] — stronger than [[LondonDispersionForces|London dispersion forces]] but weaker than [[HydrogenBonding|hydrogen bonding]].

Only [[MolecularPolarity|polar molecules]] (those with a net [[DipoleMoment|dipole moment]]) experience dipole-dipole forces. In HCl, the more [[Electronegativity|electronegative]] Cl atom carries the δ– charge and H the δ+; the δ+ end of one molecule attracts the δ– end of another.

## Evidence from Boiling Points

Comparing molecules of similar molar mass (hence similar dispersion forces) isolates the dipole-dipole contribution:

- **HCl** (polar, ~36 amu) normal bp 188 K vs **F₂** (nonpolar, ~36 amu) normal bp 85 K.
- **CO** (polar, ~28 amu) has a higher boiling point than **N₂** (nonpolar, ~28 amu).

The polar molecule always boils higher, reflecting the extra attraction.
