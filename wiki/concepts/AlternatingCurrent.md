## Definition
**Alternating current (AC)** is [[ElectricCurrent|current]] whose direction periodically reverses, typically following a sinusoid: V = V₀ sin(2πft) and I = I₀ sin(2πft). It contrasts with [[DirectCurrent|direct current]], which flows one way. AC powers nearly all residential and commercial grids (120 V / 240 V rms at 50–60 Hz).

## Key Points
- In a purely resistive AC circuit, voltage and current stay in phase, and Ohm's law holds for rms values: I_rms = V_rms/R.
- Because the simple average of a sinusoid is zero, **root-mean-square (rms)** values are used: V_rms = V₀/√2 and I_rms = I₀/√2 (e.g., 120 V rms ↔ ~170 V peak).
- Average power is P_ave = ½I₀V₀ = I_rms·V_rms = V_rms²/R = I_rms²R.
- AC dominates power distribution because transformers can step voltage up/down efficiently; high-voltage transmission lowers current and thus I²R line losses ([[ElectricPower]]).
- Visible artifacts: fluorescent lamps flicker at twice the line frequency (~120 times/s on a 60 Hz grid).

## Equations
- V = V₀ sin(2πft)
- I = I₀ sin(2πft),  I₀ = V₀/R
- V_rms = V₀/√2;  I_rms = I₀/√2
- P_ave = ½ I₀V₀ = I_rms V_rms = V_rms²/R = I_rms² R
- I_rms = V_rms / R
