# Coordinate Covalent Bond

A **coordinate covalent bond** (also called a **dative bond**) is a covalent bond in which **one atom supplies both of the shared electrons** — in contrast to an ordinary [[CovalentBonding|covalent bond]], where each atom contributes one electron. Once formed, it is indistinguishable from any other covalent bond; the "coordinate" label only records the bond's origin.

It is the bond formed in every [[LewisAcidsAndBases|Lewis acid–base reaction]]: the [[LewisBase|Lewis base]] donates the electron pair and the [[LewisAcid|Lewis acid]] accepts it, producing an acid–base adduct.

Examples:
- **H₂O + H⁺ → H₃O⁺** — oxygen's lone pair forms the new O–H bond.
- **NH₃ + H⁺ → NH₄⁺** — nitrogen's lone pair forms the new N–H bond.
- **Ag⁺ + 2 NH₃ → [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺** — each nitrogen donates a lone pair to silver, the metal–[[Ligand|ligand]] bonds of a [[ComplexIon|complex ion]].

Every metal–ligand bond in a [[CoordinationCompound|coordination compound]] is a coordinate covalent bond: the metal (Lewis acid) accepts the lone pairs donated by its ligands (Lewis bases). Ch 19 builds the whole of coordination chemistry on this bond type.
