# Third Law of Thermodynamics

The **third law of thermodynamics** states that the entropy of a **pure, perfect crystalline substance at 0 K is zero**. From the [[BoltzmannEntropyEquation|Boltzmann equation]], a perfect crystal at absolute zero has exactly one accessible microstate (W = 1):

$$S = k \ln W = k \ln(1) = 0$$

## Why It Matters
The third law supplies an **absolute reference point** for entropy. Whereas only *changes* in [[EnthalpyChemistry|enthalpy]] (and only relative [[InternalEnergyThermodynamics|internal energies]]) can be measured, the third law makes **absolute entropies** measurable: integrating heat capacity from 0 K upward gives the absolute [[StandardMolarEntropy|standard molar entropy S°]] of a substance. Consequently S° is nonzero for elements in their standard states (contrast [[StandardEnthalpyOfFormation|ΔHf°]] = 0 for elements).
