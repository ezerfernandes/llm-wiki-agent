## Definition
**Leptons** are [[FundamentalParticle|fundamental]] fermions that do **not** feel the [[StrongNuclearForce|strong nuclear force]] (unlike [[Hadron|hadrons]]); they interact via the weak force and, if charged, the electromagnetic force. They appear pointlike with no detectable internal structure.

## Key Points
- **Six leptons in three families**: electron (e⁻) + electron-neutrino (νₑ); muon (μ⁻) + muon-neutrino (νμ); tau (τ⁻) + tau-neutrino (ντ). Each has an [[Antimatter|antiparticle]].
- Each family has a conserved **lepton family number** (Lₑ, Lμ, Lτ), used to test whether decays are allowed; neutrino oscillations are a known exception.
- Charged leptons are unstable except the electron: μ⁻ → e⁻ + ν̄ₑ + νμ, τ⁻ → μ⁻ + ν̄μ + ντ (weak-force decays).
- The muon was discovered with the pion in cosmic rays but, unlike the pion, does not feel the strong force — an early clue that leptons are a distinct class.
- Together with [[Quark|quarks]], leptons are the matter content of the [[StandardModel]].

## Equations
- Lepton family numbers conserved separately: ΔLₑ = ΔLμ = ΔLτ = 0
- Muon decay: μ⁻ → e⁻ + ν̄ₑ + νμ
