## Definition
**Kinetic energy** is the energy an object has by virtue of its motion. Translational kinetic energy depends on mass and on the square of speed, KE = ½mv². Because it grows with the square of velocity, high-speed impacts carry disproportionately more energy — illustrated historically by du Châtelet's clay-penetration experiments (double the speed, four times the penetration).

## Key Points
- A scalar, measured in joules (J); always non-negative.
- Doubling speed quadruples kinetic energy.
- Change in kinetic energy equals the net [[Work]] done on the object (the [[WorkEnergyTheorem]]).
- Combines with potential energy to form [[MechanicalEnergy]].

## Equations
- KE = ½ m v²
- ΔKE = W_net  (work-energy theorem)
- v = √(2·KE/m)
