# Addition Reaction (Organic)

An **addition reaction** is the characteristic reaction of **unsaturated** [[Hydrocarbon|hydrocarbons]] ([[Alkene|alkenes]] and [[Alkyne|alkynes]]): a **[[SigmaAndPiBonds|π bond]] is broken and replaced by two new σ bonds**, adding atoms across the former multiple bond. As the π bond is consumed, the carbon **hybridization changes** ([[OrbitalHybridization]]) — sp² → sp³ for an alkene, and sp → sp² → sp³ for an alkyne.

Addition is favored because the **π bond is weaker** than a σ bond and is disrupted easily. This is why alkenes *add* halogens whereas saturated [[Alkane|alkanes]] only *substitute* (see [[SubstitutionReaction]]).

## Examples

- Alkene + halogen: C₂H₄ + Cl₂ → ClCH₂CH₂Cl (1,2-dichloroethane).
- Alkyne (two π bonds) consumes **twice** the reagent: HC≡CH + 2 Br₂ → Br₂CHCHBr₂ (1,1,2,2-tetrabromoethane).
- Acid-catalyzed **hydration**: C₂H₄ + H₂O → C₂H₅OH (ethanol).

(Note: [[AromaticHydrocarbon|aromatic]] rings resist addition and prefer substitution, to preserve their delocalized π system.)
