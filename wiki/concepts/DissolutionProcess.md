# Dissolution Process

The **dissolution process** is the formation of a [[SolutionConcentration|solution]] — a homogeneous mixture in which one or more **solutes** are dispersed on a molecular scale throughout a **solvent** (the majority component), each solute particle closely surrounded by solvent species. Dissolved solute does not settle out, and a solution's physical state usually matches that of its solvent.

## A Spontaneous Process
Dissolution is a **spontaneous process** — it "occurs under specified conditions without the requirement of energy from some external source" (stirring only speeds it up). Two criteria *favor* (but do not guarantee) spontaneous solution formation:
1. **Decrease in internal energy** — an exothermic change (heat released).
2. **Increased dispersal of matter** — an increase in entropy.

An internal energy change often, but not always, accompanies dissolution; an **increase in matter dispersal always occurs**. This is why entropy alone can drive dissolution even when it is endothermic.

## Three Competing Intermolecular Forces
Dissolution is governed by the relative strengths of three sets of [[IntermolecularForces|intermolecular forces]]:
- **solute-solute** attractions,
- **solvent-solvent** attractions,
- **solute-solvent** attractions ([[Solvation|solvation]]).

The hypothetical stepwise model decomposes dissolving into separating the solute (endothermic), expanding the solvent (endothermic), and solvating (exothermic) — see [[EnthalpyOfSolution]]. Their net determines whether dissolving is overall exothermic or endothermic.

For an [[IonicCompound|ionic compound]] in water, the interionic electrostatic forces (solute-solute) must be overcome as ion-water attractions are established (solute-solvent). When solvation dominates, dissolution is exothermic and the compound is highly soluble (e.g., NaOH); when electrostatic forces dominate, it is endothermic and the compound is poorly soluble (e.g., CaCO₃, a coral-reef component).

## When No Solution Forms
If the energy needed to separate solute and solvent species greatly exceeds the energy released by solvation, forming a homogeneous solution is thermodynamically unfavorable and dissolution does not occur appreciably (see [[LikeDissolvesLike]]).
