# Molar Mass

The **molar mass** of an element or compound is the mass in grams of one [[TheMoleConcept|mole]] of that substance. Its unit is **grams per mole (g/mol)**.

The defining convenience of molar mass is a numerical identity:

> The molar mass of any substance (in g/mol) is numerically equal to its atomic or [[FormulaMass|formula mass]] (in amu).

So carbon (atomic mass 12.01 amu) has molar mass 12.01 g/mol; water (formula mass 18.02 amu) has molar mass 18.02 g/mol. This holds because one mole contains exactly [[AvogadrosNumber|Avogadro's number]] of units, and that count was chosen so that amu-per-particle scales to grams-per-mole.

## Computing Molar Mass of a Compound

Sum the molar masses of every atom in the [[ChemicalFormula|chemical formula]]:
- Glycine (C₂H₅O₂N): 2(12.01) + 5(1.008) + 2(16.00) + 14.007 = **75.07 g/mol**
- Sucrose (C₁₂H₂₂O₁₁): **342.30 g/mol**

## Mass ↔ Mole Conversions

Molar mass is the conversion factor between grams and moles:
- **Grams → moles:** moles = mass (g) ÷ molar mass (g/mol). e.g., 28.35 g glycine ÷ 75.07 g/mol = 0.378 mol.
- **Moles → grams:** mass (g) = moles × molar mass. e.g., 0.443 mol N₂H₄ × 32.05 g/mol = 14.2 g.

Combined with [[AvogadrosNumber|Avogadro's number]], molar mass connects a weighable mass to the actual number of particles, the foundation of stoichiometry and [[Molarity|molarity]] calculations.

## From Gas Measurements (Ch 9)

For a gas, molar mass can be found from pressure, volume, temperature, and mass via the [[IdealGasLawChemistry|ideal gas law]]: combining d = m/V with PV = nRT gives the gas density d = ℳP/RT and, rearranged, **ℳ = mRT/PV**. This lets an unknown gas be identified from bulk measurements (see [[GasStoichiometry]]).
