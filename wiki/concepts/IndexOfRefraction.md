## Definition
The index of refraction `n` of a medium is the ratio of the speed of light in vacuum to its speed in that medium, `n = c/v`. It is a dimensionless number measuring how much the medium slows light; for ordinary matter `n ≥ 1`.

## Key Points
- Light always travels slower in matter than in vacuum, so `v = c/n ≤ c`.
- Larger `n` means slower light and stronger bending at a boundary (see [[SnellsLaw]]).
- `n` varies with wavelength, which causes [[OpticalDispersion]] (violet has a higher `n` than red).
- The reference speed is the vacuum speed of light, `c ≈ 3.00 × 10^8 m/s` ([[SpeedOfLightPhysics]]).
- High-index materials (diamond, zircon) produce strong refraction and a low critical angle, contributing to sparkle ([[TotalInternalReflection]]).

## Equations
- Definition: `n = c / v`
- Speed in a medium: `v = c / n`
- Vacuum speed: `c = 2.99792458 × 10^8 m/s ≈ 3.00 × 10^8 m/s`
