## Definition
Terminal velocity is the constant speed reached by an object falling through a fluid when the [[DragForce]] grows to exactly balance gravity. With zero net force, acceleration ceases and speed stays constant (see [[NewtonsSecondLaw]]).

## Key Points
- Reached when drag force equals weight: F_D = mg.
- Strongly affected by cross-sectional area A: a headfirst skydiver (small A) reaches ~98 m/s, while a spread-eagle skydiver (large A) reaches only ~44 m/s even at greater mass.
- Larger mass raises terminal velocity (as sqrt(m)); larger area, fluid density, or drag coefficient lowers it.
- Explains why small animals often survive falls: their area-to-mass ratio gives a low terminal velocity.

## Equations
- Balance condition: (1/2) C ρ A v_t² = m g
- Terminal velocity: v_t = sqrt( 2 m g / (ρ C A) )
