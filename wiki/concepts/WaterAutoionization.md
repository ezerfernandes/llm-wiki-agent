# Autoionization of Water (Kw)

**Autoionization** is the process in which like molecules of an [[Amphoteric|amphiprotic]] substance react with one another to yield ions. For water:

$$\mathrm{H_2O(l) + H_2O(l) \rightleftharpoons H_3O^+(aq) + OH^-(aq)}$$

At 25 °C only about two in a billion water molecules are ionized.

## Ion-product constant Kw

The extent of this autoionization is given by the **ion-product constant for water**:

$$K_w = [\mathrm{H_3O^+}][\mathrm{OH^-}]$$

At 25 °C, **Kw = 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁴**. In pure water [H₃O⁺] = [OH⁻], so each equals √Kw = 1.0 × 10⁻⁷ M.

Kw holds in *any* aqueous solution, so [H₃O⁺] and [OH⁻] are inversely related: adding acid (raising [H₃O⁺]) lowers [OH⁻] proportionally, and vice versa — a shift in the autoionization equilibrium explained by the [[LeChatelierPrinciple|Le Châtelier principle]].

## Temperature dependence

Autoionization is endothermic, so Kw increases with temperature. At 100 °C, Kw ≈ 5.6 × 10⁻¹³ (~50× the 25 °C value), and at 80 °C, Kw = 2.4 × 10⁻¹³. Because Kw rises, the [[pHScale|pH]] of neutral water falls below 7 at higher temperature (e.g. pH = pOH = 6.31 at 80 °C) — neutrality always means [H₃O⁺] = [OH⁻], not pH = 7.

## Why it matters

Kw is the hub of the whole chapter: it defines the [[pHScale|pH/pOH]] relationship (pH + pOH = pKw), links the [[AcidIonizationConstant|Ka]] and [[BaseIonizationConstant|Kb]] of a [[ConjugateAcidBasePair|conjugate pair]] (Ka·Kb = Kw), and sets the [[SaltHydrolysis|hydrolysis]] constants of salt ions.
