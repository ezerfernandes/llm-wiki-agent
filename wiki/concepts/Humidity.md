## Definition
**Humidity** describes the amount of water vapor in air. **Relative humidity** is the percentage
comparing the actual water-vapor content to the maximum the air can hold at that temperature; at
100% (saturation) net evaporation stops.

## Key Points
- **Saturation vapor density** is the maximum mass of water vapor (g/m³) air can hold at a given
  temperature; it rises with temperature because warmer air supports higher [[VaporPressure|vapor
  pressure]].
- **Dew point** is the temperature at which the air's current vapor content would reach saturation
  (100% RH). Cooling below it causes condensation — dew, fog, or frost.
- Vapor density can be computed from vapor pressure via the [[IdealGasLaw]].
- **Evaporative cooling**: fast molecules escape preferentially, lowering the average kinetic
  energy (and temperature) of the remaining liquid; this is why sweating and freeze-drying cool.
- Applications: dew-point monitoring for frost prediction; high humidity suppresses sweat
  evaporation and raises perceived heat; low desert humidity yields large day–night temperature
  swings from minimal latent-heat exchange.

## Equations
- Percent relative humidity = (vapor density / saturation vapor density) × 100
- n/V = P/(RT)  (vapor density from vapor pressure)
