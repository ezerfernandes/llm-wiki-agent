## Definition
The Lorentz factor γ is the dimensionless quantity γ = 1/√(1 − v²/c²) that scales every effect in [[SpecialRelativity|special relativity]]. It depends only on the relative speed v as a fraction of the [[SpeedOfLightPhysics|speed of light]] c.

## Key Points
- γ ≥ 1 always; γ = 1 when v = 0 and γ → ∞ as v → c.
- At everyday speeds (v ≪ c) γ ≈ 1, so relativistic formulas reduce to classical ones — explaining why relativistic effects are normally unnoticed.
- Appears as the multiplier in [[TimeDilation|time dilation]] (Δt = γΔt₀), the divisor in [[LengthContraction|length contraction]] (L = L₀/γ), [[RelativisticMomentum|relativistic momentum]] (p = γmu), and [[RelativisticEnergy|total energy]] (E = γmc²).
- Its divergence as v → c is the mathematical reason no massive object can reach the speed of light.

## Equations
- γ = 1 / √(1 − v²/c²)
- Sample values: v = 0.950c → γ ≈ 3.20; v = 0.990c → γ ≈ 7.09
