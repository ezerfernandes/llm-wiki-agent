## Definition
Snell's law (the law of refraction) relates the angles of incidence and refraction to the indices of refraction of the two media at a boundary: `n₁ sin θ₁ = n₂ sin θ₂`, with angles measured from the normal.

## Key Points
- Going into a higher-index medium (`n₂ > n₁`) bends the ray toward the normal; into a lower-index medium it bends away.
- Combined with `n = c/v` ([[IndexOfRefraction]]), it lets you find an unknown speed, index, or angle from the others.
- At the limiting case where the refracted angle reaches 90°, the incidence angle is the critical angle and [[TotalInternalReflection]] sets in.
- Because `n` depends on wavelength, Snell's law produces [[OpticalDispersion]].

## Equations
- Snell's law: `n₁ sin θ₁ = n₂ sin θ₂`
- Critical-angle condition (θ₂ = 90°): `n₁ sin θc = n₂`
