# VSEPR Theory

**Valence Shell Electron-Pair Repulsion (VSEPR) theory** predicts the [[MolecularGeometry|molecular geometry]] and bond angles of a molecule from the number of bonds and lone pairs in its [[LewisStructure|Lewis structure]]. The core assumption: electron pairs (regions of electron density) in a central atom's valence shell arrange themselves to **minimize repulsion** by maximizing the distance between them.

A **region (domain) of electron density** is a lone pair *or* a bond — and a single, double, or triple bond each counts as **one** region.

## Electron-Pair Geometries

| Electron Regions | Electron-Pair Geometry | Bond Angles |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | Linear | 180° |
| 3 | Trigonal planar | 120° |
| 4 | Tetrahedral | 109.5° |
| 5 | Trigonal bipyramidal | 90° and 120° |
| 6 | Octahedral | 90° |

## Lone Pairs and Repulsion

Lone pairs occupy more space than bonding pairs, so repulsion ranks: **lone pair–lone pair > lone pair–bonding pair > bonding pair–bonding pair**. Spatial size ranks: **lone pair > triple bond > double bond > single bond**. Lone pairs therefore compress bond angles below the ideal (e.g., NH₃'s H–N–H angle is slightly less than 109.5°). In trigonal bipyramidal geometry, lone pairs always take the roomier **equatorial** positions (120°) rather than axial (90°); in octahedral geometry two lone pairs sit 180° apart.

This distinction between where *electrons* are (electron-pair geometry) and where *atoms* are ([[MolecularGeometry|molecular structure]]) is central to the theory.

## Procedure

1. Draw the [[LewisStructure|Lewis structure]]. 2. Count regions of electron density on the central atom. 3. Read off the electron-pair geometry. 4. Use the number of lone pairs to find the molecular structure (minimizing repulsions).

## Link to Orbital Hybridization

VSEPR predicts shapes but not the underlying bonding. [[ValenceBondTheory|Valence bond theory]] closes that gap with [[OrbitalHybridization|orbital hybridization]], and the electron-pair geometry VSEPR gives maps **one-to-one** onto the hybridization (2 regions→sp, 3→sp², 4→sp³, 5→sp³d, 6→sp³d²) — see [[HybridizationAndVSEPRGeometry]]. The same "count regions of electron density" procedure drives both.
