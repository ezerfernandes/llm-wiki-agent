# Lattice Energy

The **lattice energy (ΔH_lattice)** of an ionic compound is the energy required to separate one mole of the solid into its component gaseous ions. It measures the strength of [[IonicBonding|ionic bonding]], the ionic-solid analogue of [[BondEnergy|bond energy]].

$$\text{MX(s)} \rightarrow \text{M}^{n+}(g) + \text{X}^{n-}(g), \quad \Delta H_\text{lattice}$$

By convention it is **positive** (endothermic). For NaCl, ΔH_lattice = 769 kJ/mol.

## Coulombic Equation

$$\Delta H_\text{lattice} = \frac{C\,(Z^{+})(Z^{-})}{R_0}$$

- **C** — a constant that depends on the crystal structure
- **Z⁺, Z⁻** — the charges on the ions
- **R₀** — the interionic distance (sum of the ionic radii)

## Trends

- **Ionic charge**: doubling both charges quadruples the lattice energy — LiF (Z = 1) 1023 kJ/mol vs. MgO (Z = 2) 3900 kJ/mol.
- **Ionic size**: smaller ions (smaller R₀) give larger lattice energy — MgF₂ 2957 vs. MgI₂ 2327 kJ/mol.

A larger lattice energy means a more stable [[IonicCompound|ionic compound]] — higher melting point and greater hardness. Lattice energies (~600–4000 kJ/mol) dwarf single covalent [[BondEnergy|bond energies]] (~150–400 kJ/mol) because they sum many electrostatic interactions across the crystal.

## Determination

Lattice energy is not measured directly; it is computed via the **[[BornHaberCycle|Born-Haber cycle]]**, applying [[HessLaw|Hess's law]] to the steps of ionic-compound formation.
