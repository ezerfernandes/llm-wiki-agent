# Organic Polymer

A **polymer** is a **large molecule made up of repeating units called monomers**. Polymers can be **natural** (starch from sugar residues; proteins from amino acids) or **synthetic** (polyethylene, PVC, polystyrene). Adding [[OrganicFunctionalGroup|functional groups]] to a polymer's structure can change its properties dramatically. (Named *OrganicPolymer* to keep the chemistry sense distinct from any unrelated "polymer.")

## Formation

- **Addition polymerization:** unsaturated monomers add to each other. **Ethylene** ([[Alkene|alkene]]) polymerizes (with transition-metal catalysts) into **polyethylene**, long chains of –CH₂– units used mainly for packaging.
- **Condensation polymerization:** monomers join by eliminating a small molecule (water). **Proteins** form when amino acids link via **peptide bonds** (an [[AminesAndAmides|amidation]] reaction); **Kevlar** is an aromatic-amide polymer.

## Plastics and recycling

**Plastics** are synthetic organic solids that can be molded — typically high-molecular-mass organic polymers. Common monomers (ethylene, propylene, vinyl chloride, styrene, ethylene terephthalate) come from petrochemicals and are not very biodegradable. **Plastic recycling** recovers and reprocesses used plastics; recycling codes 1–7 label them: 1 PETE, 2 HDPE, 3 V/PVC, 4 LDPE, 5 PP, 6 PS, 7 Other.

**Kevlar** (from 1,4-phenylene-diamine + terephthaloyl chloride) gains strength from inter-chain hydrogen bonds and **aromatic stacking** of ring p orbitals; ~5× stronger than steel by weight (body armor, helmets).
