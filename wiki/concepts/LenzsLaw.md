## Definition
**Lenz's law** states that an induced current (and its magnetic field) always flows in the direction that *opposes* the change in [[MagneticFlux|magnetic flux]] producing it. It is the physical content of the minus sign in [[FaradaysLaw|Faraday's law]] and is a direct statement of energy conservation: a system cannot generate energy from nothing, so the induced response must resist the driving change.

## Key Points
- If flux is increasing, the induced current's field points opposite to the applied field; if flux is decreasing, it points with the applied field — always to oppose the change.
- Opposition means work must be done against the induced effect, which is where the electrical energy comes from (no perpetual-motion free energy).
- Direction recipe: find the external field, decide whether flux is rising or falling, set the induced field to oppose that change, then use the right-hand rule (RHR-2) to get the current direction and emf polarity.
- Explains [[EddyCurrent|eddy-current damping]] (drag opposes motion), [[BackEMF|back emf]] (opposes applied voltage in a motor), and the self-opposition of an [[Inductance|inductor]].

## Equations
- The negative sign in `emf = −N (ΔΦ/Δt)` encodes Lenz's law.
