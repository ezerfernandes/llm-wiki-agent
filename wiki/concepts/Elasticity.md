## Definition
Elasticity is the property of a material to deform under an applied force and return to its original shape once the force is removed. Such reversible shape change is called elastic deformation; the linear relationship between force and deformation in this regime is [[HookesLaw]].

## Key Points
- Elastic behavior holds only for small deformations; beyond the elastic limit (tensile strength), deformation becomes permanent or the material fractures.
- Resistance to deformation is captured by elastic moduli: Young's modulus (Y) for length change, shear modulus (S) for shape change, and bulk modulus (B) for volume change.
- Three [[Deformation]] modes: longitudinal (tension/compression), shear (transverse), and volumetric (bulk).
- Quantified through [[Stress]] (force per area) and [[Strain]] (fractional deformation): stress = modulus × strain.
- Biological tissues show nonlinear elasticity (e.g. tendons' three-phase stress-strain curve); elastic properties decline with age.

## Equations
- Hooke's law: F = k ΔL
- Stress-strain (longitudinal): F/A = Y (ΔL/L₀)
