## Definition
General relativity is Einstein's theory of gravitation and of all relative motion, including acceleration. It treats gravity as the curvature of spacetime caused by mass-energy, generalizing [[SpecialRelativity]] (which it recovers when acceleration is zero and speeds are small compared to light).

## Key Points
- **Equivalence principle**: acceleration and gravity are locally indistinguishable. An observer in a sealed, upward-accelerating elevator cannot tell the experience apart from standing in a gravitational field.
- Because gravity is spacetime curvature, it bends the paths of even massless light. Einstein's elevator thought experiment shows a horizontal light beam appears to curve downward under acceleration — identical to gravity's effect.
- **Gravitational lensing**: massive objects bend light like a converging lens, producing multiple images of background galaxies; for lensed systems the intervening galaxy's red shift is always less than the background's.
- Confirmed dramatically by the **1919 solar eclipse**, which measured starlight bending around the Sun as predicted.
- Predicts [[BlackHole]]s, [[GravitationalWave]]s, and the cosmological constant ([[DarkEnergy]]); underpins cosmic dynamics in [[Cosmology]].
- Reconciling it with quantum mechanics is the goal of [[QuantumGravity]].

## Equations
- Schwarzschild radius (black-hole event horizon): R_S = 2GM / c²
- Reduces to [[SpecialRelativity]] and to [[NewtonsLawOfUniversalGravitation]] in appropriate limits.
