# Aldehydes and Ketones (the Carbonyl Group)

Aldehydes and ketones both contain the **carbonyl group** — *a functional group with a carbon–oxygen double bond (C=O)*. The carbonyl carbon is **trigonal planar, sp² hybridized** ([[OrbitalHybridization]]): two sp² orbitals form σ bonds to C/H, the third forms a σ bond to oxygen, and the unhybridized p orbital on carbon overlaps a p orbital on oxygen to form the **[[SigmaAndPiBonds|π bond]]**.

- **Aldehyde** — the carbonyl group is bonded to **at least one hydrogen** (group –CHO; suffix **-al**).
- **Ketone** — the carbonyl group is bonded to **two carbon atoms** (group –CO–; suffix **-one**).

## Polarity and reactivity

The C=O bond is **polar** (oxygen is more electronegative than carbon, pulling electrons toward O). Reactions usually begin with a **Lewis base attacking the carbon at the positive end** of the polar C=O bond, forming an unstable intermediate that rearranges to the product.

## Made by oxidizing alcohols

In organic chemistry, **oxidation replaces a C–H bond with a C–O bond** (reduction does the reverse). Carbon oxidation numbers climb along the series: CH₄ (–4) → CH₃OH (–2) → CH₂O aldehyde (0) → HCO₂H acid (+2) → CO₂ (+4).

- **Primary [[AlcoholsAndEthers|alcohol]] → aldehyde** (–OH on a terminal carbon): CH₃CH₂CH₂OH → CH₃CH₂CHO.
- **Secondary alcohol → ketone** (–OH carbon bonded to two other carbons): CH₃CH(OH)CH₃ → CH₃COCH₃.
- A **tertiary** alcohol (–OH carbon bonded to three carbons) has **no C–H to replace** and is **not oxidized**.

## Examples

- **Formaldehyde (HCHO):** pungent colorless gas; sold as **formalin** (~37% in water); coagulates proteins (kills bacteria, stops decay) — used in tissue preservation, embalming, soil sterilization, and Bakelite.
- **Acetone (dimethyl ketone, CH₃COCH₃):** simplest ketone; colorless liquid solvent (nail polish, lacquers, plastics) and paint remover; made by oxidizing 2-propanol or by fermentation.
