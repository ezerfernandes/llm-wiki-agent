# Nuclear Transmutation

**Nuclear transmutation** is the conversion of one nuclide into another. It happens naturally through [[RadioactiveDecayChemistry|radioactive decay]], or is induced when a nucleus is made to react with another particle. The first manmade transmutation (Ernest Rutherford, 1919) bombarded nitrogen with alpha particles:

$$^{14}_{7}\text{N} + ^{4}_{2}\text{He} \rightarrow ^{17}_{8}\text{O} + ^{1}_{1}\text{H}$$

### Particle accelerators
Charged bombarding particles must overcome the target's nuclear charge, requiring very high kinetic energies. **Particle accelerators** (cyclotrons, linear accelerators) use magnetic and electric fields to accelerate particles to those speeds in a vacuum. Neutrons — needing no acceleration because they are uncharged — are obtained from radioactive decay or nuclear reactors. (See the College Physics 2e page [[ParticleAccelerator]].)

### Transuranium elements
The **transuranium elements** are those beyond uranium (Z > 92); 22 are officially recognized by IUPAC. They are made by bombardment and successive decay, for example:

$$^{238}_{92}\text{U} + ^{1}_{0}\text{n} \rightarrow ^{239}_{92}\text{U} \xrightarrow{\beta^-} ^{239}_{93}\text{Np} \xrightarrow{\beta^-} ^{239}_{94}\text{Pu}$$

(U-239 half-life 23.5 min; Np-239 half-life 2.36 days.) Neptunium-239 (McMillan & Abelson, 1940) decays to plutonium-239. Heavier plutonium isotopes (Pu-240/241/242) form by further neutron capture; their long half-lives make storage a serious challenge.

Induced transmutation by neutron capture also underlies [[NuclearFissionChemistry|fission]] and the production of medical [[Radioisotope|radioisotopes]].
