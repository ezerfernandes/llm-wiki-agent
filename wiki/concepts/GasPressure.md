# Gas Pressure

**Pressure** is the force exerted on a surface divided by the area over which it acts:

$$P = \frac{F}{A}$$

A gas exerts pressure through the collisions of its molecules with the walls of its container (see [[KineticMolecularTheory]]). Pressure rises with larger force or smaller area, and falls with smaller force or larger area. This page is the **chemistry-domain** treatment from OpenStax *Chemistry 2e* Ch 9; for the physics/fluid-statics treatment see the separate [[Pressure]] page.

## Units and Conversions

The SI (and IUPAC-recommended) unit is the **pascal (Pa) = 1 N/m²**, but chemistry uses several practical units. The key equalities for standard atmospheric pressure:

$$1\ \text{atm} = 760\ \text{mmHg} = 760\ \text{torr} = 101{,}325\ \text{Pa} = 101.325\ \text{kPa} = 1.01325\ \text{bar} \approx 14.7\ \text{psi}$$

- **pascal (Pa)** = 1 N/m²; **kPa** = 1000 Pa.
- **bar** = 100,000 Pa; **mbar** (millibar): 1000 mbar = 1 bar.
- **torr** = 1/760 atm; **mmHg** ≈ 1 torr.
- **in. Hg** = 3386 Pa; **psi** ≈ 14.7 at sea level.

Converting between units is an application of [[DimensionalAnalysis]] (e.g., 29.2 in. Hg = 742 torr = 0.976 atm = 98.9 kPa = 989 mbar).

## Hydrostatic Pressure and Measuring Instruments

The pressure of a fluid column under gravity is:

$$p = h \rho g$$

where *h* is the column height, ρ is the fluid density, and *g* is gravitational acceleration. Standard atmospheric pressure supports a mercury column ≈ 760 mm (29.92 in.) high.

- **Barometer** — a liquid-filled tube closed at one end, inverted into a reservoir; the column height measures atmospheric pressure. Mercury is used because it is ≈ 13.6× denser than water, keeping the instrument a manageable size.
- **Closed-end manometer** — measures a confined gas directly: $P_{gas} = h\rho g$.
- **Open-end manometer** — one arm open to the atmosphere: $P_{gas} = P_{atm} \mp h\rho g$ (subtract when the liquid is higher on the gas side, add when higher on the open side).
