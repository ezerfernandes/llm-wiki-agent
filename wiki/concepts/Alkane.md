# Alkane

An **alkane**, or **saturated hydrocarbon**, is a [[Hydrocarbon|hydrocarbon]] containing **only single covalent bonds between carbon atoms**. Each carbon is **sp³ hybridized** ([[OrbitalHybridization]]) and bonds to four atoms (C or H), giving ~**109.5°** bond angles and the characteristic zigzag chain shape. Noncyclic alkanes follow the general formula **CₙH₂ₙ₊₂**.

## Nomenclature (IUPAC)

1. **Find the longest carbon chain** and name it: methane (1 C), ethane (2), propane (3), butane (4), pentane (5), hexane (6), heptane (7), octane (8), nonane (9), decane (10).
2. **Number from the end nearest the substituents**; name **[[OrganicFunctionalGroup|substituents]]** alphabetically with position numbers; use di-/tri-/tetra- for repeats. Electronegative substituents take "-o" (chloro, fluoro).

An **alkyl group** is a substituent with one less hydrogen than the parent alkane (drop "-ane," add "-yl"): methyl (CH₃–), ethyl (CH₃CH₂–), n-propyl/isopropyl, n-/sec-/iso-/tert-butyl. Carbons (and the H on them) are **primary / secondary / tertiary** depending on whether the carbon is bonded to one, two, or three other carbons.

## Physical trends

Melting and boiling points rise smoothly with carbon number (stronger London dispersion forces): C₁–C₄ are gases, C₅–C₁₃ liquids, larger ones solids. The number of structural [[OrganicIsomerism|isomers]] grows explosively — butane 2, octane 18, decane 75, octadecane 60,523.

## Reactions

- **Combustion** (highly exothermic redox; the basis of alkanes as fuels):
  CH₄(g) + 2 O₂(g) → CO₂(g) + 2 H₂O(g)
- **[[SubstitutionReaction|Substitution]]** — a halogen replaces an H; no C–C bonds break and hybridization is unchanged:
  CH₃CH₃ + Cl₂ → CH₃CH₂Cl + HCl

Crude oil (the main source of liquid alkane fuels — gasoline C₅–C₉, kerosene, diesel) is separated by **fractional distillation** using boiling-point differences.
