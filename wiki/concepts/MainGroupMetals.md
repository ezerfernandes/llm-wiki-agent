# Main-Group Metals (Groups 12–15)

This page covers the heavier **[[RepresentativeElements|representative metals]]** beyond groups 1 and 2 — the metals of groups 12, 13, 14, and 15. (The [[AlkaliMetals]] and [[AlkalineEarthMetals]] have their own pages.)

## Group 12: Zn, Cd, Hg
Representative metals because the last electron added is an *s* electron (ns²); they form 2+ cations with pseudo-noble-gas configurations. Reactivity is the **reverse** of groups 1–2: zinc most reactive, mercury least. Zn and Cd release H₂ with acids:

> Zn(s) + 2H₃O⁺(aq) + 2Cl⁻(aq) → H₂(g) + Zn²⁺(aq) + 2Cl⁻(aq) + 2H₂O(l)

Zinc passivates with a basic carbonate Zn₂(OH)₂CO₃; about half of zinc protects iron as a **sacrificial (galvanic) anode** or galvanized coating; brass = Cu+Zn. **Mercury** is the only metal liquid at 25 °C, forms **amalgams** (alloys with mercury), is harder to oxidize than hydrogen (does not displace H₂ from acids), but reacts with oxidizing acids:

> 3Hg(l) + 8HNO₃(aq) → 3Hg(NO₃)₂(aq) + 4H₂O(l) + 2NO(g)

Mercury can also show 1+ via Hg₂²⁺; all mercury compounds are toxic.

## Group 13: Al, Ga, In, Tl (with the metalloid B)
Valence configuration ns²np¹; aluminum usually uses all three valence electrons (3+). Oxides/hydroxides of Al and Ga are **[[Amphoteric|amphoteric]]**:

> 2Al(s) + 6HCl(aq) → 2AlCl₃(aq) + 3H₂(g)
> 2Al(s) + 2NaOH(aq) + 6H₂O(l) → 2Na[Al(OH)₄](aq) + 3H₂(g)

In and Tl oxides are only basic. The **inert pair effect** makes Tl⁺ more stable than Tl³⁺. These metals passivate with a tough oxide film (disrupted by mercury amalgam). Aqueous Al³⁺ is [Al(H₂O)₆]³⁺. Aluminum (light, tough, corrosion-resistant) is used in construction, transport, cans, and foil, and is a reducing agent for isolating Mo, W, Cr.

## Group 14 metals: Sn, Pb
Sn and Pb form stable divalent cations (Sn²⁺, Pb²⁺) two below the group 4+ value (**inert pair effect**) and covalent 4+ compounds (SnCl₄, PbCl₄). Tin is more easily oxidized than hydrogen; lead needs hot concentrated acid. Tin has two **allotropes**: white tin (stable above 13.2 °C) and gray tin (low T); the gray↔white conversion is "tin pest." Uses: tin-plated steel (tin cans), bronze (Cu+Sn), solder (Sn+Pb), lead storage batteries. (Carbon, silicon, and germanium of group 14 are covered under [[CarbonGroupChemistry]] / [[Metalloids]].)

## Group 15: Bismuth
Bismuth is a weakly reactive metal forming Bi³⁺; it reaches the 5+ group state only with strong oxidizers — again the inert pair effect.
