## Definition
A diffraction grating is an optical element with many equally spaced parallel slits (or reflecting grooves) that disperses light by [[WaveInterference]]. It generalizes the [[DoubleSlitExperiment]] to a large number of slits.

## Key Points
- Constructive interference occurs where path differences between adjacent slits equal an integer number of wavelengths.
- Compared with a double slit, a grating gives much sharper, brighter, and better-separated maxima; sharpness and resolution improve as the number of slits increases.
- Different wavelengths diffract to different angles, spreading white light into a spectrum (dispersion) — the basis of spectroscopy and monochromators.
- Structural color/iridescence (bird feathers, insect wings) arises from natural periodic microstructures acting as reflection gratings.

## Equations
- Grating equation: `d sin θ = m λ`, m = 0, ±1, ±2, … (d = slit spacing)
- Screen position: `y = x tan θ` (x = grating-to-screen distance)
