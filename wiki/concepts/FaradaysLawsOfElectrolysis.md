# Faraday's Laws of Electrolysis (Stoichiometry)

**Faraday's laws of electrolysis** quantify how much substance is produced or consumed at an electrode during [[ElectrolysisChemistry|electrolysis]]. The amount of product is proportional to the **electric charge** passed, and — for a given charge — to the electron count in the half-reaction. Named for [[MichaelFaraday]].

## Core Relationships
**Current** is the rate of charge flow, in amperes (A = 1 C/s). The charge passed is:
$$Q = I\,t$$
(Q in coulombs, I in amperes, t in seconds.) The charge relates to **moles of electrons** through the **Faraday constant**:
$$Q = nF, \qquad F = 96{,}485\ \text{C/mol e}^-$$
For a generic reduction Mⁿ⁺(aq) + ne⁻ → M(s), each mole of M requires **n moles of electrons**. Combining the two equations links current, time, and mass deposited.

## Worked Examples
**Mass from current × time** — 10.23 A for 1 hr through AgNO₃ (Ag⁺ + e⁻ → Ag, M = 107.9 g/mol):
- Q = It = 10.23 × 3600 = 36,830 C
- n = Q/F = 36,830 / 96,485 = 0.3817 mol e⁻ = 0.3817 mol Ag
- mass = 0.3817 × 107.9 = **41.19 g Ag**

**Time from required mass** — depositing a 0.010-mm Cr layer (Cr³⁺ + 3e⁻ → Cr) over 3.3 m², ρ = 7.19 g/cm³, I = 33.46 A:
- volume = 33 cm³ → mass = 237 g → 4.56 mol Cr
- Q = 4.56 × 3 × 96,485 = 1.32 × 10⁶ C
- t = Q/I = 1.32 × 10⁶ / 33.46 = 3.95 × 10⁴ s = **11.0 hr**

Note the role of n: a Cr³⁺ deposit needs 3 e⁻ per atom, whereas Ag⁺ needs only 1, so equal charge deposits fewer moles of Cr than of Ag.
