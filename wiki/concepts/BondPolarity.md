# Bond Polarity

**Bond polarity** describes the unequal sharing of electrons in a [[CovalentBonding|covalent bond]] between atoms of different [[Electronegativity|electronegativity]]. (Named *BondPolarity* to avoid collision with a generic `Polarity` page.)

## Pure (Nonpolar) vs. Polar Covalent

- **Pure / nonpolar covalent bond**: identical atoms share electrons *equally* (H₂, Cl₂); no partial charges.
- **Polar covalent bond**: bonding electrons are shared *unequally* and shift toward the more electronegative atom, producing a **partial negative charge (δ−)** on that atom and a **partial positive charge (δ+)** on the other. Example: in H–Cl, electrons spend more time near Cl, so Cl is δ− and H is δ+.

The δ (lowercase Greek "delta") notation with + or − marks the partial charges. The degree of sharing forms a continuum from completely equal (pure covalent) through polar covalent to no sharing at all ([[IonicBonding|ionic]]).

## Quantifying Polarity

The electronegativity difference |ΔEN| sets the bond type (< 0.4 nonpolar covalent; 0.4–1.8 polar covalent; > 1.8 ionic). For example, ordering common amino-acid bonds by increasing polarity: C–H ≈ S–H (0.4) < C–N (0.5) < N–H (0.9) < C–O (1.0) < O–H (1.4). Each polar bond carries a [[DipoleMoment|bond dipole moment]] pointing toward the more electronegative atom.
