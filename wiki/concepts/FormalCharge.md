# Formal Charge

The **formal charge** of an atom in a molecule is the hypothetical charge it would carry if the electrons in its bonds were divided *evenly* between the bonded atoms. It is a bookkeeping device — not the atom's actual charge — used to select the most reasonable [[LewisStructure|Lewis structure]].

## Equation

$$\text{formal charge} = (\text{valence electrons of free atom}) - (\text{lone-pair electrons}) - \tfrac{1}{2}(\text{bonding electrons})$$

The sum of formal charges equals 0 for a neutral molecule and equals the ion charge for an ion (a useful self-check).

## Selecting the Best Lewis Structure

1. A structure with **all formal charges zero** is preferred.
2. Otherwise, prefer the **smallest** nonzero formal charges.
3. Prefer adjacent formal charges that are **zero or of opposite sign**.
4. Among similar distributions, prefer **negative formal charge on the more electronegative atom**.

Worked examples: CO₂ → O=C=O (all zero); SCN⁻ → N=C=S (−1, 0, 0) beats N≡C–S (−2, +1, 0); N₂O → N≡N–O (0, +1, −1) beats N=N=O (−1, +2, −1); NO₂⁻ → ONO⁻ over NOO⁻.
