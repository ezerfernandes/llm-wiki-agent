## Definition
Rotational kinematics describes rotational motion — the relationships among rotation angle θ, [[AngularVelocity]] ω, [[AngularAcceleration]] α, and time t — without reference to the forces or masses that cause it. It is the direct analog of one-dimensional translational kinematics.

## Key Points
- For constant α, the four rotational kinematic equations have the same form as the linear ones, with θ↔x, ω↔v, α↔a.
- Average angular velocity is ω̄ = (ω₀ + ω)/2.
- Linear and rotational quantities connect through the radius: v = rω, a = rα, x = rθ.
- One revolution equals 2π radians; revolutions are converted to angle (θ) or distance (x = rθ).
- Useful when revolutions or angle are known but time is not (use ω² = ω₀² + 2αθ).

## Equations
- ω = ω₀ + α t
- θ = ω̄ t,  with ω̄ = (ω₀ + ω)/2
- θ = ω₀ t + ½ α t²
- ω² = ω₀² + 2 α θ
- v = r ω,  a = r α,  x = r θ,  1 rev = 2π rad
