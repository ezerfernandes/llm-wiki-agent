# States of Matter

The physical states (phases) of [[Matter]] differ in how their shape and volume respond to their container and to pressure:

- **Solid** — rigid, with a definite shape and a volume nearly independent of pressure.
- **Liquid** — flows and takes the shape of its container; forms a flat or slightly curved upper surface under gravity; volume nearly independent of pressure. (In zero gravity a liquid forms a sphere.)
- **Gas** — takes both the shape and the volume of its container.
- **Plasma** — a gaseous state containing appreciable numbers of electrically charged particles, giving it distinct properties. Plasmas occur in star interiors, lightning, some television screens, and trace-metal analytical instruments.

Transitions between states (melting, freezing, boiling, condensing) are [[PhysicalProperty|physical changes]]: chemical identity is unchanged, and total [[Matter]] is preserved per [[ConservationOfMatter]]. Which phase exists at a given temperature and pressure is set by the balance between [[IntermolecularForces|intermolecular forces]] and kinetic energy, and is captured graphically by a [[PhaseDiagramChemistry|phase diagram]]; above the [[CriticalPointChemistry|critical point]] the liquid-gas distinction vanishes (a supercritical fluid). The energetics and equilibria of the transitions are treated in [[PhaseTransitionChemistry]].
