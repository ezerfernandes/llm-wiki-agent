## Definition
**Buoyancy** is the net upward [[Force]] (the *buoyant force*) that a fluid exerts on any object placed in it. It exists because [[FluidPressure]] increases with depth (P = hρg), so the pressure pushing up on the bottom of an object exceeds the pressure pushing down on its top, leaving a net upward force.

## Key Points
- The buoyant force is quantified by [[ArchimedesPrinciple]]: it equals the weight of the fluid the object displaces.
- Whether an object floats, sinks, or hovers is set by comparing its average [[Density]] to the fluid's: lower → floats, higher → sinks, equal → neutrally buoyant.
- A floating object displaces a fluid volume whose weight equals the object's full weight; a submerged object displaces only its own volume.
- Buoyancy explains apparent weight loss underwater and is exploited by ships, balloons, submarines (ballast tanks), and life jackets.
- Acts opposite to gravity; net vertical force = buoyant force − weight (see [[MassVersusWeight]]).

## Equations
- F_B = w_fl = ρ_fl V_fl g  (buoyant force)
- Net force = F_B − w_obj
- Floats if ρ̄_obj < ρ_fl
