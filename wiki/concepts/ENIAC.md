# ENIAC (Electronic Numerical Integrator and Computer)

The **first general-purpose electronic digital computer**, completed in **1945** at the [[UniversityOfPennsylvania|University of Pennsylvania Moore School of Engineering]]. Designed by [[JohnMauchly]] and [[PresperEckert]]; commissioned by the U.S. Army to compute artillery firing tables.

## Physical characteristics

- **~17,500 [[VacuumTube|vacuum tubes]]** as the primary computing element (no transistors, no relays for the core logic)
- ~30 tons, ~1,800 sq ft, ~150 kW power draw
- **Decimal** (not binary) internal representation
- Originally programmed by **plugboard rewiring** — physically reconfiguring cables between functional units; not yet a [[StoredProgram|stored-program]] machine

## The ENIAC programmers

Per [[dis-5-1-history|*Dive into Systems* Ch 5.1]], ENIAC was programmed by **six women** recruited from the wartime *human computers* pool:

- [[JeanJenningsBartik|Jean Jennings Bartik]]
- [[BettySnyderHolberton|Betty Snyder Holberton]]
- [[KayMcNultyMauchly|Kay McNulty Mauchly]]
- [[FrancesBilasSpence|Frances Bilas Spence]]
- [[MarlynWescoffMeltzer|Marlyn Wescoff Meltzer]]
- [[RuthLichtermanTeitelbaum|Ruth Lichterman Teitelbaum]]

Working **without manuals or prior examples**, they independently invented foundational programming techniques: algorithmic flow charts, [[Subroutine|subroutines]], and systematic debugging methodologies. Their contributions were historically underrecognized — Ch 5.1 explicitly corrects this attribution gap.

## Significance

Followed two years later (1948) by ENIAC's conversion to a stored-program machine, and conceptually superseded by the [[EDVAC]] design ([[JohnVonNeumann|von Neumann]]'s 1945 paper) — the architectural template that displaced ENIAC's plugboard model.
