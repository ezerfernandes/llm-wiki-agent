## Definition
**Motional emf** is the [[ElectromotiveForce|emf]] induced when a conductor moves through a stationary [[MagneticField|magnetic field]]. As the conductor sweeps out area, the [[MagneticFlux|flux]] through the circuit changes, inducing an emf consistent with [[FaradaysLaw|Faraday's law]]. Equivalently, the magnetic force on the conductor's mobile charges separates them, building up a transverse voltage.

## Key Points
- Only relative motion matters: it makes no difference whether the conductor moves or the field moves.
- For a rod of length ℓ moving at speed v perpendicular to field B, the emf is simply `B ℓ v`.
- The same principle underlies the [[HallEffect|Hall effect]] (transverse voltage from magnetic force on moving charges).
- It scales dramatically with speed: surface measurements give microvolts, while an orbiting tethered satellite (huge v) can develop kilovolts.
- [[LenzsLaw]] sets the polarity; the magnetic drag on the resulting current converts kinetic energy to electrical energy.

## Equations
- `emf = B ℓ v`  (rod ⟂ to B, moving ⟂ to its length)
- `emf = N (ΔΦ/Δt)`  (general Faraday form)
- `F = q v B sin θ`  (force on a moving charge)
- `F = I ℓ B sin θ`  (force on a current-carrying conductor)
