# Alcohols and Ethers

Two oxygen-containing organic families distinguished by their [[OrganicFunctionalGroup|functional groups]]. (Named *AlcoholsAndEthers* to stay chemistry-explicit and avoid generic "alcohol"/"ether" collisions.)

## Alcohols (–OH)

An **alcohol** is a [[Hydrocarbon|hydrocarbon]] derivative in which an **–OH (hydroxyl) group** has replaced a hydrogen. The hydroxyl group is a **covalent** O–C bond — this distinguishes alcohols from **ionic hydroxides** (NaOH, KOH) that contain OH⁻ ions.

- **Naming:** replace the final "-e" of the hydrocarbon name with **"-ol"** and number the –OH carbon (e.g., **2-pentanol**, **2-methyl-2-pentanol**).
- **Ethanol (CH₃CH₂OH)** is made by **fermentation** of sugars by yeast — C₆H₁₂O₆(aq) → 2 C₂H₅OH(aq) + 2 CO₂(g) — or industrially by **acid-catalyzed [[AdditionReaction|addition]] of water to ethylene**: C₂H₄ + H₂O → C₂H₅OH (H₃O⁺ catalyst).
- **Polyhydric alcohols** (multiple –OH): **ethylene glycol** (1,2-ethanediol, antifreeze) and **glycerine** (1,2,3-propanetriol).
- Oxidation of alcohols is the gateway to the carbonyl families: primary → aldehyde → carboxylic acid; secondary → ketone (see [[AldehydesAndKetones]], [[CarboxylicAcidsAndEsters]]).

## Ethers (–O–)

An **ether** contains the functional group **–O–** between two hydrocarbon groups (R–O–R, identical or different).

- **Naming — two systems.** IUPAC: name the oxygen + smaller branch as an **alkoxy substituent** on the base chain. Common: name the two branches, then "ether." Example: CH₃–O–CH₂CH₃ = **methoxyethane** (IUPAC) / **ethylmethyl ether** (common).
- **Formation:** dehydration of two alcohol molecules. Ethanol + H₂SO₄ at 140 °C → **diethyl ether** + water: 2 C₂H₅OH → C₂H₅–O–C₂H₅ + H₂O.
- **Uses:** diethyl ether (volatile, flammable; a 19th-century anesthetic, now a solvent); **MTBE** (tert-butyl methyl ether, a gasoline oxygenate).

**Carbohydrates** (Cₘ(H₂O)ₙ) contain both hydroxyl and ether groups; monosaccharides link by **ether bonds** into disaccharides and polysaccharides.
