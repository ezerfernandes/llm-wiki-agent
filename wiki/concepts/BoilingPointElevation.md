# Boiling Point Elevation

**Boiling point elevation** is the [[ColligativeProperties|colligative property]] by which a solution boils at a higher temperature than the pure solvent. Because a nonvolatile solute lowers vapor pressure ([[RaoultsLaw|Raoult's law]]), a higher temperature is needed to raise the vapor pressure back to atmospheric.

$$\Delta T_b = i \, K_b \, m$$

- ΔT_b — boiling point elevation
- K_b — **ebullioscopic constant** (boiling point elevation constant), characteristic of the solvent, in °C·m⁻¹
- m — molality of all solute species
- i — [[VantHoffFactor|van 't Hoff factor]] (= 1 for nonelectrolytes; counts ions for electrolytes)

## Solvent Constants (K_b)
- Water: 0.512 °C·m⁻¹
- Benzene: 2.53 °C·m⁻¹
- Chloroform: 3.63 °C·m⁻¹

The elevation depends only on particle count, not chemical identity: 1 m sucrose and 1 m ethylene glycol solutions boil identically. Measuring ΔT_b for a known mass yields the solute's molar mass.
