## Definition
The Doppler effect is the change in observed frequency of a wave when the source and observer move relative to one another. The actual frequency change is the **Doppler shift**. First studied by [[ChristianDoppler]], it arises because relative motion alters the rate at which wave crests are emitted or intercepted, even though the wave travels at a fixed [[SpeedOfSound]] in the medium.

## Key Points
- Approach raises observed frequency (compressions bunch, shorter [[Frequency|wavelength]]); recession lowers it (compressions spread).
- Moving-source and moving-observer cases use different formulas, so their shifts are not symmetric for the same speed.
- If source and observer move together (zero relative velocity along the line joining them), there is no shift.
- Generalizes to all waves: light from receding galaxies shows a **redshift** used to estimate cosmic recession and the age of the universe (~14 billion years).

## Equations
- Moving source, stationary observer: `f_obs = f_s · v_w / (v_w ± v_s)` (− approach, + recession)
- Moving observer, stationary source: `f_obs = f_s · (v_w ± v_obs) / v_w` (+ approach, − recession)
- Both moving: `f_obs = [f_s · (v_w ± v_obs)/v_w] · v_w/(v_w ± v_s)`
