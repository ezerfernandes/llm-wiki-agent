## Definition
Tension (T) is a pulling force transmitted along a flexible connector such as a rope, cable, chain, or tendon. It acts parallel to the connector's length and can only pull — never push.

## Key Points
- A flexible connector pulls with equal magnitude but opposite directions on the objects at each end.
- For a stationary hanging mass, tension equals weight (static equilibrium).
- A perpendicular load applied at the midpoint of a nearly straight connector produces very large tension — the smaller the sag angle, the larger the tension (e.g., a tightrope, a vehicle-recovery chain).
- Tendons, brake cables, and suspension-bridge cables redirect tension around bends without losing magnitude.

## Equations
- Static hanging mass: T = mg
- Symmetric load supported by a sagging connector: T = w / (2 sin θ)
- General perpendicular force F⊥ at midpoint: T = F⊥ / (2 sin θ)
