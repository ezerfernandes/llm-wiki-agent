## Definition
**Conduction** is [[HeatTransfer|heat transfer]] through stationary matter by physical contact. Energy
moves through a material via molecular collisions — higher-energy molecules in the hot region transfer
kinetic energy to lower-energy molecules in the cold region — producing a net heat flow toward the
cooler side without any bulk motion of the medium.

## Key Points
- The conduction rate depends on three geometric/material factors: the temperature difference across
  the material, the cross-sectional area (more area → more collisions), and the thickness (more distance
  → slower transfer).
- It also depends strongly on the material's [[ThermalConductivity|thermal conductivity (k)]]: metals
  (copper, silver, aluminum) conduct well; insulators (wood, rubber, plastic, air, foam) conduct poorly.
- Insulation quality is rated by the **R-value**, R = d/k (thickness ÷ conductivity); larger R is a
  better insulator.
- Conduction is often coupled with a [[LatentHeat|latent-heat]] process — e.g. heat conducted into an
  ice box is then used to melt ice (Q = mLf), letting you compute the mass melted.
- Applications: building insulation (fiberglass, Styrofoam), cookware material choice, thermal clothing
  (blubber, down, wool, all of which trap low-conductivity air), and refrigeration.

## Equations
- Q/t = kA(T2 − T1)/d   (rate of conduction; W or kcal/s)
- R = d/k   (R-value of an insulating layer)
