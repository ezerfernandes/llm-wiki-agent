## Definition
Coulomb's law gives the magnitude of the electrostatic [[Force]] between two point [[ElectricCharge|charges]]: it is proportional to the product of the charge magnitudes and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them. Named after [[CharlesCoulomb]].

## Key Points
- The force acts along the line joining the charges: repulsive for like charges, attractive for unlike charges.
- It is an **inverse-square law** (F ∝ 1/r²), structurally identical to [[GravitationalField|Newtonian gravitation]] (g = GM/r²); the inverse-square exponent is verified experimentally to ~1 part in 10¹⁶.
- The electrostatic force is enormously stronger than gravity at small scales: for the electron–proton pair in hydrogen it exceeds gravity by ~2.27 × 10³⁹.
- Macroscopic objects are governed by gravity because they are nearly neutral, so Coulomb forces from + and − charges cancel.
- The electromagnetic force underlying it is one of the [[FundamentalForces]].

## Equations
- F = k |q₁ q₂| / r²
- Coulomb constant: k = 8.99 × 10⁹ N·m²/C²
