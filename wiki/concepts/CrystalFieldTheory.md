# Crystal Field Theory

**Crystal field theory (CFT)** explains the color, magnetism, and stability of [[CoordinationCompound|coordination compounds]] through the **electrostatic interaction between ligand electrons (treated as point charges) and the electrons in the unhybridized d orbitals** of the central metal. In its pure form CFT **ignores covalent metal–ligand bonding**.

## Octahedral splitting

When six ligands approach along the Cartesian axes, the five degenerate d orbitals split into two sets:
- **eg (higher energy):** d_z² and d_x²−y² — lobes point **directly at** the ligands → greater repulsion.
- **t₂g (lower energy):** d_xy, d_xz, d_yz — lobes point **between** ligands → less repulsion.

The gap between them is the **crystal field splitting energy, Δ_oct**. Its size grows with: stronger-field ligands (see [[SpectrochemicalSeries]]), higher metal charge, and larger d orbitals (3d < 4d < 5d).

## High-spin vs low-spin

Electron filling for d⁴–d⁷ configurations is a contest between **Δ_oct** and the **pairing energy P** (energy to put two electrons in one orbital):
- **Δ_oct > P (strong field) → low-spin:** electrons fill/pair in t₂g before touching eg, **minimizing unpaired electrons**. Example: [Fe(CN)₆]⁴⁻ (Fe²⁺ d⁶) — all six paired in t₂g, **diamagnetic**.
- **Δ_oct < P (weak field) → high-spin:** electrons singly occupy all five orbitals before pairing, **maximizing unpaired electrons**. Example: [Fe(H₂O)₆]²⁺ (Fe²⁺ d⁶) — four unpaired, **paramagnetic**.

Only **d⁴, d⁵, d⁶, d⁷** can be high- *or* low-spin; d¹–d³ and d⁸–d¹⁰ are fixed (e.g. Cr³⁺ d³ always 3 unpaired, Cu²⁺ d⁹ always 1 unpaired). This controls both [[ComplexColorAndMagnetism|color and magnetism]] and links to [[Paramagnetism]].

## Tetrahedral and square-planar fields

- **Tetrahedral:** ligands sit between the axes; the splitting **inverts** (the **e** set is lower, the **t₂** set higher) and is small — **Δ_tet ≈ (4/9)Δ_oct**. Because Δ_tet is small, **all tetrahedral complexes are high-spin**.
- **Square planar:** derived by removing the two trans (z-axis) ligands of an octahedron; d orbitals order (low → high): d_xz, d_yz < d_z² < d_xy < d_x²−y². Common for d⁸ metals.

CFT is introductory; "molecular orbital explanations... are required to understand fully the behavior of coordination complexes" (see [[MolecularOrbitalTheory]]).
