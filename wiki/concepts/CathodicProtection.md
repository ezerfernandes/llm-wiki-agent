# Cathodic Protection (Sacrificial Anode)

**Cathodic protection** is a method of preventing [[MetalCorrosion|corrosion]] by forcing the metal to be protected to act as the **cathode** of a [[GalvanicCell|galvanic cell]], so that it is reduced (protected) rather than oxidized.

## Sacrificial Anode
A **more active metal** — one with a **lower (more negative) [[StandardReductionPotential|standard reduction potential]]**, typically **zinc or magnesium** — is electrically connected to the structure. Because it oxidizes more readily, this metal becomes the **anode** and corrodes **in place of** the protected metal; hence it is called a **sacrificial anode**. It must be **replaced periodically** as it is consumed.

## Relation to Galvanization
**Galvanization** (zinc coating of iron/steel) is a built-in form of cathodic protection: the zinc layer both physically blocks air/water and, if scratched, sacrificially oxidizes to keep the underlying iron cathodic.
