# Thermal Energy

**Thermal energy** is the kinetic energy associated with the random motion of atoms and molecules in a substance. It is one form of **energy** — the capacity to supply heat or do work — alongside potential energy (due to position, composition, or condition) and kinetic energy (due to motion).

## Distinctions
- **Temperature** is a quantitative measure of "hot" or "cold" that reflects the *average* kinetic energy of the particles in a substance. Two samples at the same temperature have the same average particle kinetic energy.
- **[[HeatThermodynamics|Heat]] (q)** is the *transfer* of thermal energy between two bodies at different temperatures — not a property a body "contains."
- **Work (w)** is the process of causing matter to move against an opposing force; like heat, it is a means of transferring energy, not a stored quantity.

When thermal energy is added to a sample with no chemical reaction or phase change, its temperature rises; when removed, the temperature falls. Thermal energy flows spontaneously from a higher-temperature body to a lower-temperature one until **thermal equilibrium** (equal temperatures, equal average particle kinetic energy) is reached.

Per the **law of conservation of energy** (the [[FirstLawOfThermodynamics|first law of thermodynamics]]), energy can change form but is never created or destroyed.
