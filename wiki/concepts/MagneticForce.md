## Definition
The magnetic force is the [[Force]] that a [[MagneticField]] exerts on moving [[ElectricCharge]] — whether a free particle or the charge carriers inside a current-carrying conductor. It is always perpendicular to both the velocity (or current) and the field.

## Key Points
- On a single moving charge it is the [[LorentzForce]]: F = qvB sin θ.
- On a current-carrying wire it is the sum over all carriers, giving F = IlB sin θ; since charges cannot escape the conductor, this force is transmitted to the wire itself.
- Direction follows the right-hand rule (RHR-1): thumb = velocity/current, fingers = field, palm pushes in the force direction (reverse for negative charge).
- A current loop in a field feels a net torque (see [[ElectricMotor]]); parallel current-carrying wires exert magnetic force on each other (defining the ampere).

## Equations
- On a moving charge: F = qvB sin θ
- On a wire (length l, current I): F = IlB sin θ
- Force per unit length: F/l = IB sin θ
- Between parallel wires: F/l = μ₀I₁I₂ / (2πr)
