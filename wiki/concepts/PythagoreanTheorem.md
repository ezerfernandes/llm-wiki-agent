# Pythagorean Theorem

The **Pythagorean Theorem** relates the three sides of a **right** [[Triangle|triangle]]. OpenStax [[Prealgebra]] 2e (Chapter 9, [[prealgebra-2e-ch09-math-models-and-geometry]], §9.3) introduces it as the tool for finding a missing side of a right triangle, building directly on the [[SquareRoot|square root]].

## Statement
> In any right triangle, the sum of the squares of the two **legs** (`a`, `b`) equals the square of the **hypotenuse** (`c`):
> **a² + b² = c²**

The **hypotenuse** is the side opposite the 90° angle (the longest side); the **legs** form the right angle. The theorem applies **only** to right triangles.

## Finding an unknown side
Because squaring and the principal [[SquareRoot|square root]] are inverse operations:
- **Find the hypotenuse** (legs known): square both legs, add, take the positive square root.
  > c = √(a² + b²)
- **Find a leg** (hypotenuse and one leg known): square both, subtract the known leg's square from the hypotenuse's square, take the positive square root.
  > a = √(c² − b²)

Only the **principal (non-negative)** root is used, since a length cannot be negative — see [[SquareRoot]]. Non-perfect-square results are reported as rounded [[Decimal|decimal]] approximations with `≈`.

## Example
A right triangle with legs 3 and 4: c = √(3² + 4²) = √(9 + 16) = √25 = 5. Real-world uses include ladder reach, diagonal bracing of a gazebo, and distances measured along the two legs of a right angle — each solved with the [[ProblemSolvingStrategy|seven-step strategy]] (draw, name, translate to a² + b² = c², solve, check, answer).
