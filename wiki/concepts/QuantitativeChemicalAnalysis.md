# Quantitative Chemical Analysis

**Quantitative chemical analysis** determines how much of a substance is present in a sample by applying a reaction of **known stoichiometry**. Three classic methods are titration, gravimetric analysis, and combustion analysis — each is an application of [[ReactionStoichiometry|reaction stoichiometry]].

## Titration

A **buret** delivers measured volumes of a solution of known concentration (the **titrant**, a **standard solution**) into the sample containing the substance being measured (the **analyte**). They react with known stoichiometry.

- **Equivalence point** — where titrant and analyte have reacted in exact stoichiometric proportion.
- **End point** — the volume actually measured when the reaction *appears* complete (e.g. an **indicator** dye changes color). A well-designed method makes end point ≈ equivalence point.

**Example.** 50.00 mL HCl needs 35.23 mL of 0.250 M NaOH (HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O):
35.23 mL × (0.250 mmol/mL) × (1 mmol HCl / 1 mmol NaOH) ÷ 50.00 mL = **0.176 M HCl**. Such neutralizations connect to [[AcidBaseReaction|acid-base reactions]].

## Gravimetric Analysis

The analyte is converted by a physical/chemical change into a separable, weighable form; mass + known stoichiometry give the amount. Variants include **moisture determination** (mass before vs after heating off water) and **precipitation** (carry out a [[PrecipitationReaction|precipitation reaction]], then filter, dry, and weigh the solid).

**Example.** 0.4550 g mixture + excess Ba(NO₃)₂ gives 0.6168 g BaSO₄ (MgSO₄ + Ba(NO₃)₂ → BaSO₄ + Mg(NO₃)₂):
0.6168 g × (1 mol/233.43 g) × (1 mol MgSO₄/1 mol BaSO₄) × (120.37 g/mol) = 0.3181 g MgSO₄ → **69.91% MgSO₄**.

## Combustion Analysis

A weighed hydrocarbon-type sample is burned completely in O₂; for hydrocarbons the only products are **CO₂ and H₂O**, captured in separate preweighed absorbers (H₂O by Mg(ClO₄)₂; CO₂ by NaOH). Product masses give moles of C and H, hence the [[EmpiricalFormula|empirical formula]].

**Example.** 0.00126 g polyethylene → 0.00394 g CO₂ and 0.00161 g H₂O:
mol C = 8.95 × 10⁻⁵, mol H = 1.79 × 10⁻⁴ → H:C = 2:1 → empirical formula **CH₂**.
