# Electron

An **electron** is a negatively charged subatomic particle that occupies the space outside the [[AtomicNucleus|nucleus]] of an [[Atom]]. It carries the fundamental unit of negative charge, e = −1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ C (unit charge 1−), and is very light: mass ≈ 0.00055 amu ≈ 9.107 × 10⁻³¹ kg, more than a thousand times less massive than a [[Proton]] or [[Neutron]].

## Discovery

- [[JJThomson]] discovered the electron in the late 1800s via cathode-ray-tube experiments, using crossed electric and magnetic fields (velocity selector, v = E/B) to measure its **charge-to-mass ratio** (≈ −1.76 × 10¹¹ C/kg) and concluding atoms contain negatively charged constituents (the name "electron" was coined by George Stoney in 1891).
- [[RobertMillikan]] measured the electron's charge (≈ −1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ C) in his 1909 oil-drop experiment, balancing gravity against the electric force (m_drop·g = qₑE) and proving charge is quantized; combined with Thomson's ratio it yielded the electron's mass (mₑ ≈ 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ kg, ~1836× lighter than a [[Proton]]).

See [[EvolutionOfAtomicTheory]] for the full experimental sequence.

## Role in Chemistry

In a neutral atom, electrons equal [[Proton|protons]] in number. Gaining or losing electrons produces a charged [[ChemicalIon|ion]] (gain → anion; loss → cation). The transfer of electrons forms [[IonicCompound|ionic compounds]]; the sharing of electrons forms [[MolecularCompound|molecular (covalent) compounds]].

## Quantum Behavior (Ch 6)

Electrons are not point particles on fixed orbits; they exhibit [[WaveParticleDuality|wave-particle duality]] (de Broglie λ = h/mv), so their position and momentum cannot both be known exactly (the [[HeisenbergUncertaintyPrinciple|uncertainty principle]]). Each electron in an atom is described by a wavefunction and four [[QuantumNumbers|quantum numbers]] (n, l, m_l, m_s), occupying a probability region called an [[AtomicOrbital|atomic orbital]]. Their energies are [[EnergyQuantization|quantized]] (the [[BohrModel|Bohr model]] was the first model to capture this). The arrangement of electrons across orbitals — the [[ElectronConfiguration|electron configuration]], governed by the [[PauliExclusionPrinciple|Pauli]], [[AufbauPrinciple|Aufbau]], and [[HundsRule|Hund]] rules — determines an element's chemistry and its position in the [[PeriodicTable|periodic table]], including [[PeriodicTrends|periodic trends]] such as [[IonizationEnergy|ionization energy]] and [[ElectronAffinity|electron affinity]].
