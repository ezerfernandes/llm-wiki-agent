# Solubility

**Solubility** is "the maximum concentration that may be achieved under given conditions when the [[DissolutionProcess|dissolution process]] is at equilibrium" — a dynamic equilibrium in which solute dissolves and crystallizes at equal rates.

## Saturation Classes
- **Saturated** — solute concentration equals its solubility (at equilibrium).
- **Unsaturated** — concentration below the solubility.
- **Dilute** / **concentrated** — qualitatively low / high concentration.
- **Supersaturated** — concentration *exceeds* solubility; a relatively stable nonequilibrium state that precipitates excess solute when disturbed (see [[SupersaturatedSolution]]).

## Gases in Liquids
Gas solubility is set entirely by solute-solvent attractions ([[Solvation|solvation]]) since gases have no solute-solute interactions (O₂ ≈ 3× more soluble in water than He; ≈ 100× less than chloroform).
- **Temperature:** gas solubility usually **decreases** as temperature rises (cause of thermal-pollution fish kills from low dissolved O₂).
- **Pressure:** gas solubility **increases** with the gas's partial pressure — quantified by [[HenrysLaw|Henry's law]] (Cg = k·Pg). Opening a carbonated drink drops the pressure, supersaturating it so CO₂ escapes.

## Liquids in Liquids
Governed by [[LikeDissolvesLike|"like dissolves like"]]: liquids may be miscible, immiscible, or partially miscible depending on matching polarity.

## Solids in Liquids
Most solid solubilities **rise with temperature** (sugar, KNO₃, Na₂SO₄), though some **fall** (e.g., Ce₂(SO₄)₃). This temperature dependence is exploited to grow crystals and form supersaturated solutions (dissolve hot, cool undisturbed). Hand warmers use supersaturated sodium acetate triggered to crystallize.

## Quantitative solubility equilibrium (Ch 15)

For *slightly soluble ionic compounds*, the dissolution equilibrium is given a quantitative equilibrium constant — the [[SolubilityProduct|solubility product Ksp]] — from which the [[MolarSolubility|molar solubility]] can be calculated and vice versa. Whether a solid dissolves or precipitates is then predicted by comparing the [[ReactionQuotient|reaction quotient]] Q with Ksp. Solubility is suppressed by the [[CommonIonEffect|common-ion effect]] and can be dramatically increased by [[CoupledEquilibria|coupled equilibria]] (acid–base or complex formation).
