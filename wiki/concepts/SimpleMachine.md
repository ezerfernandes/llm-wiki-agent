## Definition
A simple machine is a device that multiplies or augments an applied force, usually at the cost of the distance over which the force is applied. Examples include levers, pulleys, inclined planes (ramps), and cranks.

## Key Points
- Simple machines obey conservation of energy: they cannot output more work than the energy supplied — force is gained only by trading off distance (or vice versa).
- Their force-multiplying ability is captured by [[MechanicalAdvantage]] (`MA = F_o / F_i`).
- A [[Lever]] multiplies force through [[Torque]] balance about its fulcrum.
- **Pulleys** multiply the applied force in integer multiples of the supporting tension depending on the number of supporting cable segments.
- **Inclined planes / ramps** reduce the force needed to raise a load compared with lifting it vertically.
- **Cranks** are rotational levers achieving high mechanical advantage through a ratio of radii (`MA = r_i / r_o`).
- A device can have MA < 1 (e.g., a shovel) to trade force for greater range or speed of motion.

## Equations
- `MA = F_o / F_i`
- Lever: `MA = l_i / l_o`
- Crank/wheel: `MA = r_i / r_o`
