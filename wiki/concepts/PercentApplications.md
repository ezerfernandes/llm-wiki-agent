# Percent Applications (Sales Tax, Commission, Discount & Mark-up)

The everyday money problems of OpenStax [[Prealgebra]] 2e (Chapter 6, [[prealgebra-2e-ch06-percents]], §6.3) are all instances of the [[Percent|percent equation]] **amount = percent × base**: a *rate* (a [[Percent|percent]] written as a [[Decimal|decimal]]) is multiplied by a base quantity to get a dollar amount, which is then added to or subtracted from the base. The chapter's general six-step strategy applies to each: (1) identify the unknown and pick a variable, (2) write a sentence with the given information, (3) translate it into an [[Equation|equation]], (4) solve, (5) check that the answer makes sense, (6) answer in a complete sentence.

## The four application formulas

| Application | Amount formula | Total / final-price formula | Sign |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sales tax** | sales tax = **tax rate × purchase price** | total cost = purchase price **+** sales tax | adds on |
| **Commission** | commission = **commission rate × total sales** | — | earned on sales |
| **Discount** | amount of discount = **discount rate × original price** | sale price = original price **−** discount | takes off |
| **Mark-up** | amount of mark-up = **mark-up rate × wholesale price** | list price = wholesale price **+** mark-up | adds on |

In every case the **rate is converted from a percent to a decimal before multiplying** (see [[Percent]]). The structure is identical; only the vocabulary and whether the amount is added or subtracted change.

## Vocabulary
- **Sales tax** — a percent of the purchase price collected on top of it; **total cost** = price + tax.
- **Commission** — a percent of total sales paid to a salesperson; the **rate of commission** sets the share.
- **Discount** — a percent *off* the original price; the **sale price** (always **less** than the original) = original − discount.
- **Mark-up** — the amount a retailer adds to the **wholesale price** (the price the retailer pays) to set the **list price** (the price the customer pays); the list price is always **more** than the wholesale price.

## Worked-example types
Finding the tax/commission/discount/mark-up amount from a rate and base; finding the total cost or sale/list price; and **working backward** to find an unknown *rate* given two prices (e.g. discount rate from original and sale price, or tax rate from tax and price) — the same "find the percent" inversion as in §6.2.
